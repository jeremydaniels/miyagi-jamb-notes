# JAMB Biology Notes: Local (Nigerian) Biomes

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 4 of the JAMB Biology syllabus: Ecology – Local (Nigerian) biomes. It covers five content items:

- a. Tropical rainforest.
- b. Guinea savanna (southern and northern).
- c. Sudan savanna.
- d. Desert.
- e. Highlands of montane forests and grasslands of the Obudu, Jos and Mambilla Plateaus.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Locate the biomes in the regions of Nigeria.
2. Apply knowledge of the features of the listed local biomes to determine the characteristics of different regions of Nigeria.

For each biome, these notes give its location (states and towns), climate (rainfall, length of the dry season and temperature), vegetation, typical plants and animals, human activities, and the adaptations of its organisms. Two biomes that are not in the syllabus list are also described briefly because they complete the map of Nigeria and are often asked about: the **mangrove and freshwater swamp forests** of the coast, and the **Sahel savanna** of the far north, which lies between the Sudan savanna and the desert.

**Important note on figures:** all rainfall and temperature figures in these notes are **approximate**. Different textbooks, atlases and weather records give different values and different boundaries between the belts, and rainfall changes from year to year. Learn the **pattern** (rainfall and the length of the rainy season decrease from south to north; temperature range increases northwards; the plateaus are cooler) rather than exact numbers.

## What is a biome?

A **biome** is a large natural region of the Earth with a characteristic **climate**, a characteristic type of **vegetation** (plant community) and the **animals** adapted to it. Biomes are usually named after their main vegetation, for example tropical rainforest, savanna (tropical grassland) and desert.

**Why biomes differ:** the type of biome in a place is decided mainly by its **climate**, especially:

1. **Rainfall:** the total amount and, very importantly, the **length of the dry season**.
2. **Temperature:** controlled by latitude and altitude.
3. Other factors, such as soil, fire, grazing and human activities (topic C1).

### Why Nigeria has several biomes

Nigeria stretches from the Atlantic coast (about 4° N) to the edge of the Sahara (about 14° N). Two air masses control its climate:

- The moist **south-west wind** from the Atlantic Ocean brings rain. The south receives it for most of the year.
- The dry, dusty **north-east trade wind (the harmattan)** from the Sahara brings the dry season. The north is under its influence for most of the year.

As a result:

- **Rainfall decreases from south to north**, from over 3,000 mm in parts of the Niger Delta to under 500 mm near Lake Chad.
- **The rainy season gets shorter** from about 8–10 months in the south to about 3–4 months in the far north.
- **Relative humidity decreases** northwards.
- **The temperature range** (difference between hot and cool months, and between day and night) **increases** northwards.

So the vegetation changes in **belts running roughly from west to east**, one after the other from the coast inland:

**Mangrove swamp → freshwater swamp forest → tropical rainforest → (derived savanna) → southern Guinea savanna → northern Guinea savanna → Sudan savanna → Sahel savanna → (desert, beyond Nigeria's northern border)**

The **highlands** (Jos, Mambilla and Obudu Plateaus) break this pattern: because they are high and cool, they carry **montane** forests and grasslands.

## Biome a: Tropical rainforest

### Location

The tropical rainforest belt lies in the **south** of Nigeria, inland from the coastal swamps. It stretches from the western border to the eastern border in a band roughly 100–250 km wide (its width varies, and much of it has now been cleared).

**States** with rainforest (or areas that were once rainforest): Ogun, Ondo, Osun, Ekiti (south), Oyo (south), Lagos (inland parts), Edo, Delta, Imo, Abia, Anambra, Ebonyi (parts), Akwa Ibom, Rivers (inland parts), Bayelsa (inland parts) and Cross River.

**Towns:** Ibadan (at the northern edge), Ijebu-Ode, Akure, Benin City, Warri (edge of the swamp), Owerri, Umuahia, Uyo, Calabar, Ikom.

**Protected areas:** **Okomu National Park** (Edo State), **Cross River National Park** (Oban and Okwangwo divisions), and forest reserves such as **Omo Forest Reserve** (Ogun State).

### Climate

| Feature | Approximate value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual rainfall | About **1,500 to over 2,500 mm** (more than 3,000 mm in parts of the south-east and near the coast) |
| Rainy season | Long: about **8 to 10 months** (roughly March to November), often with a short, drier break in August (the "August break") in the south-west |
| Dry season | Short: about **2 to 3 months** (December to February) |
| Temperature | High all year, **mean about 25–28 °C**, with a **small annual range** (only a few degrees) |
| Relative humidity | **High** all year (often 75–95%) |

### Vegetation

The tropical rainforest has the richest variety of plant and animal species of any Nigerian biome.

**Main features of the vegetation:**

1. **Tall trees growing close together**, many over 30 m high. A few **emergent** trees rise to 40–60 m above the rest.
2. **Evergreen:** the forest stays green all year, because there is no long dry season. Individual trees shed leaves at different times.
3. **Stratification (layers):** the forest is arranged in layers:
   - **Emergent layer:** the tallest trees, standing above the canopy (e.g. silk cotton, iroko, obeche).
   - **Canopy (upper tree layer):** the crowns of most tall trees meet to form a continuous **closed canopy** that intercepts most of the sunlight.
   - **Lower tree layer (understorey):** smaller, shade-tolerant trees and young trees.
   - **Shrub layer:** shrubs and saplings.
   - **Ground (herb) layer:** ferns, mosses, herbs and seedlings in deep shade. The forest floor is dark and damp, with **little undergrowth** in undisturbed forest, but a thick layer of **leaf litter**.
4. **Buttress roots:** large, plank-like roots at the base of many tall trees, which support them in the shallow soil.
5. **Lianas (woody climbers)** climb up tree trunks to reach the light.
6. **Epiphytes** (orchids, ferns, mosses) grow on branches.
7. **Broad leaves with drip tips** (pointed tips that let rainwater run off quickly).
8. **Thin, smooth bark** (no need for protection against fire).
9. **Many species mixed together:** each species is scattered; there are few pure stands of one tree.

**Typical plants:**

| Plant | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Iroko** (*Milicia excelsa*) | Large, valuable timber tree |
| **Obeche** (*Triplochiton scleroxylon*) | Tall, fast-growing timber tree with light wood |
| **African mahogany** (*Khaya* species) | Valuable timber |
| **Sapele** (*Entandrophragma cylindricum*) | Valuable timber |
| **Afara** (*Terminalia superba*) | Tall timber tree |
| **Silk cotton tree** (*Ceiba pentandra*) | Huge emergent with buttresses; seeds with cotton-like hairs |
| **Oil palm** (*Elaeis guineensis*) | Native palm; very important crop |
| **Kola** (*Cola nitida* and *Cola acuminata*) | Native trees; kola nuts |
| **Ferns, mosses and orchids** | On the forest floor and as epiphytes |

### Animals

- **Primates:** many monkeys (mona, putty-nosed and red-capped monkeys, colobus monkeys), drills (in the Cross River forests), **chimpanzees**, and the very rare **Cross River gorilla** (in forests near the Cameroon border).
- **Other mammals:** forest elephants (now rare), duikers (small forest antelopes), bush pigs, giant pouched rats, squirrels, pangolins, porcupines, leopards (rare), fruit bats.
- **Birds:** hornbills, grey parrots, turacos, sunbirds, owls, eagles.
- **Reptiles:** Gaboon viper, green mamba, pythons, chameleons, forest tortoises.
- **Amphibians:** many frogs and toads.
- **Invertebrates:** giant African land snails (*Achatina*), millipedes, termites, driver ants, butterflies, beetles, mosquitoes.

### Human activities

1. **Lumbering (timber production):** for furniture, building and export; centred in Ondo, Ogun, Edo, Delta and Cross River States. Sawmills in Sapele, Benin City and elsewhere.
2. **Tree crops (cash crops):** **cocoa** (Ondo, Osun, Ogun, Oyo, Ekiti, Cross River), **oil palm** (south-east and south-south), **rubber** (Edo, Delta), **kola** (south-west).
3. **Food crops:** cassava, yam, cocoyam, plantain, banana, maize, rice in swampy valleys, vegetables.
4. **Hunting** for bushmeat.
5. **Gathering** of forest products: firewood, chewing sticks, snails, mushrooms, leaves for wrapping, medicinal plants.
6. **Crude oil and gas exploration** (in Delta, Rivers and other states).
7. **Urbanisation:** many large cities (Lagos, Ibadan, Benin City, Port Harcourt) are in or near this belt.

**Effects:** large-scale clearing, farming and burning have destroyed much of the original rainforest. In many places it has been replaced by **secondary forest** (regrowth), farmland, plantations and **derived savanna**.

### Adaptations of organisms in the rainforest

| Organism | Adaptation | How it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tall trees | Buttress roots | Support tall trees in shallow soil |
| Canopy trees | Broad leaves with drip tips; waxy surfaces | Shed heavy rain quickly, so fungi and algae do not grow on the leaf |
| Lianas | Climbing stems with hooks or tendrils | Reach the light without building thick trunks |
| Epiphytes | Aerial roots with velamen; water-storing leaves | Get water and light high up without soil |
| Forest-floor plants | Large, thin, dark green leaves | Trap as much of the dim light as possible (shade plants) |
| Monkeys, squirrels | Grasping hands, long tails, claws | Move about in the canopy |
| Chameleons, tree frogs | Prehensile tail; adhesive toe pads | Grip branches and leaves |
| Duikers | Small size, arched back | Move quickly through dense undergrowth |
| Many animals | Camouflage (e.g. Gaboon viper's pattern matches leaf litter) | Hide from prey and predators |
| Fruit bats, hornbills, monkeys | Feed on fruits and disperse seeds | Fit the forest's year-round supply of fruit |

## Mangrove and freshwater swamp forests (not in the syllabus list)

These coastal biomes lie to the **south** of the rainforest, along the coast and in the Niger Delta. They are covered in detail as habitats in topic C3; a short summary is given here to complete the map.

| Feature | Mangrove (saltwater) swamp forest | Freshwater swamp forest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Location** | Along the coast, in brackish lagoons, creeks and estuaries: Lagos Lagoon fringes, the **Niger Delta** (Rivers, Bayelsa, Delta, Akwa Ibom) and the Cross River estuary (Calabar) | Inland of the mangroves, in the Niger Delta and in the floodplains of the lower Niger and other rivers |
| **Water** | Brackish (salty), tidal | Fresh water; seasonally flooded |
| **Rainfall** | Very high (often over 2,500–3,000 mm in the Niger Delta) | High |
| **Vegetation** | Mangroves: red mangrove (*Rhizophora*) with stilt roots and vivipary; white mangrove (*Avicennia*) with pneumatophores; Nypa palm | *Raphia* palms (raffia, used for palm wine and fibre), oil palms, swamp trees, climbers |
| **Animals** | Mudskippers, fiddler crabs, periwinkles, oysters, shrimps, fish, crocodiles, manatees, herons | Fish, crocodiles, monkeys, many birds, snakes |
| **Human activities** | Fishing, shrimping, oyster and periwinkle collection, cutting mangrove wood for fuel; oil and gas production | Fishing, raffia palm wine tapping, rice and other farming on drier land |

## Derived savanna (transition zone)

Between the rainforest and the Guinea savanna lies a belt of **derived savanna** (also called the **forest–savanna mosaic**). It was once forest, but repeated clearing for farms, bush burning and grazing have turned it into grassland with scattered trees and patches of forest. It is found, for example, around Ibadan, Oyo, Ogbomoso, Enugu, Nsukka and Abakaliki.

**Textbook variation:** some textbooks treat the derived savanna as a separate belt; others include it in the southern Guinea savanna. The syllabus does not list it separately.

## Biome b: Guinea savanna (southern and northern)

### General features of savanna

**Savanna** is tropical grassland: a cover of **grasses** with **scattered trees and shrubs**, found where there is a distinct **wet season** followed by a **long dry season**. The trees are too few to form a closed canopy, so grasses get enough light to grow. **Fire** in the dry season and **grazing** help to keep it open.

The **Guinea savanna** is the **largest vegetation belt in Nigeria**, covering much of the **middle belt** (central Nigeria). It is divided into a wetter **southern** part and a drier **northern** part.

### Southern Guinea savanna

**Location:** the southern half of the Guinea savanna, just north of the rainforest and derived savanna.

- **States:** northern Oyo, Kwara, Kogi, the Federal Capital Territory (Abuja), Nasarawa, Benue, southern Niger State, parts of Plateau (lowlands), Taraba (south) and Enugu/Ebonyi (north).
- **Towns:** Ilorin, Lokoja, Makurdi, Abuja, Lafia, Bida (near the boundary), Ogbomoso (near the boundary), Shaki.
- **Protected areas:** Old Oyo National Park (Oyo State); the Borgu sector of Kainji Lake National Park (Niger and Kwara States) lies in the Guinea savanna.

**Climate (approximate):**

| Feature | Approximate value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual rainfall | About **1,100 to 1,500 mm** |
| Rainy season | About **6 to 7 months** (roughly April to October) |
| Dry season | About **5 to 6 months** |
| Temperature | Mean about **26–29 °C**; hottest months (February to April) often above 33 °C by day |
| Relative humidity | High in the rainy season, low in the dry season |

**Vegetation:**

- **Tall grasses**, often **2 to 3 m** high or more, such as **elephant grass** (*Pennisetum purpureum*), **gamba grass** (*Andropogon gayanus*), *Hyparrhenia* and guinea grass (*Panicum maximum*).
- **Trees fairly close together**, often 10–15 m tall, some with broad leaves, giving a **woodland** appearance in places.
- **Gallery (fringing) forests**: strips of forest along rivers and streams, where there is water all year.
- Many trees are **deciduous** (shed their leaves in the dry season) and **fire-resistant**.

**Typical trees:** **shea tree** (*Vitellaria paradoxa*), **locust bean** (*Parkia biglobosa*), *Daniellia oliveri*, *Lophira lanceolata*, *Afzelia africana*, *Terminalia* species, *Burkea africana*, *Prosopis africana*, and planted trees such as **mango**, **cashew** and **teak**.

### Northern Guinea savanna

**Location:** the northern half of the Guinea savanna.

- **States:** Kaduna (southern and central), northern Niger, Plateau (lowlands), southern Bauchi, Gombe (south), Adamawa and Taraba (north), southern Kebbi and Zamfara.
- **Towns:** Kaduna, Zaria, Minna, Kontagora, Bauchi (near the northern boundary), Yola.
- **Protected areas:** **Yankari Game Reserve** (now Yankari National Park, Bauchi State) lies in the northern part of the Guinea savanna, close to the Sudan savanna.

**Climate (approximate):**

| Feature | Approximate value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual rainfall | About **900 to 1,200 mm** |
| Rainy season | About **5 to 6 months** (roughly May to October) |
| Dry season | About **6 to 7 months**, with harmattan in December–February |
| Temperature | Mean about **25–29 °C**; larger range than the south; harmattan nights cool |

**Vegetation:**

- **Grasses shorter** than in the southern Guinea savanna, often **1.5 to 2 m**.
- **Trees more widely spaced**, shorter, with smaller leaves, many with **thick, corky bark**.
- The tree ***Isoberlinia doka*** (and *Isoberlinia tomentosa*) is very characteristic of the northern Guinea savanna woodlands; *Monotes*, *Uapaca*, shea, locust bean and *Daniellia* are also common.

### Southern and northern Guinea savanna compared

| Feature | Southern Guinea savanna | Northern Guinea savanna |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position | Nearer the forest (south) | Nearer the Sudan savanna (north) |
| Rainfall (approx.) | About 1,100–1,500 mm | About 900–1,200 mm |
| Dry season | About 5–6 months | About 6–7 months |
| Grass height | Tall (2–3 m or more) | Shorter (about 1.5–2 m) |
| Trees | Taller, closer together, broader leaves; more gallery forest | Shorter, more widely spaced; *Isoberlinia* woodland common |
| Typical towns | Ilorin, Makurdi, Lokoja, Abuja | Kaduna, Zaria, Minna, Yola |
| Main crops | Yam, cassava, maize, rice, sorghum, soya bean | Sorghum, maize, millet, groundnut, cotton, ginger (southern Kaduna), soya bean |

### Animals of the Guinea savanna

- **Grazing mammals:** antelopes (kob, waterbuck, hartebeest, roan antelope, duikers, bushbuck), buffalo, warthogs.
- **Primates:** baboons, patas monkeys, tantalus (green) monkeys.
- **Carnivores:** lions (now very rare, mainly in Kainji Lake National Park and Yankari), leopards, hyenas, jackals, civets, mongooses.
- **Large mammals:** elephants (in Yankari), hippopotamus (in rivers).
- **Rodents:** grasscutters (cane rats), ground squirrels, rats.
- **Birds:** guinea fowl, francolins, bustards, hornbills, weaver birds, vultures, hawks and kites.
- **Reptiles:** crocodiles (rivers), monitor lizards, *Agama*, cobras, puff adders, pythons.
- **Insects:** termites (with many large mounds), grasshoppers, locusts, tsetse flies (in the wetter parts), mosquitoes.
- **Domestic animals:** cattle (mainly in the northern part and in the dry season), goats, sheep.

### Human activities in the Guinea savanna

1. **Arable farming** of food crops: **yam** (the Guinea savanna, especially Benue, is a major yam-growing area), cassava, maize, sorghum (guinea corn), millet, rice (in river valleys, e.g. the Niger and Benue floodplains), soya bean, beniseed (sesame), groundnut. Benue State is often called "the food basket of the nation".
2. **Cattle rearing** by Fulani herders, especially in the drier northern part and in the dry season (when herds move south).
3. **Hunting** of grasscutters and antelopes, often using bush fires.
4. **Collection of tree products:** shea nuts (for shea butter), locust bean (for "iru" or "dawadawa" seasoning), firewood and charcoal.
5. **Fishing** in rivers and lakes (Kainji, Jebba).
6. **Hydroelectric power** at the Kainji, Jebba and Shiroro dams.

### Adaptations of organisms in the Guinea savanna

| Organism | Adaptation | How it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grasses | Underground stems (rhizomes), growing points at the base, dormancy in the dry season | Survive fire, grazing and drought; regrow quickly after the rains |
| Trees (shea, locust bean) | Thick, corky, fire-resistant bark | Protect the living tissues from bush fires |
| Many trees | Deciduous (shed leaves in the dry season) | Reduce water loss by transpiration in the dry season |
| Trees | Deep tap roots | Reach water deep in the soil |
| Antelopes | Long legs, speed, living in herds; keen senses | Escape predators in open country |
| Grazing animals | Migration with the seasons | Find fresh grass and water in the dry season |
| Grasshoppers, birds | Brown and green camouflage | Hide in the grass |
| Termites | Build mounds | Control temperature and humidity inside the nest |

## Biome c: Sudan savanna

### Location

The Sudan savanna lies **north of the Guinea savanna**, covering most of the **far north-west and north-central** parts of Nigeria and part of the north-east.

- **States:** Kano, Katsina, Jigawa, Sokoto, Zamfara, Kebbi (north and centre), northern Kaduna, northern Bauchi, Gombe (north), southern Yobe and southern Borno.
- **Towns:** Kano, Katsina, Sokoto, Gusau, Birnin Kebbi, Dutse, Azare, Potiskum, Maiduguri (near the boundary with the Sahel).

### Climate (approximate)

| Feature | Approximate value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual rainfall | About **500 to 1,000 mm** |
| Rainy season | Short: about **4 to 5 months** (roughly June to September or October) |
| Dry season | Long: about **7 to 8 months**, with strong **harmattan** (dry, dusty north-east wind) from about November to February |
| Temperature | **High**, with a **large range**: hottest months (March to May) often reach **40 °C** or more by day; harmattan nights can fall to around **10–15 °C** |
| Relative humidity | **Low**, especially in the harmattan (it can fall below 20%) |

### Vegetation

1. **Short grasses**, mostly about **1 to 1.5 m** high, which dry up and turn brown in the long dry season.
2. **Few, widely scattered trees**, usually short (often under 10 m), many **thorny** and with **small leaves**.
3. Trees are mostly **deciduous**; some, such as the baobab, store water.
4. Much of the Sudan savanna is **farmed** or grazed, so the original vegetation has been heavily changed; trees that are useful to people (such as the baobab, shea, locust bean, tamarind and *Acacia albida*) are often left standing on farmland ("parkland").

**Typical plants:**

| Plant | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Baobab** (*Adansonia digitata*) | Huge, fat trunk that stores water; leaves used in soup ("kuka"); deciduous |
| ***Acacia*** species (e.g. *Faidherbia albida*, gum arabic tree *Acacia senegal*) | Thorny trees with small, divided leaves; *Acacia senegal* produces gum arabic; *Faidherbia albida* is unusual in losing its leaves in the rainy season and keeping them in the dry season |
| **Doum palm** (*Hyphaene thebaica*) | A branched palm of dry areas; its fruits are eaten |
| **Desert date** (*Balanites aegyptiaca*) | Thorny tree with edible fruits |
| **Tamarind** (*Tamarindus indica*) | Fruit used for drinks and cooking |
| **Neem** (*Azadirachta indica*) | Introduced from India; widely planted for shade, windbreaks and medicine |
| **Shea and locust bean** | Mainly in the southern part of the Sudan savanna |

**Textbook variation:** *Faidherbia albida* is listed in older books as *Acacia albida*.

### Animals

- **Domestic animals:** large herds of **cattle** (the Sudan savanna is a major cattle-rearing area, partly because there are few tsetse flies), **sheep, goats, camels** (in the drier north), **donkeys and horses**.
- **Wild mammals:** gazelles, hartebeest, hyenas, jackals, hares, gerbils and other rodents, ground squirrels, patas monkeys. Many large wild mammals have become rare because of hunting and farming.
- **Birds:** guinea fowl, bustards, weaver birds, quelea birds (serious pests of grain crops), vultures, ostriches (now very rare in the wild).
- **Reptiles:** monitor lizards, *Agama*, spitting cobras, puff adders, tortoises.
- **Insects:** locusts and grasshoppers, termites, ants, beetles.

### Human activities

1. **Arable farming:** **millet**, **sorghum (guinea corn)**, **cowpea**, **groundnut** (Kano was famous for its "groundnut pyramids" in the 1950s and 1960s), **cotton**, **sesame (beniseed)**, **onions**, tomatoes and peppers.
2. **Irrigation farming** in river valleys and floodplains (fadama) during the dry season, for rice, wheat, onions, tomatoes and sugar cane; for example, the Kano River Irrigation Project and the Hadejia Valley Irrigation Project.
3. **Livestock rearing:** cattle, sheep, goats and camels; **hides and skins**; leather work (Kano and Sokoto are famous for leather goods).
4. **Collection of gum arabic** from *Acacia senegal*.
5. **Firewood collection** and charcoal production (a major cause of tree loss).

**Problems:** drought, desertification (the southward spread of desert-like conditions), overgrazing, soil erosion by wind, and conflict over land and water.

### Adaptations of organisms in the Sudan savanna

| Organism | Adaptation | How it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baobab | Thick trunk that stores water; leafless in the dry season | Survives the long dry season |
| *Acacia* trees | Small leaves; thorns; deep roots | Reduce water loss; deter browsing animals; reach deep water |
| Grasses | Short; die back to underground parts in the dry season; seeds dormant | Survive drought and fire |
| Millet and sorghum (crops) | Short life cycle; drought-tolerant; deep roots | Produce grain in the short rainy season |
| Camels and goats | Can go long without water; eat thorny shrubs | Survive in dry areas with little pasture |
| Gerbils and other rodents | Burrow by day; very concentrated urine; nocturnal | Avoid heat and conserve water |
| Many animals | Sandy-brown colours | Camouflage in dry grass and sand |
| Many insects and snails | Aestivation | Survive the dry season |

## Sahel savanna (not in the syllabus list)

The **Sahel savanna** lies **between the Sudan savanna and the desert**. The word *Sahel* comes from Arabic for "shore" or "edge" (the edge of the Sahara).

**Note on the syllabus:** the syllabus does not list the Sahel savanna, but goes straight from the Sudan savanna to the desert. Most geography and biology textbooks include the Sahel as a separate belt in the extreme north and north-east of Nigeria. **Textbook variation:** some books include the Sahel with the Sudan savanna, and some treat it as "semi-desert".

- **Location:** a narrow belt in the **far north-east** of Nigeria, especially **northern Borno and northern Yobe**, around **Lake Chad**, and small areas along the northern border of Sokoto, Katsina and Jigawa States. Towns: Nguru, Gashua, Geidam, Monguno, Malam Fatori.
- **Climate (approximate):** rainfall **less than about 500–600 mm**, falling in a short rainy season of about **2 to 4 months**; a very long dry season of about **8 to 10 months**; very hot days (often above 40 °C before the rains); cold harmattan nights; very low humidity.
- **Vegetation:** very **short, sparse grasses** (often less than 1 m high, with bare ground between tufts), scattered **thorny shrubs and short trees** (*Acacia*, *Balanites*, doum palm, *Ziziphus*), and **date palms** where there is water.
- **Animals:** camels, goats, sheep and cattle (herded); gazelles (now very rare), jackals, foxes, gerbils, desert locusts, scorpions, and many water birds around Lake Chad.
- **Human activities:** nomadic herding, millet farming, fishing and farming around Lake Chad, irrigation, collection of gum arabic.
- **Main problem:** **desertification**, made worse by drought, overgrazing and tree-cutting.

## Biome d: Desert

### Where is the desert?

A **desert** is a region with **very low rainfall** (usually less than about **250 mm** a year), very **high evaporation**, and **sparse** vegetation.

**Nigeria does not have a true desert.** The **Sahara Desert**, the largest hot desert in the world, lies **north of Nigeria**, mainly in the Niger Republic, Chad, Algeria and Libya. However:

- the Sahel and the far north of Nigeria border the desert and have some desert-like conditions in the long dry season;
- the **harmattan** wind blows dust from the Sahara across Nigeria every dry season;
- **desertification** is pushing desert-like conditions southwards into northern Nigerian states such as Sokoto, Kebbi, Zamfara, Katsina, Kano, Jigawa, Yobe and Borno. **Sand dunes** have formed in parts of the far north (for example, in northern Yobe).

**Textbook variation:** some textbooks say Nigeria has "semi-desert" or "desert-like" vegetation in the extreme north-east; others say the desert biome lies entirely outside Nigeria. Both agree that the extreme north is the driest part of the country and is threatened by desertification. JAMB includes the desert so that you can explain the features of this extreme biome and its effect on northern Nigeria.

### Climate of the desert (approximate)

| Feature | Approximate value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual rainfall | **Less than about 250 mm**; some years almost none; rain is irregular and falls in short, heavy showers |
| Temperature | **Extreme:** days often above **40–45 °C** in the hot season; nights can be cold, sometimes near freezing in the cool season. The **daily temperature range is very large** |
| Relative humidity | **Very low** |
| Evaporation | **Very high**, much more than the rainfall |
| Wind | Strong, dry winds; sandstorms |
| Soil | Sandy or stony, with little humus; sometimes salty |

### Vegetation

- **Very sparse:** most of the ground is bare sand or rock.
- **Xerophytes** (plants adapted to dry conditions): thorny shrubs, succulents, short-lived grasses and herbs.
- **Ephemerals:** plants whose seeds lie dormant for months or years and then germinate, grow, flower and set seed within a few weeks after a rare shower of rain.
- **Oases:** where underground water comes to the surface, there are **date palms** (*Phoenix dactylifera*) and crops.

**Typical plants:** *Acacia* species, desert date (*Balanites*), date palm, doum palm, succulent spurges (*Euphorbia* species), tough desert grasses.

**Note:** cacti (such as the prickly pear, *Opuntia*), often shown in pictures of deserts, are native to the Americas. In African deserts, their place is taken by **succulent euphorbias**, which look similar. Some cacti have been introduced to Africa and are grown in northern Nigeria as hedges.

### Animals

**Camel** (dromedary), **fennec fox**, **jerboas** and **gerbils** (desert rodents), addax and dorcas gazelles (now very rare), **desert locusts**, **scorpions**, beetles, lizards, snakes such as the horned viper, and birds such as sandgrouse.

### Human activities

Nomadic herding of camels, goats and sheep; farming at oases (dates, millet, vegetables); trans-Saharan trade and transport (historically by camel caravans).

### Adaptations of desert organisms

**Plants (xerophytes):**

1. **Leaves reduced to spines or very small**, or shed in the dry season, reducing the surface for transpiration.
2. **Thick, waxy cuticle** and **sunken stomata**, often on the inner side of rolled leaves, reducing water loss.
3. **Succulent stems or leaves** that **store water** (euphorbias).
4. **Very deep tap roots** (to reach underground water) or **wide, shallow roots** (to absorb rain quickly before it evaporates).
5. **Stomata that open at night**, when it is cooler and less water is lost (in many succulents).
6. **Ephemeral life cycle:** complete the life cycle quickly after rain; seeds survive long droughts.
7. **Hairy or light-coloured surfaces** that reflect sunlight and trap moist air.

**Animals:**

| Animal | Adaptation | How it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Camel** | Hump stores **fat** (not water), which provides energy when food is scarce; can lose a large proportion of its body water and then drink a very large volume quickly; produces very concentrated urine and dry faeces; body temperature can rise during the day instead of sweating; long eyelashes and closable nostrils keep out sand; broad, padded feet do not sink in sand | Survives long periods without water or food in hot, sandy places |
| **Fennec fox** | Very large ears that radiate heat; nocturnal; burrows; pale fur; furry soles | Loses heat, avoids daytime heat, walks on hot sand |
| **Jerboas and gerbils** | Live in cool burrows by day; nocturnal; very concentrated urine; obtain water from seeds; long hind legs for jumping | Conserve water and avoid heat |
| **Desert locusts** | Can migrate long distances with the wind; eggs survive in dry sand until rain | Find food after rains; survive drought |
| **Lizards and snakes** | Scaly skin; excrete uric acid (little water lost); burrow in sand | Reduce water loss; avoid heat |
| **Many animals** | Sandy colours | Camouflage |

## Biome e: Highlands (montane forests and grasslands) of the Obudu, Jos and Mambilla Plateaus

### What is a montane biome?

**Montane** means "of the mountains". A **montane biome** is found at high **altitude** on mountains and plateaus. Because the temperature falls as altitude increases (roughly 6–6.5 °C for every 1,000 m; topic C1), the highlands are **much cooler** than the lowlands around them, and they are often **misty** and **windy**. Their vegetation is therefore different from that of the surrounding lowland forest or savanna, even though they are in the same latitude. The typical vegetation is:

- **Montane grassland:** short grasses covering the open plateau tops and slopes.
- **Montane forest:** patches of forest in sheltered valleys, gorges and along streams (gallery forests), where there is more moisture and protection from wind and fire.

Nigeria's three main highland areas named in the syllabus are described below. **Heights are approximate and vary slightly between sources.**

### 1. The Jos Plateau

- **Location:** **Plateau State**, in the centre of Nigeria, with parts in Bauchi and Kaduna States. Towns: **Jos**, Bukuru, Pankshin, Vom, Barkin Ladi.
- **Altitude:** the plateau averages about **1,200 m** above sea level, rising to roughly **1,800 m** in the Shere Hills near Jos.
- **Climate (approximate):** **cool**: mean annual temperature in Jos is only about **21–23 °C**, much cooler than the lowlands around it; harmattan nights can fall to around **10 °C** or lower. Rainfall roughly **1,300–1,500 mm**, falling from about April to October. Strong winds; misty mornings in the rainy season.
- **Vegetation:** mainly **montane grassland** with short grasses and scattered shrubs and trees on rocky hills; **gallery forests** in valleys; large areas have been farmed or changed by mining. Planted **eucalyptus** and **pine** trees are common around Jos.
- **Animals:** baboons and patas monkeys on rocky hills, rock hyraxes, small antelopes, birds, including the **Rock Firefinch** (*Lagonosticta sanguinodorsalis*), a small bird described by scientists only in 1998, known mainly from the Jos Plateau area.
- **Human activities:**
  - **Temperate crops and vegetables:** the Jos Plateau is famous for **Irish potato**, cabbage, carrot, lettuce, green beans, tomatoes and other vegetables, sent to markets all over Nigeria.
  - **Cattle rearing and dairying:** the cool climate and fewer tsetse flies suit cattle; the National Veterinary Research Institute is at **Vom**.
  - **Tin and columbite mining:** large-scale tin mining in the 1900s left many mining ponds and spoil heaps, which changed the vegetation over wide areas.
  - **Tourism:** the cool climate and rock scenery (for example, the Jos Wildlife Park and Assop Falls).

### 2. The Mambilla Plateau

- **Location:** **Taraba State**, in the east of Nigeria, along the border with Cameroon. Towns: **Gembu** (the main town), Nguroje.
- **Altitude:** averages about **1,500 m**, the highest plateau in Nigeria. **Chappal Waddi** (about **2,419 m**), on the Nigeria–Cameroon border in the Gashaka-Gumti National Park area next to the plateau, is the **highest point in Nigeria**.
- **Climate (approximate):** **cool** all year; mean temperatures roughly **18–23 °C**, and cold nights; high rainfall (higher than the surrounding savanna); often misty.
- **Vegetation:** extensive **montane grassland** with short grasses on the rolling plateau, and patches of **montane forest** in valleys and on steep slopes. Much of the original forest has been replaced by grassland because of grazing and burning.
- **Animals:** wildlife in the nearby **Gashaka-Gumti National Park** (Nigeria's largest national park) includes chimpanzees, monkeys, antelopes and many birds; the plateau itself is mainly used for grazing cattle.
- **Human activities:**
  - **Tea cultivation:** the **Mambilla Plateau** is the main tea-growing area in Nigeria (the Kakara tea estate). Tea needs a cool, wet climate.
  - **Cattle rearing:** large herds of cattle graze the grasslands; the cool climate and absence of tsetse flies favour cattle.
  - **Temperate crops and vegetables:** Irish potato, vegetables, coffee.

### 3. The Obudu Plateau

- **Location:** **Cross River State**, in the south-east, near the Cameroon border, in the Sankwala (Oshie Ridge) Mountains. Town: **Obudu** (at the foot of the plateau).
- **Altitude:** roughly **1,500–1,600 m** on top.
- **Climate (approximate):** **cool**: mean temperatures roughly **15–25 °C** on top, much cooler than the hot, humid lowland rainforest below; high rainfall; often misty and cloudy.
- **Vegetation:** **montane grassland** on the plateau top and **montane forest** in valleys, on steep slopes and along streams. The lowlands around it are rainforest, so the contrast is very clear: hot rainforest at the foot, cool grassland on the top.
- **Animals:** the forests around Obudu (part of the Okwangwo Division of Cross River National Park and nearby forests) are home to rare primates, including the **Cross River gorilla** and the **drill**, as well as chimpanzees, many birds and butterflies.
- **Human activities:**
  - **Cattle ranching:** the **Obudu Cattle Ranch** was set up in the early 1950s to rear cattle in the cool, tsetse-free highland grassland. It is now the **Obudu Mountain Resort**.
  - **Tourism:** the resort, with its cable car, cool climate and scenery, attracts many visitors.
  - Farming of vegetables and temperate crops on a small scale.

| Organism | Adaptation | How it helps |

**Example 1.** A region has an annual rainfall of about 2,000 mm, a dry season of only two to three months, high humidity throughout the year, and tall trees with buttress roots and lianas. Farmers grow cocoa and oil palm. **Which biome is this, and where in Nigeria might it be?**

- **Answer:** tropical rainforest; for example, Ondo, Edo or Cross River State in the south.
- **Reasoning:** high rainfall, a short dry season, buttresses and lianas, and cocoa and oil palm are all rainforest features.

**Example 2.** A town has about 700 mm of rain a year, falling in four to five months. The harmattan is strong from November to February, and daytime temperatures exceed 40 °C in April. The vegetation consists of short grasses with scattered baobab and *Acacia* trees, and farmers grow millet, groundnut and cowpea and keep many cattle. **Identify the biome and suggest a town.**

- **Answer:** Sudan savanna; for example, Kano or Sokoto.

**Example 3.** A region lies about 1,500 m above sea level. Although it is near the equator, its mean temperature is about 20 °C. It has open grassland with cattle, and tea is grown. **Identify the biome.**

- **Answer:** montane grassland of the highlands; this description fits the **Mambilla Plateau** in Taraba State (tea).

**Example 4.** A region has tall grasses over 2 m high, fairly dense trees such as shea and locust bean, strips of forest along rivers, a dry season of about five months, and farmers who grow yam, cassava and rice. **Identify the biome.**

- **Answer:** southern Guinea savanna; for example, around Makurdi (Benue State) or Ilorin (Kwara State).

**Example 5.** Trees with stilt roots and breathing roots grow in salty mud flooded twice a day by the tide; people collect periwinkles and oysters. **Identify the biome.**

- **Answer:** mangrove swamp; for example, the Niger Delta.

### How the biome affects life in each region

Knowing a region's biome also tells you about:

1. **Occupations:** fishing in the swamp; cocoa farming and lumbering in the rainforest; yam farming in the Guinea savanna; cattle rearing and grain farming in the Sudan savanna; potato farming and tourism on the plateaus.
2. **Diseases:** malaria is common everywhere, but mosquitoes and tsetse flies are commonest in the humid south and middle belt; **cerebrospinal meningitis** outbreaks occur mainly in the dry season in the northern savanna (the "meningitis belt" of Africa), favoured by hot, dry, dusty conditions.
3. **Environmental problems:** deforestation and erosion (rainforest); bush burning (Guinea savanna); desertification and drought (Sudan and Sahel savanna); oil pollution (Niger Delta swamps).
4. **Food:** tubers and tree crops in the south; grains and legumes in the north; vegetables from the plateaus.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Rainfall decreases from south to north** in Nigeria; the length of the dry season increases northwards. Use this to place any biome.
- **Guinea savanna is the largest vegetation belt in Nigeria.** Southern Guinea has taller grass and more trees than northern Guinea.
- **Sudan savanna** = short grass, scattered thorny trees (baobab, *Acacia*), millet, groundnut, cattle, Kano and Sokoto.
- **Nigeria has no true desert;** the Sahara is to the north. The far north suffers desertification. The **Sahel** lies between the Sudan savanna and the desert.
- **The plateaus are cool because of altitude, not latitude.** Jos (Plateau State), Mambilla (Taraba State) and Obudu (Cross River State) have montane grassland and forest.
- **Tea is grown on the Mambilla Plateau; Irish potatoes on the Jos Plateau; the Obudu Cattle Ranch is on the Obudu Plateau.**
- **Chappal Waddi** (about 2,419 m), near the Mambilla Plateau, is the highest point in Nigeria.
- **Buttress roots, lianas, epiphytes and drip tips** are rainforest features; **thick corky bark, deciduous leaves and underground stems** are savanna features (adaptations to fire and drought).
- **The camel's hump stores fat, not water.**
- **Do not memorise exact rainfall figures as fixed facts;** they differ between books. Learn the order and the pattern.

## Quick summary

1. A biome is a large region with a characteristic climate, vegetation and animals; in Nigeria, biomes run in belts from the coast northwards because rainfall and the length of the rainy season decrease northwards.
2. The tropical rainforest of the south has high rainfall (about 1,500–2,500+ mm), a short dry season and a tall, evergreen, layered forest with buttresses, lianas and epiphytes; it supports cocoa, oil palm, rubber and timber.
3. The Guinea savanna of the middle belt is Nigeria's largest belt: the southern part has tall grasses and many trees; the northern part has shorter grasses and more scattered trees such as *Isoberlinia*; trees are fire-resistant and deciduous.
4. The Sudan savanna of the north (Kano, Sokoto, Katsina) has short grasses, scattered thorny trees such as baobab and *Acacia*, a dry season of 7–8 months, and supports millet, groundnut and cattle.
5. The Sahel (far north-east) and the desert (the Sahara, north of Nigeria) have very low rainfall, xerophytes, camels and other drought-adapted organisms; desertification threatens northern Nigeria.
6. The Jos, Mambilla and Obudu Plateaus are cool because of their altitude and carry montane grassland and forest; they are used for Irish potato, tea, cattle and tourism.
7. Knowing the features of each biome lets you identify the climate, vegetation, crops and occupations of any region of Nigeria.

## Practice questions

**1. The largest vegetation belt in Nigeria is the**

- A. tropical rainforest
- B. Sudan savanna
- C. Guinea savanna
- D. mangrove swamp

**Answer: C.** The Guinea savanna covers most of the middle belt and is the largest vegetation belt in Nigeria.

**2. Buttress roots, lianas and epiphytes are characteristic of the**

- A. tropical rainforest
- B. Sudan savanna
- C. Sahel savanna
- D. montane grassland

**Answer: A.** These are features of the tall, dense, layered rainforest, where trees need support and plants compete for light.

**3. Which of the following towns is located in the Sudan savanna?**

- A. Makurdi
- B. Kano
- C. Benin City
- D. Calabar

**Answer: B.** Kano is in the Sudan savanna. Makurdi is in the southern Guinea savanna; Benin City and Calabar are in the rainforest/swamp zone.

**4. Tea is grown commercially in Nigeria on the Mambilla Plateau mainly because the plateau has**

- A. a long dry season
- B. sandy soils
- C. a cool climate due to high altitude
- D. brackish water

**Answer: C.** Tea needs cool, wet conditions; the Mambilla Plateau is cool because of its high altitude.

**5. Thick, corky bark in trees such as the shea tree is an adaptation to**

- A. flooding
- B. bush fires
- C. high humidity
- D. salty soils

**Answer: B.** Thick, corky bark insulates the living tissues of savanna trees from the heat of dry-season bush fires.

**6. As one moves from the coast of Nigeria towards the north, which of the following increases?**

- A. Annual rainfall
- B. Relative humidity
- C. Length of the dry season
- D. Height of the vegetation

**Answer: C.** The dry season gets longer northwards, while rainfall, humidity and the height of vegetation all decrease.

**7. The Obudu Plateau, famous for its cattle ranch, is located in**

- A. Plateau State
- B. Taraba State
- C. Cross River State
- D. Bauchi State

**Answer: C.** The Obudu Plateau is in Cross River State. The Jos Plateau is in Plateau State and the Mambilla Plateau in Taraba State.

**8. A region with short grasses, scattered baobab and *Acacia* trees, about 600 mm of rain a year and a dry season of about eight months is most likely the**

- A. southern Guinea savanna
- B. tropical rainforest
- C. montane forest
- D. Sudan savanna

**Answer: D.** These features (low rainfall, a long dry season, short grass, baobab and *Acacia*) describe the Sudan savanna.

**9. Which of the following is an adaptation of desert plants to their environment?**

- A. Broad leaves with drip tips
- B. Leaves reduced to spines and succulent stems
- C. Buttress roots
- D. Pneumatophores

**Answer: B.** Reduced leaves cut down water loss by transpiration, and succulent stems store water. Drip tips and buttresses are rainforest features; pneumatophores are found in mangroves.

**10. The tree that is most characteristic of the northern Guinea savanna woodlands is**

- A. *Rhizophora*
- B. *Isoberlinia*
- C. iroko
- D. date palm

**Answer: B.** *Isoberlinia* woodland is typical of the northern Guinea savanna. *Rhizophora* is a mangrove, iroko is a rainforest tree and the date palm grows at desert oases.
