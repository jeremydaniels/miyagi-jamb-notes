# JAMB Biology Notes: Living Organisms

Nov 6, 2026&#32;

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 1 of the JAMB Biology syllabus: Variety of Organisms – Living organisms. By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Tell the difference between the characteristics of living and non-living things.
2. Identify the structures of plant and animal cells.
3. Explain the functions of the parts of plant and animal cells.
4. Compare and contrast plant and animal cells.
5. Arrange the five levels of organisation in living things in the correct order: cell, tissue, organ, system, organism.

Syllabus examples you must know: Euglena and Paramecium (cell level), epithelial tissue and Hydra (tissue level), onion bulb (organ level), the reproductive, digestive and excretory systems (system level), and Chlamydomonas (organism level).

## Characteristics of living things

A living thing is anything that carries out all the life processes below. A non-living thing, like a stone or a car, may show one or two of them, but never all.

An easy way to remember them is **MR NIGER D**:

| Letter | Characteristic | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M | Movement | Changing position or moving parts of the body | A goat walks; a sunflower turns towards the sun |
| R | Respiration | Releasing energy from food inside the cells | Your cells break down glucose to give you energy |
| N | Nutrition | Taking in or making food | Plants make food by photosynthesis; animals eat |
| I | Irritability (sensitivity) | Detecting and responding to changes around them | You pull your hand away from a hot pot |
| G | Growth | A permanent increase in size and mass | A maize seedling grows into a tall plant |
| E | Excretion | Removing waste products made by the body | Humans pass out urea in urine; plants give out oxygen |
| R | Reproduction | Producing new young ones of the same kind | A hen lays eggs; a yam tuber sprouts |
| D | Death | Every living thing has a limited lifespan | All organisms eventually die |

&#91;IMAGE: Simple illustrated chart showing the 8 characteristics of living things (MR NIGER D) with a small icon for each: a running goat (movement), lungs (respiration), a plate of food (nutrition), a hand pulling away from a flame (irritability), a growing maize plant (growth), a kidney (excretion), a hen with chicks (reproduction), a wilted plant (death).\]

### Living vs non-living things

| Feature | Living things | Non-living things |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Made of cells | Yes | No |
| Respire | Yes | No |
| Grow from inside | Yes, by making new cells | No. A crystal or a heap of sand can get bigger, but only by adding material to the outside |
| Reproduce | Yes | No |
| Respond to stimuli | Yes | No |
| Excrete | Yes | No |

**Watch out:** a car moves and burns fuel to release energy, and fire "grows" and "feeds". These are not living, because they don't have cells and don't show all the characteristics.

## The cell: structure and functions

The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in all living things. Some organisms, like Amoeba, are just one cell (unicellular). Others, like humans and mango trees, are made of millions of cells (multicellular).

&#91;IMAGE: Labelled diagram of a typical animal cell (e.g. a cheek cell) showing: cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, nuclear membrane, nucleolus, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi body, small vacuoles, lysosome and centrioles. Rounded, irregular shape. Clean textbook style, white background.\]

&#91;IMAGE: Labelled diagram of a typical plant cell (e.g. a leaf palisade cell) showing: cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, large central vacuole with cell sap, chloroplasts, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi body. Regular rectangular shape. Clean textbook style, white background.\]

### Parts of the cell and what they do

| Part | Found in | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell membrane (plasma membrane) | Plant and animal cells | Thin layer around the cell. Controls what enters and leaves the cell (it is selectively permeable) |
| Cytoplasm | Plant and animal cells | Jelly-like fluid where most of the cell's chemical reactions take place. Holds the other parts in place |
| Nucleus | Plant and animal cells | Controls all the activities of the cell. Contains the chromosomes, which carry genetic information (DNA) |
| Nucleolus | Plant and animal cells | Small body inside the nucleus. Makes ribosomes |
| Mitochondria | Plant and animal cells | The "powerhouse" of the cell. Site of aerobic respiration, where energy (ATP) is released from food |
| Ribosomes | Plant and animal cells | Tiny bodies where proteins are made (protein synthesis) |
| Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) | Plant and animal cells | A network of channels that transports materials within the cell. Rough ER has ribosomes and makes proteins; smooth ER makes fats (lipids) |
| Golgi body (Golgi apparatus) | Plant and animal cells | Packages and sends out substances made by the cell, such as enzymes and hormones |
| Lysosomes | Mainly animal cells | Contain digestive enzymes that break down worn-out parts of the cell and harmful materials |
| Centrioles | Animal cells | Help in cell division by forming the spindle fibres |
| Cell wall | Plant cells only | Made of cellulose. Gives the cell a fixed shape, support and protection. Fully permeable |
| Chloroplasts | Plant cells only (green parts) | Contain chlorophyll, the green pigment that traps light energy for photosynthesis |
| Large central vacuole | Plant cells | Filled with cell sap (water, sugars and salts). Keeps the cell firm (turgid) and stores substances |
| Small vacuoles | Animal cells | Store food and water, or remove waste, e.g. contractile vacuoles in Amoeba and Paramecium remove excess water |

**Tip:** the cell membrane is *selectively* permeable (it chooses what passes), while the cell wall is *fully* permeable (it lets almost everything pass). JAMB often tests this difference.

## Comparing plant and animal cells

Plant and animal cells share the same basic parts, but a few key structures are found in only one of them.

### Similarities

Both have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi body.

### Differences

| Feature | Plant cell | Animal cell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell wall | Present (made of cellulose) | Absent |
| Shape | Fixed, regular (often rectangular) | Irregular, can change shape |
| Chloroplasts | Present in green parts | Absent |
| Vacuole | One large, central and permanent | Small, many and temporary (if present) |
| Nucleus position | Pushed to the side by the large vacuole | Usually in the centre |
| Centrioles | Absent (in most plants) | Present |
| Food stored as | Starch | Glycogen (and fat) |
| Lysosomes | Rare | Common |

&#91;IMAGE: Side-by-side comparison of a plant cell and an animal cell, with the plant-only parts (cell wall, chloroplasts, large central vacuole) highlighted in green and the animal-only parts (centrioles) highlighted in orange. Labels on both.\]

**Memory trick:** plants need a **wall** to stand up, **chloroplasts** to make food and a **big vacuole** to stay firm. Animals move around and eat their food, so they don't need these.

## Levels of organisation

In multicellular organisms, cells are organised in five levels, from the simplest to the most complex:

**Cell → Tissue → Organ → System → Organism**

&#91;IMAGE: A left-to-right flow diagram of the five levels of organisation with an arrow between each: a single muscle cell → muscle tissue → the heart (organ) → the circulatory system → a human being (organism). Each stage labelled.\]

### 1. Cell level

The simplest level. At this level, one cell carries out **all** the life processes on its own.

- **Examples:** Euglena, Paramecium, Amoeba.
- Paramecium feeds, moves (with cilia), excretes (with contractile vacuoles) and reproduces, all as one cell.

&#91;IMAGE: Labelled diagrams of Euglena (showing flagellum, eyespot, chloroplasts, nucleus, contractile vacuole, pellicle) and Paramecium (showing cilia, oral groove, food vacuoles, macronucleus, micronucleus, contractile vacuoles).\]

### 2. Tissue level

A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to do one job.

- **Examples:** epithelial tissue in animals (lines and covers surfaces like the skin and the gut), and Hydra.
- Hydra is a simple animal whose body is made of two layers of cells (ectoderm and endoderm). It is the syllabus example of an organism at the tissue level.
- Other tissues include muscle tissue, nervous tissue, and in plants, xylem and phloem.

### 3. Organ level

An organ is made of different tissues working together to do a particular job.

- **Example:** the onion bulb, which is a storage organ made of fleshy leaves.
- Other examples include the heart, the stomach, the leaf and the root.

### 4. System level

A system is a group of organs that work together to carry out a major function.

| System | Main organs | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digestive system | Mouth, oesophagus, stomach, intestines, liver, pancreas | Breaks down food and absorbs nutrients |
| Excretory system | Kidneys, ureters, bladder, skin, lungs | Removes waste products from the body |
| Reproductive system | Testes and ovaries, and related organs | Produces offspring |

### 5. Organism level

The complete living thing, where all the systems work together.

- **Syllabus example:** Chlamydomonas, a single-celled green alga that lives and functions as a whole, independent organism.
- Other examples: a human, a goat, a mango tree.

**Note:** a single-celled organism like Chlamydomonas is at the cell level and the organism level at the same time, because its one cell is the whole organism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Cell | The basic unit of structure and function in living things |
| Unicellular | An organism made of only one cell, e.g. Amoeba |
| Multicellular | An organism made of many cells, e.g. a human |
| Organelle | A small structure inside a cell with a specific job, e.g. mitochondria |
| Protoplasm | All the living contents of a cell: the cytoplasm and the nucleus |
| Selectively permeable | Allows only some substances to pass through |
| Tissue | A group of similar cells performing the same function |
| Organ | Different tissues working together to perform a function |
| System | A group of organs working together to perform a major function |
| Irritability | The ability to detect and respond to changes (stimuli) |
| Excretion | The removal of waste products made by the body's chemical reactions |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Growth in non-living things:** crystals and stalagmites get bigger by adding material to the outside. Living things grow from the inside by making new cells. JAMB uses this to trick candidates.
- **Cell wall vs cell membrane:** the cell wall is fully permeable; the cell membrane is selectively permeable. Don't mix them up.
- **Where energy is released:** mitochondria, not the nucleus. The nucleus controls the cell.
- **Where proteins are made:** ribosomes.
- **Storage food:** plant cells store starch; animal cells store glycogen.
- **Order of levels:** always cell → tissue → organ → system → organism. A common wrong option swaps tissue and organ.
- **Know the syllabus examples by heart:** Euglena and Paramecium (cell), Hydra (tissue), onion bulb (organ), Chlamydomonas (organism).
- **Chloroplasts are not in every plant cell:** root cells, for example, have none because they get no light.

## Quick summary

1. Living things show all the characteristics in MR NIGER D: movement, respiration, nutrition, irritability, growth, excretion, reproduction and death.
2. The cell is the basic unit of life. Its key parts are the cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus.
3. Mitochondria release energy, ribosomes make proteins, and the nucleus controls the cell.
4. Only plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole.
5. The five levels of organisation are cell, tissue, organ, system and organism.
6. Syllabus examples: Euglena and Paramecium (cell), Hydra (tissue), onion bulb (organ), digestive, excretory and reproductive systems (system), Chlamydomonas (organism).

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of all living things?**

- A. Respiration
- B. Photosynthesis
- C. Excretion
- D. Reproduction

**Answer: B.** Only green plants and some other organisms photosynthesise. Animals don't, but they are still living.

**2. The organelle responsible for the release of energy in the cell is the**

- A. nucleus
- B. ribosome
- C. mitochondrion
- D. Golgi body

**Answer: C.** Aerobic respiration, which releases energy, takes place in the mitochondria.

**3. Which structure is found in plant cells but NOT in animal cells?**

- A. Cell membrane
- B. Nucleus
- C. Cell wall
- D. Mitochondrion

**Answer: C.** The cellulose cell wall is found only in plant cells. The other three are in both.

**4. Which of these shows the correct order of the levels of organisation?**

- A. Cell → organ → tissue → system → organism
- B. Cell → tissue → organ → system → organism
- C. Tissue → cell → organ → organism → system
- D. Cell → tissue → system → organ → organism

**Answer: B.** Cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form systems, and systems make up the organism.

**5. The onion bulb is an example of organisation at the**

- A. cell level
- B. tissue level
- C. organ level
- D. system level

**Answer: C.** The onion bulb is a storage organ made of different tissues.

**6. Which part of the cell is selectively permeable?**

- A. Cell wall
- B. Cell membrane
- C. Vacuole
- D. Cytoplasm

**Answer: B.** The cell membrane controls which substances enter and leave. The cell wall is fully permeable.

**7. Hydra is used as an example of organisation at the**

- A. cell level
- B. tissue level
- C. organ level
- D. organism level only

**Answer: B.** Hydra's body is made of two layers of cells (tissues), with no true organs.

**8. The main storage food in animal cells is**

- A. starch
- B. cellulose
- C. glycogen
- D. glucose

**Answer: C.** Animal cells store glycogen; plant cells store starch.

**9. A crystal increases in size when placed in a salt solution. This is not considered growth in a living sense because**

- A. it is too small
- B. it adds material only to its outside surface
- C. it has a cell wall
- D. it cannot move

**Answer: B.** Living things grow from within by making new cells. Crystals only add material to the outside.

**10. In which organelle does protein synthesis take place?**

- A. Lysosome
- B. Ribosome
- C. Chloroplast
- D. Vacuole

**Answer: B.** Ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis.
