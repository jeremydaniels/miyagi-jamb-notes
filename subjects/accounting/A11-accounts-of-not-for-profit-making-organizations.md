# JAMB Accounting Notes: Accounts of Not-For-Profit-Making Organizations

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 11 of the JAMB Principles of Accounts syllabus: Accounts of Not-For-Profit-Making Organizations. It covers the preparation and interpretation of the financial statements used by clubs and societies.

It has the following content items:
- a. Objectives
- b. Receipts and payments account
- c. Income and expenditure account
- d. Statement of financial position

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. distinguish between profit oriented and Not-For-Profit-Making Organizations;
2. determine annual subscription, subscription in arrears and in advance; and
3. compute the cash balances, accumulated funds, surplus or deficit for the period.

## Objectives of Not-For-Profit-Making Organizations

**Content a: Objectives**

Most businesses you see every day—like a provision store in Mushin or a large manufacturing company—exist to make a profit for their owners. However, a **Not-For-Profit-Making Organization** (also called a Non-Profit Organization or NPO) is set up to provide services, recreation, welfare, or social benefits to its members or the general public.

Examples of not-for-profit organizations in Nigeria include:
- Sports and social clubs (e.g., Ikeja Golf Club, Ikoyi Club).
- Professional bodies and trade unions.
- Old Students' Associations and Alumni groups.
- Religious organizations (churches and mosques).
- Charities and societies (e.g., the Nigerian Red Cross).

Their primary objective is **not** to make money, but they still handle large amounts of cash. They collect money from their members and the public, and they must keep accurate financial records to prevent fraud, track their assets, and show members exactly how their money is being spent. 

## Distinguishing Profit-Oriented and Not-For-Profit-Making Organizations

**Objective i: distinguish between profit oriented and Not-For-Profit-Making Organizations**

While both types of organizations use the double-entry bookkeeping system, their focus and the terminology they use are different. The table below compares them:

| Feature | Profit-Oriented Organizations (e.g., Sole Trader, Company) | Not-For-Profit-Making Organizations (e.g., Clubs, Societies) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Motive** | To make a profit for the owners. | To provide services, welfare, and recreation for members. |
| **Source of Capital** | Capital invested by the owner(s) or shareholders. | **Accumulated Fund** (built up over time from surpluses and donations). |
| **Main Income Source** | Sales of goods and services. | **Subscriptions**, entrance fees, donations, and fund-raising events. |
| **Cash Summary Statement** | Cash Book. | **Receipts and Payments Account**. |
| **Performance Statement** | Statement of Profit or Loss (Income Statement). | **Income and Expenditure Account**. |
| **End Result of the Year** | Net Profit or Net Loss. | **Surplus** or **Deficit**. |

## Computing Cash Balances, Accumulated Funds, Surplus or Deficit

**Objective iii: compute the cash balances, accumulated funds, surplus or deficit for the period.**

When preparing the final accounts for a club or society, an accountant has three main mathematical goals:
1. **Compute the cash balances:** This is done using the **Receipts and Payments Account**. It simply adds all cash received and subtracts all cash paid to find the money left in the bank or at hand.
2. **Compute the surplus or deficit:** This is done using the **Income and Expenditure Account**. It matches the true revenue income for the year against the revenue expenses. If income is greater, it is a surplus; if expenses are greater, it is a deficit.
3. **Compute the accumulated fund:** This is the club's equivalent of capital. It is computed at the start of the year using a **Statement of Affairs**, applying the accounting equation: `Total Assets - Total Liabilities = Accumulated Fund`.

To see how these computations work, we will use a comprehensive example throughout the rest of these notes.

## Receipts and Payments Account

**Content b: Receipts and payments account**

The Receipts and Payments Account is essentially a summary of the club's Cash Book for the year. It is a **Real Account** representing the flow of cash and bank transactions. 

**Key rules of the Receipts and Payments Account:**
- It starts with the **opening cash and bank balance** on the debit side.
- **All cash receipts** are recorded on the **debit side**, no matter what year they relate to (past, present, or future).
- **All cash payments** are recorded on the **credit side**.
- It includes both **capital items** (like buying a building) and **revenue items** (like paying electricity).
- It ignores non-cash items entirely (you will never see depreciation or bad debts here).
- The balancing figure at the end is the **closing cash and bank balance**.

Let us prepare the Receipts and Payments Account for IOSA to compute the closing cash balance (fulfilling the first part of Objective iii).

| Dr | Receipts and Payments Account for the year ended 31 Dec 2023 | Cr |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Receipts** | **₦** | **Payments** | **₦** |
| Bal b/f (Opening Bank/Cash) | 50,000 | Bar purchases | 75,000 |
| Subscriptions received | 180,000 | Electricity paid | 14,000 |
| Bar sales | 120,000 | Wages of barman | 36,000 |
| Donations | 30,000 | Expenses for annual dinner | 50,000 |
| Proceeds from annual dinner | 85,000 | Bought new equipment | 40,000 |
| | | Stationery | 15,000 |
| | | **Bal c/d (Closing Bank/Cash)** | **235,000** |
| **Total** | **465,000** | **Total** | **465,000** |

**Working:** Total receipts = ₦465,000. Total payments before balance = ₦230,000. 
Closing balance = ₦465,000 - ₦230,000 = ₦235,000.

## Determining Annual Subscription, Arrears and Advance

**Objective ii: determine annual subscription, subscription in arrears and in advance**

A **subscription** is the regular fee (usually monthly or annually) paid by members to keep their membership active. It is the primary source of revenue for a club. Because members do not always pay exactly on time, we must adjust the cash received using the accrual concept.

- **Subscription in arrears (Owing):** This occurs when members have not yet paid for the current year. Because the club is owed this money, it is treated as a **Current Asset** (debtor).
- **Subscription in advance (Prepaid):** This occurs when members pay ahead for a future year. Because the club has collected money for services not yet rendered, it acts as deferred income and is treated as a **Current Liability** (creditor).

### The Formula for Subscription Income

To find the true subscription income that relates strictly to the current year (which will be transferred to the Income and Expenditure Account), we use this formula:

**True Annual Subscription** = Cash Received + Closing Arrears + Opening Advance - Opening Arrears - Closing Advance.

### The Subscription T-Account

In bookkeeping, this is calculated using a Subscription Account. 
- Opening arrears (Asset) start on the **debit side**.
- Opening advance (Liability) starts on the **credit side**.
- Cash received is credited (Double entry: Debit Cash Book, Credit Subscription Account).
- The balancing figure is the income for the year, which is placed on the debit side and transferred to the credit side of the Income and Expenditure Account.

[IMAGE: A visual representation of the Subscriptions T-account layout. A large 'T' shape. On the top left (Debit side): "Opening Arrears" (with an arrow pointing down from a calendar showing last year) and "Closing Advance". On the top right (Credit side): "Opening Advance" and "Cash Received" (with an icon of naira notes). The balancing figure "Income & Expenditure" is highlighted in bold on the debit side. Let us prepare the Subscription Account for IOSA:

| Dr | Subscription Account | Cr |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Details** | **₦** | **Details** | **₦** |
| Bal b/f (Opening Arrears) | 12,000 | Bal b/f (Opening Advance) | 8,000 |
| **Income & Expenditure (Income)** | **181,000** | Bank (Cash received) | 180,000 |
| Bal c/d (Closing Advance) | 10,000 | Bal c/d (Closing Arrears) | 15,000 |
| **Total** | **203,000** | **Total** | **203,000** |

**Working:** 
Step 1: Record opening balances (Arrears ₦12,000 Dr; Advance ₦8,000 Cr).
Step 2: Record cash received (₦180,000 Cr).
Step 3: Record closing balances (Arrears ₦15,000 Cr; Advance ₦10,000 Dr).
Step 4: Find the total of the heavier side. The Credit side is ₦8,000 + ₦180,000 + ₦15,000 = ₦203,000.
Step 5: Calculate the balancing figure on the Debit side: ₦203,000 - (₦12,000 + ₦10,000) = ₦181,000. 
This ₦181,000 is the true subscription income for 2023.

## Income and Expenditure Account

**Content c: Income and expenditure account**

The Income and Expenditure Account is a **Nominal Account** that serves the exact same purpose as the Statement of Profit or Loss for a profit-making business. It computes the **Surplus or Deficit** for the year.

**Key rules of the Income and Expenditure Account:**
- It uses the **accrual basis of accounting**. This means you must adjust figures for amounts owing (accruals) and amounts prepaid (prepayments).
- It only records **revenue items** (daily running income and expenses). 
- It **excludes capital items** (buying a building or equipment). However, the **depreciation** of those assets is included as a revenue expense.
- Expenditure is listed on the debit side, and Income on the credit side.
- If Income is greater than Expenditure, the balancing figure is a **Surplus** (like Net Profit). If Expenditure is greater, it is a **Deficit** (like Net Loss).

### Receipts and Payments vs Income and Expenditure

JAMB frequently tests the difference between these two accounts. Memorize this comparison:

| Feature | Receipts and Payments Account | Income and Expenditure Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature of Account** | Real Account | Nominal Account |
| **Sides** | Receipts (Dr) and Payments (Cr) | Expenditure (Dr) and Income (Cr) |
| **Scope of Items** | Capital and revenue items | Revenue items only |
| **Accounting Basis**| Cash basis (no adjustments) | Accrual basis (adjusts for arrears/advance) |
| **Period Covered** | Includes past, present, and future cash | Strictly current financial year only |
| **Non-cash items** | Excluded (no depreciation) | Included (depreciation, bad debts) |
| **Opening Balance** | Starts with opening cash/bank | Has no opening balance |
| **Closing Balance** | Closing cash/bank balance | Surplus or Deficit |

### Preparing IOSA's Income and Expenditure Account

Let us calculate the expenses first:
- **Electricity:** Paid ₦14,000. Add closing owing (₦6,000) and subtract opening owing (₦4,000). True expense = ₦16,000.
- **Stationery:** ₦15,000 (no adjustments).
- **Depreciation of Equipment:** 10% on total cost. Total cost is opening (₦150,000) + new (₦40,000) = ₦190,000. 10% of ₦190,000 = ₦19,000.
- **Depreciation of Clubhouse:** 5% on cost (₦500,000). 5% of ₦500,000 = ₦25,000.
- **Annual Dinner Profit:** Proceeds (₦85,000) minus Expenses (₦50,000) = ₦35,000 profit. (It is neater to offset special event expenses against their income directly).

| Expenditure (Dr) | ₦ | Income (Cr) | ₦ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Electricity | 16,000 | Subscriptions | 181,000 |
| Stationery | 15,000 | Bar Profit | 16,000 |
| Depreciation: Equipment | 19,000 | Donations | 30,000 |
| Depreciation: Clubhouse | 25,000 | Profit on Annual dinner | 35,000 |
| **Surplus (Income > Expenditure)** | **187,000** | | |
| **Total** | **262,000** | **Total** | **262,000** |

**Working:** Total Income = ₦262,000. Total Expenditure before balance = ₦75,000.
Surplus = ₦262,000 - ₦75,000 = ₦187,000. This fulfills the surplus computation for Objective iii.

## Statement of Financial Position

**Content d: Statement of financial position**

The Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet) shows the financial state of the club at the end of the year. 
Before we can prepare it, we must calculate the **Accumulated Fund** (part of Objective iii), because it replaces the "Capital" section.

### Step 1: Compute the Opening Accumulated Fund
To find the Accumulated Fund at the start of the year, we prepare a **Statement of Affairs as at 1 January 2023**. We list all opening assets and subtract all opening liabilities.

| **Statement of Affairs as at 1 January 2023** | **₦** | **₦** |

Now we construct the final balance sheet as at 31 December 2023. We will use the closing balances, the computed cash balance (₦235,000), and add our Surplus to the Accumulated Fund.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Not-For-Profit Organization** | An entity set up to provide services or welfare to members, not to make a profit. |
| **Accumulated Fund** | The equivalent of capital in a club; calculated as Total Assets minus Total Liabilities. |
| **Receipts and Payments Account** | A summary of the cash book showing all cash received and paid during the year. |
| **Income and Expenditure Account**| The equivalent of a profit or loss account; matches revenue income against revenue expenses. |
| **Surplus** | The excess of income over expenditure (equivalent to net profit). |
| **Deficit** | The excess of expenditure over income (equivalent to net loss). |
| **Subscriptions** | Regular fees paid by members to retain membership. |
| **Subscriptions in Arrears** | Subscriptions owed by members (treated as a current asset). |
| **Subscriptions in Advance** | Subscriptions paid ahead of time by members (treated as a current liability). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Mistaking capital for revenue:** Never put the purchase of a fixed asset (e.g., buying a lawnmower or equipment) into the Income and Expenditure Account. Only put its depreciation.
- **Mixing up arrears and advance:** A very common mistake is treating subscriptions in arrears as a liability. Remember: Arrears = Owed to the club = Asset. Advance = Paid early = Liability.
- **Forgetting to adjust for opening balances:** When calculating the true expense for the year (like electricity), always add the closing amount owing, and subtract the opening amount owing. 
- **Confusing R&P with I&E:** The Receipts and Payments Account ignores non-cash items. If JAMB asks "Which account contains depreciation?", the answer is the Income and Expenditure Account.
- **Life Membership / Special Funds:** If members pay a one-time life membership fee, or donate to a specific "Building Fund", this money is usually capitalized (added straight to the balance sheet), not put into the Income and Expenditure Account.

## Quick summary

1. Not-for-profit organizations (clubs, societies, charities) exist to provide welfare and services, not to make profit.
2. The Receipts and Payments Account is a Real account that summarizes cash and bank transactions. It includes both capital and revenue items but ignores accruals and non-cash items.
3. The Income and Expenditure Account is a Nominal account that determines the surplus or deficit. It uses the accrual concept and only includes revenue items and non-cash items like depreciation.
4. Subscriptions are the main income source. Subscriptions in arrears are current assets, while subscriptions in advance are current liabilities.
5. True subscription income is calculated by taking cash received, adding closing arrears and opening advance, then subtracting opening arrears and closing advance.
6. A Trading Account is often prepared first if the club runs a bar or tuck shop to find the profit to be transferred to the Income and Expenditure Account.
7. The Accumulated Fund replaces Capital in the Statement of Financial Position, and is calculated at the start of the year as Total Assets minus Total Liabilities.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following statements is a feature of an Income and Expenditure Account?**
- A. It is prepared strictly on a cash basis
- B. It includes both capital and revenue expenditures
- C. It starts with an opening cash balance
- D. It incorporates non-cash items like depreciation

**Answer: D.** The Income and Expenditure Account is prepared on an accrual basis, ignores capital expenditures, has no opening balance, but does include non-cash revenue expenses like depreciation.

**2. In the accounts of a not-for-profit making organization, the accumulated fund is determined by calculating the**
- A. total subscriptions received minus expenses
- B. total assets minus total liabilities
- C. total cash receipts minus cash payments
- D. surplus added to donations

**Answer: B.** The accumulated fund is the club's equivalent of capital and is found using the accounting equation: Assets - Liabilities = Accumulated Fund.

**3. A club has subscriptions in arrears of ₦8,000 at the beginning of the year and ₦5,000 at the end. During the year, the club received ₦100,000 as subscriptions. What is the subscription income for the year?**
- A. ₦97,000
- B. ₦105,000
- C. ₦103,000
- D. ₦95,000

**Answer: A.** Step 1: Start with cash received (₦100,000). Step 2: Add closing arrears because they belong to this year (+ ₦5,000). Step 3: Subtract opening arrears because they were for last year (- ₦8,000). ₦100,000 + ₦5,000 - ₦8,000 = ₦97,000. 

**4. Subscriptions paid by members of a club for the following year are treated in the current year's statement of financial position as**
- A. a current asset
- B. a non-current asset
- C. a current liability
- D. an accumulated fund

**Answer: C.** Subscriptions paid for a future period (in advance) represent deferred income. The club owes a service to the members, making it a current liability.

**5. Which of the following is equivalent to the capital of a sole proprietor in the accounts of a club?**
- A. Entrance fees
- B. Surplus
- C. Accumulated fund
- D. Life membership fund

**Answer: C.** The accumulated fund represents the net worth of the club, acting exactly like the capital account of a sole proprietor.

**6. A sports club bought a new lawnmower for ₦50,000 cash. In the club's financial statements, this transaction will appear in the**
- A. Income and Expenditure Account only
- B. Receipts and Payments Account and Statement of Financial Position
- C. Income and Expenditure Account and Statement of Financial Position
- D. Receipts and Payments Account only

**Answer: B.** Buying a lawnmower is a capital expenditure. The cash paid goes to the Receipts and Payments Account, and the asset itself goes to the Statement of Financial Position. It is excluded from the Income and Expenditure Account.

**7. Use the following information to answer the question.**
Bar opening inventory: ₦15,000
Bar closing inventory: ₦10,000
Bar sales: ₦90,000
Payments to bar suppliers: ₦45,000
Owing to suppliers at start: ₦5,000
Owing to suppliers at close: ₦8,000
**What is the profit from the bar?**
- A. ₦42,000
- B. ₦37,000
- C. ₦34,000
- D. ₦45,000

**Answer: B.** Step 1: Find Purchases. Purchases = Payments (₦45,000) + Closing owing (₦8,000) - Opening owing (₦5,000) = ₦48,000. Step 2: Find Cost of Goods Sold. COGS = Opening inventory (₦15,000) + Purchases (₦48,000) - Closing inventory (₦10,000) = ₦53,000. Step 3: Find Profit. Sales (₦90,000) - COGS (₦53,000) = ₦37,000.

**8. A club has the following assets and liabilities on 1st January:**
Clubhouse ₦250,000, Bank balance ₦40,000, Subscriptions in arrears ₦10,000, Owing for electricity ₦5,000. **What is the accumulated fund?**
- A. ₦290,000
- B. ₦295,000
- C. ₦300,000
- D. ₦305,000

**Answer: B.** Step 1: Add all assets (Clubhouse ₦250,000 + Bank ₦40,000 + Arrears ₦10,000 = ₦300,000). Step 2: Subtract liabilities (Owing electricity ₦5,000). Accumulated Fund = ₦300,000 - ₦5,000 = ₦295,000.

**9. If the expenditure of a not-for-profit making organization exceeds its income, the result is**
- A. a net loss
- B. a deficit
- C. a surplus
- D. a net debit

**Answer: B.** When revenue expenditure is greater than revenue income, it is called a deficit. "Net loss" is the term used by profit-making businesses.

**10. Which of the following items is found on the debit side of the Receipts and Payments Account?**
- A. Subscriptions in arrears
- B. Barman's wages
- C. Proceeds from annual dinner
- D. Depreciation of clubhouse

**Answer: C.** The debit side of the Receipts and Payments account is for cash received. Proceeds from a dinner is cash received. Subscriptions in arrears is not cash, wages are a payment (credit side), and depreciation is a non-cash item.
