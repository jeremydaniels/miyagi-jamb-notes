# JAMB Accounting Notes: Departmental Accounts

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Topic 12 of the JAMB Principles of Accounts syllabus: Departmental Accounts. It covers the following content items:

* a. Objectives
* b. Apportionment of incomes and expenses
* c. Departmental statement of profit or loss
* d. Inter-departmental transfer

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. identify the reasons for preparing departmental accounts;
2. determine the expenses and incomes attributable to departments;
3. compute departmental profit or loss; and
4. understand the Accounting treatment of inter-departmental transfer.

Many modern businesses do not sell just one type of product. A large supermarket in Lagos might have a grocery section, an electronics section, and a clothing section. A bakery in Kano might have a bread department and a pastries department. To know how well each section is doing, the business prepares **departmental accounts**. 

## Reasons for preparing departmental accounts

**Objective i: identify the reasons for preparing departmental accounts.**

When a business has different sections or departments, preparing a single combined statement of profit or loss only shows the profit of the whole business. It hides which sections are making money and which are losing money. Departmental accounts separate the performance of each section. 

The main reasons for preparing departmental accounts are:

1. **To evaluate performance:** It helps the owner find out the Gross Profit and Net Profit of each specific department.
2. **To make decisions:** If a department (e.g., the electronics section) is constantly making a loss, management can decide whether to close it down, change how it is run, or increase its prices. If another department (e.g., groceries) is highly profitable, they might decide to expand it.
3. **To reward managers:** In large businesses, each department has a manager. Departmental accounts allow the owner to calculate commissions or bonuses based on the specific profit generated by each manager's department.
4. **To allocate resources:** It helps management decide how to share capital, floor space, or advertising budgets among the departments based on their performance.
5. **To compare results:** Management can compare the performance of different departments against one another, or compare a single department's current performance against its past performance.

## Apportionment of incomes and expenses

**Objective ii: determine the expenses and incomes attributable to departments.**

To calculate the profit of a single department, we must charge that department for the expenses it causes. Expenses in a departmental business are grouped into two categories: **direct expenses** and **indirect (overhead) expenses**.

### Direct expenses
These are expenses incurred completely for one specific department. They do not need to be shared. For example, the salary of a salesman who works *only* in the Shoe Department is a direct expense for the Shoe Department. We charge the full amount directly to that department.

### Indirect expenses (Overheads)
These are expenses incurred for the business as a whole. For example, the business pays one rent bill for the entire building, or one electricity bill for the whole store. These expenses must be shared (apportioned) among the departments using a fair and logical basis. 

The most heavily tested area in JAMB for this topic is knowing the correct **basis of apportionment** for different indirect expenses.

**Table: Common Bases of Apportionment**

| Expense | Best Basis of Apportionment | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rent, Rates, and Building Insurance** | Floor area (square meters) | A department taking up more space in the building should pay a larger share of the rent. |
| **Electricity / Lighting** | Number of light points OR Floor area | The more light bulbs or space a department has, the more electricity it uses. |
| **Air Conditioning / Heating** | Floor area or Volume of space | Cooling a larger space costs more. |
| **Selling Expenses** (Advertising, Carriage Outward, Sales Commission, Bad Debts, Discount Allowed) | Sales value (Turnover) | These expenses exist to generate sales. A department with higher sales should bear more of the selling costs. |
| **Purchasing Expenses** (Carriage Inward, Discount Received) | Purchases value | These relate directly to buying goods. |
| **Depreciation and Insurance of Equipment** | Value of assets in the department | A department using expensive machinery should bear the cost of its wear and tear. |
| **Staff Welfare, Canteen, Supervision** | Number of employees | A department with more workers uses more of the staff facilities. |

If an expense cannot be logically tied to any of these bases (for example, general audit fees or the general manager's salary), it is usually **shared equally** among the departments, or shared based on an agreed arbitrary ratio.

### Example of expense apportionment
Aba Retailers has two departments: Shoes and Bags. The total rent for the year is ₦80,000. The Shoe department occupies 600 square meters, and the Bag department occupies 400 square meters. 

**Step 1:** Find the total floor area.
$600 + 400 = 1,000$ square meters.

**Step 2:** Find the ratio.
Shoes = $\frac{600}{1,000} = \frac{3}{5}$
Bags = $\frac{400}{1,000} = \frac{2}{5}$

**Step 3:** Apportion the rent.
Shoes' share = $\frac{3}{5} \times ₦80,000 = ₦48,000$
Bags' share = $\frac{2}{5} \times ₦80,000 = ₦32,000$

## Inter-departmental transfers

**Objective iv: understand the Accounting treatment of inter-departmental transfer.**

Sometimes, one department in a business supplies goods or services to another department in the same business. This is called an **inter-departmental transfer**. For example, in a large hotel, the kitchen department might transfer food to the staff canteen department.

### Accounting treatment (Double Entry)
When goods are transferred, we must treat it as if the supplying department "sold" them and the receiving department "bought" them (though no actual cash changes hands).

*   **Receiving Department:** Debit the department's trading account (treat it as an addition to Purchases).
*   **Supplying Department:** Credit the department's trading account (treat it as a deduction from Purchases, or as an addition to Sales). 

In modern accounting layouts, a separate line item called **"Transfer Inwards"** is added to the receiving department's cost of goods, and **"Transfer Outwards"** is deducted from the supplying department's cost of goods. In the "Total" column for the whole business, these transfers cancel each other out (the net effect is zero), because the business cannot buy from or sell to itself.

### Transfer pricing and Unrealized Profit
Goods can be transferred between departments at two different prices:
1.  **At Cost Price:** The goods are transferred at the exact price the supplying department paid for them. No profit is added. This is the simplest method.
2.  **At Selling Price (or Cost plus Mark-up):** The supplying department adds a profit margin before transferring the goods. 

**Common Mistake / Advanced Point:** If goods are transferred at selling price, and the receiving department has *not yet sold all of those goods* by the end of the year, the closing stock of the receiving department will include profit added by the supplying department. However, from the viewpoint of the *whole business*, this profit has not actually been earned yet (because the goods haven't been sold to an outside customer). This false profit is called **Unrealized Profit**. To fix this, a **Provision for Unrealized Profit (PUP)** is created. We debit the total Statement of Profit or Loss to remove the false profit, and credit the PUP account (which reduces the total closing stock value on the Statement of Financial Position).

## Departmental statement of profit or loss

**Objective iii: compute departmental profit or loss.**

The departmental statement of profit or loss is prepared in a **columnar format**. Instead of one column for numbers, there are separate columns for each department, plus a "Total" column that shows the combined figures for the whole business.

It shows rows for Sales, Cost of Sales, Gross Profit, Expenses, and Net Profit. Clean flat textbook style, white background.]

### Fully Worked Example

Let us prepare a complete Departmental Statement of Profit or Loss for Aba Retailers for the year ended 31 December. Aba Retailers has two departments: Shoes and Bags. 

**Given Information:**
*   **Sales:** Shoes ₦500,000; Bags ₦300,000
*   **Opening Stock:** Shoes ₦50,000; Bags ₦30,000
*   **Purchases:** Shoes ₦300,000; Bags ₦150,000
*   **Inter-departmental transfer:** The Shoe department transferred goods to the Bag department at a cost of ₦10,000.
*   **Closing Stock:** Shoes ₦40,000; Bags ₦25,000
*   **Direct Wages:** Shoes ₦40,000; Bags ₦20,000
*   **Total Rent:** ₦80,000 (Apportioned by floor area: Shoes 600 sqm, Bags 400 sqm)
*   **Total Electricity:** ₦20,000 (Apportioned by floor area)
*   **Total Discount Allowed:** ₦16,000 (Apportioned by Sales turnover)
*   **Total Depreciation:** ₦30,000 (Apportioned by asset value: Shoes ₦200,000, Bags ₦100,000)

**Step 1: Calculate the apportionments**
*   **Rent (₦80,000):** Floor area ratio is 3:2. Shoes = $\frac{3}{5} \times 80,000 = ₦48,000$. Bags = $\frac{2}{5} \times 80,000 = ₦32,000$.
*   **Electricity (₦20,000):** Floor area ratio is 3:2. Shoes = $\frac{3}{5} \times 20,000 = ₦12,000$. Bags = $\frac{2}{5} \times 20,000 = ₦8,000$.
*   **Discount Allowed (₦16,000):** Apportioned by Sales. Sales ratio is 500,000 : 300,000 (or 5:3). Shoes = $\frac{5}{8} \times 16,000 = ₦10,000$. Bags = $\frac{3}{8} \times 16,000 = ₦6,000$.
*   **Depreciation (₦30,000):** Apportioned by asset value. Asset ratio is 200,000 : 100,000 (or 2:1). Shoes = $\frac{2}{3} \times 30,000 = ₦20,000$. Bags = $\frac{1}{3} \times 30,000 = ₦10,000$.

**Step 2: Prepare the Statement**

**Aba Retailers**
**Departmental Statement of Profit or Loss for the year ended 31 December**

| Details | Basis of Apportionment | Total (₦) | Shoes (₦) | Bags (₦) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales** | Actual | **800,000** | 500,000 | 300,000 |
| *Less Cost of Sales:* | | | | |
| Opening Stock | Actual | 80,000 | 50,000 | 30,000 |
| Add Purchases | Actual | 450,000 | 300,000 | 150,000 |
| Add Transfer Inward | Actual | 10,000 | - | 10,000 |
| Less Transfer Outward | Actual | (10,000) | (10,000) | - |
| **Cost of goods available** | | 530,000 | 340,000 | 190,000 |
| Less Closing Stock | Actual | (65,000) | (40,000) | (25,000) |
| **Cost of Goods Sold** | | **465,000** | **300,000** | **165,000** |
| **Gross Profit** | | **335,000** | **200,000** | **135,000** |
| *Less Expenses:* | | | | |
| Direct Wages | Actual | 60,000 | 40,000 | 20,000 |
| Rent | Floor area | 80,000 | 48,000 | 32,000 |
| Electricity | Floor area | 20,000 | 12,000 | 8,000 |
| Discount Allowed | Sales turnover | 16,000 | 10,000 | 6,000 |
| Depreciation | Asset value | 30,000 | 20,000 | 10,000 |
| **Total Expenses** | | **206,000** | **130,000** | **76,000** |
| **Net Profit** | | **129,000** | **70,000** | **59,000** |

*Notice how the transfers (₦10,000 inward and ₦10,000 outward) cancel each other out in the Total column, leaving the net purchases for the whole business exactly at ₦450,000.*

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Departmental Accounts** | Financial statements prepared for individual sections or branches of a single business. |
| **Direct expenses** | Costs incurred exclusively for the benefit of a single department (e.g., wages of a department-specific cashier). |
| **Indirect expenses (Overheads)** | Costs incurred for the business as a whole that must be shared among departments (e.g., general rent). |
| **Apportionment** | The process of splitting indirect expenses among departments using a fair and logical basis. |
| **Inter-departmental transfer** | Goods or services provided by one department of a business to another department of the same business. |
| **Unrealized profit** | Profit included in the value of stock that has been transferred between departments at a mark-up but has not yet been sold to an outside customer. |
| **PUP** | Provision for Unrealized Profit; an adjustment made to remove unrealized profit from combined closing stock and net profit. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Know the basis of apportionment:** JAMB tests this constantly. Memorise that Rent goes with Floor Area, while Discount Allowed goes with Sales Turnover.
*   **Handling Transfers:** Remember the double entry for a transfer. Add it to the purchases (or cost of sales) of the receiving department and deduct it from the purchases of the supplying department.
*   **Total Column Checks:** A common mistake in calculations is adding inter-departmental transfers to the Total column's purchases without deducting them. Remember, a business cannot sell to itself. Transfers must sum to zero in the Total column.
*   **Direct vs Indirect:** Do not accidentally apportion a direct expense. If the question says "Wages (Grocery Dept)", that amount goes 100% to the Grocery column.

## Quick summary

1. Departmental accounts are prepared to evaluate the profitability of each section of a business, helping management make decisions on expansion, closure, or reward.
2. Expenses are split into direct (specific to one department) and indirect (incurred for the whole business).
3. Indirect expenses are apportioned using a logical basis: rent by floor area, electricity by light points or area, and selling expenses by sales turnover.
4. When one department transfers goods to another, the value is treated as an inward transfer (added to cost of sales) for the receiver, and an outward transfer (deducted from cost of sales) for the supplier.
5. If goods are transferred at a mark-up and remain unsold at year-end, a Provision for Unrealized Profit (PUP) is created to adjust the total business profit and stock.
6. The departmental statement of profit or loss uses a columnar format, ending with a Total column that represents the profit or loss of the entire business.

## Practice questions

**1. The main objective of preparing departmental accounts is to:**
- A. calculate the tax payable by the business
- B. ascertain the profitability of each section of the business
- C. increase the selling price of goods
- D. record all cash transactions of the business

**Answer: B.** The primary reason is to evaluate the performance (Gross Profit and Net Profit) of each specific section to aid decision-making.

**2. Which of the following is the most appropriate basis for apportioning rent and rates among departments?**
- A. Sales turnover
- B. Number of employees
- C. Floor area occupied
- D. Value of machinery

**Answer: C.** Rent and rates relate to the physical building, so they are apportioned based on the square meters (floor area) each department occupies.

**3. Total electricity for a business is ₦40,000. Department A has 30 light points and Department B has 10 light points. What is Department B's share of the electricity cost?**
- A. ₦10,000
- B. ₦13,333
- C. ₦30,000
- D. ₦40,000

**Answer: A.** 
Step 1: Total light points = 30 + 10 = 40 points.
Step 2: Department B's ratio = $\frac{10}{40}$.
Step 3: $\frac{10}{40} \times ₦40,000 = ₦10,000$.

**4. A business pays ₦50,000 for carriage outward. Department X had sales of ₦2,000,000 and Department Y had sales of ₦3,000,000. How much of the carriage outward should be apportioned to Department X?**
- A. ₦10,000
- B. ₦20,000
- C. ₦25,000
- D. ₦30,000

**Answer: B.** Carriage outward is a selling expense, so it is apportioned by sales. 
Step 1: Total sales = 2,000,000 + 3,000,000 = 5,000,000.
Step 2: X's ratio = $\frac{2}{5}$.
Step 3: $\frac{2}{5} \times ₦50,000 = ₦20,000$.

**5. Which of the following describes the correct accounting entry for an inter-departmental transfer of goods from Department M to Department N?**
- A. Debit Department M, Credit Department N
- B. Debit Department N, Credit Department M
- C. Debit General Manager's account, Credit Department N
- D. Debit Cash book, Credit Department M

**Answer: B.** Department N is receiving the goods, so we debit Department N (Transfer inward). Department M is giving out the goods, so we credit Department M (Transfer outward).

**6. If a department transfers goods to another department at selling price, and some of the goods remain unsold at the end of the year, the profit element in the unsold stock is known as:**
- A. Gross profit
- B. Retained profit
- C. Unrealized profit
- D. Super profit

**Answer: C.** It is termed unrealized profit because the goods have not yet been sold to an outside customer, meaning the business as a whole has not truly earned that profit yet.

**7. In a departmental statement of profit or loss, an expense that can be exclusively traced to a single department is termed a:**
- A. Direct expense
- B. Indirect expense
- C. Capital expense
- D. Overhead expense

**Answer: A.** Direct expenses (e.g., a specific department manager's salary) do not need to be apportioned; they are charged directly to the department that incurred them.

**8. For the Clothing Department, Sales are ₦100,000, Opening Stock is ₦10,000, Purchases are ₦60,000, and Closing Stock is ₦15,000. What is the Gross Profit of the Clothing Department?**
- A. ₦30,000
- B. ₦45,000
- C. ₦55,000
- D. ₦85,000

**Answer: B.** 
Step 1: Calculate Cost of Sales = Opening Stock (10,000) + Purchases (60,000) - Closing Stock (15,000) = ₦55,000.
Step 2: Calculate Gross Profit = Sales (100,000) - Cost of Sales (55,000) = ₦45,000.

**9. Department P made a net profit of ₦20,000, while Department Q made a net loss of ₦5,000. What is the total net profit of the business shown in the Total column?**
- A. ₦15,000
- B. ₦20,000
- C. ₦25,000
- D. ₦100,000

**Answer: A.** The total net profit is the algebraic sum of the departmental profits and losses. ₦20,000 + (-₦5,000) = ₦15,000.

**10. Which of the following is the most appropriate basis for apportioning staff canteen costs?**
- A. Floor area
- B. Value of assets
- C. Sales turnover
- D. Number of employees

**Answer: D.** Staff canteen costs are a welfare expense driven by people. Therefore, the fairest basis of apportionment is the number of employees working in each department.
