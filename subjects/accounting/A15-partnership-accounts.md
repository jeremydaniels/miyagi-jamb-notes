# JAMB Accounting Notes: Partnership Accounts

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Topic 15 of the JAMB Principles of Accounts syllabus: Partnership Accounts. It covers the following content items:

* **a.** Formation of partnership
* **b.** Partners current and capital accounts
* **c.** Partnership final accounts:
  * i. profit or loss account
  * ii. appropriation account
  * iii. statement of financial position
* **d.** Treatment of goodwill
* **e.** Admission/retirement of a partner
* **f.** Dissolution of partnership

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. understand the procedures for the formation of partnership;
2. identify the accounts maintained for partnership business;
3. determine the effects of admission and retirement of a partner;
4. determine profit or loss on revaluation of assets; and
5. determine the partners’ share of profit or loss on dissolution.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** Content item **b** is printed as "Partners current and capital accounts" without an apostrophe; it is grammatically correct as "Partners' current and capital accounts". Also, Content item **d** (Treatment of goodwill) does not have a matching objective of its own, but JAMB regularly tests your ability to calculate and adjust for goodwill during the admission or retirement of a partner (under objective iii). We cover it fully here.

## Formation of partnership

**Objective i: understand the procedures for the formation of partnership**
**Content a: Formation of partnership**

A **partnership** is a business owned and managed by two to twenty persons coming together to make a profit. In Nigeria, the maximum number of partners is usually twenty, except for banks (which was traditionally two to ten) and certain professional firms like accountants (ICAN members) or lawyers, which can have more. 

### The Partnership Deed
When forming a partnership, the partners usually agree on the rules of the business. This agreement, when written, is called a **Partnership Deed** (or Articles of Partnership). It contains the procedures and terms for running the firm, such as:
- The name and nature of the business.
- The amount of capital to be contributed by each partner.
- The **profit and loss sharing ratio**.
- The rate of interest on capital and interest on drawings.
- The salaries or bonuses to be paid to active partners.
- Procedures for the admission, retirement, or death of a partner.

### Rules in the absence of a Partnership Deed
If the partners do not write a deed, or if their deed is silent on certain matters, the provisions of the **Partnership Act 1890** apply. These default rules are tested heavily in JAMB:
1. Profits and losses are to be shared **equally**.
2. **No interest** is allowed on partners' capital.
3. **No interest** is charged on partners' drawings.
4. **No salary** is paid to any partner, no matter how much time they spend in the business.
5. If a partner gives a loan or advance to the business (above their agreed capital), they are entitled to **interest at 5% per annum** on that loan.

## Accounts maintained for partnership business

**Objective ii: identify the accounts maintained for partnership business**
**Content b: Partners current and capital accounts**

Unlike a sole trader who has just one capital account, a partnership must keep a separate record of each partner's investment and their share of the profits. There are two methods of maintaining these accounts: the **Fixed Capital method** and the **Fluctuating Capital method**. JAMB usually assumes the Fixed Capital method is used unless told otherwise.

### Fixed vs. Fluctuating Capital Accounts

| Feature | Fixed Capital Method | Fluctuating Capital Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accounts kept** | Two accounts for each partner: a **Capital Account** and a **Current Account**. | Only one account for each partner: the **Capital Account**. |
| **Capital Account balance** | Remains constant. It only changes if the partner brings in new permanent capital or permanently withdraws capital. | Fluctuates every year because all transactions are posted here. |
| **Treatment of adjustments** | Share of profit, interest on capital, salaries, and drawings are posted to the **Current Account**. | Share of profit, interest on capital, salaries, and drawings are posted directly to the **Capital Account**. |

### 1. The Capital Account
Under the fixed method, this account only records the initial capital and any subsequent permanent additions or withdrawals of capital.
- **Credit:** Initial capital introduced, additional capital.

### 2. The Current Account
This is used to record the day-to-day transactions between the partner and the firm.
- **Credit (increases what the firm owes the partner):** Interest on capital, partner's salary, share of profit.
- **Debit (decreases what the firm owes the partner):** Drawings, interest on drawings, share of loss.

### 3. Drawings Account
When partners take cash or goods from the business for personal use, it is recorded as Drawings. At the end of the year, the total drawings and the agreed **interest on drawings** are transferred to the debit side of the partner's Current Account.

## Partnership final accounts

**Content c: Partnership final accounts**

At the end of the financial year, a partnership prepares its final accounts. These include the Statement of Profit or Loss, the Appropriation Account, and the Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet).

### Statement of Profit or Loss (Content c i)
This is prepared exactly like that of a sole trader. Sales, cost of sales, and general operating expenses (like rent, electricity, and staff wages) are calculated to arrive at the **Net Profit**. Note that partner salaries and interest on partner capital are **not** treated as operating expenses here; they are distributions of profit. However, interest on a partner's *loan* to the firm is a true expense and must be deducted here before arriving at Net Profit.

### Appropriation Account (Content c ii)
This account is an extension of the Statement of Profit or Loss. Its purpose is to show how the Net Profit is shared (appropriated) among the partners.

**Step-by-step preparation:**
1. **Start with Net Profit:** Brought down from the Statement of Profit or Loss.
2. **Add Interest on Drawings:** This is charged to partners who took money out. It is an "income" to the partnership and increases the distributable profit.
3. **Deduct Interest on Capital:** This rewards partners for the money they invested.
4. **Deduct Partner's Salary/Bonus:** This rewards active partners for their time and effort.
5. **Determine Residual Profit:** What remains after the deductions is the residual profit (or loss).
6. **Share Residual Profit:** Divide the residual profit among the partners in their agreed profit-sharing ratio and transfer it to their Current Accounts.

#### Worked Example: Appropriation Account
Partners A and B share profits in the ratio 3:2. Their fixed capital balances are A: ₦2,000,000 and B: ₦1,000,000.
The Net Profit for the year is ₦1,500,000.
The deed states:
- Interest on capital is 10% per annum.
- Partner B is to receive a salary of ₦200,000.
- Interest on drawings charged for the year: A = ₦15,000; B = ₦10,000.

**Working:**
- Step 1: Interest on Capital: A = 10% of ₦2,000,000 = ₦200,000. B = 10% of ₦1,000,000 = ₦100,000. (Total = ₦300,000).
- Step 2: Total Available = Net Profit (₦1,500,000) + Interest on Drawings (₦25,000) = ₦1,525,000.
- Step 3: Total Deductions = Interest on Capital (₦300,000) + B's Salary (₦200,000) = ₦500,000.
- Step 4: Residual Profit = ₦1,525,000 - ₦500,000 = ₦1,025,000.
- Step 5: Share of Profit: A gets 3/5 of ₦1,025,000 = ₦615,000. B gets 2/5 of ₦1,025,000 = ₦410,000.

| **Add:** Interest on Drawings: | | |
| Partner A | 15,000 | |

### Statement of Financial Position (Content c iii)
In the Statement of Financial Position (formerly called the Balance Sheet), the "Financed by" or "Equity" section looks different from a sole trader's. You must list the Capital Accounts of all partners, followed by their Current Accounts.

**Example layout of the Equity section:**

| Partner A | 650,000 | |

| **Total Equity** | | **4,250,000** |

## Treatment of goodwill

**Content d: Treatment of goodwill**

**Goodwill** is an intangible non-current asset. It represents the value of a business's good reputation, location, brand name, and loyal customer base, which allows it to earn higher profits than a newly established firm.

When a partnership changes its structure (such as admitting a new partner or a partner retiring), the business is revalued. The old partners have spent years building the firm's reputation, so goodwill must be accounted for before a new partner comes in to share future profits.

### Accounting for Goodwill
There are two main ways JAMB tests the treatment of goodwill on admission or retirement:

**Method 1: Goodwill account is retained in the books.**
The value of goodwill is calculated and brought into the books.
- **Entry:** Debit Goodwill Account; Credit Old Partners' Capital Accounts (in their **old** profit-sharing ratio).
- The goodwill will now appear as an asset in the new Statement of Financial Position.

**Method 2: Goodwill is written off immediately (Most common in JAMB).**
The partners may decide they do not want an intangible asset like goodwill showing on their balance sheet. 
- **Step 1 (Introduce Goodwill):** Debit Goodwill Account; Credit Old Partners' Capital Accounts (in their **old** profit-sharing ratio).
- **Step 2 (Write off Goodwill):** Debit **All** Partners' Capital Accounts (including the new partner, in the **new** profit-sharing ratio); Credit Goodwill Account.
- **Result:** The new partner effectively pays the old partners for a share of the goodwill, and the Goodwill account is closed (it will not appear in the balance sheet).

## Admission and retirement of a partner

**Objective iii: determine the effects of admission and retirement of a partner**
**Content e: Admission/retirement of a partner**

### Admission of a Partner
A firm may admit a new partner to gain more capital, acquire new skills, or reward a hardworking employee. When a new partner is admitted:
1. They bring in capital: Debit Cash/Bank; Credit New Partner's Capital Account.
2. The profit-sharing ratio changes.
3. Assets and liabilities are revalued (so old partners get the benefit of past growth).
4. Goodwill is adjusted.

### Retirement of a Partner
When a partner leaves, they are entitled to their share of the business up to the date of retirement. 
1. Assets are revalued, and the retiring partner gets their share of the revaluation profit or loss.
2. Goodwill is valued, and the retiring partner is credited with their share.
3. The total amount owed to the retiring partner is calculated (Capital balance + Current balance + Share of Revaluation + Share of Goodwill - Drawings).
4. The final balance is paid out (Credit Bank) or transferred to a Loan Account if the firm cannot pay immediately (Credit Retiring Partner's Loan Account).

## Revaluation of assets

**Objective iv: determine profit or loss on revaluation of assets**

Before a new partner joins or an old partner leaves, the assets and liabilities must be adjusted to their current market values. This is done through a **Revaluation Account**.

### The Revaluation Account
This is a nominal account used to calculate the profit or loss from revaluing assets and liabilities.

- **Debit Side (Losses):** Decrease in the value of assets, increase in liabilities, creation of new provisions (e.g., provision for doubtful debts).
- **Credit Side (Gains):** Increase in the value of assets, decrease in liabilities.

If the Credit side is greater than the Debit side, the difference is a **Profit on Revaluation**. This profit is transferred to the **OLD** partners' Capital Accounts in their **OLD** profit-sharing ratio. (A new partner does not share in this past growth).

#### Worked Example: Revaluation
Partners X and Y share profits equally (ratio 1:1). They admit Z as a new partner. The following revaluations are made:
- Premises increased from ₦500,000 to ₦800,000 (Increase = ₦300,000).
- Stock decreased by ₦50,000.
- A provision for doubtful debts of ₦10,000 is created.

**Working:**
Total Gains = ₦300,000.
Total Losses = ₦50,000 + ₦10,000 = ₦60,000.
Profit on Revaluation = ₦300,000 - ₦60,000 = ₦240,000.
Share of Profit: X gets 1/2 (₦120,000); Y gets 1/2 (₦120,000).

| Revaluation Account | ₦ | Details | ₦ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stock (decrease in value) | 50,000 | Premises (increase in value) | 300,000 |
| Provision for doubtful debts | 10,000 | | |
| **Share of Profit on Revaluation:** | | | |
| Partner X (1/2) | 120,000 | | |
| Partner Y (1/2) | 120,000 | | |
| | **300,000** | | **300,000** |

## Dissolution of partnership

**Objective v: determine the partners’ share of profit or loss on dissolution**
**Content f: Dissolution of partnership**

**Dissolution** means bringing the partnership business to an end. The firm sells all its assets, pays off its liabilities, and distributes whatever cash is left to the partners. 
Reasons for dissolution include: the expiry of the partnership term, mutual agreement, continuous losses, death or bankruptcy of a partner, or a court order.

### Accounting Procedures for Dissolution
To close the books, a **Realisation Account** is opened.

**Step 1: Transfer assets:** Close all non-cash asset accounts by transferring their book values to the debit side of the Realisation Account. (Debit Realisation; Credit Assets).
**Step 2: Dissolution expenses:** Pay the costs of selling assets. (Debit Realisation; Credit Bank).
**Step 3: Realise assets:** Record the cash received from selling the assets. (Debit Bank; Credit Realisation).
**Step 4: Pay liabilities:** Pay off outside creditors. (Debit Creditors; Credit Bank). Note: If a creditor gives a discount, the discount is a gain credited to the Realisation Account.
**Step 5: Share Profit/Loss on Realisation:** Find the balance of the Realisation Account. 
- If Debit > Credit, it is a Loss on Realisation.
- If Credit > Debit, it is a Profit on Realisation.
Transfer this to the partners' Capital Accounts in their normal profit-sharing ratio.
**Step 6: Final Settlement:** Pay the partners their final capital balances. (Debit Capital Accounts; Credit Bank). This closes the Bank Account and the firm's books completely.

#### Worked Example: Dissolution
Partners P, Q, and R share profits in the ratio 2:2:1. They dissolve the firm. 
- Total assets transferred to Realisation: ₦1,500,000.
- Cash realised from selling assets: ₦1,350,000.
- Dissolution expenses paid from Bank: ₦50,000.

**Working:**
Total Debit (Cost of assets + Expenses) = ₦1,500,000 + ₦50,000 = ₦1,550,000.
Total Credit (Cash realised) = ₦1,350,000.
Loss on Realisation = ₦1,550,000 - ₦1,350,000 = ₦200,000.
Share of Loss:
- P (2/5 of ₦200,000) = ₦80,000
- Q (2/5 of ₦200,000) = ₦80,000
- R (1/5 of ₦200,000) = ₦40,000

| Realisation Account | ₦ | Details | ₦ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total Assets (transferred at book value)| 1,500,000 | Bank (cash from assets realised) | 1,350,000 |
| Bank (dissolution expenses) | 50,000 | **Share of Loss on Realisation:** | |
| | | Partner P (2/5) | 80,000 |
| | | Partner Q (2/5) | 80,000 |
| | | Partner R (1/5) | 40,000 |
| | **1,550,000** | | **1,550,000** |

### The Rule in *Garner v. Murray*
During dissolution, a partner's capital account might end up with a debit balance (they owe the firm). If this partner is bankrupt (insolvent) and cannot pay the money they owe, the loss must be borne by the remaining **solvent** partners. 
The famous legal case *Garner v. Murray* established that this deficiency is shared among the solvent partners **in the ratio of their last agreed capital balances**, NOT in their normal profit-sharing ratio.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Partnership Deed | A written agreement containing the rules, terms, and profit-sharing arrangements of the partners. |
| Fixed Capital Method | A system where initial capital is kept in a separate Capital Account, and all other adjustments go to a Current Account. |
| Appropriation Account | An account showing how the net profit is distributed among partners (salaries, interest, residual share). |
| Residual Profit | The amount of net profit left to be shared after deducting interest on capital and partners' salaries, and adding interest on drawings. |
| Goodwill | An intangible asset representing the good reputation and customer loyalty of a business. |
| Revaluation Account | An account used to adjust the value of assets and liabilities to their current market price upon the admission or retirement of a partner. |
| Realisation Account | An account opened during dissolution to record the sale of assets and payment of dissolution expenses, to find the profit or loss on closure. |
| *Garner v. Murray* Rule | A rule stating that the deficiency of an insolvent partner on dissolution must be borne by solvent partners in the ratio of their last agreed capital balances. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Absence of a deed:** Remember the strict rules! If JAMB says there is no deed, profit is shared *equally*, and *no* interest is allowed on capital or drawings, and *no* salaries are paid. Only interest on a *loan* from a partner is allowed at 5%.
- **Interest on drawings is added:** A common mistake is subtracting interest on drawings in the Appropriation Account. It must be **added** to the Net Profit because the partners are paying the firm.
- **Revaluation vs. Realisation:** Do not confuse them. **Revaluation** is for admission/retirement (the business continues). **Realisation** is for dissolution (the business ends).
- **Goodwill write-off:** If goodwill is introduced and then written off, it is debited to *all* partners in the *new* profit-sharing ratio, and credited to the *old* partners in the *old* profit-sharing ratio.
- **Profit on Revaluation:** This belongs ONLY to the **old** partners in their **old** ratio. A newly admitted partner gets nothing from past revaluations.

## Quick summary

1. A partnership is formed by two to twenty persons. Without a Partnership Deed, profits are shared equally and no partner receives a salary or interest on capital.
2. Under the fixed capital system, permanent capital is kept in the Capital Account, while profits, drawings, and interest go to the Current Account.
3. The Appropriation Account adjusts Net Profit by adding interest on drawings, and subtracting interest on capital and partner salaries, before splitting the residual profit.
4. When a partner is admitted or retires, assets are revalued. The profit or loss on revaluation is shared among the old partners in their old profit-sharing ratio.
5. Goodwill is the value of the firm's reputation. It can be retained in the books or immediately written off to the partners' capital accounts.
6. Dissolution ends the partnership. A Realisation Account is used to sell assets; any profit or loss on realisation is shared among the partners.
7. The rule in *Garner v. Murray* dictates that if a partner is insolvent on dissolution, their capital deficiency is covered by solvent partners in the ratio of their last agreed capital.

## Practice questions

**1. In the absence of a partnership deed, the Partnership Act 1890 states that**
- A. partners are entitled to a 5% salary bonus
- B. profits and losses are shared according to capital contribution
- C. interest on capital is allowed at 5% per annum
- D. profits and losses are shared equally

**Answer: D.** In the absence of a deed, the law assumes equality. No salary or interest on capital is allowed, and profits are shared equally.

**2. Partners X and Y share profits in the ratio 3:2. The Net Profit for the year is ₦1,000,000. Total interest on capital is ₦100,000 and X is entitled to a salary of ₦200,000. What is Y's share of the residual profit?**
- A. ₦280,000
- B. ₦320,000
- C. ₦420,000
- D. ₦500,000

**Answer: A.** Residual Profit = Net Profit (₦1,000,000) - Interest on Capital (₦100,000) - Salary (₦200,000) = ₦700,000. Y's share is 2/5 of ₦700,000 = ₦280,000. Option C (₦420,000) is X's share (3/5) of the residual profit; option B (₦320,000) comes from deducting only the salary (2/5 × ₦800,000); option D (₦500,000) comes from sharing the net profit equally.

**3. In a partnership, interest charged on a partner's drawings is treated in the Appropriation Account as**
- A. an addition to the Net Profit
- B. a deduction from the Net Profit
- C. an operating expense
- D. a non-current asset

**Answer: A.** Interest on drawings is charged to the partner and paid into the firm. It acts as an income to the partnership, increasing the total profit available to be shared.

**4. Under the fixed capital method, a partner's share of profit is credited to the**
- A. Capital Account
- B. Current Account
- C. Drawings Account
- D. Appropriation Account

**Answer: B.** In the fixed capital method, the Capital Account stays constant. All everyday adjustments, including the share of profit, are credited to the Current Account.

**5. Partners A and B share profits in the ratio 2:1. They admit C as a new partner. The new profit-sharing ratio is A (2), B (1), C (1). Goodwill is valued at ₦120,000 and is to be immediately written off. What is the net effect of this goodwill adjustment on A's capital account?**
- A. Credited with ₦80,000
- B. Debited with ₦60,000
- C. Credited with ₦20,000
- D. Debited with ₦20,000

**Answer: C.** Step 1: Credit old partners in old ratio (2:1). A gets 2/3 of ₦120,000 = ₦80,000 (Cr). Step 2: Debit all partners in new ratio (2:1:1 = 2/4, 1/4, 1/4). A is debited 2/4 of ₦120,000 = ₦60,000 (Dr). Net effect on A: Cr ₦80,000 - Dr ₦60,000 = Credit of ₦20,000.

**6. The account used to adjust the value of assets and liabilities upon the admission of a new partner is the**
- A. Realisation Account
- B. Revaluation Account
- C. Appropriation Account
- D. Suspense Account

**Answer: B.** The Revaluation Account is used to bring asset values up to date so that old partners benefit from past appreciation. Realisation is used only when the firm is closing down (dissolution).

**7. A retiring partner's capital balance is ₦300,000. Their share of profit on revaluation is ₦50,000, their share of goodwill is ₦40,000, and they have drawings of ₦20,000. What is the final amount due to the retiring partner?**
- A. ₦370,000
- B. ₦390,000
- C. ₦410,000
- D. ₦350,000

**Answer: A.** Amount due = Capital (₦300,000) + Revaluation (₦50,000) + Goodwill (₦40,000) - Drawings (₦20,000) = ₦370,000. A common mistake is to add drawings instead of subtracting them (which gives option C).

**8. During the dissolution of a partnership, the book value of assets transferred to the Realisation Account was ₦500,000. Dissolution expenses were ₦30,000. If the assets were sold for ₦350,000, what is the loss on realisation?**
- A. ₦120,000
- B. ₦150,000
- C. ₦180,000
- D. ₦200,000

**Answer: C.** Total Debit to Realisation = Book value (₦500,000) + Expenses (₦30,000) = ₦530,000. Total Credit = Assets sold (₦350,000). Loss = ₦530,000 - ₦350,000 = ₦180,000. A common mistake is forgetting to include the dissolution expenses (which gives option B).

**9. According to the rule in *Garner v. Murray*, if a partner becomes insolvent during dissolution, their capital deficiency must be borne by the solvent partners in the ratio of their**
- A. normal profit-sharing ratio
- B. last agreed capital balances
- C. current account balances
- D. equal proportions

**Answer: B.** The case established that the loss caused by a partner's inability to pay their debit balance is a capital loss, and therefore must be shared by solvent partners in the ratio of their last agreed capital balances, not their profit-sharing ratio.

**10. Which of the following items is NOT found on the debit side of the Revaluation Account?**
- A. Decrease in the value of stock
- B. Increase in the provision for doubtful debts
- C. Increase in the value of premises
- D. Increase in the value of creditors

**Answer: C.** The Revaluation Account is debited with losses. An increase in the value of premises is a gain, so it is posted to the credit side. The other options all represent losses.
