# JAMB Accounting Notes: Control Accounts

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section 8 of the JAMB Principles of Accounts syllabus: Control Accounts. It covers the preparation and purpose of control accounts in a business.

This file covers the following content items:
- a. Meaning and uses of control accounts
- b. Purchases ledger control account
- c. Sales ledger control account

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. understand the meaning of control accounts;
2. identify the uses of control accounts in a business enterprise;
3. differentiate between sales ledger control account and purchases ledger control account; and
4. identify the entries in control accounts.

**Note on textbook variations:** At the time of writing, International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) use the terms "Receivables" instead of "Debtors", and "Payables" instead of "Creditors". Therefore, modern textbooks often call the Sales Ledger Control Account the "Receivables Control Account", and the Purchases Ledger Control Account the "Payables Control Account". The JAMB syllabus uses the traditional terms, so we will use "Debtors" and "Creditors" throughout these notes, but you should remember that they mean the same thing. 

## Meaning and uses of control accounts

In a small business, all transactions can be kept in a single ledger. However, as a business grows, it must divide its ledger into separate books: a **Sales Ledger** for customers' personal accounts, a **Purchases Ledger** for suppliers' personal accounts, and a **General Ledger** for all other accounts. To keep track of the totals and check for errors, the business prepares control accounts.

### Meaning of control accounts

**Objective i: understand the meaning of control accounts.**

A **control account** is a summary account kept in the General Ledger that checks the arithmetical accuracy of the individual balances in a subsidiary ledger (such as the sales ledger or purchases ledger). 

It is prepared by posting the total amounts from the books of original entry (day books and cash book) instead of individual transaction amounts. Because it uses totals, the balance of the control account must perfectly equal the sum of all the individual account balances in the subsidiary ledger it controls. 

For example, if you add up the balances of the 50 individual customers in your sales ledger, the total must equal the single closing balance shown in your Sales Ledger Control Account. If they do not agree, an error has been made.

### Uses of control accounts in a business enterprise

**Objective ii: identify the uses of control accounts in a business enterprise.**

Control accounts are essential for the smooth running of an accounting system. Their main uses include:

1. **Locating errors quickly:** If the trial balance does not balance, control accounts help pinpoint where the mistake is. If the Sales Ledger Control Account agrees with the total of the debtors' schedule but the Purchases Ledger Control Account does not agree with the creditors' schedule, you instantly know the error is in the purchases ledger.
2. **Checking arithmetical accuracy:** They provide independent proof that the additions and calculations in the subsidiary ledgers are correct.
3. **Preventing fraud:** In a well-organised business, the control account is prepared by a senior staff member who does not make entries in the personal ledgers. This segregation of duties makes it difficult for a single clerk to commit and hide fraud.
4. **Providing quick totals for final accounts:** Management can obtain the total figures for Debtors and Creditors required for the Trial Balance and Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet) immediately from the control accounts, without waiting for hundreds of individual ledger accounts to be balanced.
5. **Internal control and audit:** They serve as an internal check system, making the work of internal and external auditors much easier.

## Identifying entries in control accounts

**Objective iv: identify the entries in control accounts.**

To prepare a control account, you must know what items increase or decrease the balance, and which side of the account to post them. The entries are derived from the totals of the books of prime entry at the end of the month or period.

### 1. Entries in the Sales Ledger Control Account
This account tracks total **Debtors** (an asset). Assets increase on the debit side and decrease on the credit side.

**Debit Entries (Items that increase what customers owe):**
- **Opening Debit Balance:** Total amount owed by customers at the start of the period.
- **Credit Sales:** From the total of the Sales Day Book.
- **Dishonoured Cheques:** Cheques previously received from customers that bounced (from the Cash Book). This increases their debt again.
- **Interest Charged:** Interest added to overdue accounts (from the Journal Proper).
- **Carriage Outwards:** Transport costs passed on and charged to the customer's account.

**Credit Entries (Items that decrease what customers owe):**
- **Cash and Cheques Received:** Total payments from customers (from the Cash Book).
- **Discount Allowed:** Cash discounts given to customers for prompt payment (from the Cash Book).
- **Returns Inwards:** Goods returned by customers (from the Returns Inwards Day Book).
- **Bad Debts:** Debts written off as uncollectible (from the Journal Proper).
- **Set-offs (Contra Entries):** Balances cancelled against the purchases ledger.

### 2. Entries in the Purchases Ledger Control Account
This account tracks total **Creditors** (a liability). Liabilities increase on the credit side and decrease on the debit side.

**Credit Entries (Items that increase what the business owes):**
- **Opening Credit Balance:** Total amount owed to suppliers at the start of the period.
- **Credit Purchases:** From the total of the Purchases Day Book.
- **Interest Charged by Suppliers:** Extra charges on overdue accounts.
- **Carriage Inwards:** Transport costs charged by suppliers.

**Debit Entries (Items that decrease what the business owes):**
- **Cash and Cheques Paid:** Total payments made to suppliers (from the Cash Book).
- **Discount Received:** Cash discounts given by suppliers (from the Cash Book).
- **Returns Outwards:** Goods returned to suppliers (from the Returns Outwards Day Book).
- **Set-offs (Contra Entries):** Balances cancelled against the sales ledger.

### Special Adjustments: Set-offs and Minority Balances

**Contra Entries (Set-offs):** 
Sometimes, a business sells goods to a person and also buys goods from the same person. Instead of exchanging cash, the business will offset (cancel out) the smaller balance against the larger one. This reduces both the debtor balance and the creditor balance. 
- **Accounting entry:** Debit the Purchases Ledger Control Account and Credit the Sales Ledger Control Account.

**Minority Balances:** 
Usually, the Sales Ledger Control Account has a debit balance (asset) and the Purchases Ledger Control Account has a credit balance (liability). However, sometimes a subsidiary ledger contains abnormal balances:
- **Credit balance in the Sales Ledger (Minority):** Occurs if a customer overpays, pays in advance, or returns goods after paying in full. The business now owes the customer.
- **Debit balance in the Purchases Ledger (Minority):** Occurs if the business overpays a supplier or returns goods after settling the account. The supplier now owes the business.
When balancing the control account, these minority balances are brought down on their respective opposite sides, meaning the control account will temporarily show two closing balances.

## Sales ledger control account

**Content c: Sales ledger control account.**

The Sales Ledger Control Account (SLCA), also known as the Total Debtors Account, summarises all transactions relating to credit customers. 

### Step-by-Step Preparation

**Step 1:** Open the T-account. Post the opening normal debit balance on the debit side. If there is a minority credit balance (e.g. advance payments from customers), post it on the credit side.
**Step 2:** Post all totals that increase debtors (like credit sales and dishonoured cheques) to the debit side.
**Step 3:** Post all totals that decrease debtors (like cash received, discounts allowed, returns inwards, bad debts, and set-offs) to the credit side.
**Step 4:** Balance the account to find the closing balance carried down (c/d), which represents the total outstanding debtors at the end of the period.

### Worked Example: Sales Ledger Control Account

Ojo operates a provision store in Mushin. From his books for the month of May, the following totals were extracted:

- Sales ledger balance at May 1 (Debit): ₦50,000
- Total credit sales for May: ₦200,000
- Cash and cheques received from customers: ₦150,000
- Discount allowed to customers: ₦10,000
- Returns inwards: ₦15,000
- Bad debts written off: ₦8,000
- Cheques from customers dishonoured by the bank: ₦5,000
- Set-off against purchases ledger: ₦12,000

**Solution:**

**Sales Ledger Control Account**

| Debit (Increases Debtors) | ₦ | Credit (Decreases Debtors) | ₦ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Balance b/d | 50,000 | Bank / Cash | 150,000 |
| Sales (credit) | 200,000 | Discount allowed | 10,000 |
| Bank (dishonoured cheque) | 5,000 | Returns inwards | 15,000 |
|  |  | Bad debts | 8,000 |
|  |  | Set-off (contra) | 12,000 |
|  |  | Balance c/d | 60,000 |
| **Total** | **255,000** | **Total** | **255,000** |
| Balance b/d | 60,000 | | |

*Explanation:* The debit side sum is ₦255,000. The credit entries sum up to ₦195,000. The balancing figure (₦255,000 - ₦195,000 = ₦60,000) is the closing debtors balance carried down to the next month.

## Purchases ledger control account

**Content b: Purchases ledger control account.**

The Purchases Ledger Control Account (PLCA), also known as the Total Creditors Account, summarises all transactions relating to credit suppliers.

### Step-by-Step Preparation

**Step 1:** Post the opening normal credit balance on the credit side. 
**Step 2:** Post all totals that increase creditors (like credit purchases) to the credit side.
**Step 3:** Post all totals that decrease creditors (like cash paid, discounts received, returns outwards, and set-offs) to the debit side.
**Step 4:** Balance the account to find the closing balance carried down (c/d), which represents the total outstanding creditors at the end of the period.

### Worked Example: Purchases Ledger Control Account

Amina runs a boutique in Kano. The following information relates to her suppliers for June:

- Purchases ledger balance at June 1 (Credit): ₦80,000
- Total credit purchases for June: ₦150,000
- Cheques paid to suppliers: ₦120,000
- Discount received from suppliers: ₦5,000
- Returns outwards: ₦10,000
- Set-off against sales ledger: ₦12,000

**Solution:**

**Purchases Ledger Control Account**

| Debit (Decreases Creditors) | ₦ | Credit (Increases Creditors) | ₦ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank / Cash | 120,000 | Balance b/d | 80,000 |
| Discount received | 5,000 | Purchases (credit) | 150,000 |
| Returns outwards | 10,000 |  |  |
| Set-off (contra) | 12,000 |  |  |
| Balance c/d | 83,000 |  |  |
| **Total** | **230,000** | **Total** | **230,000** |
|  |  | Balance b/d | 83,000 |

*Explanation:* The credit side sum is ₦230,000. The debit entries sum up to ₦147,000. The balancing figure (₦230,000 - ₦147,000 = ₦83,000) is the closing creditors balance carried down.

## Differences between sales ledger and purchases ledger control accounts

**Objective iii: differentiate between sales ledger control account and purchases ledger control account.**

Though both are control accounts, they track opposite elements of the business. 

| Feature | Sales Ledger Control Account | Purchases Ledger Control Account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | Summarises personal accounts of customers (Debtors). | Summarises personal accounts of suppliers (Creditors). |
| **Alternative Names** | Total Debtors Account, Receivables Control Account. | Total Creditors Account, Payables Control Account. |
| **Nature of Normal Balance** | Debit balance (It represents an Asset). | Credit balance (It represents a Liability). |
| **Main Source of Increase** | Credit Sales (posted to the debit side). | Credit Purchases (posted to the credit side). |
| **Main Source of Decrease** | Cash received, returns inwards, discount allowed (posted to the credit side). | Cash paid, returns outwards, discount received (posted to the debit side). |
| **Minority Balance Cause** | Overpayment by a customer (Credit balance). | Overpayment to a supplier (Debit balance). |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Control Account** | A summary account that acts as a check on the arithmetic accuracy of a subsidiary ledger. |
| **Sales Ledger** | A ledger containing the individual personal accounts of credit customers (debtors). |
| **Purchases Ledger** | A ledger containing the individual personal accounts of credit suppliers (creditors). |
| **Debtors** | Individuals or businesses that owe money to the enterprise for goods sold on credit. |
| **Creditors** | Individuals or businesses to whom the enterprise owes money for goods bought on credit. |
| **Contra Entry (Set-off)** | An entry made to cancel out amounts owed in both the sales and purchases ledgers when a person is both a customer and a supplier. |
| **Minority Balance** | An abnormal balance in a ledger, such as a credit balance in a debtor's account due to overpayment. |
| **Dishonoured Cheque** | A cheque from a customer that the bank refuses to pay (usually due to insufficient funds), increasing the debtor's balance again. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Cash transactions are excluded:** Do NOT include cash sales or cash purchases in control accounts. Only credit transactions are recorded here.
- **Provision for bad debts is excluded:** The provision for doubtful debts is an estimate and does not affect the individual debtors' accounts. Never include it in the Sales Ledger Control Account.
- **Bad debts recovered are excluded:** When a previously written-off bad debt is paid, it affects the Cash Book and Bad Debts Recovered Account. It does not go back into the Sales Ledger Control Account.
- **Watch out for dishonoured cheques:** A dishonoured cheque *increases* the amount the customer owes. Always debit it to the Sales Ledger Control Account.
- **Don't mix up discounts:** Discount *allowed* decreases debtors (Credit SLCA). Discount *received* decreases creditors (Debit PLCA).

## Quick summary

1. Control accounts are summary accounts in the general ledger used to check the arithmetic accuracy of the sales and purchases subsidiary ledgers.
2. They help locate errors quickly, prevent fraud, and provide rapid totals for preparing final accounts.
3. The Sales Ledger Control Account (Total Debtors Account) normally has a debit balance. It is increased by credit sales and dishonoured cheques, and decreased by cash received, returns inwards, bad debts, and discounts allowed.
4. The Purchases Ledger Control Account (Total Creditors Account) normally has a credit balance. It is increased by credit purchases and decreased by cash paid, returns outwards, and discounts received.
5. A contra entry (set-off) occurs when a business and another party act as both buyer and seller to each other; the smaller balance is cancelled out by debiting the PLCA and crediting the SLCA.
6. A minority balance is an abnormal balance (like a credit balance in the SLCA caused by a customer's overpayment) and is brought down on the opposite side of the normal balance.

## Practice questions

**1. A control account is essentially used to:**
- A. calculate the net profit of a business
- B. verify the arithmetic accuracy of the subsidiary ledgers
- C. record all cash payments and receipts
- D. replace the individual debtors and creditors accounts

**Answer: B.** A control account is a summary account that checks if the sum of all individual accounts in a subsidiary ledger matches the expected total, proving arithmetic accuracy.

**2. Which of the following items will be entered on the debit side of the Sales Ledger Control Account?**
- A. Discount allowed
- B. Returns inwards
- C. Dishonoured cheques
- D. Bad debts

**Answer: C.** A dishonoured cheque means the customer's debt is reinstated, which increases debtors. Debtors are an asset, so increases are recorded on the debit side. The other options reduce debtors and are credited.

**3. The balance of the Purchases Ledger Control Account usually appears in the Statement of Financial Position as a:**
- A. non-current asset
- B. current liability
- C. current asset
- D. long-term liability

**Answer: B.** The Purchases Ledger Control Account represents total trade creditors (payables), which are obligations the business must settle in the short term, making it a current liability.

**4. A contra entry (set-off) of ₦5,000 between the sales and purchases ledgers is recorded in the control accounts as:**
- A. Debit SLCA ₦5,000; Credit PLCA ₦5,000
- B. Debit PLCA ₦5,000; Credit SLCA ₦5,000
- C. Debit PLCA ₦5,000; Debit SLCA ₦5,000
- D. Credit PLCA ₦5,000; Credit SLCA ₦5,000

**Answer: B.** A set-off reduces the amount owed to the creditor (decreasing liability, so Debit PLCA) and simultaneously reduces the amount the debtor owes (decreasing asset, so Credit SLCA).

**5. Find the closing balance of the Sales Ledger Control Account given the following details: Opening debit balance ₦12,000; Credit sales ₦50,000; Cash received ₦35,000; Discount allowed ₦2,000; Bad debts ₦1,500; Returns inwards ₦3,000.**
- A. ₦20,500
- B. ₦24,500
- C. ₦26,500
- D. ₦24,000

**Answer: A.** 
Step 1: Calculate the total debit side (increases): Opening balance (₦12,000) + Credit sales (₦50,000) = ₦62,000.
Step 2: Calculate the total credit entries (decreases): Cash (₦35,000) + Discount allowed (₦2,000) + Bad debts (₦1,500) + Returns inwards (₦3,000) = ₦41,500.
Step 3: Subtract credit entries from debit total: ₦62,000 - ₦41,500 = ₦20,500. 
The mistake behind option D (₦24,000) is improperly ignoring the non-cash reductions like discount allowed and bad debts.

**6. Calculate the credit purchases for the period given: Opening PLCA credit balance ₦20,000; Closing PLCA credit balance ₦28,000; Cash paid to suppliers ₦75,000; Discount received ₦4,000; Returns outwards ₦6,000.**
- A. ₦77,000
- B. ₦83,000
- C. ₦133,000
- D. ₦93,000

**Answer: D.** 
Step 1: Identify the total on the debit side of the PLCA (which decreases creditors): Cash paid (₦75,000) + Discount received (₦4,000) + Returns outwards (₦6,000) + Closing balance c/d (₦28,000) = ₦113,000.
Step 2: Subtract the existing credit entry (Opening balance ₦20,000) from this total to find the missing credit purchases.
Step 3: ₦113,000 - ₦20,000 = ₦93,000.
The mistake behind option B (₦83,000) is ignoring the discount received and returns outwards.

**7. A customer overpaid his account by ₦2,000. How is this balance treated at the end of the period in the Sales Ledger Control Account?**
- A. As a normal debit balance brought down
- B. As a minority credit balance brought down
- C. Transferred to the Purchases Ledger Control Account
- D. Written off immediately as a bad debt

**Answer: B.** An overpayment by a customer means the business temporarily owes the customer money. This creates a liability, which is recorded as a minority credit balance brought down in the Sales Ledger Control Account.

**8. Which of the following is NOT recorded in a Sales Ledger Control Account?**
- A. Cash sales
- B. Credit sales
- C. Returns inwards
- D. Discount allowed

**Answer: A.** Cash sales do not create debtors because payment is received immediately. Only credit transactions and subsequent settlements of those credit transactions are recorded in the control accounts.

**9. How is the provision for doubtful debts treated in the Sales Ledger Control Account?**
- A. It is debited to increase the debtors balance
- B. It is credited to decrease the debtors balance
- C. It is not recorded in the control account
- D. It is added to the bad debts written off

**Answer: C.** A provision for doubtful debts is a general estimate and does not affect any individual customer's personal account. Therefore, it is never posted to the Sales Ledger Control Account.

**10. The total of the Returns Outwards day book is transferred to the:**
- A. Debit side of the Sales Ledger Control Account
- B. Credit side of the Sales Ledger Control Account
- C. Debit side of the Purchases Ledger Control Account
- D. Credit side of the Purchases Ledger Control Account

**Answer: C.** Returns outwards are goods sent back to suppliers. This reduces the amount the business owes to its creditors, and since the Purchases Ledger Control Account is a liability account, a decrease is recorded on the debit side.
