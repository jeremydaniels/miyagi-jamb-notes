# JAMB Agricultural Science Notes: Meaning and Scope of Agriculture

It covers the following content items:

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

## Definition of Agriculture

The word "agriculture" comes from two Latin words: ***ager***, which means field or land, and ***cultura***, which means cultivation or to till. Therefore, the literal, traditional meaning of agriculture is the cultivation of the field or land.

In the past, agriculture was defined simply as the production of crops and the rearing of animals for man's use. However, as human needs and technologies have grown, this traditional definition is no longer complete.

### Using the definition of Agriculture in modern terms

In **modern terms**, agriculture is defined as the deliberate effort made by man to cultivate crops, rear animals, and pull natural resources from the land and water, including the **production**, **processing**, and **marketing** of these products for the benefit of mankind. 

The modern definition treats agriculture as a complete business value chain with three main interconnected stages:

#### 1. Production
This is the first stage. It involves all the practical activities carried out on the farm to grow crops and raise livestock until they are ready for harvest or slaughter.
*   **Examples:** Planting maize (*Zea mays*), applying NPK fertilizer, weeding, feeding poultry, and catching fish in a pond.
*   The goal here is to yield raw materials (for example, raw cassava tubers, fresh milk, or live chickens).

#### 2. Processing
Raw agricultural products are often highly perishable (they spoil quickly) and might not be in a form consumers can eat or use directly. Processing involves changing these raw materials into finished or semi-finished products to add value, improve taste, and extend their shelf life.
*   **Examples:** 
    *   Processing raw, perishable cassava tubers into dry *garri* or cassava flour.
    *   Extracting palm oil from fresh oil palm fruits.
    *   Pasteurising fresh cow milk and turning it into yoghurt, butter, or cheese.
    *   Milling harvested paddy rice to remove the husks and bran to get white rice.
*   Processing is the bridge that links the farm to the factory and the kitchen.

#### 3. Marketing
Marketing is not just sitting and selling in a local market; it encompasses all the activities involved in moving the processed or raw farm produce from the producer (farmer) to the final consumer. 
*   **Examples:** Packaging, grading produce by size and quality, storing in warehouses or silos, advertising, and transporting produce from rural areas to urban centres (for example, moving yams from a farm in Benue State to Mile 12 market in Lagos), and final sales.
*   Marketing ensures the farmer makes a profit and the consumer gets the food they need at the right time and place.

## Branches of Agriculture

Agriculture is a very broad science. Because it covers so many different activities—from studying the soil to treating sick animals and selling farm produce—it is divided into several specialized branches. 

### Differentiating between the various branches of Agriculture

JAMB expects you to know what each branch focuses on and to be able to differentiate them clearly.

| Branch of Agriculture | Main Focus | Examples of Activities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Crop Science (Agronomy)** | The production, improvement, and management of crops and the soil they grow in. | Cultivating yams, maize, and cowpeas; applying fertilizers; practicing crop rotation. |
| **Animal Science (Animal Husbandry)** | The rearing, breeding, feeding, and management of farm animals (livestock). | Rearing poultry, cattle, sheep, and pigs for meat, milk, and eggs; formulating feed rations. |
| **Veterinary Medicine** | The prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of diseases and pests in farm animals. | Vaccinating chickens against Newcastle disease; treating a cow for foot-and-mouth disease. |
| **Soil Science** | The study of soil formation, classification, physical and chemical properties, and fertility management. | Testing soil pH; studying the soil profile; controlling soil erosion. |
| **Agricultural Economics** | The application of economic principles to agricultural business, focusing on profit, resource allocation, and finance. | Keeping farm records; calculating gross margin; securing farm credit/loans; determining produce prices. |
| **Agricultural Extension** | Educating farmers and transferring new agricultural research and technologies from the laboratory to the farm. | Teaching farmers how to use a new improved maize variety or safely apply pesticides. |
| **Forestry (Silviculture)** | The management and conservation of forest resources and trees. | Establishing timber plantations (e.g., teak, mahogany); preventing deforestation; creating forest reserves. |
| **Fishery** | The breeding, rearing, catching, and management of fishes and other aquatic organisms. | Managing a catfish or tilapia pond; deep-sea fishing; making fish feed. |
| **Wildlife Conservation** | The protection and management of wild animals and their natural habitats (game reserves). | Protecting elephants, lions, and antelopes in reserves like Yankari National Park. |
| **Agricultural Engineering** | The design, construction, and maintenance of farm tools, machinery, and structures. | Designing tractors, ploughs, incubators, and silos; planning farmstead layouts. |
| **Apiculture (Bee-keeping)** | The specialized rearing of honeybees for the production of honey and beeswax. | Setting up wooden beehives; harvesting honey safely in protective suits. |
| **Horticulture** | The cultivation of fruits (pomology), vegetables (olericulture), and ornamental plants/flowers (floriculture). | Growing tomatoes, oranges, or decorative flowers for landscaping. |

*(Note: These individual branches, such as Crop Husbandry, Livestock Management, and Farm Management, are explored in deep detail in their respective files in this series).*

## Types of Agriculture i.e., subsistence and commercial

In West Africa, agriculture is broadly classified into two main types based on the scale of operation and the primary motive of the farmer: **Subsistence Agriculture** and **Commercial Agriculture**.

### Differentiating between the various types of Agriculture

You must be able to compare these two types across several features, and identify their unique advantages, disadvantages, and the specific problems they face in Nigeria and West Africa.

#### Comparison of Subsistence and Commercial Agriculture

| Feature | Subsistence Agriculture | Commercial Agriculture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Motive** | To produce food for the farmer and their immediate family | To produce raw materials and food for sale to maximize profit |
| **Size of Land** | Small (usually less than 2 to 3 hectares) | Large (often hundreds or thousands of hectares) |
| **Tools and Power** | Simple hand tools (hoes, cutlasses); human and animal power | Complex machinery (tractors, combine harvesters); mechanical power |
| **Capital Requirement** | Low capital | Extremely high capital |
| **Labour Source** | Family labour (farmer, spouse, children) | Paid (hired) skilled and unskilled labour, and management staff |
| **Farming System** | Mixed cropping is common (growing different crops together) | Monocropping (monoculture) is common (growing one crop) |
| **Use of Inputs** | Minimal use of fertilizers, pesticides, and improved seeds | Heavy use of agrochemicals, fertilizers, and improved seeds |
| **Yield/Output** | Low yield | High yield / mass production |
| **Record Keeping** | Poor or non-existent farm records | Strict and detailed financial and production records |

#### Advantages and Disadvantages of Subsistence Agriculture

**Advantages:**
1.  **Low Capital:** It is very cheap to start because the simple tools are inexpensive and family labour is free.
2.  **Food Security for the Family:** It guarantees a steady supply of basic food for the farmer's household.
3.  **Low Risk of Complete Failure:** By practicing mixed cropping, the farmer is protected. If one crop fails due to pests or weather, another might survive.
4.  **No Need for Special Skills:** It does not require high technical knowledge, formal education, or complex machine operations.
5.  **Environmental Preservation:** The small scale and minimal use of chemicals mean it rarely causes severe environmental pollution.

**Disadvantages:**
1.  **Low Output:** The yield is too small to feed a growing national population or supply manufacturing industries.
2.  **No Surplus Income:** The farmer remains poor because there is little or nothing left over to sell for cash.
3.  **Drudgery (Hard Labour):** Using cutlasses and hoes is physically exhausting, causes early aging in farmers, and discourages youths from farming.
4.  **High Wastage:** A lack of processing and modern storage facilities leads to high post-harvest losses.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages of Commercial Agriculture

**Advantages:**
1.  **Massive Production:** It produces abundant food for the nation and raw materials for industries, ensuring national food security.
2.  **High Income:** It generates massive profits for the farmer and foreign exchange for the country (through exports like cocoa or rubber).
3.  **Job Creation:** It creates employment for drivers, mechanics, agronomists, accountants, and factory workers.
4.  **Efficiency:** The use of machinery and scientific methods makes operations faster, highly efficient, and less physically demanding.
5.  **Infrastructural Development:** Large farms often bring roads, electricity, and water boreholes to rural areas to support their operations.

**Disadvantages:**
1.  **High Capital Requirement:** It is very expensive to set up and run, making it impossible for poor rural farmers to start.
2.  **Environmental Degradation:** The heavy use of agrochemicals (fertilizers, pesticides) and heavy machinery can destroy soil structure, cause erosion, and pollute water bodies.
3.  **High Risk:** Growing only one crop (monocropping) on a massive scale means a single disease outbreak or drought can destroy the entire investment.
4.  **Displacement of Communities:** Large land acquisition can displace local communities and cause land disputes.

#### Problems of Subsistence and Commercial Agriculture

**Problems of Subsistence Agriculture:**
1.  **Land Tenure System:** The inheritance laws in Nigeria often lead to land fragmentation, where land is divided into tiny plots among children over generations, making it too small for meaningful expansion.
2.  **Lack of Finance:** Subsistence farmers lack collateral (like Certificates of Occupancy) to secure agricultural loans from banks.
3.  **Poor Tools:** Reliance on crude implements strictly limits the area of land a farmer can physically cultivate.
4.  **Illiteracy and Conservatism:** Many rural farmers cannot read or write, making it difficult for them to adopt new agricultural innovations brought by extension workers.
5.  **Poor Infrastructure:** Bad rural roads make it difficult to transport the small surplus to city markets before it spoils.

**Problems of Commercial Agriculture:**
1.  **Land Acquisition:** Securing a very large expanse of contiguous (unbroken) land is extremely difficult due to strict land tenure systems and the Land Use Act.
2.  **High Cost of Machinery:** Tractors and combine harvesters are usually imported, expensive to buy, and costly to maintain. Spare parts are often scarce.
3.  **Lack of Skilled Labour:** There is a shortage of highly trained mechanics, agronomists, and machine operators needed to run complex farm equipment in rural areas.
4.  **Pest and Disease Outbreaks:** Because commercial farms practice monocropping or keep thousands of animals packed together, a single disease outbreak (like Avian Influenza in poultry) can spread rapidly and wipe out the farm.
5.  **Inadequate Processing and Storage:** Even on a large scale, a lack of constant electricity for silos or processing plants can lead to massive financial losses if the produce cannot be processed immediately.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agriculture** | The deliberate production of crops and animals, including processing and marketing, for man's use. |
| **Production** | The activities involved in growing raw crops and rearing animals on the farm. |
| **Processing** | Changing raw agricultural products into more stable or consumable forms (e.g., cassava to garri). |
| **Marketing** | The entire process of grading, packaging, transporting, and selling agricultural products to the final consumer. |
| **Agronomy** | The branch of agriculture dealing with crop production and soil management. |
| **Agricultural Extension** | The branch that deals with taking new research and farming methods from the lab and teaching them to farmers. |
| **Subsistence Agriculture** | Farming on a small scale primarily to feed the farmer and their family, using simple tools. |
| **Commercial Agriculture** | Farming on a large scale primarily to sell products for profit, using machinery. |
| **Mixed Cropping** | Growing two or more different crops on the same piece of land at the same time (common in subsistence farming). |
| **Monocropping** | Growing only one type of crop on a large area of land (common in commercial farming). |
| **Drudgery** | Hard, exhausting physical labour, typical of using crude farm tools like hoes and cutlasses. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Modern vs Traditional Definition:** If a JAMB question asks for the *modern* definition of agriculture, look for the option that includes **processing and marketing**, not just growing crops and rearing animals. A common mistake is selecting an option that only mentions production.
*   **Agronomy vs Botany:** Botany is a pure biology subject (the study of plants). Agronomy is the *agricultural branch* concerned with producing crops and managing soils for human use.
*   **Confusing Subsistence Farming with Mixed Farming:** *Subsistence farming* describes the *motive* and *scale* (farming for family survival). *Mixed farming* is a farming *system* where a farmer grows crops and rears animals on the same farm. Do not confuse the two.
*   **Branch Functions:** Remember that Agricultural *Economics* deals with prices, markets, loans, and profits. Agricultural *Extension* deals with teaching farmers and spreading new ideas.
*   **Characteristics in questions:** Whenever you see "simple tools", "small land", and "family labour", it points to Subsistence Agriculture. Whenever you see "machinery", "monocropping", and "profit motive", it points to Commercial Agriculture.

## Quick summary

1. Agriculture originates from Latin (*ager* = field, *cultura* = cultivate) and modernly encompasses the deliberate production, processing, and marketing of crops and livestock.
2. Production provides raw materials; processing adds value and prevents rapid spoilage; marketing ensures the food reaches the consumer and the farmer makes a profit.
3. Agriculture is divided into branches like Agronomy (crops), Animal Science (livestock), Veterinary Medicine (animal health), and Agricultural Extension (educating farmers).
4. Subsistence agriculture is small-scale farming done primarily to feed the farmer's family, using simple tools and family labour.
5. Subsistence farming requires low capital and involves low risk of complete failure, but it suffers from poor tools, lack of finance, and land fragmentation.
6. Commercial agriculture is large-scale farming done for profit, using heavy machinery, huge capital, and skilled labour.
7. Commercial farming provides mass production and job creation, but faces problems like difficulty acquiring large contiguous lands, high machinery costs, and severe risks from pest outbreaks due to monocropping.

## Practice questions

**1. The word agriculture is derived from two Latin words, *ager* and *cultura*, which mean:**
- A. field and cultivation
- B. animal and rearing
- C. crop and harvesting
- D. land and marketing

**Answer: A.** *Ager* means field or land, and *cultura* means cultivation or tilling. 

**2. Which of the following best describes the modern concept of agriculture?**
- A. The clearing of land and planting of seeds
- B. The cultivation of crops and rearing of animals only
- C. The production, processing, and marketing of farm products
- D. The provision of food for the farmer's family

**Answer: C.** The modern definition of agriculture goes beyond just production; it encompasses the entire value chain, including processing raw materials and marketing them to consumers. The most tempting wrong option is B, which stems from confusing the outdated, traditional definition of agriculture with the comprehensive modern definition.

**3. The branch of agriculture that deals specifically with the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of livestock diseases is:**
- A. Animal Husbandry
- B. Veterinary Medicine
- C. Agricultural Extension
- D. Zoology

**Answer: B.** Veterinary Medicine focuses on animal health, disease diagnosis, prevention, and treatment. A common mistake is choosing Animal Husbandry (A), which deals with general rearing, feeding, and management, rather than medical treatment.

**4. A major distinguishing characteristic of commercial agriculture is that it:**
- A. relies heavily on family labour
- B. requires very little capital investment
- C. is primarily aimed at making maximum profit
- D. involves the use of simple farm tools

**Answer: C.** Commercial agriculture is a business venture aimed at mass production and maximizing profit. Options A, B, and D are characteristics of subsistence agriculture. Students often confuse the two if they don't read the word "commercial" carefully.

**5. Which of the following is a major problem of subsistence agriculture in West Africa?**
- A. Excessive use of agrochemicals causing environmental pollution
- B. Land fragmentation due to local inheritance systems
- C. Difficulty in managing hired skilled labour
- D. The high cost of importing and maintaining tractors

**Answer: B.** Subsistence farming is hindered by local land tenure systems that share land among many heirs, leaving each farmer with a fragmented, tiny plot. The mistake behind options A, C, and D is assuming they apply to small-scale farming, when they are actually problems or features associated with large-scale commercial farming.

**6. Turning fresh cow milk into yoghurt or cheese is an example of which stage of modern agriculture?**
- A. Production
- B. Marketing
- C. Harvesting
- D. Processing

**Answer: D.** Processing involves transforming raw agricultural produce (milk) into a finished or semi-finished product (yoghurt) to add value and extend shelf life. Choosing option A (Production) is a mistake, as production only covers rearing the cow and extracting the raw milk.

**7. An agricultural extension worker's primary duty is to:**
- A. sell agricultural machinery to rural farmers at subsidized rates
- B. teach farmers new and improved farming techniques
- C. treat sick farm animals in the village
- D. calculate the gross margin and net profit for the farmer

**Answer: B.** Agricultural Extension serves as the educational bridge between research institutes and the farmer, teaching them modern techniques and innovations. Option C describes a veterinary doctor, and option D describes an agricultural economist.

**8. Which of the following is an advantage of commercial agriculture?**
- A. It requires very low initial capital to set up.
- B. It relies on the natural fertility of the soil without needing fertilizers.
- C. It creates employment opportunities for citizens.
- D. It relies on mixed cropping to prevent total crop failure.

**Answer: C.** Commercial agriculture creates jobs for drivers, machine operators, agronomists, and administrative staff. Options A, B, and D are incorrect because they describe features and advantages of subsistence agriculture.

**9. The branch of agriculture responsible for determining the prices of farm produce, securing loans, and managing farm accounts is:**
- A. Agricultural Economics
- B. Agronomy
- C. Agricultural Extension
- D. Farm Mechanization

**Answer: A.** Agricultural Economics deals with the business and financial aspects of farming, including pricing, marketing, budgeting, and resource allocation. A common slip is choosing Agronomy (B), which actually deals with crop and soil science.

**10. Why is drudgery considered a major disadvantage of subsistence farming?**
- A. It causes rapid loss of soil fertility over time.
- B. It makes the crops highly susceptible to insect pests.
- C. It involves exhausting physical labour that discourages youths from farming.
- D. It requires expensive machinery that the farmer cannot afford.

**Answer: C.** Drudgery refers to the hard, back-breaking manual labour involved in using crude tools like hoes and cutlasses. This exhausting work leads to early aging and discourages young people from taking up farming. The mistake in option D is associating drudgery with machinery; drudgery is actually the *result* of lacking machinery.
