## What are Farm Inputs?

**Farm inputs** are all the resources, materials, and equipment that a farmer uses to produce crops and raise livestock. If farming is seen as a factory process, the farm inputs are the "raw materials" and "machinery" that go in, and the harvested crops or animal products are the "outputs" that come out.

For a farm to be successful and profitable, a farmer must select the right quality and quantity of inputs, use them efficiently, and ensure they are cost-effective.

## Classification of Farm Inputs

**Objective: classify different types of farm inputs and their uses.**

Textbooks generally classify farm inputs in two main ways: by their **nature** (what they are) and by their **economic lifespan** (how long they last on the farm). 

### 1. Classification by Economic Lifespan

In agricultural economics, farm inputs are divided into two categories based on how they are used up during the production process:

*   **Consumable (Variable) Inputs:** These are materials that are completely used up in one production season or cycle. The farmer has to buy them again for the next planting or rearing season. Examples include seeds, fertilizers, agrochemicals, animal feeds, fuel for tractors, and day-old chicks.
*   **Capital (Fixed) Inputs:** These are inputs that are not used up in a single season. They last for a long time and are used repeatedly over several production cycles. Examples include farm buildings, tractors, incubators, wheelbarrows, cutlasses, and the farm land itself. (Note: The cost of these inputs is spread over time, a concept called *depreciation*, which is covered in `D4-farm-management.md`).

### 2. Classification by Nature and Use

For practical farming and JAMB questions, farm inputs are grouped by what they actually are. The major categories are:

1.  **Planting Materials** (used to start crop production)
2.  **Agrochemicals** (used to protect crops and animals from pests and diseases)
3.  **Fertilizers and Soil Amendments** (used to improve soil fertility)
4.  **Animal Inputs and Feeds** (used to start and maintain livestock production)
5.  **Farm Tools and Machinery** (used to perform farm operations)

## Planting Materials

**Content item: planting materials.**

Planting materials are the parts of a plant used to establish a new crop. Without them, crop farming cannot begin. They are classified into two main types: **sexual** (seeds) and **asexual** (vegetative materials).

### 1. Seeds (Sexual Planting Materials)
Seeds are the true, fertilized, and ripened ovules of flowers. They are the most common planting materials used by farmers in Nigeria.

*   **Uses:** Used to grow crops that produce large numbers of seeds and do not easily grow from cuttings. 
*   **Examples:** Maize, rice, cowpea (beans), groundnut, sorghum (guinea corn), millet, tomatoes, pepper, and cotton.
*   **Advantages:** They are easy to store, transport, and plant. They can be treated with chemicals to prevent diseases. 
*   **Disadvantages:** Some seeds take a long time to germinate or grow into mature fruit-bearing plants (for example, growing cocoa or citrus from seed takes years).

### 2. Vegetative Materials (Asexual Planting Materials)
Vegetative materials are parts of the parent plant—such as the stem, root, or leaf—used to grow a new plant. This process is called vegetative propagation. 

*   **Uses:** Used for crops that do not produce viable seeds, produce very few seeds, or when the farmer wants the new plant to be exactly the same as the parent plant (a clone) and grow faster.
*   **Examples of vegetative materials:**
    *   **Stem cuttings:** Sections of the stem are cut and buried in the soil. Used for **cassava** and **sweet potato**.
    *   **Yam setts:** Cut pieces of the yam tuber (a modified stem) used to plant yams.
    *   **Suckers:** Shoots that grow from the base of the parent plant. Used for **plantain**, **banana**, and **pineapple**.
    *   **Crowns and Slips:** The leafy top of a fruit (crown) or leafy shoots below the fruit (slips). Used for **pineapple**.
    *   **Rhizomes:** Underground stems used to plant **ginger**.
    *   **Bulbs:** Modified fleshy leaves used to plant **onions**.
    *   **Budding and Grafting materials:** Buds or scions taken from a desired plant and attached to a rooted rootstock. Used for tree crops like **citrus** (oranges), **rubber**, and **cocoa**.

### Seed Rate and Plant Population
When using planting materials, farmers calculate the **seed rate**, which is the amount of seed or planting material required to plant one hectare of land (measured in kg/ha or setts/ha). This ensures the correct **plant density** (the number of plants per hectare) to maximize yield without overcrowding. (Calculations for plant population are covered in `B8-cropping-systems-and-planting-patterns.md`).

## Agrochemicals

**Content item: agrochemicals.**

Agrochemicals (agricultural chemicals) are chemical products used on the farm to control pests, diseases, and weeds, or to regulate plant growth. While chemical fertilizers are technically agrochemicals, the term is usually used in exams to mean **pesticides**.

A **pesticide** is a general term for any chemical used to kill or control a pest. Pests are organisms (insects, rodents, fungi, weeds) that damage crops or harm livestock.

### Classification of Agrochemicals by Target Pest

| Type of Agrochemical | Target Pest | Meaning and Uses | Common Nigerian Farm Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Herbicides** | Weeds | Chemicals used to kill unwanted plants (weeds) in the field to stop them from competing with crops. | Clearing land before planting maize; controlling weeds in rice paddies. |
| **Insecticides** | Insects | Chemicals used to kill harmful insects (biting, boring, or sucking insects). | Controlling **cotton stainers**, stem borers in maize, or aphids on cowpea. |
| **Fungicides** | Fungi | Chemicals used to kill or prevent the growth of fungal diseases. | Spraying cocoa trees to prevent **black pod disease**; treating seeds before planting to prevent rot. |
| **Nematicides** | Nematodes | Chemicals used to kill microscopic roundworms (nematodes) in the soil that attack plant roots. | Controlling root-knot nematodes in **tomato** and **yam** fields. |
| **Rodenticides** | Rodents | Poisonous chemicals (often mixed with bait) used to kill rats, mice, and other rodents. | Protecting stored grains in barns or silos from rats. |
| **Avicides** | Birds | Chemicals used to control or repel pest birds. | Protecting rice or sorghum fields from weaver birds. |

### How Insecticides Work
Insecticides are further classified by their *mode of action* (how they kill the insect):
1.  **Stomach poisons:** The chemical is sprayed on the plant leaves. When the insect (e.g., a grasshopper or caterpillar) eats the leaf, the poison enters its stomach and kills it.
2.  **Contact poisons:** The chemical kills the insect directly when it touches the insect's skin (exoskeleton). Used for insects that don't necessarily chew the leaves but walk on them.
3.  **Systemic poisons:** The chemical is absorbed by the plant's roots or leaves and moves through the plant's sap. When a sucking insect (e.g., an aphid or mealybug) pierces the plant to suck the sap, it takes in the poison and dies.
4.  **Fumigants:** The chemical is released as a toxic gas that enters the insect's breathing pores (spiracles). Commonly used in enclosed grain stores or silos.

### Safe Handling and Application of Agrochemicals
Agrochemicals are poisonous to humans, livestock, and beneficial insects (like bees) if not used properly. 

**Step-by-step safety precautions:**
1.  **Read the label:** Always read the manufacturer's instructions for the correct dilution rate and safety warnings.
2.  **Wear Protective Clothing (PPE):** A farmer must wear protective gear. This includes a long-sleeved overall, rubber gloves, rubber boots, protective goggles, and a nose mask/respirator to prevent inhaling chemical fumes or getting it on the skin.
3.  **Check the weather:** Never spray agrochemicals on a very windy day (the chemicals will blow away or into the farmer's face) or just before heavy rain (the rain will wash the chemical off the plants into nearby rivers). 
4.  **Spray direction:** Always spray **along or across** the direction of the wind (with the wind at your back). **Never spray against the wind.**
5.  **Do not eat or smoke:** Never eat, drink, or smoke while applying chemicals to avoid accidental poisoning.
6.  **Disposal:** Wash equipment thoroughly after use and bury empty chemical containers far away from water sources. Never wash knapsack sprayers in rivers used for drinking water.

## Fertilizers and Soil Amendments (Other Inputs)

To maintain high crop yields, farmers must add nutrients back to the soil. These inputs are divided into organic and inorganic fertilizers.

### 1. Organic Fertilizers (Manure)
These are natural materials derived from the decay of plants and animal waste. 
*   **Uses:** They supply a broad mix of nutrients, improve soil structure, increase water-holding capacity, and encourage beneficial soil microbes.
*   **Examples:** 
    *   **Poultry droppings and cow dung** (Farmyard manure).
    *   **Compost** (decayed plant materials and kitchen waste).
    *   **Green manure** (growing a leguminous plant like cowpea and ploughing it directly into the soil while it is still green).

### 2. Inorganic Fertilizers (Chemical Fertilizers)
These are artificially manufactured chemicals containing specific plant nutrients (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium).
*   **Uses:** They release nutrients very quickly to correct specific soil deficiencies and boost fast crop growth.
*   **Examples:** 
    *   **NPK 15:15:15** (a compound fertilizer containing equal percentages of Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium).
    *   **Urea** (a straight fertilizer supplying only Nitrogen).
    *   **Single Super Phosphate (SSP)** (supplies phosphorus).

### Comparison of Organic and Inorganic Fertilizers

| Feature | Organic Fertilizer (Manure) | Inorganic Fertilizer (Chemical) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source** | Natural plant and animal waste | Factory manufactured |
| **Nutrient content** | Low concentration of nutrients per kg | High concentration of specific nutrients |
| **Nutrient release** | Very slow (must decompose first) | Very fast (dissolves quickly in water) |
| **Effect on soil structure** | Improves soil structure and binds particles | Does not improve soil structure; can destroy it if overused |
| **Risk of leaching/burning** | Very low | High (can wash away or "burn" crops if applied in excess) |

### 3. Soil Amendments (Liming Materials)
Sometimes the soil is too acidic for crops to grow well. Farmers use inputs called **liming materials** (like agricultural limestone or wood ash) to increase the soil pH (reduce acidity) and supply calcium and magnesium to the soil.

## Animal Feeds and Livestock Inputs (Other Inputs)

Livestock farmers (raising poultry, cattle, pigs, or fish) rely heavily on inputs specific to animal production.

### 1. Foundation Stock (Animals)
Before farming begins, the farmer must buy the initial animals.
*   **Uses:** To start the flock, herd, or school.
*   **Examples:** **Day-old chicks** (for poultry), **fingerlings** or **juveniles** (for fish ponds like catfish or tilapia), **weaners** (young pigs), or semen for **artificial insemination** in cattle.

### 2. Animal Feeds
Feed is usually the most expensive consumable input in livestock farming, often taking up to 70% of the production cost in poultry and piggery.
*   **Concentrates:** Feeds high in carbohydrates and proteins but low in fibre. Examples include formulated poultry feeds like **Layers mash** (for egg-laying hens), **Broiler starter**, and maize bran.
*   **Roughages:** Feeds high in fibre but relatively low in easily digestible nutrients. Examples include pasture grasses and legumes (e.g., Guinea grass, *Centrosema*), hay, and silage. Used mainly for ruminant animals like **White Fulani cattle**, sheep, and goats.
*   **Feed Supplements and Additives:** Small quantities of inputs added to the feed to provide missing nutrients or improve growth. Examples include **bone meal** (for calcium), **oyster shell**, vitamin premixes, and salt licks.

### 3. Livestock Medications
Just like crops need agrochemicals, livestock need veterinary inputs to stay healthy.
*   **Vaccines:** Used to prevent viral and bacterial diseases (e.g., Newcastle disease vaccine for poultry).
*   **Antibiotics:** Used to treat bacterial infections.
*   **Anthelmintics (Dewormers):** Chemicals used to kill internal parasites like roundworms and tapeworms in animals.
*   **Acaricides / Dips:** Chemicals used to kill external parasites like ticks and lice on cattle and sheep.

## Farm Tools and Machinery (Capital Inputs)

While consumable inputs are used up quickly, capital inputs are the equipment used to apply those materials and manage the farm. (These are briefly noted here as inputs but are studied in detail in Section E of the syllabus).

*   **Simple Farm Tools:** Cheap, hand-operated inputs like the cutlass (for clearing), hoe (for making heaps and weeding), head pan, and wheelbarrow.
*   **Farm Machinery:** Expensive, heavy motorized equipment. The main example is the **tractor**, which provides the power. 
*   **Implements:** Equipment attached to the tractor, such as ploughs (for primary tillage), harrows (for breaking soil clods), and planters (for sowing seeds).

---

## Summary of the Uses of Farm Inputs

To quickly answer classification questions on the use of farm inputs:
1.  **To establish the farm:** Land, seeds, vegetative planting materials, foundation stock (chicks, fingerlings).
2.  **To nourish and grow:** Fertilizers (NPK, manure), animal feeds (concentrates, roughages).
3.  **To protect and maintain:** Agrochemicals (herbicides, insecticides, fungicides), vaccines, veterinary drugs.
4.  **To execute farm operations:** Capital inputs like tractors, hoes, cutlasses, harvesters, and human labour.

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Farm Input** | Any resource, material, or equipment used in agricultural production. |

| **Concentrate** | Animal feed that is high in nutrients and low in fibre (e.g., maize bran, layers mash). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Confusing pesticide categories:** Remember the specific targets. *Herbicides* are for weeds (herbs), *insecticides* are for insects, *fungicides* are for fungi, and *nematicides* are for nematodes. A common JAMB trick is to ask what controls weeds and offer "pesticide" and "herbicide" as options. Choose herbicide, as it is the most specific correct answer.
*   **Misunderstanding planting materials:** Not all crops are planted from seeds. If asked for the planting material of yam, the answer is *yam setts* (or seed yams). For cassava, it is *stem cuttings*. For plantain, it is *suckers*.
*   **Spraying direction:** A frequent safety question asks about the direction of spraying. You must spray **with** the wind (the wind should blow from behind you, carrying the chemical away from your face). Never spray *against* the wind.
*   **Capital vs. Consumable inputs:** If a question asks for a variable/consumable input, look for something that gets "used up" like fertilizer or fuel. If it asks for a fixed/capital input, look for tools, land, or machinery.
*   **Organic vs. Inorganic fertilizers:** Remember that organic fertilizers (manure) improve soil structure, while inorganic (chemical) fertilizers do not, and can even destroy soil structure if overused.

## Quick summary

1.  Farm inputs are all the raw materials and resources (seeds, chemicals, equipment, livestock) a farmer uses to produce food.
2.  Inputs are classified economically into consumable (variable) inputs, which are used up in one season, and capital (fixed) inputs, which last for years.
3.  Planting materials can be sexual (seeds like maize and beans) or asexual/vegetative (stems, suckers, and setts like cassava, plantain, and yam).
4.  Agrochemicals are chemicals used on the farm. They include herbicides (for weeds), insecticides (for insects), fungicides (for fungi), and rodenticides (for rats).
5.  Pesticides can kill by stomach poison (eaten), contact poison (touched), or systemic poison (absorbed by the plant's sap).
6.  Safety is critical when handling agrochemicals: farmers must wear PPE (masks, boots, overalls) and avoid spraying against the wind.
7.  Fertilizers supply nutrients to the soil. Organic fertilizers (manure) improve soil structure but act slowly; inorganic fertilizers (NPK) act fast but do not improve soil structure.
8.  Animal inputs include foundation stock (chicks, fingerlings), feeds (concentrates for poultry, roughage for cattle), and medications (vaccines, dewormers).

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following groups contains only vegetative planting materials?**
- A. Cassava cuttings, maize seeds, yam setts
- B. Yam setts, plantain suckers, cassava cuttings
- C. Pineapple crowns, rice seeds, sweet potato cuttings
- D. Cowpea seeds, tomato seeds, onion bulbs

**Answer: B.** Yam setts, plantain suckers, and cassava cuttings are all parts of the parent plant used for asexual reproduction. Maize, rice, cowpea, and tomato are planted using sexual seeds.

**2. A farmer notices that his tomato plants have swollen, knotty roots caused by microscopic worms in the soil. Which agrochemical should he apply?**
- A. Herbicide
- B. Insecticide
- C. Nematicide
- D. Rodenticide

**Answer: C.** Nematicides are chemicals specifically used to kill nematodes (microscopic roundworms) that cause root-knot diseases in crops like tomatoes and yams.

**3. Which of the following is considered a capital (fixed) farm input?**
- A. NPK fertilizer
- B. Day-old chicks
- C. Tractor
- D. Cassava stem cuttings

**Answer: C.** A tractor is a capital input because it is not completely used up in a single farming season and provides service over many years. Fertilizers, chicks, and cuttings are consumable (variable) inputs.

**4. When applying agrochemicals on a farm, a major safety rule is to**
- A. spray against the direction of the wind to ensure the chemical settles fast.
- B. wash empty chemical containers in a nearby stream.
- C. wear protective clothing such as overalls, masks, and boots.
- D. apply the chemicals just before a heavy rainfall.

**Answer: C.** Wearing Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) prevents the poison from entering the farmer's body. Spraying against the wind is dangerous, washing containers in a stream causes water pollution, and spraying before rain washes the chemicals away.

**5. The main function of an herbicide in crop production is to**
- A. control insect pests on the farm.
- B. prevent fungal diseases from spreading.
- C. improve the fertility of the soil.
- D. kill unwanted plants competing with the main crop.

**Answer: D.** Herbicides are specifically formulated to kill or control weeds (unwanted plants) that compete with crops for nutrients, sunlight, and water.

**6. Which of the following livestock inputs is classified as a roughage?**
- A. Layers mash
- B. Guinea grass
- C. Oyster shell
- D. Fish meal

**Answer: B.** Guinea grass is a pasture plant high in fibre, making it a roughage. Layers mash and fish meal are concentrates, while oyster shell is a mineral supplement.

**7. An insecticide that is absorbed by the roots of a plant and moves into the plant's sap to kill sucking insects is called a**
- A. contact poison.
- B. stomach poison.
- C. fumigant.
- D. systemic poison.

**Answer: D.** Systemic poisons are absorbed into the plant's system. When pests like aphids pierce the plant to suck the sap, they ingest the poison and die. Contact poisons kill on touch, and stomach poisons kill when the insect chews the leaf.

**8. One advantage of using organic fertilizer (manure) over inorganic fertilizer is that organic fertilizer**
- A. releases its nutrients much faster to the crops.
- B. contains a higher concentration of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium.
- C. improves the soil structure and binds soil particles together.
- D. is factory manufactured and easier to carry in bags.

**Answer: C.** Organic fertilizers like compost and cow dung add humus to the soil, which improves soil structure and water-holding capacity. Inorganic fertilizers act faster and are more concentrated, but they do not improve soil structure.

**9. Cocoa farmers often spray their pods to prevent the spread of black pod disease. The appropriate chemical for this operation is a**
- A. fungicide.
- B. nematicide.
- C. herbicide.
- D. avicide.

**Answer: A.** Black pod disease in cocoa is caused by a fungus (*Phytophthora* species). Therefore, a fungicide is used to prevent and control the disease. 

**10. In livestock production, day-old chicks, fingerlings, and weaner pigs represent which category of farm input?**
- A. Concentrates
- B. Foundation stock
- C. Capital tools
- D. Additives

**Answer: B.** Foundation stock refers to the young animals or initial parent stock purchased to start or expand a livestock farming business.
