# JAMB Agricultural Science Notes: Floriculture

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 11 of the JAMB Agricultural Science syllabus: Floriculture. It has one combined content item:

*   **Identification, establishment, maintenance and uses of ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers**

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1.  distinguish between common ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers.
2.  determine their uses and maintenance.

## What is Floriculture?

**Floriculture** is the branch of horticulture (and by extension, agriculture) that deals with the cultivation, management, and uses of **ornamental plants** and flowers. Ornamental plants are grown primarily for their beauty, environmental benefits, and aesthetic value rather than for food or raw materials for heavy industries. 

## Identification of ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers

**Objective i: distinguish between common ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers.**
**Content: Identification of ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers**

Ornamental plants are grouped into three main categories based on their size, stem structure, and growth habit: **trees**, **shrubs**, and **herbaceous flowers**. Knowing how to identify and separate them is a key JAMB requirement.

### 1. Ornamental Trees
Trees are large, woody plants. They have a single, thick main stem called a **trunk** that grows quite tall before branching out. They can live for many years (perennials) and often provide heavy shade or act as windbreaks.

**Common examples in Nigeria:**
*   **Flamboyant tree** (*Delonix regia*): Known for its bright red or orange flowers that bloom beautifully before the rainy season.
*   **Neem / Dongoyaro** (*Azadirachta indica*): Highly valued in Nigeria for its dense shade and medicinal properties.
*   **Royal palm** (*Roystonea regia*): A tall, elegant palm tree with a smooth, swollen trunk, often planted along driveways or at the entrances of estates.
*   **Casuarina** (Whistling pine): Looks like a pine tree with needle-like leaves; excellent for blocking strong winds (windbreaks).
*   **Frangipani** (*Plumeria*): A smaller tree with thick, fleshy branches and highly fragrant white, yellow, or pink flowers.

### 2. Ornamental Shrubs
Shrubs are woody plants, but they are generally shorter than trees (usually less than 4 metres high). Unlike a tree, a shrub lacks a single main trunk; instead, it has **multiple stems** arising from or very close to the ground, giving it a bushy appearance. They are often planted close together to form a living fence or **hedge**.

**Common examples in Nigeria:**
*   **Hibiscus** (*Hibiscus rosa-sinensis*): A very common bushy plant in Nigerian compounds with large red, yellow, or pink bell-shaped flowers.
*   **Bougainvillea:** A tough, climbing or bushy shrub with thorny stems and brightly coloured, papery leaves (bracts) that hide the tiny true flowers.
*   **Ixora** (*Ixora coccinea*): A dense shrub with clusters of small, bright red, yellow, or orange flowers. It is the most common plant used for hedges in Nigerian schools and homes.
*   **Pride of Barbados** (*Caesalpinia pulcherrima*): A beautiful flowering shrub with feathery leaves and striking red-and-yellow flowers.
*   **Croton:** Grown for its brightly coloured, patterned leaves (variegated leaves) rather than its flowers.
*   **Yellow bush** (*Tecoma stans*): A fast-growing shrub with bright yellow, trumpet-shaped flowers.

### 3. Ornamental Flowers (Herbaceous plants)
These are smaller, non-woody (succulent) plants grown specifically for their attractive blooms or foliage. Their stems are soft and green. They usually have a shorter life cycle (annuals or biennials) and are planted in flower beds, borders, or pots.

**Common examples in Nigeria:**
*   **Marigold** (*Tagetes*): A small plant with strong-smelling, bright orange or yellow flowers. It helps repel soil pests like nematodes.
*   **Morning Glory** (*Ipomoea purpurea*): A soft climbing plant (vine) with trumpet-shaped purple or blue flowers that open in the morning.
*   **Canna lily:** A tall herbaceous plant with broad, banana-like leaves and bright red or yellow flowers.
*   **Balsam** (*Impatiens balsamina*): A soft, fleshy plant with colourful flowers that pop their seeds out when touched.
*   **Zinnia:** A popular garden flower with strong, upright stems and daisy-like flower heads in many colours.

### Comparison Table: Trees, Shrubs, and Flowers

| Feature | Ornamental Trees | Ornamental Shrubs | Herbaceous Flowers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stem structure** | Hard and woody | Hard and woody | Soft, green, and fleshy (non-woody) |
| **Main axis** | One main trunk | Multiple stems from the base | Usually a single soft stem, or creeping vines |
| **Height** | Very tall (often 5 to 20+ metres) | Medium (usually under 4 metres) | Short (usually a few centimetres to 1.5 metres) |
| **Lifespan** | Perennials (live for many decades) | Perennials (live for many years) | Mostly annuals or biennials (live for 1 to 2 years) |
| **Main landscape use** | Shade, windbreaks, avenues | Hedges, fencing, borders | Flower beds, pots, decorative borders |
| **Examples** | Flamboyant, Neem, Royal palm, Casuarina | Hibiscus, Ixora, Bougainvillea, Croton | Marigold, Canna lily, Morning glory, Zinnia |

## Establishment of ornamental plants

**Content: Establishment of ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers**

Establishing an ornamental garden or landscape involves careful planning and execution. The steps below outline how ornamental plants are introduced to a new environment.

### Step 1: Site selection and planning
Before planting, the farmer or landscaper must choose a suitable site. The site must have well-drained, fertile soil, adequate sunlight, and access to a water source. The layout is planned to ensure trees are far from building foundations (to prevent root damage) and shrubs are lined up where hedges are needed.

### Step 2: Procurement of planting materials
Ornamental plants can be propagated (reproduced) by two main methods:
*   **Seeds:** Used for most herbaceous flowers (e.g., Marigold, Zinnia) and some trees (e.g., Flamboyant).
*   **Vegetative propagation:** Using plant parts instead of seeds. This includes **stem cuttings** (very common for Hibiscus, Croton, and Bougainvillea), **bulbs/rhizomes** (for lilies), and **budding/grafting** (for special varieties of roses and citrus).

### Step 3: Nursery practices
Most ornamental seeds and cuttings are not planted directly in the final garden. They are first raised in a nursery.
1.  Topsoil mixed with organic manure is placed in **polythene bags** (nursery bags) or seed boxes.
2.  Seeds or cuttings are planted in these bags.
3.  They are watered daily and protected from direct, harsh sunlight by a shade (palm fronds or a shade net).
4.  They are cared for until they develop strong roots and leaves.

### Step 4: Land preparation
While the seedlings are in the nursery, the permanent site is prepared. This involves **clearing** the land of weeds and debris, **stumping** (removing old tree roots), and **levelling** the soil. Flower beds and borders are marked out. Holes are dug for trees and shrubs, often spaced according to the size of the mature plant.

### Step 5: Transplanting
Once the seedlings are strong enough (usually after a few weeks to months, depending on the plant), they are moved to the permanent site.
1.  Transplanting is best done in the **cool hours of the morning or evening** to reduce heat stress (transpiration pull).
2.  The polythene bag is carefully torn away without scattering the ball of earth around the roots.
3.  The seedling is placed in the dug hole, covered with topsoil mixed with compost, firmed down, and immediately watered.

## Maintenance of ornamental plants

**Objective ii: determine their uses and maintenance.** (Maintenance portion)
**Content: Maintenance of ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers**

After establishment, ornamental plants require routine care to keep them healthy and attractive. These management practices include:

### 1. Watering
Ornamental plants must be watered regularly, especially during the dry season and immediately after transplanting. Water is essential for photosynthesis and keeps the plants from wilting. This can be done using a watering can, a hose, or sprinklers.

### 2. Weeding
Weeds compete with ornamental plants for water, soil nutrients, sunlight, and space. Weeds must be removed regularly by hand-pulling, using a small hoe, or using a cutlass. This keeps the garden looking neat and prevents pests from hiding in the weed cover.

### 3. Fertilizer and manure application
To maintain bright flowers and deep green foliage, the soil must be rich in nutrients. Well-rotted organic manure (poultry droppings, compost) or inorganic fertilizers (like NPK) should be applied carefully around the base of the plants.

### 4. Pruning and Trimming
This is one of the most critical practices in floriculture. 
*   **Pruning** is the removal of dead, diseased, or overgrown branches and stems. It encourages the plant to bring out fresh, healthy shoots and bigger flowers.
*   **Trimming (or clipping)** is used to give shrubs a specific shape. For example, Ixora and Lime hedges are regularly trimmed with **shears** to keep them looking like neat, flat-topped fences. 

### 5. Pest and disease control
Ornamental plants are often attacked by caterpillars, aphids, mealybugs, and fungal diseases. Maintenance involves:
*   Hand-picking large insects.
*   Spraying appropriate insecticides and fungicides.
*   Removing and burning heavily diseased plant parts to stop infections from spreading.

### 6. Provision of shade and support (Staking)
Young seedlings may need temporary shading. Climbing ornamental plants, such as Morning Glory or certain types of Bougainvillea, need a stick, trellis, or wall for support (staking) so they can grow upward and display their flowers properly.

## Uses of ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers

**Objective ii: determine their uses and maintenance.** (Uses portion)
**Content: Uses of ornamental trees, shrubs and flowers**

Why do we spend time and money growing plants that we do not eat? Ornamental plants have aesthetic, environmental, economic, and cultural values. 

### 1. Aesthetic and Decorative Uses (Beautification)
*   **Landscaping:** Ornamental plants are used to beautify the surroundings of homes, schools, offices, parks, and highways.
*   **Indoor decoration:** Potted plants and cut flowers (like roses and lilies) are used to decorate the insides of buildings and events (weddings, conferences).
*   **Floral arrangements:** Flowers are cut and arranged into bouquets, wreaths, and garlands for ceremonies, funerals, and gifts.

### 2. Environmental and Ecological Uses
*   **Provision of shade:** Broad-leaved trees like the Flamboyant and Neem provide cool shade for people to rest under during hot sunny days.
*   **Windbreaks:** Tall, strong trees like Casuarina (Whistling pine) are planted in rows to break the force of heavy winds, protecting buildings from losing their roofs during storms.
*   **Erosion control:** Plants (especially creeping herbaceous plants and grasses) cover the soil and their roots hold the soil particles tightly, preventing water and wind erosion.
*   **Air purification:** Like all green plants, ornamentals absorb carbon (IV) oxide from the air and release oxygen, improving air quality.
*   **Noise reduction:** Thick, dense hedges of shrubs can absorb and muffle noise from busy streets, keeping compounds quiet.

### 3. Boundary Demarcation and Fencing
*   Shrubs with dense foliage (like Ixora) or thorns (like Bougainvillea) are planted closely together to form a **hedge**. This acts as a living fence to mark property boundaries and keep out stray animals or trespassers.

### 4. Economic and Commercial Uses
*   **Income generation:** Floriculture is a highly profitable business. Florists and horticulturists make money by selling seedlings, potted plants, and cut flowers.
*   **Employment:** It provides jobs for gardeners, landscape architects, and nursery operators.

### 5. Medicinal and Cultural Uses
*   Many ornamental plants in West Africa have medicinal value. For example, the leaves and bark of the **Neem (Dongoyaro)** tree are widely boiled to treat malaria and skin infections.
*   Certain flowers have deep cultural or religious meanings and are used in traditional ceremonies.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Floriculture** | The branch of agriculture/horticulture dealing with the cultivation and management of ornamental plants. |
| **Ornamental plant** | A plant grown primarily for its beauty, decorative value, or landscaping purposes. |
| **Tree** | A large, tall, woody plant with a single main stem or trunk. |
| **Shrub** | A medium-sized, woody plant with multiple stems arising from the base; smaller than a tree. |
| **Herbaceous plant** | A plant with soft, green, non-woody stems; mostly grown for its flowers. |
| **Hedge** | A dense line of shrubs planted closely together to act as a living fence or boundary. |
| **Pruning** | The cutting away of dead, overgrown, or unwanted branches to encourage healthy growth and flowers. |
| **Windbreak** | A line of tall trees planted to block or reduce the force of strong winds. |
| **Transplanting** | The process of moving a seedling from a nursery and planting it in its permanent location. |
| **Landscaping** | The art of arranging gardens, plants, and terrain to make a piece of land more attractive. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Tree vs. Shrub:** A very common JAMB question asks you to distinguish between these two based on their stems. Always remember: a **tree** has one single, thick trunk; a **shrub** has multiple stems growing from near the ground.
*   **Pruning vs. Weeding:** Do not confuse these maintenance practices. Weeding removes *other unwanted plants* from the soil. Pruning removes *unwanted branches* from the *actual ornamental plant*.
*   **Know your examples:** JAMB will use specific plant names. Know that *Casuarina* is a tree used for windbreaks, *Neem* is a shade tree, *Ixora* is a shrub used for hedges, and *Marigold* is a herbaceous flower. 
*   **Meaning of Floriculture:** Remember that floriculture is restricted to ornamental plants and flowers, not fruit trees (which is *pomology*) or vegetable farming (which is *olericulture*).
*   **Time of Transplanting:** Transplanting is always best done in the cool periods of the day (morning or late evening) to prevent the plant from dying of heat stress (wilting).

## Quick summary

1.  Floriculture is the cultivation and management of ornamental trees, shrubs, and herbaceous flowers.
2.  Ornamental trees (e.g., Flamboyant, Neem, Casuarina) are tall, woody, and have a single trunk. They provide shade and windbreaks.
3.  Ornamental shrubs (e.g., Hibiscus, Ixora, Bougainvillea) are shorter, woody plants with multiple stems. They are excellent for hedges and fencing.
4.  Herbaceous flowers (e.g., Marigold, Balsam, Morning Glory) have soft, green stems and are grown in beds for their attractive blooms.
5.  Establishing ornamental plants involves site selection, nursery preparation (using seeds or cuttings), land preparation, and careful transplanting.
6.  Routine maintenance includes watering, weeding, fertilizer application, pruning/trimming to maintain shapes, and pest control.
7.  Ornamental plants are highly useful for beautification, shade, wind protection, reducing noise and erosion, marking boundaries, and generating income.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following is the primary purpose of floriculture?**
- A. Cultivation of vegetables for household consumption
- B. Cultivation of fruit-bearing trees for export
- C. Cultivation of ornamental plants and flowers for aesthetic purposes
- D. Cultivation of pasture crops for livestock feeding

**Answer: C.** Floriculture is the branch of horticulture that specifically deals with growing ornamental plants and flowers for beautification and landscaping.

**2. A woody ornamental plant that has multiple stems arising from its base and is less than 4 metres tall is best described as a**
- A. tree
- B. shrub
- C. herbaceous plant
- D. creeping vine

**Answer: B.** The defining characteristic of a shrub is having multiple woody stems branching from the ground level, unlike a tree which has a single main trunk.

**3. Which of the following ornamental plants is commonly used as a windbreak in Nigeria?**
- A. *Casuarina* (Whistling pine)
- B. *Ixora*
- C. Marigold
- D. Morning Glory

**Answer: A.** *Casuarina* (Whistling pine) is a tall, strong tree commonly planted in rows to block wind and protect buildings. Ixora is a shrub for hedges, while Marigold and Morning Glory are soft, herbaceous plants.

**4. The maintenance practice that involves the cutting back of overgrown branches of a shrub to give it a neat shape is called**
- A. staking
- B. weeding
- C. stumping
- D. pruning

**Answer: D.** Pruning (or trimming) is the removal of parts of a plant (like overgrown branches) to maintain its shape, encourage new growth, and keep it healthy. Stumping is the removal of tree roots from the soil.

**5. Which of the following is a common ornamental shrub used for forming hedges in Nigeria?**
- A. Neem
- B. Flamboyant
- C. *Ixora*
- D. Canna lily

**Answer: C.** *Ixora* is a dense, woody shrub that responds well to trimming, making it one of the most common plants used for hedges and living fences in Nigeria. Neem and Flamboyant are trees.

**6. Seedlings of ornamental plants are usually raised in a nursery before being moved to the permanent site. This moving process is called**
- A. propagation
- B. broadcasting
- C. transplanting
- D. weeding

**Answer: C.** Transplanting is the transfer of a seedling from the nursery (where it was germinated and protected) to its permanent location in the field or garden.

**7. Why is it recommended to transplant ornamental seedlings either early in the morning or late in the evening?**
- A. To allow the roots to absorb more nutrients at night
- B. To reduce heat stress and water loss from the plant
- C. To prevent insect pests from attacking the leaves
- D. To make the plant grow taller faster

**Answer: B.** Transplanting during cool hours prevents excessive transpiration (water loss) due to harsh sunlight, which can cause the young, stressed plant to wilt and die.

**8. Which of the following sets of plants consists entirely of herbaceous ornamental flowers?**
- A. Marigold, Zinnia, Balsam
- B. Neem, Flamboyant, Royal palm
- C. Hibiscus, Bougainvillea, Croton
- D. Casuarina, Canna lily, Ixora

**Answer: A.** Marigold, Zinnia, and Balsam are soft-stemmed (herbaceous) flowering plants. Option B contains trees, and Option C contains shrubs.

**9. An economic importance of establishing ornamental plants is that it**
- A. provides foreign exchange through the export of heavy timber
- B. generates income through the sale of cut flowers and landscaping services
- C. provides a major source of dietary protein for farmers
- D. yields raw materials for the textile industry

**Answer: B.** Floriculture is highly lucrative; florists and landscape designers make money by raising and selling ornamental plants, bouquets, and beautifying environments. Ornamental plants are not used for timber, food protein, or textiles.

**10. A school compound is situated close to a very busy and noisy highway. Which of the following uses of ornamental plants will directly solve this problem?**
- A. Planting tall Flamboyant trees for shade
- B. Planting Marigold flowers in beds for aesthetic appeal
- C. Planting thick hedges of shrubs to act as a noise barrier
- D. Using creeping plants to control soil erosion

**Answer: C.** Thick, dense hedges formed by ornamental shrubs can absorb and muffle sound waves, serving effectively as noise barriers (noise reduction) for the compound.
