## Species, Breeds, Distribution and Uses

In West Africa, farm animals are kept primarily for economic purposes. The major ones are cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, rabbits, and poultry. Each belongs to a specific species and is divided into breeds based on size, origin, and the specific purpose for which they are raised.

### Classification of Farm Animal Breeds

**Objective i: classify various breeds of farm animals.**

A **species** is a group of related animals that share similar characteristics and can breed to produce fertile offspring. A **breed** is a specific group of animals within a species that have been selectively bred by humans to have distinct, uniform traits (like coat colour, body size, or milk production) that they pass onto their offspring.

#### 1. Cattle
Cattle belong to the genus *Bos*. They are divided into humped cattle (*Bos indicus*, also known as Zebu) and humpless cattle (*Bos taurus*). They are classified based on their primary economic uses:
- **Beef breeds**: Raised primarily for meat production. They have blocky, heavily built bodies and grow fast. Examples: N'dama, Muturu, Kuri (known for massive, bulbous horns), and Keteku.
- **Dairy breeds**: Raised primarily for milk production. In Nigeria, these are mostly exotic (foreign) breeds. Examples: Holstein-Friesian, Jersey, and Brown Swiss.
- **Dual-purpose breeds**: Raised for both meat and milk. They are the most common in West Africa. Examples: White Fulani (Bunaji), Sokoto Gudali, and Red Bororo (Rahaji).

#### 2. Sheep
Sheep belong to the species *Ovis aries*. In West Africa, they are mostly covered in hair rather than heavy wool, and are classified by their size and the region they are adapted to:
- **Balami**: The largest breed. They are entirely white, with a prominent convex (curved) face and long, pendulous (drooping) ears.
- **Uda**: Large and long-legged. They have a distinct colour pattern: the front half of the body is black or brown, and the back half is white.
- **Yankasa**: The most widespread breed in Nigeria. Medium-sized with a white coat and black patches around the eyes, muzzle, and ears.
- **West African Dwarf (WAD) Sheep**: The smallest breed, with short legs. They are adapted to the southern forest zones.

#### 3. Goats
Goats belong to the species *Capra hircus*. They are also classified by their region and use:
- **Red Sokoto (Maradi)**: Recognised by its reddish-brown coat. It is medium-sized and internationally famous for its high-quality skin.
- **Sahel Goat (Desert Goat)**: Tall, long-legged, and adapted to walking long distances to browse in the dry northern regions.
- **West African Dwarf (WAD) Goat**: Small, short-legged, and compact. Adapted to the humid southern forests.

#### 4. Pigs
Pigs belong to the species *Sus scrofa domesticus*. They are classified into exotic and indigenous breeds:
- **Exotic breeds**: Kept in modern commercial farms because they grow fast and produce large amounts of meat. Examples: Large White (all white, erect ears), Landrace (all white, drooping ears, very long body), Duroc (reddish-brown), and Hampshire (black with a white belt).
- **Indigenous breed**: The West African Dwarf pig. It is small, hardy, and usually black or brown, but grows slowly and produces more fat than meat.

#### 5. Rabbits
Rabbits belong to the species *Oryctolagus cuniculus*. They are classified by their adult size and primary use:
- **Large/Meat breeds**: Raised for meat. Examples: Flemish Giant (very large and heavy), New Zealand White (all white with pink eyes; the most popular commercial meat breed).
- **Medium/Fur breeds**: Raised for meat and their fur (pelt). Examples: Chinchilla (greyish-blue fur), Californian (white with dark ears, nose, tail, and feet).

#### 6. Poultry
Poultry refers to all domesticated birds, including the domestic fowl (chicken), turkey, duck, guinea fowl, and pigeon. The domestic fowl (*Gallus gallus domesticus*) is the most economically important and is classified by its use:
- **Egg producers (Layers)**: Light body weight, active, and bred to lay high numbers of eggs. Examples: White Leghorn, Brown Leghorn.
- **Meat producers (Broilers)**: Heavy, broad-breasted birds that grow to market weight very quickly. Examples: Cornish, Sussex.
- **Dual-purpose**: Medium-weight birds that produce a good amount of both eggs and meat. Examples: Rhode Island Red, Plymouth Rock.

### Location of Farm Animals

**Objective ii: locate where they are found.**

The distribution of farm animals in West Africa is largely determined by the climate (ecology), culture, and the presence of diseases. The most important disease factor is **trypanosomiasis** (Nagana), which is transmitted by the **tsetse fly**. Tsetse flies thrive in the bushy, humid rainforests of the South and the Middle Belt, but are rare in the dry, open Savanna of the North.

- **Cattle**: Over 80% of cattle in West Africa (mostly the humped Zebu breeds like White Fulani and Sokoto Gudali) are located in the **Sudan and Sahel Savanna** (Northern Nigeria). The North provides vast open grasslands for grazing and is mostly free of tsetse flies. Conversely, humpless breeds like the **Muturu** and **N'dama** are located in the **Rainforest** (Southern Nigeria) because they possess a genetic trait called **trypanotolerance**, allowing them to survive tsetse fly bites.
- **Sheep and Goats**: Balami, Uda, and Yankasa sheep, as well as Red Sokoto and Sahel goats, are primarily found in the Savanna. The West African Dwarf sheep and goats are found in the Rainforest because, like the N'dama cattle, they are trypanotolerant.
- **Pigs**: Pigs are predominantly raised in the **South and Middle Belt**. There are two major reasons for this:
  1. **Physiology**: Pigs have a thick layer of fat and very few functional sweat glands. They cannot tolerate the extreme heat of the northern savanna and need shade and water/mud to wallow in to cool down.
  2. **Culture/Religion**: A large population in the far north practices Islam, which forbids the rearing and consumption of pigs.
- **Rabbits**: Rabbits can be found nationwide. However, commercial production is concentrated in the South and Middle Belt, where they are kept intensively in wire or wooden cages (hutches) to protect them from heat, heavy rain, and predators.
- **Poultry**: Domestic fowls are ubiquitous. Local breeds roam freely in almost every village across the country. Large-scale commercial poultry farms are heavily concentrated in the South and near major urban centres nationwide to meet high market demand and to access commercial feed mills. Guinea fowls are native to and heavily reared in the northern savanna.

### Uses of Farm Animals

**Objective iii: Identify the uses of different species of farm animals.**

Different species provide varying products and services to farmers and the economy.

- **Cattle**:
  - **Meat**: Adult cattle provide **beef**. (Meat from a young calf is called veal).
  - **Milk**: Highly nutritious, consumed fresh or processed into butter, cheese, and yoghurt.
  - **Hides**: The thick skin of large animals is called a hide; it is tanned into leather for shoes, bags, and belts.
  - **Draught power**: Strong bulls (oxen) are used to pull ploughs, carts, and agricultural implements (animal traction).
  - **Manure**: Cow dung is an excellent organic fertiliser for crops.
- **Sheep**:
  - **Meat**: Meat from an adult sheep is called **mutton**.
  - **Festivals**: Rams are in massive demand for religious ceremonies, especially the Islamic Eid-el-Kabir (Ileya) festival.
  - **Wool/Hair**: Used for making clothing, rugs, and blankets.
  - **Skin**: Used for light leather products.
- **Goats**:
  - **Meat**: Goat meat, known as **chevon**, is a highly prized delicacy in West African soups and stews.
  - **Skin**: The Red Sokoto goat produces premium quality skin that is tanned into internationally renowned "Morocco leather".
  - **Milk**: Highly nutritious and easy to digest, though less commonly consumed in Nigeria than cow milk.
- **Pigs**:
  - **Meat**: Meat from a pig is called **pork**. It is also cured and processed into bacon, ham, and sausages.
  - **Lard**: Rendered (melted) pig fat, used in cooking, baking, and making soap.
  - **Bristles**: The stiff hairs on the pig's back are used to make brushes.
- **Rabbits**:
  - **Meat**: Rabbit meat is white, high in protein, and very low in fat and cholesterol, making it excellent for human health.
  - **Pelt**: The skin with the fur still attached is used to make warm clothing and soft crafts.
  - **Research**: Widely used as laboratory animals to test drugs, vaccines, and cosmetics.
- **Poultry**:
  - **Eggs and Meat**: Cheap, high-quality sources of animal protein.
  - **Feathers**: Used for stuffing pillows, making dusters, and traditional fans.
  - **Manure**: Poultry droppings (guano) are extremely rich in nitrogen and phosphorus, making them the most potent animal manure for crop production.

## External Features of Major Farm Animals

To properly manage farm animals—such as housing them, feeding them, and checking them for diseases—you must be familiar with their physical structures.

### Identifying Characteristic Features

**Objective: identify their characteristic features.**

#### 1. Cattle
The body of cattle is divided into the head, stout neck, heavy trunk, limbs, and tail.
- **Head**: Features a broad muzzle, large eyes, and usually two **horns** (though some breeds are naturally hornless or "polled").
- **Neck**: In humped (Zebu) cattle, there is a large, fleshy fold of skin hanging loosely under the neck and chest called the **dewlap**. This helps with heat loss.
- **Trunk**: Barrel-shaped. Zebu cattle have a prominent muscular and fatty **hump** on the back, just behind the neck, used as an energy reserve.
- **Limbs**: Four strong legs. Each foot ends in a **cloven hoof** (a hoof split into two distinct toes).
- **Tail**: Long, ending in a tuft of long hair called a **switch**, used to swat flies away.
- **Udder**: Females (cows) have a large udder hanging between the hind legs. It has four mammary glands (quarters), each with its own teat for milking.

#### 2. Sheep
Sheep have a compact body usually covered in short hair in West Africa (or wool in temperate regions).
- **Head**: They have a narrow snout with a distinct vertical split down the upper lip (a philtrum). Males (rams) often have large, backward-curving or spiral horns, while females (ewes) are often hornless. Some breeds, like the Balami, have long, **pendulous (drooping) ears**.
- **Body**: Compact. They **lack a beard** and do not have a strong body odour.
- **Tail**: The tail hangs **downwards**.
- **Limbs**: Slender legs ending in cloven hooves. Sheep possess small scent glands between their toes and "tear pits" (glands) just under their eyes.

#### 3. Goats
Goats are agile, hardy browsers covered in hair.
- **Head**: Both males (bucks) and females (does) usually possess horns that curve backwards. Unlike sheep, their ears are usually **erect** (pointing upwards or forwards).
- **Neck**: Males often have a distinct **beard** under the chin. Many goats also have two small fleshy appendages hanging from the throat called **wattles**.
- **Body**: Leaner than sheep. Adult uncastrated males emit a very strong, distinct odour, especially during the breeding season.
- **Tail**: Short and turns **upwards**.
- **Limbs**: Sturdy legs with cloven hooves, adapted for climbing and standing on hind legs to eat leaves.

#### 4. Pigs
Pigs have a stout, heavy body covered in thick skin.
- **Head**: Elongated, ending in a prominent, tough, flat **snout**. The snout contains a cartilaginous disc used for rooting (digging up soil to find food).
- **Neck**: Very short and thick, blending almost seamlessly into the shoulders.
- **Trunk**: Cylindrical and barrel-shaped. The thick skin is covered with sparse, coarse hairs called **bristles**. Beneath the skin is a thick layer of fat.
- **Limbs**: Short legs. Each foot has four toes; the two middle toes form a cloven hoof that touches the ground, while the two outer, higher toes (dewclaws) do not bear weight.
- **Tail**: Short, often curly in exotic breeds.

#### 5. Rabbits
Rabbits are small, heavily furred mammals adapted for speed and burrowing.
- **Head**: Most recognisable by very long ears called **pinnae**, which move independently to catch sounds and release body heat. They have large, prominent eyes on the sides of the head. The upper lip is split, revealing large front teeth (**incisors**) that grow continuously and are used for gnawing.
- **Body**: Compact, curved back, completely covered in soft fur.
- **Limbs**: The forelimbs are short. The hindlimbs are very long, muscular, and powerful, specifically adapted for hopping and leaping away from danger.
- **Tail**: Very short and fluffy, known scientifically as a **scut**.

#### 6. Poultry (Domestic Fowl)
The body of a fowl is adapted for flight, even though domestic fowls spend most of their time on the ground.
- **Head**: Small. The mouth is a hard, pointed, toothless **beak**. On top of the head is a fleshy red projection called the **comb**. Hanging below the lower beak are two fleshy red flaps called **wattles**. They also have fleshy **earlobes**. The comb and wattles help regulate body temperature and indicate the bird's maturity.
- **Body**: Boat-shaped and entirely covered in **feathers**, which provide insulation and aid in flight.
- **Limbs**:
  - **Forelimbs**: Modified into **wings**.
  - **Hindlimbs**: The lower part of the legs (shanks) is devoid of feathers and covered in rough **scales**. Each foot usually has four toes ending in sharp **claws** for scratching the ground. Adult males (cockerels) have a sharp, bony projection on the back of the leg called a **spur**, which they use for fighting.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Breed | A group of animals within a species selectively bred to have uniform, distinct traits |
| Trypanotolerance | The natural genetic ability of an animal to survive infection by trypanosomiasis (transmitted by the tsetse fly) |
| Mutton | The meat of a mature sheep |
| Chevon | The meat of a goat |
| Pork | The meat of a pig |
| Lard | Rendered (melted) fat from a pig |
| Pelt | The skin of a small animal (like a rabbit) with the fur still attached |
| Dewlap | A loose fold of skin hanging from the neck of cattle (especially Zebu breeds) |
| Switch | The tuft of long hair at the end of a cow's tail |
| Pinnae | The large, external, sound-catching ears of a rabbit |
| Scut | The short, fluffy tail of a rabbit |
| Comb and Wattles | Fleshy, red projections on the head and neck of poultry that help regulate body temperature |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Sheep vs Goats:** This is a very common exam area. Remember: Sheep have tails pointing down and produce mutton. Goats have tails pointing up, beards in males, and produce chevon.
- **Tsetse fly distribution:** Do not assume Zebu cattle stay in the North just because of the grass. The main limiting factor for keeping Zebu cattle in the South is the **tsetse fly**, which transmits the deadly disease trypanosomiasis.
- **Trypanotolerant breeds:** Examiners frequently ask which breeds can survive in the South. The answers are the **Muturu** and **N'dama** cattle, and the **West African Dwarf** sheep, goats, and pigs.
- **Pig distribution:** Remember that pigs are concentrated in the South because they **lack sufficient sweat glands** and cannot survive the harsh heat of the North without wallowing, combined with religious restrictions in the North.
- **Vocabulary:** Do not confuse the hide (skin of a large animal like a cow) with a pelt (furry skin of a rabbit). Know that a rabbit's tail is a **scut**, not a switch.
- **Breed names:** Match the breed to the correct animal. Balami and Uda are sheep. Red Sokoto and Sahel are goats.

## Quick summary

1. Farm animals are grouped into species and further divided into breeds based on specific characteristics and economic uses (e.g., beef vs dairy cattle, layers vs broilers).
2. Large Zebu cattle (White Fulani, Sokoto Gudali) are found in the northern Savanna to avoid tsetse flies, while trypanotolerant breeds (Muturu, N'dama) are kept in the southern Rainforest.
3. Pigs are mostly reared in the South because they have few sweat glands, need water to stay cool, and are affected by religious practices in the North.
4. Cattle provide beef, milk, hides, and draught power. Sheep provide mutton; goats provide chevon and fine skin (Morocco leather from Red Sokoto). Pigs provide pork and lard, and rabbits provide meat and pelts.
5. Zebu cattle feature a prominent hump, a dewlap, and a hairy tail switch.
6. Goats can be distinguished from sheep by their erect ears, upward-pointing tails, presence of beards and wattles, and their browsing habits.
7. Rabbits have long ears (pinnae), powerful hind legs, continuously growing incisors, and a short tail called a scut. Poultry have feathers, wings, a beak, scaly legs, and fleshy combs and wattles.

## Practice questions

**1. The breed of cattle that is humpless and highly tolerant to trypanosomiasis is the**

- A. White Fulani
- B. Sokoto Gudali
- C. N'dama
- D. Red Bororo

**Answer: C.** N'dama (along with the Muturu) is a humpless breed native to the rainforest zone, genetically adapted to survive tsetse fly bites (trypanotolerance).

**2. A major reason why pig farming is concentrated in the southern and middle-belt parts of Nigeria is because pigs**

- A. feed heavily on pasture grasses
- B. have a thick coat of hair for the rainforest
- C. possess very few sweat glands and need water to cool down
- D. are highly resistant to tsetse flies

**Answer: C.** Pigs cannot sweat effectively to regulate their body temperature. The hot, dry conditions of the northern savanna are unsuitable, as pigs need shade and water/mud to wallow in. (Religious restrictions also play a role in the far North).

**3. The flesh of a mature sheep is referred to as**

- A. beef
- B. chevon
- C. pork
- D. mutton

**Answer: D.** The meat of a mature sheep is mutton. Beef is from cattle, chevon is from goats, and pork is from pigs.

**4. Which of the following is a characteristic feature that distinguishes a goat from a sheep?**

- A. Possession of a tail that hangs downwards
- B. Presence of a beard and an upward-pointing tail
- C. Lack of wattles on the neck
- D. Grazing heavily on low grasses

**Answer: B.** Goats generally have an upward-pointing tail and males often have beards. Sheep have downward-hanging tails, lack beards, and are grazers rather than browsers.

**5. Which of the following poultry breeds is specifically developed for meat production?**

- A. White Leghorn
- B. Cornish
- C. Brown Leghorn
- D. Rhode Island Red

**Answer: B.** The Cornish breed is a heavy, broad-breasted bird raised as a broiler for meat. The Leghorns are layers (egg producers).

**6. The fleshy, red projection on the top of the head of a domestic fowl is called the**

- A. wattle
- B. comb
- C. earlobe
- D. spur

**Answer: B.** The comb is located on top of the head. Wattles hang below the beak, and the spur is a bony projection on the back of the leg.

**7. The prominent structure located behind the neck of Zebu cattle that stores fat is the**

- A. dewlap
- B. wattle
- C. hump
- D. switch

**Answer: C.** The hump is a large muscular and fatty mound on the back of Zebu cattle used as an energy reserve. The dewlap is the loose skin under the neck.

**8. Balami, Uda, and Yankasa are all breeds of**

- A. cattle
- B. sheep
- C. goat
- D. pig

**Answer: B.** These are the major breeds of sheep in West Africa. Balami and Uda are found in the North, while Yankasa is widely distributed.

**9. The term 'scut' in animal anatomy refers to the**

- A. cartilaginous digging disc on the snout of a pig
- B. long, sound-catching ears of a rabbit
- C. short, fluffy tail of a rabbit
- D. sharp bony projection on a cockerel's leg

**Answer: C.** The scut is the scientific name for the short, fluffy tail of a rabbit. The long ears are called pinnae.

**10. The Red Sokoto breed is mostly found in the savanna zone and is internationally famous for the production of**

- A. high-quality milk
- B. premium quality leather
- C. fine wool
- D. white meat

**Answer: B.** The Red Sokoto is a goat breed highly valued for its skin, which is tanned into premium "Morocco leather".
