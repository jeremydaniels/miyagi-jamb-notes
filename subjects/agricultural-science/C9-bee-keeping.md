## Meaning and importance of apiculture

**Apiculture** (commonly known as bee-keeping) is the art, science, and practice of rearing, breeding, and managing honey bees in artificial hives for the production of honey and other bee products. A place where bees are kept is called an **apiary**, and the person who manages the bees is called an **apiarist**. 

Bee-keeping requires relatively low capital to start. Bees do not compete with livestock or humans for food, and they do not require daily feeding because they forage for their own nectar and pollen in the surrounding environment.

### Relate bee-keeping to economic development

Bee-keeping is not just a hobby; it plays a vital role in the economic development of Nigeria and other West African nations. Its major economic contributions include:

1. **Increased agricultural yield through crop pollination:** As bees move from flower to flower collecting nectar, they transfer pollen grains. This increases the fruit and seed yield of agricultural crops (like citrus, mangoes, melons, and vegetables) by up to 30–40%. This indirect benefit is considered the most significant economic value of bees to a nation's agriculture.
2. **Source of income and employment:** Selling bee products provides a steady stream of income for rural farmers. It also creates secondary jobs for hive makers, protective gear tailors, honey processors, and marketers, which helps reduce rural poverty.
3. **Foreign exchange earnings:** High-quality honey and refined beeswax can be exported to other countries, bringing foreign exchange into the economy.
4. **Optimal land use:** Bee hives can be placed on marginal or poor agricultural land (such as rocky outcrops or forest edges) that is not suitable for growing crops, thereby making unproductive land economically useful.
5. **Industrial raw materials:** Products from the hive are vital raw materials for manufacturing. Beeswax is used in cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, and polishes; propolis is used in natural medicines.
6. **Medicinal uses and health:** Honey is known for its antibacterial properties and is widely used in local medicine to soothe coughs and dress wounds, contributing to the health of the workforce.

## Types of bees: exotic and indigenous bees

Honey bees belong to the insect genus *Apis*. To manage bees properly, farmers must understand the different types of bees available, which are broadly grouped into **indigenous** (local) and **exotic** (foreign) bees.

**Indigenous (African) bees** are native to the African continent. The most common indigenous bee managed in West Africa is the **African honey bee** (*Apis mellifera scutellata*). They are naturally adapted to the hot African climate, local vegetation, and native pests.

**Exotic (European) bees** are imported from temperate regions for commercial farming. Examples include the **Italian honey bee** (*Apis mellifera ligustica*) and the **Carniolan honey bee**.

### Differentiate between various types of bees

The table below differentiates the characteristics of indigenous bees and exotic bees.

| Feature | Indigenous Bees (e.g., African honey bee) | Exotic Bees (e.g., Italian honey bee) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Temperament / Aggression** | Highly aggressive and defensive. They sting readily and in large numbers if disturbed. | Docile, gentle, and less defensive. Much easier for the bee-keeper to handle. |
| **Swarming and absconding** | High tendency to swarm (divide the colony) and abscond (abandon the hive completely if conditions are poor). | Low tendency to swarm or abscond; they usually stay in the hive year-round. |
| **Adaptation to environment** | Excellent adaptation to the harsh tropical climate, local predators, and diseases. | Poorly adapted to tropical heat and highly susceptible to local African pests and diseases. |
| **Foraging ability** | Excellent foragers; can fly long distances and work aggressively even in hot weather. | Moderate foragers in the tropics; they may struggle and become sluggish in extreme heat. |
| **Physical size** | Slightly smaller in body size. | Slightly larger in body size. |

**The Castes of Bees**
Whether indigenous or exotic, every honey bee colony is a highly organised society containing three different **castes** (types of bees based on their role):

1. **The Queen:** There is only one fertile female per hive. Her sole job is to lay eggs (up to 2,000 a day). She is the largest bee, with a long abdomen extending past her wings.
2. **The Drones:** These are the fertile males. Their only function in life is to mate with a virgin queen on her mating flight. They are stout and bulky, do not have stingers, and do not collect food.
3. **The Workers:** There are thousands of sterile females in the colony. They perform all the labour: foraging for nectar and pollen, producing wax, building the comb, cleaning, nursing the young (brood), and guarding the hive. They possess stingers and will die after stinging a mammal.

## Methods of bee-keeping: traditional and modern

Bee-keeping methods are determined by the type of hive used and how the honey is harvested. They are classified into two main systems: **traditional** and **modern**. 

### Classify methods of bee-keeping

#### 1. Traditional bee-keeping (Fixed-comb system)
In traditional bee-keeping, hives are made from naturally available local materials. The bees attach their wax combs permanently to the walls and roof of the hive. This is known as a **fixed-comb system**.

- **Materials used:** Hollowed-out tree logs, clay pots, woven grass baskets, or empty gourds. These are usually hung high on tree branches to attract wild swarms.
- **Harvesting:** The farmer normally harvests at night using a fire torch or thick smoke to drive away or kill the bees. The farmer reaches in and cuts out the entire honeycomb.
- **Honey extraction:** The combs—which contain honey, pollen, and developing bee larvae (brood)—are all mashed together and squeezed by hand.

#### 2. Modern bee-keeping (Movable-comb system)
Modern bee-keeping uses specially designed wooden boxes where bees build their combs on removable frames or bars. This is known as a **movable-comb system**, which allows the farmer to inspect and manage the colony without destroying the nest.

- **Types of modern hives:** 
  - **Langstroth hive:** A rectangular wooden box with four-sided wooden frames hanging vertically inside. The frames usually have a wax foundation. It is the standard for commercial bee-keeping globally.
  - **Kenyan Top-Bar hive (KTB):** A trough-shaped wooden box with individual wooden bars placed across the top. Bees build their combs hanging down naturally from these bars. It is very popular in West Africa because it is cheaper to build than the Langstroth hive.
- **Harvesting:** The farmer uses a modern smoker to calm the bees, inspects the frames or bars, and removes only the combs that are full of ripe honey, leaving the brood (baby bees) safely intact.
- **Honey extraction:** For Langstroth frames, combs are placed in a centrifuge (honey extractor) that spins the honey out, leaving the empty wax comb intact so it can be returned to the hive for the bees to reuse.

**Comparison of Traditional and Modern Methods**

| Feature | Traditional Bee-keeping | Modern Bee-keeping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Comb attachment** | Fixed permanently to the hive; cannot be inspected without breaking. | Built on movable frames/bars; easily inspected without damage. |
| **Colony survival** | Harvesting often kills the brood or destroys the colony. | Brood is preserved; the colony survives and continues producing. |
| **Honey quality** | Low quality. Often contaminated with squashed larvae, ash, and dirt. | High quality. Pure, clean honey extracted without mixing in brood or dirt. |
| **Cost** | Very cheap; uses local, scavenged materials. | Expensive to set up; requires purchased timber, frames, and extractors. |
| **Yield** | Low honey yield because bees must use significant energy and nectar to rebuild wax combs from scratch after every harvest. | High honey yield because empty combs are returned; bees spend energy gathering nectar instead of making wax. |

## Equipment and safety measures in bee-keeping

Bee-keeping requires specialised equipment to manage the hive and extract products efficiently. Because African honey bees are highly defensive, working with them also requires proper animal handling techniques and strict safety precautions to protect both the apiarist and the public.

### Identify bee-keeping equipment

To successfully and safely manage an apiary, a bee-keeper must be able to identify essential bee-keeping equipment, understand their uses, and apply necessary precautionary measures.

**Essential Bee-keeping Equipment and Their Uses:**

1. **Bee hive (Langstroth or Top-bar):** The artificial wooden home provided for the bees to live, rear their brood, and store honey.
2. **Smoker:** A metal cylinder with a funnel at the top and a bellows attached to the side. It burns smouldering material (like dried leaves or wood shavings) to produce cool, white smoke. 
   * **Use:** The smoke masks the bees' alarm pheromones and makes them gorge on honey, which calms them and makes them less likely to sting during inspection.
3. **Bee suit (Protective clothing):** A full-body coverall made of thick, light-coloured, smooth cloth. 
   * **Use:** Protects the bee-keeper's body from bee stings.
4. **Bee veil:** A wide-brimmed hat with a fine wire or fabric mesh net that drapes down to cover the head, face, and neck. 
   * **Use:** Prevents bees from stinging the face and eyes, and stops them from getting tangled in the hair.
5. **Leather gloves and boots:** Thick gloves extending past the wrists, and sturdy rubber or leather boots.
   * **Use:** Protects the hands, wrists, and feet from stings.
6. **Hive tool:** A flat, metallic, crowbar-like hand tool with a curved edge.
   * **Use:** Used to pry open the hive lid, separate frames that bees have stuck together with propolis, and scrape off excess wax.
7. **Queen excluder:** A flat metal or plastic grid placed horizontally between the brood chamber (bottom box) and honey supers (top boxes) in a Langstroth hive.
   * **Use:** The holes are exactly large enough for worker bees to pass through with nectar, but too small for the queen. It prevents the queen from going up and laying eggs in the honeycombs, ensuring pure honey at harvest.
8. **Honey extractor:** A cylindrical metal drum containing a spinning basket that uses centrifugal force.
   * **Use:** Extracts honey from the comb without destroying the delicate wax structure, allowing the comb to be reused by the bees.
9. **Feeder:** Plastic or wooden containers holding sugar syrup.
   * **Use:** Used to feed bees artificial nectar during the dry season when flowers are scarce, preventing the colony from starving or absconding.

**Necessary Precautionary and Safety Measures:**

To prevent severe stinging incidents and ensure the welfare of the bees, the following safety measures must be strictly observed:

1. **Always wear complete protective gear:** Never open a hive without wearing a bee suit, veil, gloves, and boots. Ensure all zippers are fully closed and trouser legs are tucked into boots.
2. **Use the smoker correctly:** Always puff smoke gently into the hive entrance a few minutes before opening, and use it sparingly while working. Do not use hot fire or heavy black smoke, which harms the bees and makes them highly aggressive.
3. **Proper timing of operations:** Inspect hives and harvest honey either in the **late afternoon to evening** (when most bees are returning to the hive and are less active) or very early in the morning. Avoid working during the hot midday when bees are highly active and irritable.
4. **Avoid strong scents and dark colours:** Bees are highly irritated by the smell of perfume, alcohol, and human sweat. They also react aggressively to dark colours, which resemble their natural predators (like bears or honey badgers). Always wear clean, light-coloured clothing (especially white).
5. **Move calmly:** Make slow, deliberate movements. Swatting at bees or making sudden jerks triggers their defensive instincts. Handle frames gently to avoid crushing bees, which releases alarm pheromones.
6. **Proper apiary location:** Site the apiary far away from human dwellings, public roads, schools, and livestock pens to prevent innocent bystanders or animals from being attacked. A distance of at least 100 to 200 metres from activity centres is recommended.
7. **Protect from chemicals:** Never spray chemical insecticides or herbicides near the apiary, as these will kill the foraging workers and wipe out the colony.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Apiculture** | The art and science of rearing and managing honey bees for honey and other products. |
| **Apiary** | A location where bee hives are kept. |
| **Colony** | A family of bees living together, consisting of a queen, drones, and thousands of workers. |
| **Comb / Honeycomb** | A hexagonal wax structure built by worker bees to rear brood and store honey and pollen. |
| **Swarming** | The natural reproduction of a colony where the old queen leaves with a large group of workers to start a new hive. |
| **Absconding** | When an entire colony abandons a hive due to stress, lack of food, or pests, leaving it completely empty. |
| **Propolis** | "Bee glue"; a sticky plant resin collected by bees, used to seal cracks and sanitize the hive. |
| **Smoker** | Equipment used to produce cool smoke to mask alarm pheromones and calm bees during hive inspection. |
| **Queen excluder** | A grid that prevents the queen from accessing the honey storage area to lay eggs. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Pollination is the highest economic value:** Students often assume honey is the most economically important part of apiculture. While honey is valuable, the increase in crop yield due to bee pollination is economically much larger for a nation.
- **Traditional vs. Modern comb types:** Remember that traditional methods destroy the comb (**fixed comb**), while modern methods preserve it (**movable comb/frames**), leading to cleaner honey and higher yields.
- **Function of the smoker:** The smoker does *not* kill bees, put them to sleep, or drive them permanently away. It **calms** them by masking alarm pheromones and causing them to gorge on honey.
- **Workers sting, drones do not:** Only worker bees (and the queen, rarely, to kill rival queens) have stingers. Drones are males and absolutely cannot sting.
- **Colour of clothing:** Bees are aggressive towards dark colours. Always choose white or light-coloured protective clothing.

## Quick summary

1. Apiculture is the rearing of honey bees to produce honey, beeswax, propolis, royal jelly, and most importantly, to aid in crop pollination which boosts agricultural yields.
2. The three castes of bees in a colony are the Queen (fertile female layer), Drones (males for mating), and Workers (sterile females that perform all labour and sting).
3. Indigenous African bees are highly adapted to the tropics but highly aggressive, while exotic European bees are gentle but poorly adapted to hot, tropical climates.
4. Traditional bee-keeping uses local materials (pots, logs), destroys the comb during harvest, and produces lower-quality honey.
5. Modern bee-keeping uses wooden movable-comb hives (Langstroth, Top-Bar), preserves the comb and brood, and produces high-quality pure honey.
6. A smoker is an essential piece of equipment used to calm bees before and during hive inspection. A queen excluder keeps the queen out of the honey supers.
7. Bee-keepers must wear light-coloured protective clothing (veil, suit, gloves) and work slowly and calmly to avoid stings.
8. Apiaries should be sited far from human dwellings and livestock, and operations should be done in the early morning or late evening for safety.

## Practice questions

**1. The primary economic importance of rearing bees near an orchard is to**
- A. produce propolis for the farmer
- B. provide employment for youth
- C. increase crop yield through pollination
- D. prevent thieves from stealing fruits

**Answer: C.** As bees forage for nectar, they pollinate the flowers of the orchard crops, which significantly increases fruit set and crop yield. The most tempting wrong option is A, because propolis is a real bee product, but pollination has a far greater economic value than any hive product.

**2. Which of the following is a characteristic of indigenous African honey bees compared to exotic European breeds?**
- A. They are very docile and easy to handle
- B. They have a low tendency to swarm
- C. They are highly aggressive and defensive
- D. They cannot survive in hot climates

**Answer: C.** African honey bees (*Apis mellifera scutellata*) are known for their highly defensive behaviour and tendency to attack aggressively if disturbed. The most tempting wrong option is A, which actually describes exotic European bees (like the Italian bee), not indigenous ones.

**3. In modern bee-keeping, the equipment used to prevent the queen from laying eggs in the honey storage chamber is the**
- A. hive tool
- B. queen excluder
- C. honey extractor
- D. smoker

**Answer: B.** The queen excluder is a grid with holes large enough for workers to pass through with nectar, but too small for the queen's larger body, keeping the honey super free of brood. Option A is a common mistake because a hive tool is essential for managing the hive, but it is used for prying frames apart, not restricting the queen.

**4. A major disadvantage of traditional bee-keeping methods is that**
- A. the materials used are too expensive
- B. the honey is extracted using a centrifuge
- C. combs are destroyed during harvesting
- D. the hives are easily carried away by wind

**Answer: C.** Traditional hives are fixed-comb systems. To harvest the honey, the farmer must cut out and destroy the combs, which often kills the brood and forces bees to rebuild from scratch. The most tempting wrong option is B, but centrifugal extraction is a major *advantage* of modern bee-keeping, not a disadvantage of traditional methods.

**5. The main function of the smoker in apiculture is to**
- A. suffocate and kill aggressive bees
- B. mask alarm pheromones and calm the bees
- C. melt the beeswax for easy extraction
- D. drive bees permanently out of the hive

**Answer: B.** The cool smoke makes bees gorge on honey and masks the alarm pheromones they release, keeping them calm during inspection. Option A is a common mistake because students often confuse a bee smoker with chemical fumigators or insecticides intended to kill or suffocate insects.

**6. Which caste of bees is responsible for foraging, guarding the hive, and producing wax?**
- A. The drones
- B. The workers
- C. The queens
- D. The nymphs

**Answer: B.** Worker bees (sterile females) perform all the labour in the hive, including foraging, nursing, guarding, and building the combs. The most tempting wrong option is A, because students might think "drones" sound like worker robots, but in a bee colony, drones are males whose only job is to mate with the queen.

**7. When is the safest and most appropriate time of day for a bee-keeper to harvest honey?**
- A. Early morning or late evening
- B. Hot midday
- C. Midnight with bright floodlights
- D. During heavy rainfall

**Answer: A.** Early morning or late evening is best because most bees are inside, the temperature is cooler, and they are less aggressive than during the heat of midday. Option B is a common mistake because farmers often do general farm work at midday, but for bees, high heat makes them highly active and irritable.

**8. Protective clothing for bee-keeping is usually white or light-coloured because**
- A. white cloth is cheaper to buy
- B. dark colours trigger aggressive behaviour in bees
- C. white absorbs more heat from the sun
- D. light colours attract bees to produce more honey

**Answer: B.** Bees evolved to defend against dark-coloured natural predators (like bears or honey badgers). Dark clothing makes them aggressive, while light colours keep them calmer. The most tempting wrong option is C, because students know colour affects heat absorption, but white actually *reflects* heat; the real reason is behavioural, to avoid triggering the bees' defensive instincts.

**9. The sticky substance collected by bees from tree buds to seal cracks in the hive is called**
- A. beeswax
- B. royal jelly
- C. propolis
- D. bee venom

**Answer: C.** Propolis, or "bee glue", is a resinous mixture used by bees to seal unwanted open spaces and sanitize the hive. Option A is a common mistake because students know bees use beeswax for building, but wax is secreted from their bodies to make combs, while propolis is collected from plants to seal cracks.

**10. A modern piece of equipment that extracts honey using centrifugal force without destroying the comb is the**
- A. top-bar hive
- B. honey extractor
- C. queen excluder
- D. wax melter

**Answer: B.** The honey extractor spins the frames rapidly (centrifugal force), throwing the honey out against the walls of the drum while leaving the delicate wax comb completely intact. The most tempting wrong option is A, because the top-bar hive is indeed modern equipment, but it is a type of hive, not the extraction machine.
