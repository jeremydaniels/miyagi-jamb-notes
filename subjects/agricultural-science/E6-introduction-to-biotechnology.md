## What is Agricultural Biotechnology?

**Biotechnology** is the use of living organisms, cells, or their components to create useful products, improve plants and animals, or develop microorganisms for specific agricultural uses. 

In the past, farmers used **traditional biotechnology**, such as using naturally occurring yeast to ferment cassava into garri or brewing palm wine. Today, **modern biotechnology** uses scientific techniques to change the genes of organisms or to grow tissues in laboratories. This allows agricultural scientists to solve farming problems much faster and more precisely than traditional plant and animal breeding.

## Basic Terms in Biotechnology

**Objective i: use basic terms in biotechnology.**
**Content: Basic terms, e.g. tissue and anther culture in vitro fertilization and genetic engineering.**

To understand biotechnology, you must understand the basic techniques and terms used by scientists.

### 1. Tissue Culture (Micropropagation)

**Tissue culture** is the laboratory process of growing whole plants from very small pieces of plant tissue (called **explants**) on a specially prepared, sterile artificial nutrient medium. 

Because plant cells are **totipotent** (meaning a single cell has the genetic ability to grow into a whole new plant), scientists can multiply one plant into thousands of identical copies (clones) very quickly.

**Steps in tissue culture:**
1. **Selection:** A healthy, high-yielding parent plant is chosen.
2. **Explant cutting:** A tiny piece of tissue is cut, usually from the rapidly growing shoot tip (meristem). The meristem is chosen because it is usually free from viruses.
3. **Sterilization:** The explant is cleaned with mild chemicals to kill bacteria and fungi.
4. **Inoculation:** The explant is placed in a glass jar containing a sterile, jelly-like nutrient medium (agar) mixed with plant hormones (auxins and cytokinins) and sugars.
5. **Incubation:** The jar is kept in a specialized growth room with controlled light and temperature. The cells multiply to form a mass called a **callus**, which then develops tiny shoots and roots.
6. **Hardening:** The young plantlets are moved into soil in a shaded greenhouse to get used to the natural environment before being planted on the farm.

In Nigeria, institutes like the **International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA)** in Ibadan and the **National Root Crops Research Institute (NRCRI)** in Umudike use tissue culture to rapidly produce thousands of disease-free yam (*Dioscorea* spp.), cassava (*Manihot esculenta*), and plantain suckers for farmers.

### 2. Anther Culture

**Anther culture** is a specific type of plant tissue culture where the **anthers** (the male parts of a flower that produce pollen) or the pollen grains themselves are grown on a nutrient medium.

- **Why anthers?** Pollen grains contain only half the normal number of chromosomes (they are **haploid**). 
- When anthers are cultured, they grow into **haploid plants**. 
- Scientists then use chemicals to double the chromosomes, creating a plant that is perfectly **homozygous** (pure-breeding) for every single trait. 
- In traditional plant breeding, it takes many generations (years) of self-crossing to get a pure line. Anther culture achieves this in just one generation, greatly speeding up the creation of new crop varieties (especially in rice and wheat).

### 3. *In vitro* Fertilization (IVF)

The Latin term ***in vitro*** literally means "in glass" (referring to laboratory test tubes or petri dishes), as opposed to ***in vivo***, which means "inside the living body".

***In vitro* fertilization (IVF)** is the process of joining a male sperm and a female egg outside the animal's body in a laboratory dish. In agriculture, this is widely used to improve livestock breeds, particularly cattle.

**How IVF is used in farm animals:**
1. **Egg collection:** Unfertilized eggs (ova) are extracted from the ovaries of a highly productive "donor" cow (e.g., a high milk-yielding White Fulani or an exotic breed).
2. **Sperm preparation:** High-quality semen is collected from a superior bull.
3. **Fertilization:** The eggs and sperm are mixed in a petri dish containing a nutrient liquid. The sperm fertilizes the eggs to form **embryos**.
4. **Incubation:** The embryos are allowed to grow in a special incubator for a few days.
5. **Embryo Transfer (ET):** The developing embryos are then carefully placed into the uteruses of ordinary, less expensive "surrogate" cows, who will carry the pregnancy to term and give birth to superior calves. 

This allows a single top-quality cow to produce dozens of superior offspring in her lifetime, rather than just one calf a year.

### 4. Genetic Engineering

**Genetic engineering** (also known as genetic modification or recombinant DNA technology) is the artificial manipulation of an organism's genes. It involves taking a specific, desirable gene (a piece of DNA) from one organism and inserting it into the DNA of a completely different organism.

Organisms that have had their DNA altered in this way are called **Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs)** or **transgenic organisms**.

**Example in Nigerian Agriculture: The Bt Cowpea**
The cowpea pod borer (*Maruca vitrata*) is a devastating insect pest that destroys cowpea (beans) on farms across West Africa. Using genetic engineering:
1. Scientists isolated a gene from a common soil bacterium called ***Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt)**. This gene produces a natural protein that is highly toxic to the pod borer caterpillar but completely safe for humans and animals.
2. They inserted this Bt gene into the DNA of the cowpea plant.
3. The new genetically modified crop, called **Bt Cowpea** (or Pod Borer Resistant Cowpea), produces its own protection. When the caterpillar tries to eat the plant, it dies. 
4. This breakthrough was developed in Nigeria by the **Institute for Agricultural Research (IAR)** in Zaria, helping farmers reduce the use of dangerous chemical sprays and increase their bean yields.

| Feature | Traditional Plant Breeding | Genetic Engineering |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **How traits are mixed** | Pollen from one plant fertilizes another; thousands of genes mix randomly. | A specific single gene is isolated and inserted intentionally. |
| **Source of genes** | Must be from the same or a closely related species. | Can be from any living organism (bacteria, animals, other plants). |
| **Time taken** | Many years (often 10–15 years) of crossing and selection. | Much faster, as the specific trait is added directly. |
| **Precision** | Low precision; unwanted traits are often passed along. | High precision; only the desired gene is transferred. |

## Importance and Application of Biotechnology

**Objective ii: provide reasons for the importance and application of biotechnology.**

Biotechnology is fundamentally changing modern agriculture. Below are the key applications and the reasons why they are highly important to farmers, consumers, and the economy.

### 1. Applications in Crop Production

*   **Pest and Disease Resistance:** Crops are genetically engineered to resist pests and diseases. 
    *   *Reason for importance:* It drastically reduces the need for expensive and environmentally harmful chemical pesticides. Example: Bt Cotton and Bt Cowpea.
*   **Herbicide Tolerance:** Crops are engineered to survive specific weed-killers (herbicides). 
    *   *Reason for importance:* Farmers can spray entire fields to kill weeds without killing the crops, making weed control cheaper and requiring less farm labour.
*   **Rapid Multiplication of Disease-Free Plants:** Using tissue culture.
    *   *Reason for importance:* Allows farmers to get thousands of clean, virus-free planting materials (yam, cassava, banana) very quickly, regardless of the season, leading to massive yield increases.
*   **Improved Nutritional Quality (Biofortification):** Changing crops to contain more essential nutrients.
    *   *Reason for importance:* Helps fight malnutrition and deficiency diseases in developing countries. Example: "Golden Rice" enriched with Vitamin A, or Provitamin A cassava developed in Nigeria.

### 2. Applications in Animal Production

*   **Improved Breeding Capabilities:** Using *in vitro* fertilization (IVF), Artificial Insemination (AI), and Embryo Transfer.
    *   *Reason for importance:* It accelerates the spread of excellent genetic traits (like high meat and milk yield or disease resistance) through a herd much faster than natural mating.
*   **Animal Health and Vaccines:** Biotechnology is used to produce cheaper, safer, and more effective vaccines against livestock diseases (e.g., Newcastle disease in poultry, Anthrax in cattle).
    *   *Reason for importance:* Reduces livestock mortality, increasing profits and ensuring a steady supply of protein for the population.
*   **Transgenic Animals:** Animals engineered for specific traits (e.g., salmon engineered to grow to market size in half the time).

### 3. Applications in Food Processing and Storage

*   **Fermentation Technology:** Using engineered microbes or specific natural strains to produce yogurt, cheese, and traditional foods like garri and iru (locust beans).
*   **Prolonging Shelf Life:** Genetically modifying crops so they do not spoil or rot quickly after harvest.
    *   *Reason for importance:* Reduces post-harvest losses, meaning more food reaches the market and waste is minimized.

### 4. General Importance of Biotechnology in Agriculture (Summary)

To answer exam questions on "why biotechnology is important", remember these key reasons:
1.  **Food Security:** By increasing crop and livestock yields, biotechnology helps feed the rapidly growing human population.
2.  **Environmental Protection:** It reduces the heavy reliance on toxic agrochemicals (pesticides and fertilizers).
3.  **Climate Change Adaptation:** Biotechnology can create crop varieties that tolerate drought, extreme heat, or high soil salinity, ensuring food is produced even in harsh environments.
4.  **Conservation of Germplasm:** Tissue culture allows the safe storage of rare or important plant genes in laboratory "gene banks" for future use, taking up very little space compared to field farms.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Biotechnology** | The use of living organisms or their cellular components to create useful products and processes. |
| **Tissue culture** | Growing thousands of whole plants from tiny pieces of plant tissue on an artificial, sterile nutrient medium. |
| **Explant** | The small piece of plant tissue (often a meristem tip) used to start a tissue culture. |
| **Anther culture** | Culturing plant anthers or pollen to rapidly produce haploid plants, which can be converted to pure homozygous lines. |
| ***In vitro*** | Latin for "in glass"; processes occurring outside a living organism in a laboratory environment (like a test tube or petri dish). |
| ***In vitro* fertilization (IVF)** | The joining of male and female sex cells (sperm and egg) outside the body in a laboratory dish. |
| **Genetic engineering** | The technology of intentionally modifying the DNA of an organism, often by transferring a gene from one species to another. |
| **Transgenic organism** | An organism that contains genetic material artificially introduced from another species (a GMO). |
| **Callus** | A mass of unspecialized, dividing cells that forms during the early stages of plant tissue culture. |
| **Totipotency** | The ability of a single plant cell to divide and develop into a complete, full-grown plant. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Confusing *in vitro* and *in vivo*:** Remember, *in vitro* means "in glass" (in the lab), while *in vivo* means inside the living body. If fertilization happens in a petri dish, it is *in vitro*.
*   **Tissue culture vs. Anther culture:** Tissue culture (using shoots/stems) produces **clones** (genetically identical diploid plants). Anther culture uses male pollen to produce **haploid** plants, which are mainly used by breeders to create pure lines quickly.
*   **Knowing Nigerian Examples:** JAMB likes practical West African examples. Know that **Bt Cowpea** is genetically engineered for pest resistance, and **tissue culture** is heavily used for cassava, yam, and plantain multiplication.
*   **Misunderstanding Genetic Engineering:** A common mistake is thinking genetic engineering only happens between plants of the same family. No, genetic engineering can take a gene from a bacterium (like *Bacillus thuringiensis*) and put it into a plant (like cotton or cowpea).
*   **Safety of GMOs:** While there are public debates about GMOs, strictly from an agricultural science syllabus perspective, GM crops like Bt cowpea are taught as beneficial tools for increasing yield and reducing pesticide use. Answer questions based on their scientific utility.

## Quick summary

1. Agricultural biotechnology uses living systems, cells, and genes to develop tools and products that improve plants, animals, and food processing.
2. Tissue culture is used to produce thousands of identical, disease-free plants from a small tissue sample (explant) in a sterile lab environment.
3. Anther culture involves growing pollen grains into haploid plants to rapidly develop pure-breeding (homozygous) crop varieties.
4. *In vitro* fertilization (IVF) involves fertilizing an egg with sperm in a laboratory dish, often used alongside embryo transfer to rapidly multiply superior livestock.
5. Genetic engineering is the artificial transfer of specific genes from one organism to another, creating Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs) with highly desirable traits.
6. Biotechnology is important because it increases crop and animal yields, reducing hunger (food security).
7. It also protects the environment by developing pest-resistant crops (like Bt cotton and cowpea), which reduces the need for toxic chemical pesticides.
8. Biotechnology allows for the conservation of plant genetic resources in space-saving *in vitro* gene banks.

## Practice questions

**1. The laboratory technique of growing whole plants from very small pieces of plant tissue on a sterile artificial medium is known as**
- A. genetic engineering
- B. tissue culture
- C. in vitro fertilization
- D. anther culture

**Answer: B.** Tissue culture (or micropropagation) involves taking tiny explants and growing them into full clones on a sterile nutrient medium.

**2. Which of the following is the primary advantage of using anther culture in plant breeding?**
- A. It produces plants that are highly resistant to all known pests.
- B. It produces plants that have double the normal size.
- C. It allows for the rapid production of pure homozygous plant lines.
- D. It creates genetically modified organisms by inserting animal genes.

**Answer: C.** Anther culture produces haploid plants (from pollen). When their chromosomes are doubled, they become perfectly pure-breeding (homozygous) lines in a very short time, which is highly valuable for breeders.

**3. In biotechnology, the term *in vitro* literally translates to**
- A. in the living body
- B. inside the farm
- C. in glass
- D. in the soil

**Answer: C.** *In vitro* is Latin for "in glass", referring to biological processes made to occur in laboratory containers like test tubes or petri dishes.

**4. A major benefit of using *in vitro* fertilization (IVF) and embryo transfer in cattle is to**
- A. reduce the amount of feed a cow consumes daily
- B. rapidly increase the number of offspring from a superior cow
- C. prevent the cows from contracting viral diseases
- D. eliminate the need for any female cows on the farm

**Answer: B.** IVF and embryo transfer allow eggs from one superior donor cow to be fertilized and carried by multiple surrogate cows, multiplying her excellent genetics much faster than natural reproduction.

**5. The process of intentionally transferring a specific gene from one organism to another to introduce a new trait is called**
- A. genetic engineering
- B. tissue culture
- C. artificial insemination
- D. cross-pollination

**Answer: A.** Genetic engineering (or recombinant DNA technology) is the artificial manipulation of DNA to transfer traits, creating transgenic organisms.

**6. The "Bt" in Bt Cowpea or Bt Cotton stands for *Bacillus thuringiensis*, which provides the crop with**
- A. tolerance to drought
- B. resistance to specific insect pests
- C. increased vitamin A content
- D. ability to fix nitrogen without root nodules

**Answer: B.** The Bt gene produces a protein that is toxic to specific caterpillar pests, giving the plant built-in insect resistance and reducing the need for chemical pesticide sprays.

**7. Which of the following best explains why agricultural scientists use the rapidly dividing shoot tips (meristems) for tissue culture?**
- A. They are the only parts of the plant containing DNA.
- B. They are usually free from plant viruses.
- C. They contain fully mature seeds.
- D. They do not require light to grow.

**Answer: B.** The apical meristem (shoot tip) grows so rapidly that viruses often cannot reach it. Using it as an explant helps scientists produce clean, disease-free plant clones.

**8. All of the following are reasons for the importance and application of biotechnology in agriculture EXCEPT**
- A. development of crops that can tolerate harsh environmental stress
- B. reduction in the use of harmful chemical pesticides
- C. total elimination of the need for soil to grow any crops
- D. improvement of the nutritional value of foods

**Answer: C.** While biotechnology has many benefits, it does not completely eliminate the need for soil in global agriculture. Soil remains the primary medium for mass crop production, even with genetically modified seeds.

**9. The undifferentiated mass of cells that develops initially during plant tissue culture is called**
- A. an explant
- B. an embryo
- C. a meristem
- D. a callus

**Answer: D.** Once the explant is placed on the nutrient medium, its cells divide rapidly to form an unspecialized lump of tissue known as a callus, before developing shoots and roots.

**10. How does agricultural biotechnology primarily contribute to environmental conservation?**
- A. By requiring farmers to clear more forests for farming
- B. By developing pest-resistant crops that reduce the need for chemical sprays
- C. By increasing the amount of chemical fertilizers used on farms
- D. By replacing all farm animals with laboratory-grown meat

**Answer: B.** By using genetically engineered crops like Bt cowpea, farmers spray far fewer toxic insecticides, which protects beneficial insects, soil health, and water sources from chemical pollution.
