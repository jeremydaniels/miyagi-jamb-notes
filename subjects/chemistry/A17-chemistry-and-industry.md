## Chemical industries

A **chemical industry** is a business or manufacturing enterprise that converts raw materials (such as water, air, minerals, fossil fuels, and agricultural products) into valuable products using chemical processes. Unlike a school laboratory where chemicals are mixed in small test tubes to observe a reaction, industrial chemistry scales up these processes to produce thousands or millions of tonnes of materials safely, efficiently, and profitably.

### Classifying chemical industries by products

**Objective j: classify chemical industries in terms of products.**

Chemical industries are vast and interconnected. The standard way to classify them is by the final products they manufacture for consumers or for other businesses. The major classes include:

- **Heavy Chemicals Industry:** Produces basic, essential chemicals (such as acids, alkalis, and salts) that are used as starting materials by almost all other industries.
- **Petrochemical Industry:** Refines crude oil and natural gas to produce fuels (petrol, diesel, aviation fuel) as well as organic compounds used to make plastics, synthetic rubbers, and solvents.
- **Agrochemical Industry:** Manufactures chemicals used in farming, such as fertilizers (NPK, urea, ammonium trioxonitrate(V)), pesticides, and herbicides.
- **Pharmaceutical Industry:** Formulates life-saving medicines, vaccines, vitamins, and antibiotics.
- **Metallurgical Industry:** Extracts and refines metals from their natural ores (e.g., iron, steel, aluminium, copper, tin) and creates metal alloys.
- **Cement and Ceramics Industry:** Produces building materials like cement, bricks, roofing tiles, and sanitary wares.
- **Glass Industry:** Manufactures transparent silicate materials for windows, bottles, and laboratory glassware.
- **Food and Beverage Industry:** Processes agricultural goods into edible products, including sugar refining, brewing (beer and wine), and soft drinks.
- **Soaps and Detergents Industry:** Manufactures cleaning agents, washing powders, and toiletries.
- **Paints and Dyes Industry:** Produces decorative and protective coatings (emulsion and gloss paints), varnishes, and fabric colorants.

### Distinguishing between fine and heavy chemicals

**Objective iii: distinguish between fine and heavy chemicals.**

Chemical products are divided into two broad categories based on the scale of their production and the strictness of their purity requirements.

**Heavy Chemicals** are produced in massive quantities (often millions of tonnes annually). Because they are usually sold to other manufacturing plants to be used as raw materials, a moderate degree of purity is acceptable, making them relatively cheap. 
*Examples:* Tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$), sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$), ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$), chlorine ($\text{Cl}_2$), and sodium trioxocarbonate(IV) ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$).

**Fine Chemicals** are produced in small, specialized batches. Because they are often consumed directly by humans or used in highly sensitive laboratory tests, they must be of the highest possible purity, which makes them expensive.
*Examples:* Drugs (like paracetamol and aspirin), analytical laboratory reagents, perfumes, food additives (like vanilla extract), and specialized dyes.

| Feature | Heavy Chemicals | Fine Chemicals |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Volume of Production** | Massive (bulk quantities) | Small (limited batches) |
| **Purity Required** | Moderate (industrial grade) | Extremely high (analytical/medical grade) |
| **Cost per Unit** | Low (cheap) | High (expensive) |
| **End User** | Other industries (as raw materials) | Consumers, healthcare, laboratories |
| **Examples** | $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, $\text{NaOH}$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{Cl}_2$ | Antibiotics, perfumes, food flavours, analytical reagents |

**Textbook variation:** Some modern textbooks also mention a third category called *Specialty Chemicals* (chemicals sold for a specific function rather than their composition, like adhesives or specialized lubricants), but for JAMB, the core distinction you must know is between heavy and fine chemicals.

### The relevance of chemical industries

**Objective iv: enumerate the relevance of each of these industries.**

Chemical industries are the backbone of modern civilization. They improve the quality of human life and drive the economy. Here is why the major classes are relevant:

- **Agrochemicals:** Ensure food security for a growing population by significantly boosting crop yields (fertilizers) and preventing crops from being destroyed by pests and diseases (pesticides).
- **Pharmaceuticals:** Directly impact human health by curing diseases, managing chronic conditions, preventing infections (via vaccines), and ultimately increasing human life expectancy.
- **Petrochemicals:** Provide the primary energy source for global transportation and electricity generation. Furthermore, petrochemical derivatives (plastics, synthetic fibres like nylon) are lightweight and durable, replacing traditional wood and metal in modern packaging and clothing.
- **Metallurgy and Cement:** Are the foundation of physical infrastructure. They provide the heavy-duty materials required to construct durable housing, bridges, skyscrapers, roads, and transport vehicles.
- **Water Treatment (Heavy Chemicals):** The mass production of chemicals like chlorine ($\text{Cl}_2$) is highly relevant for purifying municipal water supplies, thereby preventing deadly waterborne diseases such as cholera and typhoid.

### Relating industrial processes to biotechnology

**Objective v: relate industrial processes to biotechnology.**

**Biotechnology** is the application of biological systems, living organisms (especially microorganisms like bacteria and yeast), or their cellular components (like enzymes) to manufacture useful industrial products.

While traditional industrial chemistry relies on extreme heat, high pressure, and metallic catalysts to force non-living chemicals to react, biotechnology harnesses the natural, highly efficient chemical reactions that occur inside living cells, usually at mild temperatures.

Major industrial applications of biotechnology include:

1. **The Brewing and Beverage Industry:**
   This is one of the oldest forms of biotechnology. The industry relies on **fermentation**. Yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) produces an enzyme called zymase, which converts simple sugars (from barley or sorghum) into ethanol (alcohol) and carbon(IV) oxide gas. This same process is traditionally used across Nigeria to ferment palm sap into palm wine.
   $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\text{(aq)} \xrightarrow{\text{zymase in yeast}} 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH(aq)} + 2\text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$$
2. **The Pharmaceutical Industry:**
   Many life-saving drugs are produced biologically rather than synthesized from scratch. For example, the antibiotic penicillin is grown using the fungus *Penicillium*. Today, human insulin (for treating diabetes) is produced industrially by genetically modified bacteria grown in massive tanks called fermenters.
3. **The Food Industry:**
   The baking industry uses yeast; the carbon(IV) oxide produced during fermentation gets trapped in dough, making bread rise. Additionally, the production of cheese and yoghurt relies on the action of lactic acid bacteria on milk.
4. **Waste Management and Biofuels:**
   In biogas production, specific bacteria (methanogens) decompose agricultural or animal waste (like cow dung) in the absence of oxygen to produce methane gas ($\text{CH}_4$), which is used as a renewable fuel for cooking and electricity.

### Worked industrial calculations

While the chemical industry involves many theoretical concepts, industrial plants rely on precise stoichiometric calculations to maximize profit and efficiency. Here are three examples of how stoichiometry applies to different industrial sectors.

**Example 1: Agrochemical formulation (Fertilizer)**
A fertilizer plant in Port Harcourt produces ammonium trioxonitrate(V) ($\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3$) for the agrochemical industry. Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in this fertilizer. (H = 1, N = 14, O = 16)
1. Write the formula of the compound: $\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3$.
2. Calculate the molar mass: $14 + (4 \times 1) + 14 + (3 \times 16) = 14 + 4 + 14 + 48 = 80 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
3. Find the total mass of nitrogen: There are 2 moles of N atoms per mole of compound, so $2 \times 14 = 28 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
4. Calculate the percentage: $\frac{28 \text{ g mol}^{-1}}{80 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} \times 100\%$.
5. State the final answer: $35.0\%$. (Note: Percentages are ratios and have no unit).

**Example 2: Biotechnology yield (Bioethanol)**
A local bio-refinery uses biotechnology to ferment cassava starch into bioethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$). If $360.0 \text{ g}$ of glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) derived from cassava is completely fermented, what mass of ethanol is produced? (H = 1, C = 12, O = 16)
1. Write and balance the fermentation equation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH(aq)} + 2\text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$.
2. Calculate the molar mass of glucose: $(6 \times 12) + (12 \times 1) + (6 \times 16) = 72 + 12 + 96 = 180 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
3. Find the moles of glucose used: $\frac{360.0 \text{ g}}{180 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 2.0 \text{ mol}$.
4. Use the stoichiometric mole ratio: $1 \text{ mol}$ of glucose yields $2 \text{ mol}$ of ethanol. Therefore, $2.0 \text{ mol}$ of glucose yields $4.0 \text{ mol}$ of ethanol.
5. Calculate the molar mass of ethanol: $(2 \times 12) + (6 \times 1) + 16 = 24 + 6 + 16 = 46 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
6. Convert moles of ethanol to mass: $4.0 \text{ mol} \times 46 \text{ g mol}^{-1} = 184.0 \text{ g}$.

**Example 3: Heavy Chemical extraction (Cement production)**
A cement factory in Obajana, Kogi State, roasts limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$) to produce quicklime ($\text{CaO}$). Calculate the volume of carbon(IV) oxide gas released at s.t.p. when $50.0 \text{ g}$ of limestone is heated. (Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16, Molar volume at s.t.p. = $22.4 \text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$)
1. Write the balanced thermal decomposition equation: $\text{CaCO}_3\text{(s)} \rightarrow \text{CaO(s)} + \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$.
2. Calculate the molar mass of $\text{CaCO}_3$: $40 + 12 + (3 \times 16) = 100 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
3. Find the moles of limestone: $\frac{50.0 \text{ g}}{100 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.5 \text{ mol}$.
4. Use the mole ratio: $1 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$ produces $1 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CO}_2$. Therefore, $0.5 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$ produces $0.5 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CO}_2$.
5. Convert moles of gas to volume at s.t.p.: $0.5 \text{ mol} \times 22.4 \text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$.
6. State the final answer with its unit: $11.2 \text{ dm}^3$.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Chemical Industry** | An enterprise that processes raw materials into manufactured products through chemical reactions. |
| **Raw Material** | An unprocessed natural resource (e.g., crude oil, limestone, air) used to manufacture finished goods. |
| **Heavy Chemical** | A chemical produced in massive quantities at moderate purity, primarily to serve as a raw material for other industries. |
| **Fine Chemical** | A chemical produced in small batches at an extremely high purity for specialized, direct applications. |
| **Biotechnology** | The use of living organisms, cells, or biological enzymes to carry out industrial manufacturing processes. |
| **Fermentation** | A biological process where microorganisms (like yeast) break down sugars into alcohol and carbon(IV) oxide in the absence of oxygen. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Do not mistake "heavy" for atomic mass:** When a chemical is classified as a "heavy chemical" (like $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$), it does *not* mean the molecule has a high molar mass. "Heavy" refers to the massive *volume* of production in the industry.
- **Glass vs. Cement raw materials:** Students frequently confuse the raw materials for these two industries. Both require **limestone** ($\text{CaCO}_3$). However, cement requires **clay**, whereas glass requires **sand** ($\text{SiO}_2$) and **sodium trioxocarbonate(IV)** ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$).
- **Biotechnology is strictly biological:** Physical processes like the fractional distillation of crude oil or the thermal cracking of heavy oils are *not* examples of biotechnology. Biotechnology must involve living agents (bacteria, yeast, fungi) or their enzymes.
- **Versatility of raw materials:** Remember that a single raw material can serve multiple industries. For example, crude oil yields fuels for the petrochemical industry and solvents for the paints industry. Limestone is used in cement, in glass, and as a flux in the extraction of iron.

## Quick summary

1. Chemical industries convert natural raw materials into valuable products and are classified based on the types of goods they produce (e.g., agrochemicals, petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals).
2. Raw materials are drawn directly from nature; they include minerals (like limestone and metallic ores), fossil fuels (like crude oil), air, water, and agricultural produce.
3. Heavy chemicals (such as $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and $\text{NaOH}$) are manufactured in huge volumes at moderate purities to serve as starting materials for other industries.
4. Fine chemicals (such as drugs and analytical reagents) are produced in small quantities but demand exceptionally high purity and safety standards.
5. Chemical industries are highly relevant to modern society, providing food security (fertilizers), healthcare (medicines), infrastructure (cement and metals), and energy.
6. Biotechnology is the industrial use of living organisms or enzymes to synthesize products.
7. Classic industrial applications of biotechnology include the use of yeast in brewing and baking, bacteria in producing human insulin and biogas, and fungi in manufacturing antibiotics.

## Practice questions

**1. What mass of carbon(IV) oxide is produced when $100.0 \text{ g}$ of limestone decomposes in the cement industry? (Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)**
- A. $10.0 \text{ g}$
- B. $44.0 \text{ g}$
- C. $56.0 \text{ g}$
- D. $100.0 \text{ g}$

**Answer: B.**
1. Write the balanced equation: $\text{CaCO}_3\text{(s)} \rightarrow \text{CaO(s)} + \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$.
2. Calculate the molar mass of $\text{CaCO}_3$: $40 + 12 + (3 \times 16) = 100 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
3. Find moles of $\text{CaCO}_3$ reacting: $\frac{100.0 \text{ g}}{100 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 1.0 \text{ mol}$.
4. Use the mole ratio: $1 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$ yields $1 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CO}_2$.
5. Calculate the molar mass of $\text{CO}_2$: $12 + (2 \times 16) = 44 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
6. Convert moles of $\text{CO}_2$ to mass: $1.0 \text{ mol} \times 44 \text{ g mol}^{-1} = 44.0 \text{ g}$.
*Common mistake:* Selecting C because $56.0 \text{ g}$ is the mass of the quicklime ($\text{CaO}$) produced, not the gas.

**2. Which of the following chemical industries produces tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid in massive quantities?**
- A. Agrochemical industry
- B. Pharmaceutical industry
- C. Heavy chemical industry
- D. Fine chemical industry

**Answer: C.**
1. Recall the definition: Heavy chemicals are produced in very large volumes for use by other industries.
2. Identify the chemical: Tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) is the most widely produced industrial chemical.
3. Compare the options: The pharmaceutical industry (Option B) produces fine chemicals (e.g., medicines) in small batches.
4. State the answer: Therefore, it belongs to the heavy chemical industry.
*Common mistake:* Choosing A because the acid is used to make fertilizers, but the primary manufacturer of the pure acid is the heavy chemical industry.

**3. In the industrial production of alcohol via biotechnology, which biological catalyst converts glucose to ethanol?**
- A. Zymase
- B. Amylase
- C. Tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid
- D. Sodium hydroxide

**Answer: A.**
1. Define the process: Fermentation uses biotechnology to convert sugars into alcohol.
2. Write the chemical equation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH(aq)} + 2\text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$.
3. Identify the catalyst: This biological reaction is catalyzed by the enzyme zymase, which is secreted by living yeast cells.
4. Evaluate wrong options: Option C ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) is a heavy inorganic catalyst, not a biological enzyme.
*Common mistake:* Selecting B, amylase, which breaks down starch into simple sugars but does not convert glucose directly to ethanol.

**4. Urea is a vital fertilizer in the agrochemical industry. What is the percentage by mass of nitrogen in urea, $\text{CO(NH}_2\text{)}_2$? (H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16)**
- A. $23.3\%$
- B. $46.7\%$
- C. $60.0\%$
- D. $28.0\%$

**Answer: B.**
1. Write the formula: Urea is $\text{CO(NH}_2\text{)}_2$.
2. Calculate the molar mass of urea: $12 + 16 + 2 \times (14 + 2 \times 1) = 28 + 32 = 60 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
3. Find the total mass of nitrogen in one mole: The formula contains $2$ nitrogen atoms, so $2 \times 14 = 28 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
4. Use the percentage mass formula: $\frac{28 \text{ g mol}^{-1}}{60 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} \times 100\%$.
5. State the final answer: $46.67\%$, which rounds to $46.7\%$. (Note: Percentages are ratios and have no unit).
*Common mistake:* Choosing A ($23.3\%$) by forgetting to multiply the nitrogen mass by 2, using $14 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$ instead of $28 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.

**5. What are the primary raw materials required for the manufacture of ordinary glass?**
- A. Limestone, clay, and gypsum
- B. Sand, limestone, and sodium trioxocarbonate(IV)
- C. Iron ore, coke, and limestone
- D. Bauxite and sodium hydroxide

**Answer: B.**
1. Identify the industry: The glass manufacturing industry produces transparent materials based on silicates.
2. Recall the raw materials: The standard ingredients are sand ($\text{SiO}_2$), limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$), and sodium trioxocarbonate(IV) ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$).
3. Evaluate the options: Option A lists the raw materials for the cement industry.
4. State the correct answer: Option B correctly lists the glass precursors.
*Common mistake:* Choosing A, confusing the raw materials of cement with those of glass because both industries use limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$).

**6. What volume of carbon(IV) oxide gas is evolved at s.t.p. when $90.0 \text{ g}$ of glucose is completely fermented by yeast? (H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, Molar volume at s.t.p. = $22.4 \text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$)**
- A. $11.2 \text{ dm}^3$
- B. $22.4 \text{ dm}^3$
- C. $44.8 \text{ dm}^3$
- D. $5.6 \text{ dm}^3$

**Answer: B.**
1. Write the balanced equation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH(aq)} + 2\text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$.
2. Calculate the molar mass of glucose: $(6 \times 12) + (12 \times 1) + (6 \times 16) = 180 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
3. Find moles of glucose reacting: $\frac{90.0 \text{ g}}{180 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.5 \text{ mol}$.
4. Use the mole ratio: $1 \text{ mol}$ of glucose produces $2 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CO}_2$. Therefore, $0.5 \text{ mol}$ produces $1.0 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{CO}_2$.
5. Convert moles to volume at s.t.p.: $1.0 \text{ mol} \times 22.4 \text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1} = 22.4 \text{ dm}^3$.
*Common mistake:* Choosing A ($11.2 \text{ dm}^3$) by incorrectly assuming a 1:1 mole ratio between glucose and carbon(IV) oxide.

**7. Which of the following correctly describes a characteristic of fine chemicals compared to heavy chemicals?**
- A. They are produced in massive volumes for general use.
- B. They have very high purity and are produced in small batches.
- C. They are relatively cheap and have moderate purity.
- D. They are used exclusively in the metallurgical industry.

**Answer: B.**
1. Define fine chemicals: These are specialized products like analytical reagents and pharmaceuticals.
2. Identify production scale: Because they are used in sensitive applications (like human medicine), they require extremely high purity and are produced in smaller quantities.
3. Compare with heavy chemicals: Heavy chemicals (like $\text{NaOH}$) are produced in massive volumes with lower purity standards (Options A and C).
4. State the answer: Therefore, fine chemicals are produced in small batches with strict purity.
*Common mistake:* Selecting A, confusing the definition of fine chemicals with that of heavy chemicals.

**8. If an industrial fermenter produces $9.2 \text{ kg}$ of ethanol, what mass of glucose was completely broken down? (H = 1, C = 12, O = 16)**
- A. $9.2 \text{ kg}$
- B. $18.0 \text{ kg}$
- C. $36.0 \text{ kg}$
- D. $4.6 \text{ kg}$

**Answer: B.**
1. Write the balanced equation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH(aq)} + 2\text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$.
2. Calculate the molar mass of ethanol: $(2 \times 12) + (6 \times 1) + 16 = 46 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
3. Convert mass to grams and find moles of ethanol: $9.2 \text{ kg} = 9200 \text{ g}$. Moles = $\frac{9200 \text{ g}}{46 \text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 200 \text{ mol}$.
4. Use the mole ratio: $2 \text{ mol}$ of ethanol requires $1 \text{ mol}$ of glucose. Therefore, $200 \text{ mol}$ requires $100 \text{ mol}$ of glucose.
5. Calculate the molar mass of glucose: $180 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
6. Convert moles of glucose to mass: $100 \text{ mol} \times 180 \text{ g mol}^{-1} = 18000 \text{ g} = 18.0 \text{ kg}$.
*Common mistake:* Choosing C ($36.0 \text{ kg}$) by ignoring the 1:2 mole ratio and assuming 1 mol of glucose yields 1 mol of ethanol.

**9. The primary raw material required for the petrochemical industry is**
- A. iron ore
- B. limestone
- C. crude oil
- D. synthetic rubber

**Answer: C.**
1. Identify the industry: The petrochemical industry produces fuels and organic solvents.
2. Recall the raw materials: The industry processes natural hydrocarbons.
3. Evaluate the options: Crude oil (petroleum) is fractionally distilled to separate its hydrocarbon components, such as octane ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18}$).
4. State the answer: Therefore, crude oil is the primary raw material.
*Common mistake:* Choosing D, synthetic rubber. Synthetic rubber is a *product* of the petrochemical industry, not a raw material.

**10. What is the primary industrial relevance of mass-producing chlorine gas ($\text{Cl}_2$)?**
- A. To extract iron from its ore
- B. To purify municipal water supplies
- C. To ferment sugars into alcohol
- D. To serve as a flux in cement manufacturing

**Answer: B.**
1. Identify the chemical: Chlorine ($\text{Cl}_2$) is a heavy chemical produced via the electrolysis of brine.
2. Analyze its chemical properties: Chlorine acts as a powerful oxidizing agent and biocide.
3. Relate property to relevance: Because it kills bacteria, it is widely used in water treatment plants to prevent diseases like cholera.
4. Conclude the correct option: Option B correctly states its role in purifying water.
*Common mistake:* Choosing D, confusing chlorine's applications with those of limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$).
