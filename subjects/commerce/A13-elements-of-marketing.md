## Importance and functions of marketing

**Objective i: highlight the importance and functions of marketing.**
**Content (i): Importance and Functions**

In simple terms, **marketing** is the management process responsible for identifying, anticipating, and satisfying customer requirements profitably. It is not just "selling" or "advertising"; it starts long before a product is made (by finding out what people need) and continues long after the sale (to ensure the customer is happy).

### Functions of marketing

Marketing involves a series of activities that ensure goods and services move smoothly from producers to final consumers. These functions are generally grouped into three categories:

1. **Exchange functions:**
   - **Buying:** Businesses must buy raw materials to produce goods, or wholesalers and retailers must buy finished goods to resell. Marketing ensures the right quality and quantity of goods are bought.
   - **Selling:** This is the core exchange function. It involves finding buyers, creating demand (through advertising and promotion), and transferring ownership of goods for money.

2. **Physical distribution functions:**
   - **Transportation:** Moving goods from where they are produced to where they are needed. For example, moving yams from Benue State to markets in Lagos.
   - **Storage (Warehousing):** Holding and preserving goods from the time they are produced until they are needed. This prevents scarcity and ensures steady prices (e.g., storing rice after harvest so it can be sold all year round).

3. **Facilitating functions:**
   - **Standardisation and Grading:** Standardisation establishes uniform specifications for products (e.g., all 50cl bottles of Coca-Cola taste and look the same). Grading is sorting products into classes based on quality (e.g., sorting eggs into small, medium, and large sizes).
   - **Financing:** Providing the money or credit necessary to produce, transport, store, and sell goods.
   - **Risk-bearing:** Accepting the possibility of losses (e.g., goods getting damaged, stolen, or going out of fashion). Marketers often use insurance to manage this.
   - **Market information (Market research):** Gathering, analysing, and distributing data about what consumers want, what competitors are doing, and what prices are acceptable.

### Importance of marketing

Why is marketing so important to businesses and society in Nigeria?

- **It links producers and consumers:** Without marketing, a factory in Aba making shoes would not easily find buyers in Kano.
- **It improves the standard of living:** By discovering what consumers need and providing better products (like smartphones and improved food products), marketing raises the quality of life.
- **It creates employment:** Millions of Nigerians work directly or indirectly in marketing—as sales representatives, advertisers, market researchers, drivers, and warehouse managers.
- **It generates revenue and profit:** By effectively selling goods and services, a business makes the money needed to survive, pay its workers, and grow.
- **It creates utility:** Marketing specifically creates **place utility** (through transport), **time utility** (through storage), and **possession utility** (through selling).

## The marketing concept

**Objective ii: discuss the marketing concept.**
**Content (ii): The marketing concept (consumer orientation, customer satisfaction, integrated marketing, etc)**

In the old days, businesses focused purely on making things efficiently (production orientation) or pushing people to buy whatever they had made (sales orientation). Modern businesses use the **marketing concept**.

The **marketing concept** is a business philosophy which holds that the key to achieving organisational goals lies in determining the needs and wants of target markets and delivering the desired satisfactions more effectively and efficiently than competitors. 

Simply put: *Find out what the customer wants, then make it and sell it at a profit.*

### Pillars of the marketing concept

1. **Consumer orientation (The customer is king):** All business decisions must start with the customer. Instead of saying "We make this soap, let's find someone to buy it", a consumer-oriented business says, "People want a soap that kills germs but doesn't dry out their skin; let's create it for them."
2. **Customer satisfaction:** The goal is not just to make a one-off sale, but to leave the customer happy so they come back and recommend the product to others. Happy customers build brand loyalty.
3. **Integrated marketing:** All departments in a business (production, finance, human resources, and marketing) must work together as a team to satisfy the customer. If the marketing team promises fast delivery, the logistics team must make sure it happens.
4. **Organisational goals (Profitability):** Satisfying the customer must be done in a way that allows the business to achieve its own goals, usually making a profit. A business cannot sell goods below cost just to make customers happy, or it will go bankrupt.

## Elements of the marketing mix

**Objective iii: assess the elements of marketing mix.**
**Content (iii): Marketing mix (product, price, place and promotion)**

The **marketing mix** refers to the combination of four controllable variables—known as the **4Ps**—that a business uses to influence consumer demand and achieve its marketing goals.

### 1. Product
A product is anything offered to a market for attention, acquisition, use, or consumption that might satisfy a want or need. It can be a physical good (like a loaf of Agege bread or a Tecno phone) or an intangible service (like a haircut or banking services).
- **Decisions include:** Design, features, quality, branding (the name or logo), packaging (the container or wrapper), and warranties. 
- *Nigerian example:* Indomie is a product; its packaging, its various flavours (Onion Chicken, Bellefull), and the seasoning inside are all part of the product mix.

### 2. Price
Price is the amount of money charged for a product or service. It is the only "P" that brings in revenue; the other three represent costs. 
- **Decisions include:** Setting the base price, offering discounts (trade or cash discounts), credit terms, and pricing strategies.
- If the price is too high, customers might buy a competitor's product. If it is too low, the business might lose money or customers might think the product is of low quality.

### 3. Place (Distribution)
Place involves all the activities that make the product available to target consumers where and when they want it. It refers to the **channels of distribution**.
- **Decisions include:** Which middlemen to use (wholesalers, retailers, agents), transport methods, inventory management, and location of sales outlets. 
- *Nigerian example:* Coca-Cola's "Place" strategy ensures that almost every kiosk, supermarket, and hawker in Nigeria has cold bottles of Coke available.

### 4. Promotion
Promotion covers all the ways a business communicates with consumers about its product and persuades them to buy it. 
- **Decisions include:** 
  - *Advertising:* Paid communication through mass media (TV, radio, newspapers).
  - *Sales promotion:* Short-term incentives like "buy one get one free", free samples, or discount coupons.
  - *Personal selling:* Face-to-face interaction between a sales representative and a customer (common in car sales or insurance).
  - *Public relations:* Building a good corporate image (discussed further below).

## Market segmentation

**Objective iv: explain market segmentation.**
**Content (iv): Market Segmentation**

**Market segmentation** is the process of dividing a large, diverse target market into smaller, more similar groups of consumers who have similar needs, characteristics, or behaviours. 

Because a business cannot be "all things to all people", it breaks the market into segments and creates a specific marketing mix for one or more of those segments.

### Bases for market segmentation

Marketers usually use four main criteria (bases) to segment a market:

| Base of Segmentation | Meaning | Examples in Nigeria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demographic** | Dividing the market based on measurable population characteristics like age, gender, income, occupation, education, or family size. | - **Age/Gender:** Cosmetics marketed specifically to adult women; baby food (e.g., Cerelac) for infants.<br>- **Income:** Luxury cars (Mercedes) targeted at high-income earners, while budget phones (itel) target lower-income groups. |
| **Geographic** | Dividing the market based on physical locations like regions, states, cities, or climate. | - Selling thick sweaters in Jos (due to the cold climate) and light cotton clothes in hot cities like Maiduguri.<br>- Launching a product only in urban areas like Lagos and Abuja before rural areas. |
| **Psychographic** | Dividing the market based on social class, lifestyle, hobbies, or personality traits. | - Marketing expensive gym memberships and health foods to health-conscious individuals.<br>- Promoting VIP lounge experiences to people who value social status. |
| **Behavioural** | Dividing the market based on consumer knowledge, attitudes, uses, or responses to a product. | - **Usage rate:** Targeting "heavy users" (e.g., MTN offering special data plans for people who stream videos all day).<br>- **Loyalty:** Giving rewards to frequent flyers on an airline. |

## Public relations and customer service

**Objective v: examine public relations and customer service.**
**Content (v): Public relations and Customer Service.**

### Public Relations (PR)
**Public relations** is the management function that establishes and maintains mutually beneficial relationships between an organisation and the publics (customers, employees, government, host communities) on whom its success or failure depends. 

Unlike advertising, which directly tries to sell a product and is paid for, PR aims to build a **good image** and **goodwill** for the company, often using unpaid media.

- **Tools of PR:** Press releases, press conferences, sponsorships (e.g., Globacom sponsoring the Nigerian Professional Football League), charitable donations (CSR), and managing crises (apologising and fixing things when something goes wrong).
- **Importance:** It creates trust. If Nigerians trust a company because it builds schools in the community, they are more likely to buy its products.

**Comparison: Advertising vs. Public Relations**
| Feature | Advertising | Public Relations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost** | Always paid for by the sponsor. | Mostly unpaid (relies on earned media/news). |
| **Objective** | To boost sales of a specific product. | To build a positive corporate image and goodwill. |
| **Control** | The business controls exactly what is said and when. | The media decides how to report the story, so control is lower. |

### Customer Service
**Customer service** is the provision of support, assistance, and advice to customers before, during, and after they buy or use a product. 

- **Elements of customer service:**
  - *Pre-sale:* Helping customers choose the right product, providing clear information.
  - *During-sale:* A polite cashier, a clean store, or a smooth checkout process on a website.
  - *Post-sale (After-sales service):* Delivering the product, installing it, handling returns, honouring warranties, and running a call centre for complaints.
- **Importance:** Excellent customer service leads to customer satisfaction. A satisfied customer becomes a repeat buyer and tells others, which is the cheapest form of promotion (word-of-mouth).

## E-marketing

**Objective vi: explain e-marketing.**
**Content (vi): E-marketing**

**E-marketing** (Electronic marketing), also known as digital marketing or online marketing, is the use of the internet and digital technologies to market a product or service. 

Instead of traditional billboards or newspaper ads, businesses use electronic channels to reach consumers directly on their computers or smartphones.

### Tools and channels of E-marketing
1. **Social Media Marketing:** Using platforms like Facebook, Instagram, X (formerly Twitter), and TikTok to promote products. Many small businesses in Nigeria thrive by posting goods on Instagram.
2. **Email Marketing:** Sending promotional messages or newsletters directly to the inboxes of interested customers.
3. **Search Engine Optimisation (SEO):** Making sure a company's website appears at the top of Google results when a customer searches for related products.
4. **E-commerce Websites:** Platforms like Jumia and Konga where goods are displayed, ordered, and paid for online.
5. **Mobile Marketing:** Reaching customers via SMS alerts (common with Nigerian banks and telecom operators) or mobile apps.

### Advantages of E-marketing
- **Global reach:** A seller in Alaba International Market, Lagos, can sell a laptop to a buyer in Kano or even Ghana without physical travel.
- **24/7 Availability:** Online stores never close; customers can order at midnight.
- **Targeted advertising:** Social media allows businesses to show ads only to specific people (e.g., targeting young mothers with ads for baby clothes).
- **Measurability:** Marketers can see exactly how many people clicked an ad and bought a product, which is hard to do with a radio advert.
- **Cost-effective:** It is generally cheaper to run an online campaign than to pay for television commercials.

### Disadvantages of E-marketing
- **Dependence on technology:** It requires internet access, smartphones, and electricity, which can be inconsistent in parts of Nigeria.
- **Security and fraud risks:** Fear of cybercrime ("yahoo yahoo") makes some customers afraid to put their ATM card details online.
- **Delivery and trust issues:** Customers cannot physically touch or try the product before buying, leading to disappointment if the delivered item ("what I ordered") looks different from the picture ("what I got").

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Do not confuse Promotion with Public Relations:** Promotion is the broad "P" that covers all communication. Public relations is just one *part* of Promotion.
- **Know the 4Ps by heart:** Product, Price, Place, Promotion. If a question asks which element deals with channels of distribution, the answer is Place.
- **Selling vs. Marketing:** Selling is just exchanging goods for money. Marketing covers everything from finding out what to make, making it, pricing it, distributing it, and finally selling it. Marketing is much broader.
- **Identify the correct segmentation base:** If a question mentions dividing by "lifestyle" or "social class", it is *Psychographic*. If it mentions "income" or "age", it is *Demographic*.
- **Public relations aims for goodwill, not instant sales:** A common mistake is thinking PR's direct goal is to sell goods. Its direct goal is building a good image; sales are an indirect result.

## Quick summary

1. Marketing is the process of satisfying consumer needs profitably through a series of exchange, physical distribution, and facilitating functions.
2. The marketing concept shifts the focus from the product to the consumer, emphasising customer satisfaction, integrated marketing, and profitability.
3. The marketing mix consists of four elements (the 4Ps): Product, Price, Place, and Promotion.
4. Market segmentation divides a large market into smaller, manageable groups based on demographic, geographic, psychographic, or behavioural characteristics.
5. Public relations builds a positive corporate image and manages relations with the public, often using unpaid media, while advertising is paid.
6. Customer service involves supporting the buyer before, during, and after a sale to build satisfaction and loyalty.
7. E-marketing uses the internet and digital tools (social media, websites, email) to reach consumers globally, though it faces challenges like cyber fraud and internet access.

## Practice questions

**1. The marketing concept places the greatest emphasis on:**
- A. maximising production output
- B. aggressive sales and advertising
- C. customer satisfaction
- D. lowering the price of products

**Answer: C.** The marketing concept revolves around identifying and satisfying customer needs better than competitors. Option B refers to a sales orientation, while A refers to a production orientation.

**2. Which of the following is a facilitating function of marketing?**
- A. Buying
- B. Selling
- C. Transportation
- D. Standardisation and grading

**Answer: D.** Standardisation and grading facilitate trade by ensuring uniformity and quality. Buying and selling are exchange functions, while transportation is a physical distribution function.

**3. In the marketing mix, the element that deals with channels of distribution and getting goods to the consumer is:**
- A. Product
- B. Price
- C. Place
- D. Promotion

**Answer: C.** "Place" involves the locations, logistics, and middlemen required to make the product available where the customer needs it.

**4. A company that produces different types of clothing for men, women, and children is using which base of market segmentation?**
- A. Geographic
- B. Demographic
- C. Psychographic
- D. Behavioural

**Answer: B.** Dividing the market by age and gender are demographic variables. A common mistake is confusing this with psychographic, which deals with lifestyle and personality.

**5. The main objective of public relations in an organisation is to:**
- A. forcefully persuade customers to buy goods immediately
- B. build and maintain a positive corporate image and goodwill
- C. set the optimal price for new products
- D. arrange the transportation of goods from the factory

**Answer: B.** Public relations focuses on managing communication between the organisation and its publics to build goodwill and a good image. Advertising is what directly persuades customers to buy.

**6. Which of the following best describes e-marketing?**
- A. Selling goods exclusively in physical supermarkets
- B. The use of digital technologies and the internet to market products
- C. Advertising products on television and radio
- D. Conducting face-to-face personal selling

**Answer: B.** E-marketing involves the internet, social media, emails, and websites. Television and radio (Option C) are traditional mass media, not e-marketing.

**7. An airline offering a "Frequent Flyer" program that rewards customers who travel with them often is an example of:**
- A. Geographic segmentation
- B. Demographic segmentation
- C. Behavioural segmentation
- D. Psychographic segmentation

**Answer: C.** Rewarding frequent flyers is based on the consumer's "usage rate" and "brand loyalty", which are behavioural factors.

**8. Which of the following is NOT an element of the traditional marketing mix (4Ps)?**
- A. Product
- B. Profit
- C. Price
- D. Promotion

**Answer: B.** The 4Ps are Product, Price, Place, and Promotion. Profit is a goal of business, but not one of the controllable variables in the marketing mix.

**9. Providing warranty support and repairing a product if it breaks down shortly after purchase is an example of:**
- A. Pre-sale customer service
- B. During-sale customer service
- C. Post-sale customer service
- D. Public relations

**Answer: C.** After-sales (post-sale) service covers everything that happens to support the customer after the transaction has been completed, such as warranties and repairs.

**10. A business decides to sponsor the Nigerian national football team, the Super Eagles. This promotional activity falls under:**
- A. Personal selling
- B. Sales promotion
- C. Direct marketing
- D. Public relations

**Answer: D.** Sponsorship is a major tool of public relations used to improve brand visibility and create goodwill among the public. A common mistake is picking sales promotion, but that involves short-term incentives like discount coupons or free samples.
