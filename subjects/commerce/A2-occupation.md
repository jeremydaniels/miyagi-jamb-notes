# JAMB Commerce Notes: Occupation

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 2 of the JAMB Commerce syllabus: Occupation. It covers three main content items:

*   (i) Meaning and importance
*   (ii) Types (industrial, commercial and services)
*   (iii) Factors that determine choice of occupation

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1.  State the importance of occupation.
2.  Compare the different types of occupation.
3.  Identify the factors determining the choice of occupation.

**Note on textbook variations:** Some textbooks classify "Services" as a sub-division under "Commerce" (as an aid to trade). However, the JAMB syllabus distinctly divides occupations into three separate branches: industrial, commercial, and services. You must follow the JAMB classification for your exam.

## Meaning of occupation

**Occupation** can be defined as any legal, legitimate, and productive economic activity that a person engages in regularly to earn a living. It is the work, business, or profession a person does to generate income to satisfy their basic needs and wants.

Key ideas in this definition:
*   **Legal and legitimate:** The activity must be acceptable by the laws of the country. Illegal activities such as armed robbery, smuggling, kidnapping, or cybercrime are **not** occupations in Commerce.
*   **Economic activity:** It must involve the creation of goods or the provision of services that have value.
*   **Earning a living:** The primary aim is to receive an income (salary, wages, profit, or commission) to support oneself and one's dependents.

## Importance of occupation

**Objective i: State the importance of occupation.**

People engage in occupations for various reasons, and these activities are vital for both the individual and the nation. The importance of occupation includes:

1.  **Generation of Income:** It provides people with wages, salaries, or profits. This income is used to buy food, clothing, shelter, and other necessities.
2.  **Improvement of Standard of Living:** By earning a steady income, individuals can afford a better lifestyle, access quality healthcare, and provide good education for their children.
3.  **Economic Growth and Development:** When people are employed in various occupations, they produce goods and services, which increases the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and drives the economic growth of Nigeria.
4.  **Provision of Goods and Services:** Occupations ensure that raw materials are extracted, processed into finished goods, and distributed to consumers. Without occupations, we would not have food, roads, healthcare, or schools.
5.  **Government Revenue:** People who work pay personal income tax (e.g., PAYE), and businesses pay company tax. The government uses this revenue to build infrastructure like roads and hospitals.
6.  **Reduction in Crime Rate:** An idle mind is the devil's workshop. When citizens are gainfully employed, the rate of poverty-driven crimes, theft, and social vices drops significantly.
7.  **Self-Actualization and Skill Development:** Working allows people to discover their talents, develop new skills, and achieve a sense of purpose and personal satisfaction.

## Types of occupation

**Content ii: Types (industrial, commercial and services).**

The various occupations available in an economy can be grouped into three broad categories: **Industrial**, **Commercial**, and **Services**.

### 1. Industrial Occupations
Industry refers to all activities involved in the physical extraction of raw materials, their conversion into finished goods, and the assembly of parts into structures. Industrial occupations are divided into three:

#### a. Extractive Industry (Primary Industry)
This involves workers who extract (pull out or harvest) raw materials directly from nature (land, water, or forests). This is the oldest form of occupation.
*   **Farming/Agriculture:** Growing crops (yams in Benue, cocoa in Ondo) and rearing animals.
*   **Mining and Quarrying:** Extracting solid minerals and fossil fuels from the earth. Examples include crude oil extraction in Rivers State, coal mining in Enugu, and tin mining in Jos.
*   **Fishing:** Catching fish from rivers, lakes, and oceans (e.g., the Argungu fishing festival, coastal fishing in Lagos).
*   **Lumbering/Forestry:** Cutting down timber in forests to provide wood for furniture and construction.

#### b. Manufacturing Industry (Secondary Industry)
This involves converting the raw materials produced by the extractive industry into finished or semi-finished goods using machines and human labour. The raw material changes its form completely.
*   **Examples:** Turning cotton into textiles (textile mills in Kano), processing cocoa into chocolate, refining crude oil into petrol, and turning limestone into cement (like Dangote Cement). Factory workers, machine operators, and production managers fall into this category.

#### c. Constructive Industry
This involves combining or assembling manufactured goods to build physical structures. Workers here do not produce the materials; they use them to build.
*   **Examples:** Building houses, constructing roads and bridges (e.g., civil engineers at Julius Berger), assembling cars from imported parts, and shipbuilding. Bricklayers, architects, and carpenters belong here.

> **Common mistake:** Students often confuse manufacturing and constructive industries. Remember: **Manufacturing** creates the building materials (making blocks, producing cement, cutting timber). **Constructive** puts those materials together to form a stationary structure (building the house or road).

### 2. Commercial Occupations
Commerce is concerned with the distribution and exchange of goods produced by the industries to the final consumers. (This is covered in depth in `A1-commerce.md`, `A4a-trade-home-trade.md`, and `A6a-aids-to-trade-advertising-banking-communication.md`). Commercial workers do not make goods; they ensure the goods reach the people who need them.
*   **Trade:** Buying and selling of goods. This includes retail trade (the mallam running a provision store, market women in Balogun market) and wholesale trade, as well as foreign trade (import and export).
*   **Aids to Trade:** Activities that make trade easy and possible. Occupations here include bankers, insurance brokers, truck drivers (transportation), warehouse keepers, and advertising agents.

### 3. Services
Service occupations involve providing intangible (non-physical) benefits that satisfy human wants. People in this category provide their time, skills, and expertise rather than physical goods. Services are divided into two:

#### a. Direct or Personal Services
These are services paid for **directly** by the person receiving or enjoying the service. The consumer and the service provider usually interact face-to-face, and the provider works for personal profit.
*   **Examples:** A private medical doctor running a private clinic, a barber in a salon, an entertainer (like a Nigerian musician or Nollywood actor), a private school teacher, a tailor, a domestic servant, or a commercial bus driver.

#### b. Indirect or Public Services
These are services provided for the general public, and they are paid for **indirectly** through taxes collected by the government. The people enjoying the service do not pay the provider face-to-face; the government pays their salaries.
*   **Examples:** A police officer providing security, soldiers in the Nigerian Army, public school teachers, civil servants working in government ministries, judges, and doctors working in a government general hospital.

> **Exam Tip:** The classification of a doctor or teacher depends on who pays them. A teacher in a government-owned public school is an **indirect service** worker. A teacher in a private school is a **direct service** worker. If an exam question simply says "a public servant", it always means indirect service.

## Comparing the different types of occupation

**Objective ii: Compare the different types of occupation.**

To clearly understand the differences, let us contrast the three main types of occupation based on their roles, output, and examples.

| Feature | Industrial Occupation | Commercial Occupation | Service Occupation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main Role** | Focuses on the physical creation, extraction, or construction of goods. | Focuses on the distribution, buying, and selling of goods, and facilitating trade. | Focuses on providing intangible skills, care, advice, or entertainment. |
| **Output / Product** | Produces tangible (touchable) raw materials or finished physical goods. | Does not produce goods; adds time, place, and possession utility to existing goods. | Produces intangible (non-touchable) benefits and satisfaction. |
| **Nature of Work** | Heavily involves machinery, physical labour, natural resources, and factories. | Involves logistics, marketing, finance, risk-bearing, and warehousing. | Relies heavily on human knowledge, talent, physical presence, and specialised training. |
| **Examples of Workers** | Farmers, miners, factory workers, bricklayers, civil engineers. | Wholesalers, retailers, importers, bankers, insurance agents. | Doctors, lawyers, musicians, police officers, barbers. |
| **Dependency** | The starting point of all economic activities. | Depends entirely on industry to have physical goods to distribute. | Supports both industry and commerce, and satisfies personal human needs directly. |

## Factors determining the choice of occupation

**Objective iii: Identify the factors determining the choice of occupation.**

When a secondary school leaver or graduate in Nigeria is deciding what work to do, several factors influence their decision. 

1.  **Salary and Remuneration:** The amount of money, allowances, and bonuses attached to a job is often the most significant factor. People naturally gravitate towards occupations that pay well (such as roles in the oil and gas sector or IT) to ensure a high standard of living.
2.  **Education and Training:** You cannot choose to be a surgeon or a lawyer if you do not have the required years of formal education, degrees, and licenses. The level of education and specific skills a person acquires dictate the occupations available to them. 
3.  **Natural Aptitude and Interest:** People tend to choose jobs they are passionate about and naturally good at. A person with a talent for singing might choose a career in the entertainment industry, while someone who loves numbers might become an accountant.
4.  **Risks and Hazards:** Some jobs are highly dangerous. Occupations like deep-sea diving, military combat, and underground mining carry high risks of injury or death. Many people avoid these jobs, while others take them up because hazardous jobs sometimes offer "hazard pay" (higher salaries).
5.  **Working Conditions and Hours:** The environment and hours matter. Some prefer 9-to-5 office jobs with air conditioning, while others prefer the flexibility of being an entrepreneur or the outdoor environment of a civil engineer. Jobs requiring constant night shifts or heavy physical strain might discourage some applicants.
6.  **Government Policies:** The government can encourage or discourage certain occupations. For example, if the government bans the importation of foreign rice, it creates more opportunities for local rice farmers and millers, encouraging people to enter that occupation. Conversely, government bans on certain activities can wipe out related jobs.
7.  **Availability of Vacancies (Employment Opportunities):** In economies with high unemployment, people cannot always choose their dream jobs. A trained engineer might end up driving a taxi or becoming a trader simply because those are the only available ways to earn a legitimate living.
8.  **Influence of Parents and Peer Groups:** In many Nigerian families, parents heavily influence their children to take up "prestigious" professions like medicine, law, or engineering. Alternatively, a child might inherit and continue the family business.
9.  **Social Status and Prestige:** Some people choose an occupation simply because it is highly respected in society, even if the starting salary is not the highest.
10. **Regularity of Employment:** Some jobs are seasonal or contractual, while others offer permanent, pensionable employment (like the civil service). Many choose government jobs for job security.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Occupation** | Any legal and legitimate economic activity that people engage in to earn a living. |
| **Industrial Occupation** | Work that involves the extraction, processing, or assembling of raw materials into goods. |
| **Extractive Industry** | The primary industry involved in getting raw materials directly from nature (e.g., farming, mining). |
| **Manufacturing Industry** | The secondary industry that converts raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods (e.g., textiles, food processing). |
| **Constructive Industry** | The industry that uses manufactured goods to build structures like houses, roads, and bridges. |
| **Commercial Occupation** | Work involved in the distribution and exchange of goods, making trade possible. |
| **Direct Services** | Services paid for directly by the person enjoying them (e.g., private doctor, barber). |
| **Indirect Services** | Services rendered to the general public and paid for by the government through taxes (e.g., police, soldiers). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Legitimacy is key:** Always remember that for an activity to be called an occupation in Commerce, it must be **legal**. Smuggling, prostitution, and armed robbery are excluded, no matter how much money they generate.
*   **Manufacturing vs. Constructive:** Manufacturing makes the parts (e.g., making cement, cutting wood). Constructive puts the parts together in a fixed location (e.g., building a house or road).
*   **Direct vs. Indirect Services:** The trick to identifying them is asking "Who pays?" If the consumer pays out of their pocket directly to the provider, it is a direct service (private tutor). If the government pays the provider's salary from tax money, it is an indirect service (civil servant).
*   **Classifying teachers and doctors:** A doctor in a private hospital is engaged in direct service. A doctor in a government teaching hospital is engaged in indirect service. Read the question carefully to see if "public" or "private" is mentioned.
*   **Extractive industry is primary:** Know that fishing, farming, mining, and lumbering all belong to the extractive industry because they take resources exactly as nature provides them.

## Quick summary

1.  Occupation is any legal and legitimate economic activity undertaken to earn a living. 
2.  Occupations are important because they provide income, improve living standards, promote economic growth, and reduce crime.
3.  The three main branches of occupation are Industrial, Commercial, and Services.
4.  Industrial occupations are divided into extractive (primary), manufacturing (secondary), and constructive industries.
5.  Commercial occupations include Trade (buying and selling) and Aids to Trade (banking, transport, insurance, etc.).
6.  Services are divided into direct services (paid for by the consumer, e.g., barber) and indirect services (paid for by the government, e.g., army).
7.  The choice of occupation is determined by factors such as salary, education, aptitude, hazards, working conditions, and government policies.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following is not a legal requirement for an activity to be classified as an occupation?**
- A. It must be legitimate.
- B. It must involve producing physical goods.
- C. It must be a means of earning a living.
- D. It must be an economic activity.

**Answer: B.** An occupation does not have to produce physical goods; service occupations (like teaching or medicine) provide intangible benefits but are still valid occupations.

**2. A worker whose job is to turn raw cotton into textile materials belongs to which category of industry?**
- A. Extractive
- B. Constructive
- C. Manufacturing
- D. Commercial

**Answer: C.** The manufacturing industry converts raw materials (cotton) into finished or semi-finished goods (textiles) using machinery.

**3. In the classification of occupations, the police force and the armed forces belong to**
- A. Direct services
- B. Indirect services
- C. Commercial services
- D. Constructive services

**Answer: B.** They provide security for the general public and are paid indirectly by the government through taxes, making them indirect services.

**4. The construction of the Third Mainland Bridge in Lagos is an example of**
- A. Extractive industry
- B. Manufacturing industry
- C. Constructive industry
- D. Direct service

**Answer: C.** The constructive industry involves assembling manufactured materials to build stationary structures like roads, bridges, and houses.

**5. Which of the following activities cannot be grouped under the extractive industry?**
- A. Tin mining in Jos
- B. Farming yams in Benue
- C. Catching fish in Argungu
- D. Baking bread in a bakery

**Answer: D.** Baking bread is a manufacturing activity (turning flour into bread). Extractive industries involve taking raw materials directly from nature (mining, farming, fishing).

**6. Ade wants to become a pilot, but he is afraid of heights and airplane crashes. Which factor is heavily influencing his reluctance to choose this occupation?**
- A. Education and training
- B. Government policies
- C. Risks and hazards
- D. Salary and remuneration

**Answer: C.** His fear of heights and crashes relates directly to the risks and hazards associated with the job of a pilot.

**7. Compare industrial and commercial occupations. Which of the following statements is true?**
- A. Industry distributes goods, while commerce extracts goods.
- B. Industry creates physical goods, while commerce facilitates their distribution to consumers.
- C. Both industry and commerce provide only intangible services.
- D. Commerce involves manufacturing, while industry involves banking.

**Answer: B.** Industrial occupations deal with the physical creation or extraction of goods, whereas commercial occupations ensure those goods reach the final consumers through trade and aids to trade.

**8. A private school teacher and a government school teacher belong to which classes of occupation respectively?**
- A. Direct service and Indirect service
- B. Indirect service and Direct service
- C. Commercial service and Industrial service
- D. Extractive service and Constructive service

**Answer: A.** The private school teacher is paid directly by the clients (parents) and provides a direct service. The government school teacher is paid by the state through taxes, providing an indirect service.

**9. Which of the following is an economic importance of occupation?**
- A. It increases the crime rate in the country.
- B. It relies solely on government funds.
- C. It generates revenue for the government through taxation.
- D. It reduces the availability of goods and services.

**Answer: C.** People who are gainfully employed pay income taxes, which serves as a major source of revenue for the government to build infrastructure.

**10. If the Federal Government places a complete ban on the importation of foreign poultry, how does this policy most likely affect occupations?**
- A. It will force all poultry farmers to change their jobs.
- B. It will create more opportunities in the local poultry farming and feed manufacturing industries.
- C. It will increase the number of importers of foreign goods.
- D. It will completely destroy the commercial sector.

**Answer: B.** A ban on foreign poultry removes foreign competition, encouraging more people to take up occupations in local poultry farming and the production of poultry feed due to higher demand.
