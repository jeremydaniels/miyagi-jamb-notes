# JAMB Commerce Notes: Purchase and Sale of Goods

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 5 of the JAMB Commerce syllabus: Purchase and Sale of Goods. It covers how trade is conducted, the documents involved, how pricing is agreed upon, and how payment is made.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Examine the procedures and documents used in the purchase and sale of goods.
2. Determine the terms of trade (discounts and pricing terms).
3. Distinguish between cash and credit forms of payment.
4. Explain the meaning and types of credit.
5. Analyse the merits and demerits of credit transactions.
6. Identify the means of payment.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** In the official syllabus, the items under "Terms of payments" are lettered "a" (Cash) and "b" (Credit) instead of using Roman numerals. In the "Means of payments" section, "mail, transfer" contains a stray comma and actually means "mail transfer," a traditional banking method. These notes follow the syllabus fully while using the correct terms.

## Procedures and documents used in the purchase and sale of goods

**Objective i: examine the procedures and documents used in the purchase and sale of goods.**  
**Content i: Procedure and documentation.**

When a business buys or sells goods, they do not simply hand over money and take the items as you might in a street market. They follow a step-by-step commercial procedure and use standard documents to keep records and avoid disputes. 

### The step-by-step procedure of buying and selling

Here is the typical sequence of events and documents used when a retailer (e.g., a shop in Ikeja) buys from a wholesaler (e.g., in Alaba International Market):

**Step 1: Enquiry**
The buyer sends an **enquiry** letter to the seller to ask about the availability of goods, their prices, and the terms of sale (discounts and delivery).

**Step 2: Quotation or Catalogue**
The seller replies with a **quotation** or a catalogue. A quotation states the specific prices, trade discounts, cash discounts, and delivery terms for the goods requested. A catalogue is a booklet showing all the goods the seller has, often with a price list attached.

**Step 3: Order**
If the buyer is happy with the quotation, they place an **order**. An order is a formal request on a printed **order form** stating the exact quantity, description, and agreed price of the goods to be supplied.

**Step 4: Delivery and Consignment Note**
The seller packs the goods and sends them to the buyer. The goods are accompanied by a **consignment note** (also called a delivery note or waybill). The buyer checks the goods against this note and signs it to confirm that the goods have arrived safely. If the goods are sent through a hired transporter (a carrier), the carrier will require the signed consignment note as proof of delivery.

**Step 5: Invoice**
The seller sends an **invoice** to the buyer. This is a very important document that details the quantity, description, unit price, and total cost of the goods supplied, minus any trade discounts. **The invoice is a demand for payment.**

**Step 6: Statement of Accounts**
If the buyer and seller trade frequently on credit, the seller will not demand payment for every single invoice immediately. Instead, at the end of the month, the seller sends a **statement of accounts**. This document summarizes all the invoices, payments, and returns made during the month and shows the final balance the buyer owes.

**Step 7: Payment and Receipt**
The buyer makes payment (e.g., by cheque or bank transfer), and the seller issues a **receipt** as proof that the money has been received.

### Other important documents in home trade

- **Proforma Invoice:** This is an invoice sent *before* goods are supplied. It is used as a quotation, or to demand payment in advance from a new customer, or for customs purposes if the goods cross borders. It shows what the real invoice will look like.
- **Credit Note:** Sent by a seller to a buyer when the buyer has been **overcharged** on an invoice, or when the buyer returns damaged goods. It reduces the amount the buyer owes.
- **Debit Note:** Sent by a seller to a buyer when the buyer has been **undercharged** on an invoice (e.g., a mistake was made in adding up the figures). It increases the amount the buyer owes.

### Documents used in foreign trade

When goods cross international borders (e.g., importing from China to Nigeria), special documents are used:

- **Indent:** An order sent by an importer to an agent in a foreign country to buy goods on their behalf. An *open indent* lets the agent choose the manufacturer; a *closed indent* specifies exactly which manufacturer to buy from.
- **Consular Invoice:** An invoice that has been signed by the consul (an embassy official) of the importing country who is stationed in the exporting country. It proves that the goods are accurately priced and prevents dumping (selling goods too cheaply to destroy local industries).
- **Bill of Lading:** A crucial document used when goods are sent by sea. It acts as a receipt for the goods on the ship, a contract of carriage, and a **document of title** (whoever holds it legally owns the goods and can claim them at the destination port).
- **Certificate of Origin:** A document signed by a Chamber of Commerce in the exporting country proving exactly where the goods were manufactured. It is often used to get lower customs duties if the two countries have a trade agreement.

## Terms of trade

**Objective ii: determine the terms of trade.**  
**Content ii: Terms of trade.**

"Terms of trade" refers to the conditions under which a seller is willing to sell goods to a buyer. These include discounts (reductions in price), guarantees of quality, and who pays for transport and insurance.

### 1. Discounts

Sellers use discounts to encourage buyers to act in a certain way.

- **Trade Discount:** A reduction from the catalogue or list price given by a manufacturer to a wholesaler, or by a wholesaler to a retailer. It allows the buyer to make a profit when they resell the goods at the list price. It is also used to encourage bulk buying.
- **Quantity Discount:** A specific type of discount given solely because a buyer purchases a very large volume of goods. 
- **Cash Discount:** A reduction from the net price given to a credit customer to encourage them to pay their debt quickly (prompt payment). For example, "5% one month" means a 5% discount if the debt is paid within one month.

#### Comparing Trade and Cash Discounts

| Feature | Trade Discount | Cash Discount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | To encourage bulk buying and allow middlemen a profit margin | To encourage prompt payment of debts |
| **Calculation** | Calculated first, on the gross catalogue or list price | Calculated second, on the net amount (after trade discount) |
| **Accounting** | Deducted on the invoice; not entered in the double-entry ledger | Recorded in the discount columns of the cash book |
| **Given to** | Usually given to other traders (wholesalers, retailers) | Given to any debtor who pays within the agreed time limit |

#### Worked Example: Calculating discounts

A manufacturer sells a consignment of yams with a list price of ₦500,000. He offers a trade discount of 10% to his regular wholesalers, and a cash discount of 5% if payment is made within 14 days. If a wholesaler pays on the 5th day, how much will they pay?

**Step 1: Calculate the trade discount.**
$$ \text{Trade Discount} = 10\% \text{ of } ₦500,000 = \frac{10}{100} \times 500,000 = ₦50,000 $$

**Step 2: Find the net price (the invoice amount).**
$$ \text{Net Price} = \text{List Price} - \text{Trade Discount} $$
$$ \text{Net Price} = 500,000 - 50,000 = ₦450,000 $$

**Step 3: Calculate the cash discount (always calculated on the net price, NOT the list price).**
$$ \text{Cash Discount} = 5\% \text{ of } ₦450,000 = \frac{5}{100} \times 450,000 = ₦22,500 $$

**Step 4: Find the final amount to pay.**
$$ \text{Amount to Pay} = \text{Net Price} - \text{Cash Discount} $$
$$ \text{Amount to Pay} = 450,000 - 22,500 = ₦427,500 $$

**The wholesaler will pay ₦427,500.**

### 2. Price quotations and terms of delivery

Sellers use abbreviations to explain exactly what is included in the price quoted. 

- **E. & O.E. (Errors and Omissions Excepted):** Printed at the bottom of an invoice, this means the seller reserves the right to correct any mathematical mistakes or missing items discovered later.
- **C.O.D. (Cash on Delivery):** The buyer must pay the delivery driver in cash or transfer exactly when the goods arrive. If they do not pay, the goods are taken back.
- **F.O.B. (Free on Board):** Common in international trade. The quoted F.O.B. price includes the cost of the goods and the cost of transporting them to the port and loading them onto the ship. Once on the ship, the buyer is responsible for paying ocean freight and marine insurance.
- **C.I.F. (Cost, Insurance, Freight):** The quoted price includes the cost of the goods, marine insurance, and the ocean freight to deliver the goods to the buyer's destination port. 

#### Worked Example: Calculating C.I.F. Price

A Nigerian importer buys electronics from Japan. The cost of the goods including loading onto the ship (the F.O.B. price) is ₦10,000,000. The shipping line charges an ocean freight of ₦800,000, and marine insurance costs ₦200,000. What is the C.I.F. price?

**Step 1: State the C.I.F. formula.**
$$ \text{C.I.F. Price} = \text{Cost (F.O.B.)} + \text{Insurance} + \text{Freight} $$

**Step 2: Add the values together.**
$$ \text{C.I.F. Price} = 10,000,000 + 200,000 + 800,000 = ₦11,000,000 $$

**The C.I.F. price is ₦11,000,000.**

### 3. Warranties
A **warranty** is a promise or guarantee given by the seller or manufacturer that a product is of good quality and will work correctly. If the product develops a fault within a specified period (e.g., a one-year warranty on a new generator), the seller promises to repair or replace it for free.

## Terms of payments

**Content iii: Terms of payments.**

Payment for goods can be made in two main ways: cash or credit. 

### Cash: Spot and prompt payment

**Content a: Cash – Spot/Prompt payment.**

A cash transaction means goods are paid for before or at the time they are received, or very shortly after. It does not literally mean "physical naira notes"—a bank transfer paid immediately is still a cash transaction in commerce.

- **Spot payment:** Payment is made instantly on the spot. For example, paying for a textbook in a bookshop and walking out with it. 
- **Prompt payment:** Payment is made within a very short, agreed period (usually 7 or 14 days) after the invoice is received. Because it is so fast, sellers treat it like cash and often offer a cash discount as a reward.

### Distinguishing between cash and credit forms of payment

**Objective iii: distinguish between cash and credit forms of payment.**

| Feature | Cash Transaction | Credit Transaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Time of payment** | Immediate (spot) or very short term (prompt) | Deferred to an agreed future date (e.g., 30 or 90 days) |
| **Possession vs Ownership** | The buyer takes possession and full legal ownership instantly upon payment | The buyer takes possession, but in some types (like hire purchase), ownership remains with the seller until final payment |
| **Cost of goods** | Usually cheaper because no interest is charged, and it may attract a cash discount | Usually more expensive because the seller may add an interest charge to cover the delay |
| **Risk to seller** | Zero risk of bad debts | High risk of bad debts (buyer might fail to pay) |

## Credit transactions: Meaning and types

**Objective iv: explain the meaning and types of credit.**  
**Content b: Credit- Meaning; Types and functions.**

### Meaning of credit
**Credit** is a system of trading where a seller allows a buyer to take possession of goods or services immediately, with an agreement to pay for them at a future date. It is based on trust (the word credit comes from the Latin *credere*, meaning "to trust").

### Types of credit

1. **Trade Credit:** When a supplier provides goods to a business customer without demanding immediate payment (e.g., a wholesaler giving a retailer 30 days to pay). This allows the retailer to sell the goods and use the proceeds to pay the wholesaler.
2. **Bank Loan:** A fixed sum of money borrowed from a bank for a specific period, paid back with interest.
3. **Bank Overdraft:** An arrangement where a bank allows a current account holder to withdraw more money than they have in their account, up to an agreed limit. Interest is charged daily on the overdrawn amount.
4. **Hire Purchase:** A credit system where a buyer takes possession of expensive goods (like a car or machinery) after paying an initial deposit, and pays the rest in regular installments. The buyer is only "hiring" the goods; **legal ownership remains with the seller** until the final installment is paid. If the buyer defaults, the seller can repossess the goods.
5. **Deferred Payment:** Similar to hire purchase, but the legal ownership of the goods passes to the buyer immediately upon taking possession. If the buyer defaults, the seller can sue them for the money, but usually cannot take the goods back.
6. **Leasing:** Renting equipment or a building for a fixed period. The person renting (lessee) never intends to own the asset; they just pay for its use.
7. **Consumer Credit (Credit Cards):** Mostly used by individuals to buy personal items. A bank issues a card with a credit limit; the consumer buys goods, the bank pays the seller, and the consumer pays the bank later.
8. **Esusu (Club Credit):** A traditional Nigerian savings and credit system where members contribute a fixed sum regularly, and one member takes the entire collection in turn.

### Functions of credit
- It enables production to continue smoothly (factories can buy raw materials before selling finished goods).
- It provides capital for businesses to expand.
- It bridges the time gap between production and final consumption.
- It raises the standard of living by allowing consumers to enjoy expensive items (like cars and houses) while paying for them slowly.

## Merits and demerits of credit transactions

**Objective v: analyse the merits and demerits of credit transactions.**

**Merits (Advantages):**
1. **Increases sales:** Sellers can attract more customers who do not have cash instantly, leading to higher profits.
2. **Improves standard of living:** Consumers can use items like refrigerators and televisions immediately instead of saving for years.
3. **Helps businesses survive:** Retailers can stock their shelves and start trading using trade credit, even if they lack large capital.
4. **Convenience:** Buyers don't have to carry large amounts of cash, which reduces the risk of theft.

**Demerits (Disadvantages):**
1. **Risk of bad debts:** The biggest danger to the seller is that the buyer may refuse or be unable to pay, causing financial loss.
2. **Ties up capital:** The seller's money is locked in unpaid goods, which can cause cash-flow problems and prevent them from buying new stock.
3. **Encourages over-spending:** Consumers may buy things they do not need and cannot afford, leading to severe debt problems.
4. **Higher prices:** To cover the risk of bad debts and the cost of waiting, sellers often charge higher prices or add interest to credit sales.

## Means of payment

**Objective vi: Identify the means of payment.**  
**Content iv: Means of payments.**

Once a purchase is made, the buyer must settle the debt. Commerce offers many means of payment to suit different amounts and situations.

1. **Legal Tender (Bank Notes and Coins):**
   This is money backed by the government (like the Naira issued by the Central Bank of Nigeria). By law, it must be accepted by a creditor in settlement of a debt. It is best for small, everyday, face-to-face transactions.

2. **Cheques:**
   A written order from an account holder directing their bank to pay a specific sum of money to a named person. Cheques are safe because they do not involve carrying cash. If a cheque is **crossed** (two parallel lines drawn on the face), it cannot be cashed over the counter; it must be paid into a bank account, making it very secure.

3. **Bank Draft:**
   A cheque drawn by a bank on itself rather than on a customer’s account. A buyer pays their bank in cash, and the bank issues a draft. Sellers prefer drafts over personal cheques because a bank draft cannot "bounce" (be returned for lack of funds).

4. **Standing Order:**
   An instruction given by an account holder to their bank to pay a fixed, regular amount to a specific person or organization (e.g., paying ₦50,000 every month for shop rent).

5. **Postal Orders and Money Orders:**
   Issued by the post office (NIPOST). They are a safe way to send small amounts of money through the post to people who might not have a bank account. A buyer buys the order at the post office, posts it, and the receiver cashes it at their local post office. (Less common today, but still in the syllabus).

6. **Stamps:**
   In the past, very small debts (e.g., ordering a cheap booklet by mail) could be paid by sending unused postage stamps, which the seller could then use on their own letters.

7. **Bills of Exchange:**
   Used mostly in international trade. It is an "unconditional order in writing, addressed by one person to another, signed by the person giving it, requiring the person to whom it is addressed to pay on demand or at a fixed or determinable future time a sum certain in money to or to the order of a specified person." The three parties are the **drawer** (the seller writing the bill), the **drawee** (the buyer who must pay), and the **payee** (the person receiving the money, often the drawer's bank).

8. **Promissory Note:**
   A written promise made by one person to pay a specific sum of money to another on demand or at a future date. Unlike a bill of exchange, it is a two-party instrument (a promise, not an order).

9. **Mail Transfer:**
   An older banking method where a bank sends a written instruction by mail to another bank branch to pay a specific sum to a payee.

10. **Telegraphic Transfer:**
    The fast, electronic transfer of funds between banks. It is commonly used in international trade to wire money securely across borders.

11. **Traveler’s Cheques:**
    Pre-printed cheques of fixed denominations used by tourists. If lost or stolen, they can be replaced, making them safer than carrying foreign cash.

12. **E-payment (Electronic Payment):**
    Modern electronic methods including Point of Sale (POS) terminals, mobile banking apps, USSD codes, and web payment gateways (like Paystack or Flutterwave). They are fast, convenient, and leave a digital trail.

13. **Recharge Card:**
    Pins from telecom recharge cards (e.g., MTN, Airtel) are sometimes used as a crude but effective micro-payment method in Nigeria, where a user sends the pin to a vendor to pay for a small digital service or subscription.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Quotation** | A reply to an enquiry stating the prices and terms under which a seller will supply goods. |
| **Invoice** | A commercial document issued by a seller detailing goods supplied and demanding payment. |
| **Proforma Invoice** | A document that acts like a quotation or a demand for advance payment, showing what the actual invoice will look like. |
| **Bill of Lading** | A document used in sea transport that acts as a receipt, a contract, and a document of title to the goods. |
| **Trade Discount** | A deduction from the list price to encourage bulk buying and allow a profit margin for retailers. |
| **Cash Discount** | A deduction from the net price to encourage the prompt payment of a debt. |
| **F.O.B. (Free on Board)** | A price quotation that includes the cost of goods and loading them onto a ship; the buyer pays freight and insurance. |
| **C.I.F. (Cost, Insurance, Freight)** | A price quotation that includes the cost of goods, marine insurance, and ocean transport to the destination. |
| **Hire Purchase** | A credit system where the buyer pays in installments and does not own the goods until the final payment is made. |
| **Bill of Exchange** | An unconditional written order requiring a person to pay a certain sum of money on demand or at a future date. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Discount calculations:** The most common mistake in exams is calculating cash discount on the gross list price. **Always deduct the trade discount first**, and then calculate the cash discount on the remaining net price.
- **Hire purchase vs Deferred payment:** In hire purchase, ownership does not pass until the final payment. In deferred payment, ownership passes immediately.
- **Proforma invoice:** Remember it has three main uses: as a quotation, to demand payment in advance, and for customs clearance.
- **E. & O.E.:** Stands for "Errors and Omissions Excepted." It allows the seller to correct mistakes on an invoice later.
- **Bill of Lading vs Consignment Note:** A bill of lading is a document of title for sea transport. A consignment note is just a delivery receipt for road transport and is *not* a document of title.

## Quick summary

1. The standard purchasing procedure is: Enquiry → Quotation → Order → Consignment Note → Invoice → Statement of Accounts.
2. An invoice is a demand for payment; a credit note is used if the buyer was overcharged, and a debit note if the buyer was undercharged.
3. Foreign trade requires special documents like the Bill of Lading (a document of title), Consular Invoice, and Certificate of Origin.
4. Trade discount encourages bulk buying and is deducted first. Cash discount encourages quick payment and is deducted from the net total.
5. C.I.F. includes Cost, Insurance, and Freight. F.O.B. covers the Cost of goods up to loading them Free On Board a ship.
6. Credit trading allows buyers to take goods now and pay later. It increases sales for the seller but carries the heavy risk of bad debts.
7. Payments can be settled using legal tender, secure bank drafts, bills of exchange, e-payments, or cheques.

## Practice questions

**1. A buyer who wishes to find out the price and availability of goods from a supplier will send an**
- A. order form
- B. enquiry
- C. invoice
- D. indent

**Answer: B.** An enquiry is a letter sent by a buyer asking for a price list, a catalogue, or terms of sale. The seller will reply with a quotation.

**2. What does the abbreviation E. & O.E. printed on a commercial document stand for?**
- A. Errors and Omissions Excepted
- B. Expenses and Overhead Estimated
- C. Early and On-time Expected
- D. Exports and Orders Executed

**Answer: A.** Errors and Omissions Excepted. It is printed on an invoice to protect the seller legally, allowing them to correct any accidental mistakes made when drawing up the document.

**3. A wholesaler sells a motorcycle at a list price of ₦400,000. He offers a trade discount of 15% to retailers and a cash discount of 5% for payment within 7 days. If a retailer pays on the third day, how much will the retailer pay?**
- A. ₦320,000
- B. ₦323,000
- C. ₦340,000
- D. ₦380,000

**Answer: B.** 
Step 1: Calculate trade discount (15% of ₦400,000 = ₦60,000). 
Step 2: Find net price (₦400,000 - ₦60,000 = ₦340,000). 
Step 3: Calculate cash discount on the net price (5% of ₦340,000 = ₦17,000). 
Step 4: Subtract cash discount (₦340,000 - ₦17,000 = ₦323,000). Option A is wrong because it incorrectly adds the percentages together (20% of 400,000 = 80,000).

**4. Which of the following is a document of title used in international trade?**
- A. Consular invoice
- B. Bill of lading
- C. Consignment note
- D. Statement of accounts

**Answer: B.** A bill of lading is a document of title. Whoever legally holds it is the owner of the goods and can claim them at the seaport.

**5. A major disadvantage of selling goods on credit to the seller is the**
- A. increase in the volume of sales
- B. risk of bad debts
- C. improvement in the buyer's standard of living
- D. reduction in warehouse storage space

**Answer: B.** When a seller gives goods on credit, they take the risk that the buyer might fail to pay the money owed, leading to bad debts and financial loss.

**6. An importer bought goods from Japan. The F.O.B. price of the goods is ₦8,200,000, the ocean freight is ₦600,000, and marine insurance is ₦300,000. What is the C.I.F. price?**
- A. ₦8,200,000
- B. ₦8,800,000
- C. ₦8,500,000
- D. ₦9,100,000

**Answer: D.** The formula is C.I.F. = Cost (F.O.B.) + Insurance + Freight. 
Step 1: Add the figures (8,200,000 + 300,000 + 600,000 = 9,100,000). The C.I.F. price is ₦9,100,000. Option A is the F.O.B. price, and B and C are missing one of the additions.

**7. Which means of payment is an unconditional order in writing, addressed by one person to another, requiring the person to pay a certain sum of money on demand?**
- A. Promissory note
- B. Bill of exchange
- C. Postal order
- D. Bank draft

**Answer: B.** This is the exact legal definition of a bill of exchange. A promissory note (Option A) is a promise, not an order.

**8. The main purpose of a proforma invoice is to**
- A. demand payment for goods that have been bought on credit for a month
- B. serve as a quotation or a demand for payment before goods are supplied
- C. correct an undercharge on a previous invoice
- D. summarise all transactions between a buyer and seller at the end of a month

**Answer: B.** A proforma invoice shows what a real invoice will look like. It is used as a formal quotation, to demand advance payment, or to help goods clear customs.

**9. In which of the following forms of credit does the buyer not become the legal owner of the goods until the final installment is paid?**
- A. Deferred payment
- B. Trade credit
- C. Hire purchase
- D. Bank overdraft

**Answer: C.** In hire purchase, the buyer is only hiring the goods while paying installments. Ownership legally remains with the seller until the very last payment is made. 

**10. The fundamental difference between cash and credit transactions is the**
- A. time when the buyer takes possession of the goods
- B. time when payment is made for the goods
- C. type of goods that can be bought
- D. document used to order the goods

**Answer: B.** In both transactions, the buyer usually takes possession of the goods immediately. The difference is that in a cash transaction, payment is made instantly (or very promptly), whereas in a credit transaction, payment is delayed to a future date.
