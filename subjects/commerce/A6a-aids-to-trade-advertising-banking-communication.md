## 1. Advertising

### Meaning and role of advertising

**Objective i: explain the meaning and role of advertising.**

**Advertising** is the non-personal, paid form of communication used by an identified sponsor to promote ideas, goods, or services to a target audience. It is an aid to trade because it bridges the information gap between producers and consumers, making the consumers aware that a product exists.

Key elements of this definition:
- **Non-personal:** It is directed at a mass audience, not an individual face-to-face interaction (unlike personal selling).
- **Paid for:** The sponsor (business) must pay for the space or time used (unlike publicity or public relations, which is often free news coverage).
- **Identified sponsor:** The brand or company paying for the advert is always known (e.g., Indomie, MTN, Peak Milk).

**Roles of advertising in Commerce:**
1. **Creating Awareness:** It informs the public about the existence of new goods and services in the market.
2. **Boosting Sales (Demand Creation):** By highlighting the benefits of a product, advertising persuades consumers to buy, thereby increasing sales and turnover for the business.
3. **Educating Consumers:** It teaches consumers how to use a product, its features, and safety measures.
4. **Maintaining Market Share:** Continuous advertising keeps the product in the minds of consumers, preventing them from switching to competitors.
5. **Brand Image Building:** It helps to build a positive image and goodwill for the company.
6. **Information on Quality and Prices:** It helps consumers compare prices, quality, and features of different products without having to visit multiple shops.

### Types of advertising, its media and methods

**Objective ii: identify the different types of advertising, its media and methods.**

#### Types of advertising

Advertising can be categorized based on its purpose:

| Type of Advertising | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Informative Advertising** | Designed purely to provide facts, announce new products, or explain how something works without heavily pushing for a sale. | Government adverts on voters' registration; a company announcing a change in its factory location. |
| **Persuasive Advertising** | Aimed at convincing consumers to buy a specific brand by appealing to their emotions, desires, or status. | A soft drink advert showing young, happy people having fun at a beach, suggesting the drink brings happiness. |
| **Competitive Advertising** | Used to show that one brand is superior to rival brands. It focuses on the unique selling points (USP) that beat the competition. | An advert comparing the washing power of "Omo" directly against other unnamed detergents. |
| **Mass / Generic Advertising** | Promotes a general class of products or a whole industry rather than a specific brand. Usually paid for by trade associations. | "Drink Milk for Strong Bones" (promotes all milk, not just Peak or Dano) or "Eat Nigerian Rice." |
| **Specific / Direct Advertising** | Focuses directly on a particular product or brand manufactured by a specific company. | An advert specifically for "Toyota Corolla 2024 model." |

#### Advertising Media

The **medium** (plural: media) is the channel or vehicle through which the advertising message is carried to the public.

1. **Print Media:** Includes newspapers (e.g., *The Punch*, *Vanguard*) and magazines. They are good for detailed information, but readers must be literate.
2. **Broadcast (Electronic) Media:** Radio and Television.
   - **Radio:** Covers a wide geographical area, reaches the grassroots, and can use local Nigerian languages (e.g., Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo), but lacks visual appeal.
   - **Television:** Combines sight, sound, and motion, making it highly persuasive and memorable, but it is very expensive.
3. **Digital/Internet Media:** Social media (Facebook, Instagram, X/Twitter), search engines, and websites. It is highly targeted and cost-effective, allowing interaction.
4. **Outdoor (Hoarding) Media:** Billboards, neon signs, posters, and transit adverts (on BRT buses or danfos). They are excellent for local awareness but offer limited space for text.
5. **Direct Mail:** Sending promotional letters, catalogues, or leaflets directly to a targeted list of potential customers.

#### Advertising Methods

Advertising methods refer to the strategic approach or appeal used to deliver the message:
- **Direct Method:** Targets a specific group of people who are likely to buy the product. For instance, sending a catalogue of medical equipment specifically to doctors.
- **Indirect Method:** Broadcasts the message to the general public, hoping that the potential buyers within the crowd will take notice. E.g., a billboard on the Third Mainland Bridge in Lagos.
- **Emotional Appeal:** Uses feelings like fear, love, or pride (e.g., an insurance advert showing a protected family).
- **Rational Appeal:** Focuses on logic, cost savings, and practical benefits (e.g., a car advert highlighting fuel efficiency).
- **Endorsement:** Using celebrities or experts (e.g., a popular Nigerian musician promoting a smartphone).

### Advantages and disadvantages of advertising

**Objective iii: analyse the advantages and disadvantages of advertising.**

#### Advantages of advertising

1. **Mass Production and Economies of Scale:** By increasing demand, businesses can produce in large quantities, which lowers the cost per unit.
2. **Improves Standard of Living:** It introduces consumers to new and better products that make life easier.
3. **Funds the Media:** Revenue from adverts is the primary way newspapers, radio, and television stations survive, keeping the cost of news and entertainment low for the public.
4. **Generates Employment:** It creates jobs for advertising agencies, copywriters, models, graphic designers, and media house staff.
5. **Encourages Competition:** Advertising forces producers to improve the quality of their products and keep prices reasonable to win customers.

#### Disadvantages of advertising

1. **Increased Cost of Goods:** The massive cost of advertising campaigns is eventually added to the price of the product, meaning the consumer pays for it.
2. **Misleading or False Claims:** Some persuasive adverts exaggerate product capabilities, deceiving consumers into buying inferior or harmful goods.
3. **Creates False Wants:** It often manipulates people (especially children and teenagers) into desiring things they do not need or cannot afford, leading to wasteful spending.
4. **Promotes Monopolies:** Large companies with huge advertising budgets can dominate the market and squeeze out small businesses that cannot afford to advertise.
5. **Nuisance and Visual Pollution:** Unsolicited internet pop-ups, frequent TV commercial breaks, and too many billboards can be highly irritating to the public.

## 2. Banking

Banking is a crucial aid to trade that involves the safekeeping of money, lending of funds, and facilitation of payments, ensuring that businesses have the finance and mechanisms needed to operate smoothly.

### Types of bank

**Objective iv: categorize the different types of bank.**

Banks are categorized based on their functions and target customers:

1. **Central Bank:** The apex financial institution in a country, responsible for controlling and supervising the entire monetary and financial system. In Nigeria, this is the **Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)**. It does not deal with ordinary citizens; rather, it acts as the government's bank and the bankers' bank (a lender of last resort).
2. **Commercial Banks:** These are retail banks that deal directly with the general public and businesses. They accept deposits, grant loans, and facilitate everyday trade. Examples in Nigeria include First Bank, Zenith Bank, UBA, and GTBank.
3. **Merchant Banks:** Sometimes called acceptance houses or wholesale banks, they cater mainly to large corporate organizations and international trade. They handle large-scale financing, discounting bills of exchange, and advising companies on mergers and issuing shares. (Many Nigerian merchant banks have now merged with or transitioned into commercial banking under universal banking, but the category remains distinct in syllabus theory).
4. **Development Banks:** Specialized banks established by the government to provide long-term loans and expert advice for specific sectors of the economy (e.g., agriculture, industry). Examples: **Bank of Industry (BOI)**, **Bank of Agriculture (BOA)**.
5. **Mortgage Banks:** Financial institutions that provide long-term housing and real estate loans (mortgages) to individuals and property developers. Example: Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria (FMBN).
6. **Microfinance Banks:** Designed to provide small loans (microcredit) and basic financial services to low-income individuals, market women, and small-scale businesses who lack the collateral required by commercial banks.

### Services rendered by banks

**Objective v: assess the services rendered by banks.**

The services below are primarily rendered by **commercial banks**, as they are the ones most directly involved in aiding day-to-day trade:

1. **Acceptance of Deposits:** Banks provide a safe place for people to keep their money. They offer different types of accounts:
   - *Savings Account:* For individuals to save money and earn interest.
   - *Current Account:* Mainly for businesses; allows the use of cheques. It does not usually pay interest and banks charge a maintenance fee (COT/Account maintenance).
   - *Fixed/Time Deposit Account:* Money is locked away for a specific period at a higher interest rate.
2. **Granting of Credit (Lending):**
   - *Loans:* A specific sum is borrowed and credited to the customer's account, to be repaid with interest over an agreed period.
   - *Overdrafts:* A facility on a **current account** that allows a business to withdraw more money than they actually have in their account, up to an agreed limit. Interest is charged only on the overdrawn amount.
3. **Facilitation of Payments:** Banks transfer money safely on behalf of customers through cheques, standing orders (automatic regular payments of fixed amounts), and direct debits (automatic payments of varying amounts, like utility bills).
4. **Provision of Foreign Exchange:** Banks assist importers and exporters by selling and buying foreign currencies and issuing Letters of Credit (a guarantee that a seller will be paid).
5. **Safekeeping of Valuables:** Providing strong rooms and safe deposit boxes for customers to store important documents (wills, title deeds) and jewelry.
6. **Discounting Bills of Exchange:** A bank can buy a bill of exchange from a business before it is due for payment, giving the business immediate cash (minus a discount fee).
7. **Financial Advice and References:** Banks advise businesses on investments and act as referees to vouch for a customer’s creditworthiness to other businesses.

### Challenges facing banks

**Objective vi: identify the challenges facing banks.**

1. **Non-Performing Loans (Bad Debts):** Many customers default on their loans. High levels of unrecovered loans deplete the bank's capital and can lead to failure.
2. **Fraud and Cybercrime:** Banks suffer huge losses from internet hackers, ATM fraud, forged cheques, and insider fraud by dishonest employees.
3. **Inconsistent Government Policies:** Frequent changes in monetary policy (like the Cash Reserve Ratio or interest rates) by the Central Bank can disrupt banking operations and profitability.
4. **Infrastructural Deficits:** Poor power supply and unreliable telecommunication networks increase the operational costs for banks in Nigeria, as they must run diesel generators and private internet links.
5. **Fierce Competition:** Traditional banks face intense competition not only from other banks but from Fintech companies (like OPay, Moniepoint, and PalmPay) offering faster, cheaper services.
6. **High Rate of Inflation:** Inflation reduces the real value of money, discouraging savings and making long-term lending highly risky.

### E-banking

**Objective vii: explain e-banking.**

**E-banking** (Electronic Banking) refers to the use of electronic systems, telecommunication networks, and the internet to provide banking services, allowing customers to access their accounts and perform transactions without visiting a physical bank branch.

**Key types of E-banking:**
- **ATMs (Automated Teller Machines):** Machines that allow customers to withdraw cash, check balances, and transfer funds 24/7 using a debit card.
- **POS (Point of Sale) Terminals:** Devices used at retail shops (and by mobile money agents) to process card payments directly from the customer's account to the merchant's account.
- **Mobile Banking (USSD and Apps):** Banking done via mobile phones. This includes using smartphone applications or USSD codes (e.g., `*894#`, `*737#`) to transfer money, buy airtime, or pay bills.
- **Internet/Online Banking:** Accessing bank accounts through a secure bank website using a personal computer or smartphone.

**Advantages of E-banking:**
- **Convenience:** Banking can be done anywhere, anytime (24/7).
- **Speed:** Transactions (like fund transfers) are processed instantly.
- **Reduces Bank Crowds:** It decongests banking halls, saving time for both staff and customers.
- **Global Access:** Customers can monitor their accounts and make international payments from anywhere in the world.

**Disadvantages of E-banking:**
- **Cybercrime Risks:** Customers are vulnerable to phishing scams, identity theft, and hacking.
- **Network Failures:** Poor internet connectivity can cause delayed or failed transactions.
- **Digital Divide:** It excludes people who are not technologically literate or who do not have access to smartphones or the internet (especially in rural areas).

## 3. Communication

Communication in business is the continuous process of transmitting and receiving information, ideas, or instructions between individuals or organizations to achieve business goals.

### Stages in the communication process

**Objective viii: assess the different stages in the communication process.**

Communication is not just talking; it is a step-by-step cycle that is only complete when the receiver understands the message and responds.

**The 7 Stages of the Communication Process:**
1. **The Sender (Source):** The person or organization that has an idea or information they want to share.
2. **Encoding:** The sender translates the idea into a communicable form (words, symbols, pictures, or gestures).
3. **The Message:** The actual physical product of the encoding process (e.g., a written letter, a spoken sentence, a chart).
4. **The Channel (Medium):** The pathway or vehicle used to transmit the message (e.g., telephone, email, face-to-face, courier).
5. **The Receiver:** The person to whom the message is directed.
6. **Decoding:** The receiver interprets and translates the message to understand its meaning. (If decoding fails due to big grammar or poor signal, communication fails).
7. **Feedback:** The receiver's reaction or response sent back to the sender. This tells the sender that the message was received and correctly understood, completing the loop.

### Types of communication

**Objective ix: analyse the types of communication.**

Communication can be broadly classified by the medium used:

1. **Oral (Verbal) Communication:** Exchanging information by spoken word.
   - *Examples:* Face-to-face meetings, telephone calls, intercoms, interviews, and conferences.
   - *Pros:* Instant feedback, allows for tone of voice, builds personal relationships.
   - *Cons:* No permanent record (unless recorded), easily forgotten, unsuitable for long legal documents.
2. **Written Communication:** Exchanging information using written or printed symbols.
   - *Examples:* Business letters, emails, memorandums (memos), reports, notices, and invoices.
   - *Pros:* Provides a permanent legal record, allows time for careful drafting, good for complex information.
   - *Cons:* Slow, delayed feedback, lacks personal touch, requires literacy.
3. **Non-verbal Communication:** Conveying messages without words.
   - *Examples:* Body language, facial expressions, gestures, eye contact. Often accompanies oral communication to show true feelings.
4. **Visual Communication:** Using visual aids to transmit information quickly.
   - *Examples:* Graphs, pie charts, organizational charts, road signs, and company logos.

Communication can also be classified by direction within an organization:
- **Internal:** Communication within the business (e.g., between a manager and an employee).
- **External:** Communication between the business and the outside world (e.g., customers, suppliers, government).

### Contributions of courier services, GSM, etc. (Trends)

**Objective x: appraise the contributions of courier services, GSM, etc., to businesses.**

Modern trends have shifted business communication from slow post offices to instant digital and fast logistical networks.

#### GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications)
GSM operators (like MTN, Airtel, Globacom) have completely transformed Nigerian business communication.
- **Instant Connectivity:** Businessmen can contact suppliers and clients anywhere in the world instantly via calls or SMS.
- **Mobile Internet:** GSM providers supply data networks (4G/5G) enabling email, video conferencing (Zoom), and social media marketing.
- **Mobile Money and Banking:** GSM makes USSD banking possible, allowing rapid payments without needing cash or a physical bank.
- **Remote Work:** Employees can work from home or in the field while staying connected to the office network.

#### Courier Services
Courier services (like DHL, FedEx, UPS, NIPOST EMS, and local bike dispatch riders) are private firms specializing in the fast, secure, and tracked delivery of letters and parcels.
- **Speed and Reliability:** They deliver crucial business documents (like contracts and bills of lading) much faster than standard postal services.
- **Security:** They guarantee the safety of sensitive parcels and offer compensation if lost.
- **Door-to-door Delivery:** They pick up from the sender's office and deliver directly to the receiver's desk.
- **Tracking:** Businesses can track their shipments in real-time online, improving inventory planning.
- **E-commerce Support:** The boom in online shopping in Nigeria relies heavily on courier dispatch riders for last-mile delivery to customers.

### Merits and demerits of communication

**Objective xi: state the merits and demerits of communication.**

| Merits (Advantages) of Communication | Demerits (Disadvantages) of Communication |
| --- | --- |
| **Speeds up decision making:** Quick information transfer allows managers to solve problems faster. | **Costly:** Installing internet networks, PBX phones, and paying for courier services or airtime can be very expensive. |
| **Expands market reach:** Through the internet and phones, a business in Aba can sell to customers in Kano or London. | **Information Overload:** Too many emails, calls, and messages can overwhelm staff and reduce productivity. |
| **Improves coordination:** Helps different departments in a company work together efficiently. | **Technology Breakdown:** Power outages, server crashes, or bad network (common in Nigeria) can halt business activities. |
| **Creates permanent records:** Written communication acts as evidence in legal disputes. | **Security Risks:** Electronic communication is vulnerable to hacking, leaks of trade secrets, and cyber fraud. |
| **Boosts morale:** Good internal communication makes employees feel valued and understood. | **Misinterpretation:** Poorly written emails or lack of tone can lead to severe misunderstandings and conflict. |

### Barriers to communication

**Objective xii: outline the barriers to communication.**

Barriers (or "noise") are obstacles that prevent a message from being received or understood correctly.

1. **Language and Semantic Barriers:** Arises when the sender uses vocabulary or jargon the receiver does not understand, or if they speak different languages (e.g., an engineer using technical terms to explain a problem to a non-technical customer).
2. **Physical / Noise Barriers:** Environmental disturbances that interfere with the message. Examples include a loud generator blocking out a conversation, poor telephone network static, or a badly printed document.
3. **Psychological Barriers:** The emotional state of the receiver (e.g., anger, prejudice, distrust) can cause them to ignore or misinterpret the message. If a worker dislikes their boss, they might assume a neutral memo is an attack.
4. **Cultural Barriers:** Different cultures have different norms regarding communication, body language, and respect. A gesture considered polite in one culture might be highly offensive in another.
5. **Information Overload:** Giving the receiver too much information at once causes them to lose focus and forget key points.
6. **Poor Listening Skills:** Often, receivers are just waiting for their turn to speak rather than actively paying attention to what is being said.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Publicity vs. Advertising:** Remember that advertising is *always paid for* by an identified sponsor. Publicity (like a news story about a company) is not directly paid for.
- **Commercial vs. Central Banks:** Do not confuse their roles. Commercial banks deal with the public; Central banks *do not*. The Central Bank acts as the "lender of last resort" to commercial banks.
- **Overdrafts:** Only available to *current account* holders, not savings account holders.
- **Generic Advertising:** Also called mass advertising; it promotes a class of products (e.g., "Eat Apples") without naming a specific brand.
- **Communication Process Order:** Memorize the exact order: Sender -> Encoding -> Message -> Channel -> Receiver -> Decoding -> Feedback. JAMB often asks what comes before or after a specific step.
- **Semantics:** If a question asks about communication failure due to "big grammar" or technical terms, the answer is a *semantic* barrier.

## Quick summary

1. Advertising creates awareness, boosts sales, and educates consumers, but it can be costly and sometimes misleading. Its types include informative, persuasive, competitive, and generic.
2. Banks are vital to trade. Types include Central, Commercial, Merchant, Development, and Mortgage banks.
3. Commercial banks offer services like safekeeping deposits, granting loans and overdrafts, and facilitating payments, but face challenges like bad debts, competition, and cyber fraud.
4. E-banking (ATMs, POS, Mobile, Internet) provides 24/7 convenience and speed but carries risks of network failure, the digital divide, and cybercrime.

6. GSM and courier services have revolutionized business by enabling instant global contact and trackable door-to-door delivery, though communication can face semantic, physical, psychological, and cultural barriers.

## Practice questions

**1. The primary purpose of competitive advertising is to:**

- A. inform the public about a new government policy
- B. promote a general class of products
- C. persuade consumers that a specific brand is superior to rival brands
- D. educate consumers on how to safely use a product

**Answer: C.** Competitive advertising focuses on the unique selling points that make a specific brand better than its competitors. Option B refers to generic advertising.

**2. Which of the following is a disadvantage of advertising to the consumer?**

- A. It creates mass production
- B. It may create false wants and encourage wasteful spending
- C. It improves the quality of products
- D. It provides funding for media houses

**Answer: B.** A major criticism of advertising is that it uses psychological persuasion to make people buy things they do not need or cannot afford, unlike the other options which are either advantages or do not negatively impact the consumer.

**3. The banking facility that allows a customer to draw more money than they actually have in their account is known as:**

- A. a loan
- B. a fixed deposit
- C. discounting a bill
- D. an overdraft

**Answer: D.** An overdraft is a facility granted on a current account allowing the customer to overdraw their balance up to an agreed limit. Option A is a fixed borrowed sum, while C is a method of raising early cash on trade bills.

**4. Which type of bank is specifically established to provide long-term capital for agriculture and industry?**

- A. Central bank
- B. Development bank
- C. Commercial bank
- D. Merchant bank

**Answer: B.** Development banks (like the Bank of Industry or Bank of Agriculture) are specialized government-backed banks that provide long-term funding for specific sectors. Commercial banks (Option C) generally provide short-to-medium-term loans.

**5. In the communication process, the step where the receiver interprets the message to understand its meaning is called:**

- A. encoding
- B. feedback
- C. decoding
- D. channeling

**Answer: C.** Decoding is the translation and interpretation of the message by the receiver. Encoding (Option A) is done by the sender before the message is transmitted.

**6. A major advantage of written communication over oral communication in business is that it:**

- A. provides instant feedback
- B. allows for the observation of body language
- C. provides a permanent legal record
- D. is generally faster

**Answer: C.** Written documents (letters, memos, contracts) can be filed away and used as proof in the future, unlike spoken words which leave no permanent record. Options A, B, and D are advantages of oral communication.

**7. Which of the following barriers to communication occurs when a manager uses highly technical jargon to explain a concept to a factory worker?**

- A. Physical barrier
- B. Semantic barrier
- C. Psychological barrier
- D. Cultural barrier

**Answer: B.** Semantic (or language) barriers arise when words, vocabulary, or jargon are not understood by the receiver. Option A would involve environmental noise like a loud machine.

**8. E-banking has greatly improved commercial activities, but it faces a major challenge in Nigeria due to:**

- A. the reduction of bank branches
- B. cybercrime and network failures
- C. the printing of too much paper money
- D. the high interest rates on loans

**Answer: B.** The primary disadvantages and challenges of e-banking are security risks (fraud/hacking) and infrastructural issues like poor internet network connectivity, whereas Option D affects lending generally.

**9. Courier services are highly preferred by businesses for sending important documents because they offer:**

- A. cheap, mass-market delivery
- B. verbal communication methods
- C. slow, careful handling
- D. speed, security, and trackability

**Answer: D.** Courier companies specialize in the rapid and secure door-to-door delivery of parcels, offering tracking services that regular postal services usually lack. Option A better describes ordinary post.

**10. "Drink more milk for strong bones." This statement, sponsored by the Dairy Farmers Association without mentioning a specific brand, is an example of:**

- A. persuasive advertising
- B. direct advertising
- C. generic advertising
- D. competitive advertising

**Answer: C.** Generic (or mass) advertising promotes a general class of products or an entire industry, rather than a specific company's brand. Option D would be used if one milk brand compared itself to another.
