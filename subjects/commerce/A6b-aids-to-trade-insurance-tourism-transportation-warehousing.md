## Insurance

Insurance is an arrangement in which a company (the insurer) provides a guarantee of compensation for a specified loss, damage, illness, or death in return for payment of a specified premium. It works on the concept of **pooling of risks**, where many people contribute a little money to a common fund, which is then used to compensate the unfortunate few who actually suffer a loss.

### Types of risk in insurance

Not every danger or uncertainty can be insured against. In commerce, risks are classified into different categories to determine if an insurance company will accept them.

**1. Pure vs. Speculative Risks**
- **Pure Risk:** A situation that holds only two possibilities: either a loss occurs or no loss occurs. There is absolutely no chance of making a profit. For example, the risk of a factory catching fire is a pure risk (either it burns and you lose, or it doesn't burn and things remain normal). Pure risks are generally **insurable**.
- **Speculative Risk:** A situation where there is a chance of a loss, no loss, **or a profit/gain**. Examples include starting a new business, buying shares on the stock exchange, or gambling. Because individuals choose to take these risks hoping for a profit, they are **non-insurable**.

**2. Fundamental vs. Particular Risks**
- **Fundamental Risk:** A risk that arises from broad economic, social, or natural causes and affects a large group of people or society as a whole. Examples include earthquakes, wars, national inflation, and widespread floods. Because the damage is so massive and widespread, they are generally **non-insurable** by private companies (though governments sometimes provide relief).
- **Particular Risk:** A risk that arises from individual events and only affects a specific person or a localized group. Examples include a car accident, a house burglary, or a localized shop fire. These are generally **insurable**.

#### Insurable vs. Non-insurable risks

| Feature | Insurable Risks | Non-insurable Risks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | Risks that meet the criteria of insurance companies (they can be calculated and compensated). | Risks that insurance companies will not cover because they cannot be statistically measured or carry a chance of profit. |
| **Examples** | Fire, burglary, motor accidents, death, marine perils. | Gambling, poor business management, loss of profit due to competition, wars, earthquakes. |
| **Nature of Risk** | Usually **Pure** and **Particular** risks. | Usually **Speculative** or **Fundamental** risks. |
| **Statistics** | Past records exist, allowing the actuary to calculate the probability of the loss and set a fair premium. | No reliable past records exist to accurately predict the frequency or severity of the loss. |

### Types of insurance

Insurance is broadly divided into two main categories: **Life Assurance** and **General (Non-Life) Insurance**. 

**Note on terminology:** The term **Assurance** is traditionally used when the event being covered is a certainty (e.g., death—everyone will eventually die; it is only a question of *when*). **Insurance** is used when the event may or may not happen (e.g., fire, theft). 

#### 1. Life Assurance
This provides financial protection for the dependents of the insured in the event of death, or acts as a savings plan for old age. 
- **Whole Life Policy:** Premiums are paid throughout the insured's life, and the sum insured is only paid out to the beneficiaries (dependents) after the insured dies.
- **Endowment Policy:** A mixture of insurance and investment. The sum insured is paid out at a specified future date (e.g., at age 60) or upon death, whichever comes first.
- **Term Policy:** Covers the insured for a specific period (e.g., 10 years). If the insured dies within this period, the payout is made. If they survive the term, the policy expires with no payout.

#### 2. General (Non-Life) Insurance
This covers properties, liabilities, and other unforeseen events. The major types include:

- **Fire Insurance:** Protects business properties, factories, and homes against damage caused by fire, lightning, and sometimes explosions.
- **Motor Insurance:** Required by law for anyone driving on Nigerian roads. 
  - *Third-Party Motor Insurance:* Covers only the damage or injury caused by the insured vehicle to other people (third parties) and their property. It does not cover the insured's own car.
  - *Comprehensive Motor Insurance:* Covers damage to third parties as well as accidental damage, fire, or theft of the insured’s own vehicle.
- **Marine Insurance:** Vital for foreign trade. It covers ships (Hull insurance), the goods being transported (Cargo insurance), the shipping fee (Freight insurance), and damages the ship might cause to others (Shipowner’s Liability).
- **Accident and Liability Insurance:** Includes:
  - *Fidelity Guarantee:* Protects employers against financial loss caused by dishonest employees (e.g., cashiers embezzling funds).
  - *Employer’s Liability (Workmen’s Compensation):* Covers compensation payable to employees who get injured or die while performing their duties.
  - *Cash-in-Transit:* Covers loss of money while it is being moved from the business to the bank.
  - *Burglary and Theft:* Covers loss of property or stock due to forced entry.

### Principles of insurance applied to life situations

Insurance contracts are governed by fundamental legal principles. JAMB expects you to understand how these apply to real-life situations.

**1. Utmost Good Faith (*Uberrimae fidei*)**
Both the insured and the insurer must declare all material facts honestly before signing the contract. A material fact is any information that would influence the insurer's decision to accept the risk or determine the premium. 
- *Life situation:* If Mr. Ade has a terminal heart condition but hides it while filling out a life assurance form, he has breached Utmost Good Faith. If he dies from a heart attack, the insurance company can legally refuse to pay the claim.

**2. Insurable Interest**
You can only insure something if you will suffer a direct financial loss from its destruction, or benefit financially from its continued existence.
- *Life situation:* You can insure your own house, but you cannot insure your neighbour's house because if it burns down, you lose nothing financially. You have an insurable interest in your own life and your spouse's life, but not in a stranger's life.

**3. Indemnity**
Insurance is designed to restore you to the exact financial position you were in just before the loss occurred. It is a shield, not a tool for making a profit. **Note:** This principle does *not* apply to life assurance, because a human life cannot be valued in money.
- *Life situation:* If your ₦5 million car is stolen, the insurance company will pay you the current market value of the car (perhaps ₦3.5 million due to depreciation), not the price of a brand-new car, so you do not profit from the theft.

**4. Subrogation**
This is a follow-up to the principle of indemnity. Once an insurance company fully compensates you for a total loss, they take over all your legal rights and ownership of the destroyed property.
- *Life situation:* Step 1: Your car is completely wrecked in a crash caused by a careless truck driver. Step 2: Your insurer pays you full compensation. Step 3: The insurer takes ownership of the wrecked car (scrap metal) and gains the right to sue the truck driver. You cannot sell the scrap or sue the driver yourself to get double payment.

**5. Contribution**
If you insure the same property with two or more insurance companies, you cannot claim the full amount from all of them to make a profit. They will share (contribute to) the loss proportionately.
- *Life situation:* You insure a ₦100,000 warehouse with Company A for ₦100,000 and Company B for ₦100,000. If a fire causes ₦40,000 in damage, Company A pays ₦20,000 and Company B pays ₦20,000. You only get ₦40,000 in total.

**6. Proximate Cause (*Causa proxima*)**
For a claim to be paid, the active, direct, and most dominant cause of the loss must be one of the perils you are insured against.
- *Life situation:* You have an accident policy but not a life policy. While riding a horse, you suffer a heart attack, fall off, and die. The proximate cause of death was the heart attack (illness), not the fall (accident). The insurer will not pay.

### Terms in insurance

- **Premium:** The regular amount of money paid by the insured to the insurance company in exchange for cover.
- **Insured:** The person or business seeking protection against a risk.
- **Insurer:** The insurance company offering the protection.
- **Policy:** The legal document containing the terms, conditions, and details of the insurance contract.
- **Cover Note:** A temporary document given to the insured to serve as proof of insurance while the main policy document is being prepared.
- **Actuary:** A highly skilled mathematician who uses statistics and probability to calculate life expectancy, risks, and the correct premiums to charge.
- **Underwriter:** An official of the insurance company who assesses a risk, decides whether to accept it, and determines the terms.
- **Surrender Value:** The amount of money a life assurance policyholder will receive if they decide to cancel their endowment or whole-life policy before it matures.
- **Re-insurance:** A situation where an insurance company insures part of a huge risk it has taken with another, larger insurance company to protect itself from massive financial ruin (e.g., insuring a multi-billion-naira oil rig).
- **Average Clause:** A rule used in property insurance to penalize **under-insurance**. If you insure property for less than its true value, you are considered your own insurer for the uninsured balance, and any partial claim will be reduced proportionately.

#### Calculation: The Average Clause
Whenever property is under-insured and a partial loss occurs, the claim is calculated using this formula:
$\text{Claim} = \frac{\text{Sum Insured}}{\text{Actual Value}} \times \text{Loss}$

**Worked Example:**
A trader has a shop valued at ₦1,000,000. To save on premiums, he insured it against fire for only ₦600,000. A fire breaks out and causes damage worth ₦400,000. Calculate his compensation.
- **Step 1:** State the formula: $\text{Claim} = \frac{\text{Sum Insured}}{\text{Actual Value}} \times \text{Loss}$
- **Step 2:** Insert the figures: $\text{Claim} = \frac{600,000}{1,000,000} \times 400,000$
- **Step 3:** Simplify the fraction: $\frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5}$
- **Step 4:** Multiply by the loss: $\frac{3}{5} \times 400,000 = \text{₦}240,000$.
- **Answer:** The insurance company will pay him **₦240,000**. The trader bears the remaining ₦160,000 loss himself.

### Importance of insurance

1. **Provides security and peace of mind:** Business owners can operate without the constant fear that a single disaster will bankrupt them.
2. **Encourages international trade:** Exporters and importers confidently ship goods across risky oceans because marine insurance covers potential losses.
3. **Provision of funds for investment:** Insurance companies collect huge amounts of money in premiums (a pool of funds). They invest these funds into government bonds, real estate, and businesses, aiding national economic development.
4. **Ensures business continuity:** By providing compensation (indemnity), businesses can rebuild and resume operations quickly after a disaster.
5. **Loss prevention:** Insurers often require businesses to install safety equipment (like fire extinguishers or burglar alarms) before issuing a policy, which reduces the rate of accidents.

## Tourism

### Meaning and forms of tourism

**Tourism** refers to the temporary movement of people from their normal places of residence to other locations for leisure, business, health, religion, or education, and the activities they undertake during their stay. People who engage in this are called **tourists**.

**Forms of tourism include:**
- **Domestic tourism:** Travel within a person’s own country (e.g., a family leaving Lagos to visit Yankari National Park in Bauchi).
- **International tourism:** Travel across national borders (e.g., a Nigerian flying to Dubai or London).
- **Leisure tourism:** Travelling purely for relaxation, holidays, and sightseeing.
- **Business tourism:** Travelling for conferences, trade fairs, or business meetings.
- **Medical tourism:** Travelling to seek specialized medical care or surgeries not available or affordable at home.
- **Religious tourism:** Travelling for pilgrimages or religious gatherings (e.g., the Hajj in Mecca, or visiting major religious camps in Nigeria).
- **Eco-tourism:** Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the well-being of local people.

### Importance of tourism

1. **Generation of foreign exchange:** International tourists bring foreign currencies (dollars, pounds, euros) into the country, which strengthens the local currency and economy.
2. **Employment creation:** The industry creates direct and indirect jobs for tour guides, hotel staff, drivers, pilots, artisans, and entertainers.
3. **Infrastructural development:** To attract tourists, governments build and upgrade roads, airports, electricity, and water supply around tourist centres, which also benefits local residents.
4. **Cultural exchange and peace:** It fosters understanding between different cultures, reduces prejudices, and promotes global peace.
5. **Preservation of cultural heritage:** Tourism encourages the preservation of local arts, crafts, historical monuments, and wildlife.
6. **Diversification of the economy:** It provides an alternative source of government revenue, reducing Nigeria's heavy reliance on crude oil.

### Agencies that promote tourism in Nigeria

Several government and private agencies are tasked with regulating and promoting tourism:

1. **Nigerian Tourism Development Corporation (NTDC):** The chief agency responsible for planning, promoting, and regulating tourism in Nigeria. It handles the registration and classification of hospitality enterprises. 
   > **Note:** Following a recent legislative bill, the NTDC has been transitioned into the **Nigerian Tourism Development Authority (NTDA)** to give it more regulatory power. However, standard textbooks and JAMB past questions heavily use "NTDC". Know both, but prioritize NTDC if asked in an exam.
2. **National Institute for Hospitality and Tourism (NIHOTOUR):** A government parastatal responsible for providing training, education, and professional capacity-building for workers in the hospitality and tourism sector.
3. **State Tourism Boards:** Every state in Nigeria has a board or ministry (e.g., Lagos State Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture) responsible for developing and marketing tourist sites within their specific state.
4. **Private Agencies:** These include travel agencies, tour operators, and associations like the Federation of Tourism Associations of Nigeria (FTAN).

### Challenges facing tourism in Nigeria

Despite its huge potential, tourism in Nigeria struggles due to several factors:
1. **Insecurity:** Threats of terrorism, banditry, kidnapping, and communal clashes make tourists afraid to visit certain areas.
2. **Poor infrastructure:** Bad road networks, lack of rail connections to tourist sites, and epileptic power supply make travel frustrating and uncomfortable.
3. **Poor maintenance culture:** Many existing tourist centres are poorly maintained, leaving facilities dilapidated and unattractive.
4. **Inadequate funding and investment:** Both government and the private sector have not invested enough money into developing standard tourist resorts.
5. **Poor international image and publicity:** Negative media coverage of Nigeria discourages foreign tourists, and the government does not run enough global advertising campaigns.
6. **Difficult visa processes:** The difficulty and cost of obtaining a Nigerian tourist visa deter many international visitors.

### Tourist centres in Nigeria

JAMB frequently tests knowledge of the locations of major tourist attractions in Nigeria. Key sites include:
- **Yankari National Park (Bauchi State):** Famous for wildlife (elephants, baboons) and the Wikki Warm Springs.
- **Obudu Mountain Resort / Cattle Ranch (Cross River State):** Known for its temperate climate, cable cars, and beautiful mountain scenery.
- **Olumo Rock (Ogun State):** A massive outcrop of granite rocks with historical significance as a refuge for the Egba people during inter-tribal wars.
- **Ikogosi Warm Springs (Ekiti State):** A unique geographical site where a warm spring flows side-by-side with a cold spring and they meet at a confluence.
- **Erin-Ijesha (Olumirin) Waterfalls (Osun State):** A majestic seven-step waterfall popular for leisure.
- **Zuma Rock (Niger State):** A massive monolith featuring a "human face" located on the road from Abuja to Kaduna.
- **Ogbunike Caves (Anambra State):** A historic collection of interconnected caves used for religious and historical purposes.
- **Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove (Osun State):** A UNESCO World Heritage site featuring shrines and art deep in a forest along the Osun River.

## Transportation

Transportation is the movement of people, goods, and services from one place to another. It is the lifeblood of commerce, ensuring that production reaches the final consumer.

### Relevance of the various modes of transportation

The syllabus requires you to know the modes and appraise their relevance.

**1. Road Transport**
- **Mode:** Cars, buses, lorries, trailers, motorcycles.
- **Relevance:** It is the most flexible and widely used mode in Nigeria. It is highly relevant for moving passengers and goods over short to medium distances, and it is the only mode that can offer **door-to-door** service (picking goods from a factory and dropping them exactly at a warehouse).

**2. Rail Transport**
- **Mode:** Trains running on standard or narrow-gauge tracks (managed in Nigeria by the Nigerian Railway Corporation, NRC).
- **Relevance:** Highly relevant for transporting very heavy and bulky goods (like cement, coal, and agricultural produce) over long distances over land. It reduces the wear and tear on roads.

**3. Water Transport**
- **Mode:** Ships, boats, ferries, canoes, tankers. Divided into inland waterways (Rivers Niger and Benue) and ocean navigation (using ports like Apapa, Tin Can Island, and Onne).
- **Relevance:** The backbone of **foreign (international) trade**. It is the only cost-effective way to move massive quantities of heavy and bulky goods (like crude oil, imported cars, and containers of electronics) between continents.

**4. Air Transport**
- **Mode:** Aeroplanes and helicopters (using airports like Murtala Muhammed in Lagos or Nnamdi Azikiwe in Abuja).
- **Relevance:** Relevant for speed. It is used for transporting passengers, highly perishable goods (fresh flowers, vital medicines), or very high-value, low-weight goods (diamonds, urgent documents, gold).

**5. Pipeline Transport**
- **Mode:** Underground or surface pipes equipped with pumping stations.
- **Relevance:** Exclusively relevant for the continuous movement of liquids and gases, such as crude oil, refined petroleum, and natural gas, from drilling sites to refineries or seaports.

### Importance of transportation

1. **Bridging the geographical gap:** It moves goods from areas where they are produced to areas where they are needed by consumers.
2. **Prevents wastage:** Fast transportation (like air or refrigerated trucks) ensures that perishable agricultural goods reach the market before they spoil.
3. **Encourages large-scale production:** By widening the market (connecting a factory to the whole country or the world), it allows manufacturers to produce in massive quantities.
4. **Price stabilization:** It prevents localized scarcities by moving goods from areas of surplus (where prices are low) to areas of scarcity (where prices are high), balancing out the prices.
5. **Facilitates mobility of labour:** It allows workers to commute to factories and offices daily.

### Advantages and disadvantages of transportation

#### Land: Road vs. Rail
| Feature | Road Transport | Rail Transport |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | - Highly flexible; offers door-to-door delivery.<br>- Fast and convenient over short distances.<br>- Cheaper to set up compared to rail.<br>- Route can easily be changed if there is a blockage. | - Best for bulky and extremely heavy goods over long distances.<br>- Not affected by traffic jams.<br>- Cheaper per unit for bulk haulage.<br>- Safer; lower risk of fatal accidents compared to roads. |
| **Disadvantages** | - Limited carrying capacity per vehicle.<br>- Highly susceptible to traffic congestion.<br>- Higher risk of accidents and armed robbery.<br>- Heavy vehicles damage roads over time. | - Highly inflexible; limited strictly to tracks.<br>- Cannot offer door-to-door delivery; requires road transport to finish the journey.<br>- Huge capital needed to lay tracks and buy trains.<br>- Often runs strictly on fixed timetables. |

#### Water vs. Air
| Feature | Water Transport (Ocean) | Air Transport |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | - The cheapest mode for moving goods internationally.<br>- Unlimited carrying capacity (massive cargo ships).<br>- Best for bulky, heavy items (cars, machinery).<br>- Safe route (the ocean has no need for physical tracks). | - The fastest mode of transportation.<br>- Ideal for urgent, perishable, and high-value goods.<br>- Operates on a straight line without geographical barriers like mountains.<br>- High level of safety and security for cargo. |
| **Disadvantages** | - Extremely slow; not suitable for urgent or perishable goods.<br>- Subject to weather hazards (storms, typhoons).<br>- Requires specialized ports which are expensive to build.<br>- Risk of piracy in some ocean routes. | - The most expensive mode of transport.<br>- Severely limited carrying capacity for bulky goods.<br>- Highly dependent on weather; flights are grounded in bad conditions.<br>- Requires road transport to link airports to final destinations. |

#### Pipeline Transport
- **Advantages:** Very low operating costs once built; operates continuously 24/7; reduces the number of heavy fuel tankers on roads; extremely safe for the public.
- **Disadvantages:** Highly inflexible (only moves liquids/gases); huge initial construction cost; vulnerable to sabotage and vandalism (pipeline vandalism is a major issue in the Niger Delta); hard to detect leaks immediately.

## Warehousing

Warehousing is the act of storing goods in a safe place from the time they are produced or imported until they are demanded by consumers. A warehouse is the physical building where this storage takes place.

### Importance of warehousing

At a macroeconomic level, warehousing plays critical roles:
1. **Stabilization of prices:** By storing goods when there is a surplus (harvest season) and slowly releasing them during periods of scarcity, warehousing prevents extreme price fluctuations.
2. **Continuous production:** It holds raw materials in wait, ensuring that factories do not have to halt production while waiting for supplies.
3. **Protection of goods:** It protects goods from physical deterioration caused by weather (sun, rain) and guards against theft and pests.
4. **Provision for seasonal goods:** Agricultural products like yam and maize are produced seasonally but demanded all year round. Warehousing bridges this time gap.

### Types and functions of warehousing

**Types of warehouses:**
1. **Ordinary / Wholesale Warehouse:** Owned and operated by wholesalers to store goods bought in bulk from manufacturers before selling them to retailers.
2. **Manufacturer’s Warehouse:** Owned by producers (like Dangote or Nigerian Breweries) to store finished goods pending dispatch, and to stockpile raw materials.
3. **Public Warehouse:** Owned by individuals, private companies, or the state, and rented out to anyone (merchants, importers) who needs storage space for a fee.
4. **State / Government Warehouse:** Owned by government agencies to store properties and supplies of the state, or to hold relief materials for emergencies (like NEMA warehouses).
5. **Bonded Warehouse:** A highly secure warehouse controlled by the **Customs Authority**. It is used to store imported goods for which import duties have not yet been paid. The importer cannot remove the goods until the duty is fully settled.

| Feature | Ordinary Warehouse | Bonded Warehouse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Location** | Anywhere close to markets or factories. | Usually located at seaports, airports, or borders. |
| **Control** | Controlled by the private owner (wholesaler/producer). | Strictly supervised by Customs officers. |
| **Duties** | Goods stored here have no pending customs duties. | Goods stored here are awaiting payment of import duties. |

**Functions of warehousing:**
- **Storage:** The primary function is keeping goods safe.
- **Break-bulk and packaging:** Large consignments are divided into smaller, manageable units, and then graded, branded, and packaged for final sale.
- **Processing:** Some goods require storage to improve their quality. For example, wine is left in warehouses to mature, and tobacco or timber is stored to cure/dry.

### Contributions of warehouses to businesses

At the microeconomic level, warehousing directly aids businesses (Objectives xxvii) by:
1. **Facilitating bank loans (Financing):** Goods stored in a public or bonded warehouse are issued a **Warehouse Warrant**. This document acts as a title to the goods and can be used by the business owner as collateral to secure a bank loan.
2. **Allowing mass production:** Businesses can produce goods in large quantities to enjoy economies of scale, knowing they have space to store the excess until demand catches up.
3. **Reducing business risks:** Safe storage reduces the risk of loss due to spoilage, theft, and fire (and goods in warehouses are easily insured).
4. **Preparing goods for sale:** The warehouse serves as a processing centre where a business can sort, grade, and package goods directly before dispatching to retailers.

### Factors that determine the siting of warehouses

When deciding where to build or rent a warehouse, businesses must evaluate several factors:
1. **Nearness to the factory / producer:** Manufacturer's warehouses should be close to the production plant to minimize the cost of moving finished goods off the production line.
2. **Nearness to the market / consumers:** Wholesale warehouses should be close to retail centres to ensure quick delivery of goods to consumers.
3. **Availability of transport and communication networks:** A warehouse must be sited where there are good roads, railway links, or near a port, allowing trucks to load and unload goods easily.
4. **Cost and availability of land:** Large warehouses require vast plots of land. Businesses often site them on the outskirts of cities where land is cheaper, rather than in expensive city centres.
5. **Availability of regular power and water:** Especially important for cold-storage warehouses (for meat, fish, medicines) which require uninterrupted electricity to run freezers.
6. **Security:** The location must have a low crime rate and support security infrastructure to prevent burglary.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Insurance vs. Assurance:** Remember that *insurance* covers possibilities (like fire), while *assurance* covers certainties (like death).
- **Indemnity does not apply to life:** You cannot value a human life. If someone dies, the exact sum insured is paid out; they cannot be "restored" to their previous state.
- **Speculative vs. Pure Risk:** A common mistake is confusing these. Speculative risks (gambling, starting a business) can yield a profit, so they are **not insurable**. Pure risks only offer loss or no loss.
- **Subrogation vs. Contribution:** Subrogation is about the insurer taking the scrap after a total loss. Contribution is about sharing claims among multiple insurers.
- **The Average Clause:** Be ready for simple calculations. Formula: `(Sum Insured ÷ Actual Value) × Loss`.
- **Modes of Transport:** Don't just list them; know their strict limits. Water is the *cheapest* for international bulk, Air is the *fastest* but most expensive, Pipeline is *only* for liquids/gases.
- **Bonded Warehouses:** These are explicitly tied to **imported goods and unpaid customs duties**. If a question mentions customs, the answer is usually a bonded warehouse.

## Quick summary

1. Insurance works on the pooling of risks; pure and particular risks are insurable, while speculative and fundamental risks are generally uninsurable.
2. The core principles of insurance include Utmost Good Faith, Insurable Interest, Indemnity, Subrogation, Contribution, and Proximate Cause.
3. Tourism involves travel for leisure, business, or health; it provides foreign exchange, creates jobs, and is promoted in Nigeria by agencies like the NTDC and NIHOTOUR.
4. Major Nigerian tourist centres include Yankari (Bauchi), Obudu (Cross River), and Olumo Rock (Ogun).
5. Transportation bridges the gap between producers and consumers. Road offers flexibility, rail is for heavy inland bulk, ocean is for international bulk, and air is for urgent, high-value goods.
6. Warehousing stabilizes prices and protects goods. A bonded warehouse holds imports until duties are paid, while public and ordinary warehouses offer storage to merchants.
7. Warehousing aids businesses heavily by providing warehouse warrants which can be used as collateral to secure bank loans.

## Practice questions

**1. A businessman insured his factory valued at ₦2,000,000 for only ₦1,500,000 against fire. A fire outbreak caused a partial loss of ₦800,000. Under the average clause, how much compensation will he receive?**
- A. ₦800,000
- B. ₦600,000
- C. ₦1,500,000
- D. ₦2,000,000

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** 
Step 1: State the formula for the average clause: $\text{Claim} = \frac{\text{Sum Insured}}{\text{Actual Value}} \times \text{Loss}$.
Step 2: Substitute the values: $\text{Claim} = \frac{1,500,000}{2,000,000} \times 800,000$.
Step 3: Simplify the fraction: $\frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4}$.
Step 4: Multiply by the loss: $\frac{3}{4} \times 800,000 = \text{₦}600,000$.
Option A is incorrect because it ignores the average clause penalty for under-insuring.

**2. Which of the following risks is non-insurable because it involves the chance of making a profit?**
- A. Pure risk
- B. Particular risk
- C. Speculative risk
- D. Fundamental risk

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** Speculative risks involve a chance of loss, no loss, or a profit (e.g., gambling or starting a business). Insurers do not cover them. A common mistake is choosing fundamental risk; while also non-insurable, fundamental risk is uninsurable because of its massive scale, not because it offers a chance of profit.

**3. Mr. Audu insured his car comprehensively. Following a severe accident caused by a negligent truck driver, the insurance company paid Mr. Audu the full value of the car. The right of the insurance company to take over the wrecked car is based on the principle of**
- A. Utmost good faith
- B. Subrogation
- C. Contribution
- D. Insurable interest

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Subrogation gives the insurer the right to step into the shoes of the insured, take ownership of the remains, and sue the third party after paying full indemnity.

- A. State warehouse

**5. Which of the following modes of transportation is the most suitable and cost-effective for a Nigerian exporter sending 5,000 tonnes of cocoa to Europe?**
- A. Rail transport
- B. Road transport
- C. Air transport
- D. Water transport

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** Water (ocean) transport is the cheapest and most appropriate mode for carrying heavy, bulky goods over long, international distances. Air is too expensive and lacks the capacity, while rail and road cannot cross oceans.

**7. Which of the following documents can a businessman use as collateral to secure a loan from a commercial bank?**
- A. Insurance policy
- B. Cover note
- C. Warehouse warrant
- D. Customs declaration

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** A warehouse warrant is a document of title given to a depositor of goods in a warehouse. Because it proves ownership of the stored goods, banks accept it as collateral for loans.

**8. The primary difference between life assurance and property insurance is that in life assurance,**
- A. the principle of utmost good faith does not apply
- B. the principle of indemnity does not apply
- C. premiums are paid only once
- D. there is no need for insurable interest

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Indemnity means restoring the exact financial loss. Since a human life cannot be valued in terms of money, life assurance pays out a predetermined sum, not a calculated "replacement value." Hence, indemnity does not apply.

**10. A major disadvantage of pipeline transportation is that it is**
- A. highly prone to traffic congestion
- B. strictly limited to the movement of liquids and gases
- C. very expensive to maintain on a daily basis
- D. highly dependent on good weather conditions

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Pipelines lack flexibility; they can only transport fluid substances (liquids like crude oil, or gases). They do not suffer from traffic congestion (Option A) and their daily running costs are actually very low once built (Option C).
