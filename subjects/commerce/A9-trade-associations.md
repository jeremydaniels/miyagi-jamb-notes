## Objectives and functions of trade and manufacturer's associations

**Objective i: discuss the objectives and functions of trade and manufacturer’s associations.**
**Content i: Objectives and functions of trade and manufacturer’s associations (Cocoa Farmers’ Association, Garri Sellers’ Association, Poultry Farmers’ Association, etc.)**

In the business world, a single business owner acting alone can be weak when dealing with the government, large suppliers, or foreign competition. To gain strength, businesses carrying out the same type of trade or manufacturing often join together to form an association. 

### What is a Trade Association?

A **trade association** is a voluntary organisation formed by independent businesses, traders, or producers engaged in the **same line of trade or industry**, with the primary aim of protecting and promoting their common business interests. 

Trade associations can be formed by wholesalers, retailers, farmers, or service providers. Because they operate in the same industry, they understand the specific problems facing their trade. 

**Named Examples in the Syllabus:**
- **Cocoa Farmers' Association:** An association of cocoa growers (common in states like Ondo, Osun, and Cross River). They come together to negotiate better prices with international cocoa buyers, share modern farming techniques, and buy farming chemicals or fertilisers in bulk to distribute to members cheaply.
- **Garri Sellers' Association:** Found in local Nigerian markets (like Bodija in Ibadan or Ogbete in Enugu). Market women selling garri form this association to regulate the local price of garri, standardise the measuring cups (such as the "mudu" or "congo"), settle disputes over market stalls, and prevent outsiders from dumping cheap products and ruining their business.
- **Poultry Farmers’ Association of Nigeria (PAN):** An association of poultry farmers. They protect their members by lobbying the government to ban the importation of frozen chicken (which harms local farmers), organising the bulk purchase of poultry feed, and educating members on preventing diseases like avian influenza (bird flu).

### What is a Manufacturer's Association?

A **manufacturer's association** is a specific type of trade association strictly for companies involved in the manufacturing of goods. 

The most prominent example in Nigeria is the **Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN)**. It was established to protect the interests of local industrialists. MAN lobbies the federal government for favourable economic policies (such as tax rebates, uninterrupted electricity supply, and lower import duties on industrial raw materials) and provides a unified voice for Nigerian factories.

### Objectives and Functions of Trade and Manufacturer's Associations

Though they represent different specific industries, the core objectives and functions of all trade and manufacturer's associations are very similar:

1. **Protection of members' interests:** The primary objective is to act as a pressure group. They lobby the government to pass laws that favour their industry (e.g., higher tariffs on imported substitutes) or to remove harsh taxes. 
2. **Regulation of prices:** To prevent ruinous "cut-throat" competition among themselves, trade associations often agree on a minimum selling price for their goods. This ensures all members can make a reasonable profit.
3. **Maintenance of quality and standards:** They set ethical codes of conduct and quality standards for their products. For example, a garri sellers' association will ensure members do not mix bad garri with good ones, protecting the reputation of the trade.
4. **Provision of information and research:** They conduct market research and provide their members with valuable information about new production techniques, market trends, and changes in government regulations.
5. **Settlement of disputes:** If two members clash over a supplier, a market stall, or a customer, the association acts as a mediator to settle the dispute peacefully without going to court.
6. **Joint advertising and sales promotion:** Sometimes, they pool money together to advertise their general product (e.g., "Drink more milk") rather than advertising individual brands, which boosts overall sales for the entire industry.
7. **Credit and financial assistance:** They may form cooperative societies within the association to grant soft loans to members or stand as guarantors for members seeking bank loans.
8. **Uniform trade practices:** They ensure members adopt uniform terms of trade, such as standardising weights and measures, working hours, and credit policies to customers.

## Objectives and functions of Chambers of Commerce

**Objective ii: list the objectives and functions of Chambers of Commerce.**
**Content ii: Objectives and functions of Chambers of Commerce.**

While a trade association is restricted to a *single* trade or industry, a Chamber of Commerce brings together *all* types of businesses in a specific locality.

### What is a Chamber of Commerce?

A **Chamber of Commerce** is a voluntary association of businessmen, industrialists, financiers, professionals, and corporate bodies in a **particular town, city, or country**, formed to promote, protect, and develop the general commercial and industrial interests of that specific area.

There are three main types of Chambers of Commerce:
1. **City/Local Chambers:** Covering a specific city, such as the Lagos Chamber of Commerce and Industry (LCCI)—the oldest in Nigeria, founded in 1888—or the Kano Chamber of Commerce.
2. **National Chambers:** The umbrella body representing all local chambers in a country. In Nigeria, this is the **Nigerian Association of Chambers of Commerce, Industry, Mines and Agriculture (NACCIMA)**.
3. **Bilateral Chambers:** Formed to promote trade between two different countries, such as the Nigerian-British Chamber of Commerce or the Nigerian-American Chamber of Commerce.

### Objectives and Functions of Chambers of Commerce

Chambers of Commerce focus broadly on improving the economic life of their region. Their main objectives and functions include:

1. **Promoting home and foreign trade:** They actively look for ways to expand trade for their members locally and internationally.
2. **Organising trade fairs and exhibitions:** Chambers of Commerce are responsible for organising major commercial events. A famous example is the **Lagos International Trade Fair**, organised annually by the LCCI to showcase local and foreign products.
3. **Issuing Certificates of Origin:** This is a crucial function for international trade. A Chamber of Commerce issues a document called a Certificate of Origin to an exporter, proving to the foreign buyer's customs authority the exact country where the goods were manufactured.
4. **Advising the government:** They study government budgets, economic policies, and trade regulations, and provide expert advice to the government on how these policies will affect the general business community.
5. **Collecting and disseminating business statistics:** They gather vital economic data (like inflation rates, employment figures, and market sizes) and publish them in journals and bulletins for their members.
6. **Providing libraries and reading rooms:** They maintain reference libraries stocked with trade directories, government gazettes, and foreign trade publications for members to use.
7. **Arbitration of commercial disputes:** Like trade associations, they offer arbitration services to settle complex business disputes out of court, saving members time and money.
8. **Infrastructural development advocacy:** They pressure the local or state government to improve basic infrastructure (roads, water, electricity, security) in industrial areas, as this benefits all businesses.

### Comparison: Trade Association vs. Chamber of Commerce

| Feature | Trade Association | Chamber of Commerce |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Membership scope** | Members belong to the **same** trade or industry (e.g., all are poultry farmers). | Members come from **different** trades and industries (banks, farms, factories). |
| **Geographical scope** | Can be nationwide or local, but strictly limited to one profession. | Usually limited to a specific town, city, or region. |
| **Primary focus** | Protecting the specific interests of one trade against outsiders and the government. | Promoting the overall economic and commercial development of a locality. |
| **Certificates of Origin** | Does not issue Certificates of Origin. | Issues Certificates of Origin for foreign trade. |

## Forms of business combination and mergers

**Objective iii: Explain the different forms of business combination/merger**
**Content iii: Forms of business combination/merger**

As businesses grow, they sometimes find that operating alone is no longer the best strategy. They may choose to join forces with other companies.

A **business combination** is the coming together of two or more independent business units to form a larger, single enterprise, or to cooperate closely under a unified control. 

**Why do businesses combine?**
- To enjoy **economies of scale** (producing in large quantities to lower average costs).
- To **eliminate wasteful competition** (and sometimes create a monopoly to control prices).
- To pool capital and resources for massive projects.
- To guarantee a steady supply of raw materials or secure a guaranteed market for their finished goods.
- To rescue a failing business from total collapse.

A **merger** is a specific type of combination where two or more companies completely fuse to become one single legal entity. (An **acquisition** or **takeover** is when one larger, stronger company completely buys over another company, absorbing it). 

### The Three Main Forms of Integration (Mergers)

When companies merge, the nature of their combination is classified based on where they stand in the chain of production.

#### 1. Horizontal Combination (Horizontal Merger)
This occurs when two or more firms in the **same industry** and at the **same stage of production** join together. They sell the exact same type of product or service.
- **Main goal:** To eliminate competition, increase market share, and achieve economies of scale.
- **Nigerian Example:** Access Bank merging with Diamond Bank. Both were commercial banks operating at the exact same stage in the financial industry.

#### 2. Vertical Combination (Vertical Merger)
This happens when two or more firms in the **same industry** but at **different stages of production** combine. It is about taking control of the supply chain. Vertical combinations are divided into two types:
- **Backward Vertical Integration:** A company merges with another company that supplies its raw materials. It is moving "backward" towards the source of production.
  - *Example:* A bakery buying a wheat farm or a flour mill. The bakery now controls its raw materials.
- **Forward Vertical Integration:** A company merges with another company that serves as its distributor or retailer. It is moving "forward" towards the final consumer.
  - *Example:* A shoe factory buying a chain of retail shoe shops to guarantee an outlet for its manufactured shoes.

#### 3. Conglomerate Combination (Lateral Merger / Amalgamation)
This occurs when firms engaged in completely **different, unrelated industries** combine. 
- **Main goal:** **Diversification**. If one industry suffers an economic downturn, the company can rely on profits from its other, unrelated industries.
- **Nigerian Example:** The Dangote Group, which operates in cement manufacturing, sugar refining, salt processing, and petroleum refining. If the price of sugar falls, the company still makes money from cement.

### Other Important Forms of Business Combination

Apart from permanent mergers, businesses can combine in other structures, some of which are temporary or allow the companies to keep their separate identities.

#### 4. Cartel
A cartel is an association of independent producers in the same industry who agree to **restrict output (set quotas)** and **fix prices** to limit competition and maximise profits, while still remaining separate companies. 
- Cartels are often frowned upon because they create artificial scarcity and force consumers to pay higher prices.
- *Example:* The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (**OPEC**), of which Nigeria is a member, acts as an international cartel. It dictates how much crude oil member countries can pump, which directly influences the global price of oil.

#### 5. Holding Company
A holding company is a corporate entity that exists entirely to buy and hold the **controlling shares** (usually more than 50%) of other companies. 
- The companies being controlled are called **subsidiary companies**.
- The subsidiary companies retain their separate names and legal identities, but the holding company's board of directors controls their major decisions.

#### 6. Trust
A trust is a powerful form of combination where shareholders of several competing companies transfer their shares to a single **Board of Trustees**. In return, the shareholders receive "trust certificates."
- The board of trustees now manages all the companies as a single massive monopoly. 
- This form of combination is largely historical and is heavily restricted today by "anti-trust" laws because it destroys fair competition.

#### 7. Syndicate (or Consortium)
A syndicate is a **temporary** combination of different firms who pool their resources together to execute a specific, massive project that is too large for one firm to handle alone.
- Once the project is completed and the profits are shared, the syndicate is disbanded.
- *Example:* Three different construction companies temporarily joining forces to build a massive bridge across the Niger River, or several banks teaming up to underwrite a massive issue of shares for a multinational company.

#### 8. Pool
A pool is an arrangement where competing firms agree to share markets, share profits, or pool their total output into a common fund to be sold at a fixed price. Like a cartel, they retain their independence but surrender control over a specific part of their business (e.g., price or market territory) to eliminate "cut-throat" competition.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Trade Association** | An organisation of independent businesses in the same trade or industry formed to protect and promote their common interests. |
| **Chamber of Commerce** | An association of businesses, industrialists, and professionals from different trades in a specific locality aimed at promoting the region's overall commerce. |
| **Certificate of Origin** | A document issued by a Chamber of Commerce to an exporter, stating the country where the exported goods were manufactured. |
| **Merger** | The permanent fusion of two or more previously independent businesses into a single legal entity. |
| **Horizontal Combination** | A merger between firms in the same industry at the same stage of production (e.g., two commercial banks). |
| **Vertical Combination** | A merger between firms in the same industry but at different stages of production (forward or backward). |
| **Conglomerate Combination** | A merger between firms producing completely unrelated products, primarily for diversification. |
| **Cartel** | An agreement among independent producers to limit output and fix prices to eliminate competition (e.g., OPEC). |
| **Holding Company** | A company that acquires controlling shares in other companies (subsidiaries) to direct their operations. |
| **Syndicate / Consortium** | A temporary coming together of firms to execute a specific, large-scale project. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Do not confuse Trade Associations with Chambers of Commerce.** Remember the key distinction: Trade Associations = **One specific trade** anywhere; Chambers of Commerce = **All trades** in one specific locality.
- **Certificate of Origin:** This is a very frequent exam question. Only the **Chamber of Commerce** issues it, not a Trade Association, not Customs, and not the Ministry of Trade.
- **Horizontal vs. Vertical:** A common mistake is misidentifying examples of combinations. If a farm buys another farm, it is **horizontal**. If a farm buys a supermarket to sell its produce, it is **forward vertical**. If a supermarket buys a farm to supply its shelves, it is **backward vertical**. 
- **The goal of a Conglomerate:** Students often forget why a business would buy a completely unrelated business. The answer is almost always **diversification** (spreading risk so that if one market crashes, the other sustains the business).
- **Cartels and Monopolies:** Remember that cartels control prices by controlling **quotas** (how much is produced). Less supply = higher prices.

## Quick summary

1. A trade association is formed by businesses in the same industry (e.g., Garri Sellers' Association) to protect their common interests, regulate prices, and maintain standards.
2. A manufacturer's association (like MAN) protects the interests of industrialists, lobbying the government for better industrial policies.
3. A Chamber of Commerce brings together all types of businesses in a specific town or city to promote general commercial development and home/foreign trade.
4. A vital function of the Chamber of Commerce is issuing the Certificate of Origin for international trade and organising trade fairs.
5. A business combination (or merger) occurs when two or more companies join to achieve economies of scale, reduce competition, or pool capital.
6. A horizontal combination involves firms at the same stage of production (e.g., two shoe factories merging).
7. A vertical combination involves firms at different stages of production; it can be backward (securing raw materials) or forward (securing retail outlets).
8. A conglomerate merger joins unrelated businesses to spread risk through diversification.

## Practice questions

**1. An association formed by businesses engaged in the same line of activity to protect their common interests is called a**
- A. Chamber of Commerce
- B. Trade Association
- C. Trade Union
- D. Consumer Cooperative

**Answer: B.** A trade association is restricted to members of the same trade or industry. A Chamber of Commerce includes businesses from all different trades in a locality. A trade union is for employees (workers), not businesses.

**2. Which of the following is a primary function of a Chamber of Commerce?**
- A. Fixing minimum wages for all factory workers
- B. Issuing Certificates of Origin to exporters
- C. Providing soft loans to the government
- D. Regulating the internal affairs of a specific trade

**Answer: B.** Issuing the Certificate of Origin (which verifies where goods were manufactured) is a unique and primary function of a Chamber of Commerce in international trade. 

**3. The coming together of two commercial banks to form a larger bank is an example of**
- A. horizontal integration
- B. vertical integration
- C. lateral integration
- D. a conglomerate

**Answer: A.** Horizontal integration occurs when two firms at the exact same stage of production in the same industry combine. Both are commercial banks, making this a horizontal merger.

**4. A bakery deciding to acquire a wheat farm to ensure a steady supply of flour is engaged in**
- A. forward vertical integration
- B. horizontal integration
- C. backward vertical integration
- D. a syndicate

**Answer: C.** The bakery is moving "backward" along the chain of production toward the source of its raw materials. This is backward vertical integration.

**5. The main reason a company might choose to form a conglomerate combination is to**
- A. eliminate all competitors in its primary industry
- B. secure a steady supply of raw materials
- C. diversify its operations and spread business risks
- D. execute a single temporary project

**Answer: C.** Conglomerates involve unrelated industries. The primary advantage is diversification; if one industry suffers a loss, profits from the unrelated industries can keep the overall company afloat.

**6. An international organisation like OPEC, where independent member countries agree to limit oil output to influence global prices, is operating as a**
- A. holding company
- B. trust
- C. cartel
- D. consortium

**Answer: C.** A cartel is an agreement among independent producers to restrict supply (output quotas) and fix prices, which perfectly describes the function of OPEC.

**7. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN)?**
- A. Advising the government on industrial policies
- B. Establishing prices for all agricultural farm produce
- C. Protecting the interests of local factory owners
- D. Campaigning for better infrastructural facilities

**Answer: B.** Establishing prices for agricultural produce would be the job of specific agricultural trade associations (like a Farmers' Association or a Commodity Board), not the Manufacturers Association, which focuses on industrial factory output.

**8. A company that is formed primarily to acquire the controlling shares in other companies is a**
- A. subsidiary company
- B. cartel
- C. holding company
- D. syndicate

**Answer: C.** A holding company buys more than 50% of the shares in other companies (subsidiaries) so it can control their management and policies.

**9. Temporary combinations formed by firms to pool resources to undertake a massive project, and dissolved after the project's completion, are known as**
- A. syndicates
- B. amalgamations
- C. pools
- D. holding companies

**Answer: A.** A syndicate (or consortium) is specifically characterised by its temporary nature, formed solely to execute a large project that one firm could not finance or manage alone.

**10. Market women selling garri in Ogbete market decide to form an association. A major objective of this association would likely be to**
- A. organise international trade fairs in Enugu
- B. issue Certificates of Origin for exported garri
- C. prevent unhealthy competition and standardise measurements
- D. merge their stalls into one single conglomerate

**Answer: C.** A local trade association (like the Garri Sellers' Association) focuses on internal regulation, such as preventing price wars (unhealthy competition), settling disputes, and ensuring standard measurements (like the "mudu") to protect the trade's reputation.
