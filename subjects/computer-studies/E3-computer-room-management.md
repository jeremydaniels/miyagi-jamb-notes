# JAMB Computer Studies Notes: Computer Room Management

## What JAMB expects

This topic is part of Section E (Computer Maintenance and Safety Measures) of the JAMB Computer Studies syllabus. The syllabus does not list separate content items for this topic, but it sets out six clear objectives.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Define proper sitting arrangement
2. Position the monitor, keyboard, CPU, Mouse and other peripherals appropriately
3. Ensure Proper illumination of the computer room
4. Maintain a dust free environment
5. Keep liquid away from computer room
6. Keep strictly to laboratory rules and regulations

**Note on the syllabus wording:** In objective ii, the syllabus asks you to position the "CPU". In everyday language and many exams, "CPU" is used to mean the entire computer casing or "system unit". In strict computing terms, the CPU is just the processor chip inside the case. We will use the term "system unit" in the notes while acknowledging the syllabus's wording.

## Proper sitting arrangement and ergonomics

**Objective i: Define proper sitting arrangement**

When you use a computer for a long time, the way you sit greatly affects your health. A bad sitting posture can cause backaches, neck pain, and a condition called **Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI)**, which is pain in the muscles, nerves, and tendons (often in the wrists and hands) caused by doing the same movement repeatedly.

The science of designing the workplace and sitting arrangement to fit the user comfortably and safely is called **ergonomics**. 

A proper sitting arrangement ensures that the user's body is supported in a natural, relaxed position. Here is how a proper sitting arrangement is defined:

1. **Chair adjustment:** The chair should be fully adjustable (height and backrest). 
2. **Back support:** The lower back (lumbar region) must be supported by the backrest of the chair to prevent slouching.
3. **Feet placement:** Feet should rest flatly and firmly on the floor. If a student's legs are too short to reach the floor, a footrest should be used. Never dangle your feet.
4. **Knees and hips:** The knees should be at or slightly below the level of the hips, forming an angle of about 90 to 100 degrees.
5. **Elbows and arms:** Forearms should be parallel to the floor (horizontal). The elbows should rest comfortably at your sides, bent at an angle of 90 to 110 degrees.
6. **Neck and head:** The head should be kept upright, directly over the shoulders, without straining forward or tilting backward.

## Positioning computer equipment appropriately

**Objective ii: Position the monitor, keyboard, CPU, Mouse and other peripherals appropriately**

An ergonomic sitting arrangement only works if the computer equipment is placed in the right positions on the desk. Proper positioning prevents physical strain and protects the hardware from accidental damage.

### 1. The Monitor
- **Distance:** The monitor screen should be positioned about an arm's length away from your eyes (roughly 20 to 24 inches or 50 to 60 cm).
- **Height:** The top edge of the monitor should be exactly at or slightly below eye level. This ensures you look slightly downward, which reduces neck strain and keeps your eyes from drying out.
- **Alignment:** Place the monitor directly in front of you. You should not have to twist your neck to look at the screen.

### 2. The Keyboard
- **Placement:** Position the keyboard directly in front of you, close to the edge of the desk so you do not have to stretch your arms forward.
- **Angle:** The keyboard should lie flat on the desk or tilt slightly away from you. A heavily tilted keyboard forces the wrists to bend awkwardly, which can cause RSI.
- **Wrists:** Do not rest your wrists on the hard edge of the desk while typing. Use a soft wrist rest if available, and let your hands float naturally over the keys.

### 3. The Mouse
- **Placement:** The mouse should be placed right next to the keyboard on the same level.
- **Usage:** Keep it close so you do not have to overreach, which causes shoulder strain. Keep your wrist straight when holding the mouse.

### 4. The System Unit (Syllabus calls it "CPU")
- **Ventilation:** The system unit generates a lot of heat. It must be positioned in a well-ventilated spot, with at least a few inches of space around its cooling fans and vents. Never push the vents flat against a wall.
- **Location:** It can be placed flat on the desk (desktop case) or stood upright on the floor (tower case). If placed on the floor, it should be kept on a stand or an elevated platform to protect it from dust, accidental kicks, and floor moisture when sweeping or mopping.

### 5. Other Peripherals (Printers, Scanners, UPS)
- **UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply):** Place it on the floor or a sturdy lower shelf, away from foot traffic so that power cables are not tripped over.
- **Printers and Scanners:** Place these on a secondary desk or to the side of the main workspace. They do not need to be directly in front of the user since they are not looked at continuously.

## Ensuring proper illumination

**Objective iii: Ensure Proper illumination of the computer room**

Proper illumination (lighting) in a computer laboratory is essential to prevent **eye strain**, headaches, and fatigue. The lighting must not be too bright or too dim. 

### Why lighting matters
When a room is too dark, your eyes work too hard to read documents on the desk. When a room is too bright, the light washes out the computer screen, making text hard to read.

### Rules for proper illumination:
1. **Preventing Glare:** Glare happens when a bright light (like the sun or a bulb) reflects off the monitor screen into your eyes. To avoid glare:
   - Do not position monitors directly facing a bright window (which puts glare on the screen) or directly in front of a window (which puts bright light directly in the user's eyes). Monitors should be placed sideways to windows.
   - Use **anti-glare screens** (filters placed over the monitor) if the room lighting cannot be controlled.
2. **Using Blinds and Curtains:** Windows in the computer room should be fitted with blinds, curtains, or tinted glass. This allows the teacher to control the amount of natural sunlight entering the room, especially during bright sunny afternoons in Nigeria.
3. **Appropriate Artificial Lighting:** Use diffused fluorescent lighting or LED panels on the ceiling rather than harsh, direct spotlights. The light should be spread evenly across the room.
4. **Task Lighting:** If the room is dimmed for a projector presentation, small desk lamps (task lights) can be used to read printed notes without shining light on the computer screen.

## Maintaining a dust-free environment

**Objective iv: Maintain a dust free environment**

Computers are highly sensitive to dust. In many parts of Nigeria, especially during the dry Harmattan season, fine dust is very common.

### Why is dust dangerous to computers?
- **Overheating:** Dust acts like a blanket. If dust builds up inside the system unit, it coats the components (like the motherboard) and clogs the cooling fans and the heat sink. This traps heat, causing the computer to overheat, slow down, or shut down completely.
- **Electrical issues:** Some types of dust can conduct electricity when there is high humidity, which can cause short circuits on the motherboard.
- **Mechanical damage:** Dust entering optical drives, keyboards, and printers can cause the moving parts to jam or fail.

### How to maintain a dust-free computer room:
1. **Use Dust Covers:** When computers are shut down and not in use, cover the monitors, system units, keyboards, and printers with proper protective dust covers.
2. **Close Windows and Doors:** Keep windows closed, particularly during dusty weather or Harmattan. Use an **Air Conditioner (AC)** to cool the room instead of relying on open windows.
3. **No Outdoor Shoes:** Some computer laboratories have a strict "no shoes" policy, or require users to leave outdoor footwear at the door and wear specific lab sandals, to prevent tracking dirt and dust from the school compound into the lab.
4. **Regular Cleaning:** The laboratory must be cleaned regularly. Use a vacuum cleaner or a slightly damp mop on the floor instead of a dry broom, because sweeping throws dust into the air where it can settle inside the computers.
5. **Blowing the System:** During routine maintenance, a specialized computer blower is used to gently blow accumulated dust out of the system unit.

## Keeping liquid away from the computer room

**Objective v: Keep liquid away from computer room**

Liquids and electricity are a deadly combination. Keeping water, soft drinks, tea, or any fluid out of the computer lab is a universal safety rule.

### The dangers of liquid spills:
1. **Short Circuits and Fire:** If water spills into a system unit, monitor, or keyboard while it is powered on, the liquid will conduct electricity across parts that are not meant to touch. This creates a short circuit, which can permanently destroy the hardware, cause a fire, or blow a fuse.
2. **Electrocution Risk:** A student wiping a wet spill near a power socket or a live UPS could suffer a severe or fatal electric shock.
3. **Sticky Keys and Rust:** Sugary drinks (like sodas) spilled on a keyboard will leave a sticky residue even after drying, causing the keys to jam. Over time, moisture causes internal metal parts to rust.

### Safety measures for liquids:
- **Absolute ban on food and drinks:** Never bring water bottles, snacks, or drinks into the computer room. If a student is thirsty, they must step outside the lab to drink.
- **Keep away from rain:** Ensure roof leaks are fixed immediately. Computers should be placed away from windows so that sudden rain (if windows are accidentally left open) does not splash onto the equipment.
- **Emergency response:** If a spill accidentally occurs on a keyboard or computer, **do not touch the liquid**. The very first step is to cut off the power supply from the wall socket immediately to prevent electric shock and limit hardware damage.

## Laboratory rules and regulations

**Objective vi: Keep strictly to laboratory rules and regulations**

A computer laboratory contains expensive and fragile equipment. Strict rules are established to protect the hardware, secure data, and ensure the safety of students and staff.

Every computer room should have a printed list of rules placed on the wall. While rules can vary slightly between schools or JAMB CBT centres, the standard regulations are grouped below:

### 1. Access and behavioral rules
- **Authorized entry only:** Students must not enter the computer laboratory without a teacher or lab instructor present.
- **Log in and out:** Always sign the laboratory logbook or attendance register when entering and leaving. This helps track who used a machine if a fault is discovered later.
- **No bags or heavy coats:** Leave school bags outside or in a designated rack. Bags can be used to steal small parts (like mice or flash drives) and can easily knock over equipment in narrow aisles.
- **Maintain silence and order:** Do not run, play, or shout in the computer room. Running can lead to tripping over power cables.

### 2. Equipment handling rules
- **Do not move equipment:** Never attempt to move monitors, system units, or unplug cables at the back of the computer. If a mouse is not working, report it to the instructor rather than trying to fix it or swap it with a neighboring computer.
- **No foreign storage devices without scanning:** Do not insert personal USB flash drives or memory cards into the school computers without the teacher's permission, as they might spread computer viruses.
- **Avoid touching the screen:** Do not point at the monitor with a pen, pencil, or dirty fingers, as this leaves marks or damages the screen surface.
- **Switch on/off properly:** Always follow the proper Booting and Shutting Down procedures. Never turn off a computer by directly pulling the plug from the wall while it is running.

### 3. Safety and environmental rules
- **No food or drink:** (As covered above).
- **Report faults immediately:** If a computer makes a strange noise, smells of burning rubber or smoke, or shows a blue error screen, step away and alert the teacher immediately. Do not attempt to repair it.
- **Keep the room tidy:** Leave the workstation exactly as you found it. Arrange the chair, cover the computer with its dust cover, and take all your papers with you.
- **Fire safety:** Ensure you know where the fire extinguisher is located. In case of an electrical fire, never use water. A Carbon (IV) Oxide (CO₂) fire extinguisher is used for electrical fires.
- **Use power protection:** Always power the computer through a UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) and a stabilizer to protect against sudden power surges or cuts, which are common with the national grid (e.g., NEPA/PHCN).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Ergonomics** | The study of designing the workplace and equipment to fit the user comfortably, reducing physical strain and injury. |
| **Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI)** | Pain or damage in muscles, nerves, and tendons caused by performing the same physical action (like typing) over and over in a poor posture. |
| **System Unit (CPU)** | The main computer case containing the processor, motherboard, and power supply. The syllabus often refers to this casing simply as the "CPU". |
| **Anti-glare screen** | A filter placed over a monitor to reduce the reflection of external light, protecting the user's eyes. |
| **UPS** | Uninterruptible Power Supply; a device that provides temporary battery backup to the computer when the main power fails, allowing for a safe shutdown. |
| **Dust Cover** | A protective cloth or plastic placed over computer parts when not in use to keep dust from entering the vents. |
| **Short Circuit** | An electrical fault caused when electric current travels along an unintended path (often caused by liquid spills or metallic dust), causing damage or fire. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Common mistake:** Confusing the position of the monitor. The *top* of the screen should be at eye level, not the middle or bottom. If the middle is at eye level, you have to look up, which hurts your neck.
*   **Vocabulary warning:** JAMB questions often refer to the "CPU" when asking about where to place the computer case. Remember they mean the system unit, which should be kept away from kicks and well-ventilated, not the tiny processor chip inside.
*   **Lighting trick:** A common trick question asks if computer rooms should be "as bright as possible." This is false. Too much light causes screen glare. The light should be balanced and diffused.
*   **Fire safety:** If an exam question asks what to use for a computer fire, the answer is a CO₂ (carbon dioxide) fire extinguisher or a dry powder extinguisher, **never water**.
*   **First action on spill:** If liquid is spilled on a live computer, the immediate correct action is to turn off the main power/unplug it, *not* to grab a towel to wipe it while it is still on.

## Quick summary

1. A proper sitting arrangement (ergonomics) requires a straight back, feet flat on the floor, elbows at 90-110 degrees, and the top of the monitor at eye level.
2. Proper positioning prevents Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI) and physical fatigue. The keyboard and mouse should be close to the user on the same level.
3. The system unit (often called CPU) must be placed in a well-ventilated area, away from dust and accidental kicking.
4. Computer rooms need balanced lighting to prevent screen glare and eye strain. Blinds or anti-glare screens help control bright sunlight.
5. Dust acts as an insulator; it blocks cooling fans and causes computers to overheat. Dust covers, ACs, and closed windows prevent dust buildup.
6. Liquids cause electrical short circuits and rust, so eating and drinking are strictly forbidden in the computer laboratory.
7. Strict lab rules—such as logging in, not moving equipment, and scanning for viruses—protect both the users and the expensive equipment.

## Practice questions

**1. The study of how to arrange a computer workstation to fit the user comfortably and safely is called:**
- A. Economics
- B. Ergonomics
- C. Digitisation
- D. Biometrics

**Answer: B.** Ergonomics is the science of designing the workplace environment and equipment to maximize productivity and reduce operator fatigue and discomfort.

**2. To prevent neck strain, how should the computer monitor be positioned?**
- A. The center of the monitor should be far above eye level.
- B. The monitor should be placed on the far right of the keyboard.
- C. The top edge of the monitor should be at or slightly below eye level.
- D. The monitor should be tilted extremely backwards on the desk.

**Answer: C.** The top edge of the monitor should be at eye level. This allows the user's eyes to look slightly downward, which is the most natural resting position. Option A forces the user to tilt their head backward.

**3. Why is it important to maintain a dust-free environment in the computer laboratory?**
- A. Dust causes the monitor to become brighter over time.
- B. Dust builds up on the cooling fans, causing the computer to overheat.
- C. Dust increases the processing speed of the CPU.
- D. Dust makes the keyboard keys type faster.

**Answer: B.** Dust accumulation blocks air vents and coats internal parts (like the heat sink and fans). This prevents heat from escaping, leading to severe overheating and hardware failure.

**4. What is the immediate and most important action to take if a student accidentally spills a bottle of water over a running computer?**
- A. Bring a mop to dry the floor quickly.
- B. Use a dry cloth to wipe the motherboard.
- C. Turn off the main power supply from the wall socket.
- D. Restart the computer to see if it is still working.

**Answer: C.** Turning off the main power supply is the first priority to prevent electrocution and stop electrical short circuits from destroying the computer.

**5. Which of the following best describes the consequence of poor sitting posture while using a computer for a long time?**
- A. Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI)
- B. Short circuiting
- C. Computer viruses
- D. Glare on the screen

**Answer: A.** Poor posture and repetitive movements can cause Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI), which affects the muscles, nerves, and tendons.

**6. To prevent screen glare in a computer laboratory during a bright afternoon, the laboratory manager should:**
- A. open all the windows wide.
- B. use window blinds or curtains to control the sunlight.
- C. replace the monitors with older models.
- D. turn on very bright spotlights pointed at the screens.

**Answer: B.** Blinds, curtains, or tinted windows block harsh direct sunlight, which causes glare on the monitor screens. Options A and D will only make the glare worse.

**7. Which of the following is NOT a standard rule in a computer laboratory?**
- A. Users must not eat or drink near the computers.
- B. Users are allowed to open the system unit to fix faults themselves.
- C. Users must log in and out of the attendance register.
- D. Users must shut down the computer using the proper process.

**Answer: B.** Users (especially students) should never attempt to repair or open computer equipment themselves. All hardware faults must be reported to the laboratory instructor.

**8. The correct position for the mouse on a computer desk is:**
- A. placed behind the monitor to save space.
- B. placed on the system unit.
- C. placed right next to the keyboard on the same level.
- D. placed on the floor for easy foot access.

**Answer: C.** The mouse should be placed right next to the keyboard so the user does not have to stretch or strain their shoulder and arm to reach it.

**9. Why are students usually advised to leave their bags outside or in a designated rack when entering the computer room?**
- A. Bags can block narrow aisles and cause people to trip over cables.
- B. Bags emit magnetic fields that format hard drives.
- C. Bags attract computer viruses.
- D. Bags prevent the air conditioner from working properly.

**Answer: A.** Large bags on the floor or around desks create tripping hazards in a space full of electrical cables and fragile equipment. They also make unauthorized removal of small components easier.

**10. When a computer is powered off and not in use for the weekend, what should be done to protect the monitor and keyboard?**
- A. Wash them with a wet cloth.
- B. Cover them with protective dust covers.
- C. Unplug them and pack them into a box.
- D. Leave them exactly as they are with the windows open.

**Answer: B.** Dust covers should be placed over the equipment to keep dust from settling in the vents, keys, and ports while the computers are not in use.
