# JAMB Computer Studies Notes: Communication Systems

## What JAMB expects

This topic is part of Section F (Information & Communication Technology) of the JAMB syllabus. It covers the foundations of modern communication systems.

The syllabus covers two main contents:
- **a.** Definitions and Acronym of ICT
- **b.** Types and examples of ICT

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. State the full meaning of the acronym ICT.
2. Define ICT.
3. List types of ICT (Broadcasting, Telecommunication, Data Network, Information Systems, Satellite Communication, etc.).
4. Give examples of Broadcasting.
5. List types of Telecommunications.
6. List types of Data Network.
7. List types of Information System.

## Definitions and Acronym of ICT

### The full meaning of the acronym ICT
**Objective i: State the full meaning of the acronym ICT**

The acronym **ICT** stands for **Information and Communication Technology**. 

### Definition of ICT
**Objective ii: Define ICT**

**ICT** is an umbrella term that describes the diverse set of technological tools and resources used to communicate, create, process, disseminate, store, and manage information. It brings together traditional computing (Information Technology) and high-speed communication links (Telecommunications) that carry data, sound, and video. 

Whenever you use a smartphone to send a message, browse a website, or listen to the radio, you are using ICT. The integration of these tools makes it possible for users across the world to access and share information seamlessly.

## Types and examples of ICT

### Types of ICT
**Objective iii: List types of ICT**

ICT covers a very wide range of technologies. The JAMB syllabus classifies the major types of ICT into the following categories:
1. Broadcasting
2. Telecommunication
3. Data Network
4. Information Systems
5. Satellite Communication

Each of these types plays a specific role in how information is shared and processed globally. 

### Examples of Broadcasting
**Objective iv: Give examples of Broadcasting**

**Broadcasting** is the one-way distribution of audio or video content to a large, scattered audience through a mass communications medium. Because it is one-way, the sender transmits the information, and the receivers simply tune in to listen or watch without sending data back.

Examples of broadcasting include:
- **Radio broadcasting:** The transmission of sound using radio waves. Examples are local FM and AM stations, such as Wazobia FM or Radio Nigeria.
- **Television broadcasting:** The transmission of moving images and sound. This can be done using terrestrial antennas (like NTA) or through cable networks.
- **Satellite broadcasting:** Broadcasting programmes directly to home satellite dishes from satellites orbiting the Earth. In Nigeria, popular examples include DStv and GOtv.

### Types of Telecommunications
**Objective v: List types of Telecommunications**

**Telecommunications** involves the two-way transmission of signals, messages, words, images, and sounds over significant distances using wire, radio, optical, or other electromagnetic systems. 

Types of telecommunications systems include:
- **Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) Land Line:** This is the traditional, wired telephone system used globally. It relies on underground copper cables and central switching centres (for example, the old NITEL lines in Nigeria). 
  *Note on the syllabus wording: The syllabus prints this with a missing closing bracket as "(PSTN Land Line".*
- **Mobile phone system:** Cellular networks that provide wireless voice and data communication to mobile devices using a network of cell towers. The GSM networks in Nigeria, such as MTN, Airtel, Glo, and 9mobile, are prime examples.
- **Circuit Switched Packet Telephone System (CSPT):** 
  *Note on the syllabus wording: "CSPT" is not a standard term used in most computer networking textbooks. Usually, phone networks are either "circuit-switched" (a dedicated physical line is kept open for the whole call, like PSTN) or "packet-switched" (data is broken into small packets and routed, like internet calls). The syllabus combines them into this single term. For the exam, simply recognise CSPT as a type of telecommunication system.*
- **Satellite Telephone System:** Mobile phones that connect directly to orbiting satellites instead of ground-based cell towers. They are highly useful in remote locations like the middle of the ocean or deep deserts where standard mobile signals cannot reach.
- **Fixed Wireless Telephone System:** A system where the telephone equipment in a home or office stays in a fixed position (like a desktop phone) but connects to the telephone exchange wirelessly using radio signals, rather than through physical wires.

### Types of Data Network
**Objective vi: List types of Data Network**

A **Data Network** is a system that allows computers and other digital devices to connect and exchange data. Networks are usually classified by the geographical area they cover.

Types of data networks include:
- **Personal Area Network (PAN):** The smallest type of network, covering a range of only a few metres around an individual. It is used to connect personal devices, such as linking a smartphone to wireless earbuds via Bluetooth, or connecting a laptop to a printer via a short USB cable.
- **Local Area Network (LAN):** A network confined to a small geographical area, such as a single room, a building, or a school campus. A JAMB CBT centre or a cybercafe relies on a LAN (often connected by Ethernet cables or Wi-Fi) so all computers can share resources.
- **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN):** A network that covers a larger physical area than a LAN but smaller than a WAN, typically spanning a city or a large university campus. It connects multiple LANs together.
- **Wide Area Network (WAN):** A network covering a massive geographical area, such as a state, country, or even across continents. The largest example of a WAN is the Internet.
- **Intranet:** A private, internal network belonging to a specific organisation. It uses the same technology as the Internet (websites, servers) but is securely restricted only to the organisation's employees or members (e.g., a university portal accessible only to registered students and staff).
- **Internet:** The global, public system of interconnected computer networks that anyone can access.

| Feature | LAN (Local Area Network) | MAN (Metropolitan Area Network) | WAN (Wide Area Network) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Area Covered** | Small (room, building, school) | Medium (city, large campus) | Large (country, continent, global) |
| **Ownership** | Usually private (one person/school) | Private or public (ISP) | Usually public or distributed |
| **Examples** | Cybercafe, computer lab | City-wide cable TV network | The Internet |

### Types of Information System
**Objective vii: List types of Information System**

An **Information System (IS)** is a combination of hardware, software, infrastructure, and trained personnel organised to collect, process, store, and distribute data to support decision-making in an organisation. While ICT focuses on the technology itself, an information system includes the people and processes that use the technology. 

Types of information systems highlighted in the syllabus include:
- **Data Processing System:** A system that processes large volumes of routine, daily transactions to turn raw data into useful information. Examples include a bank's account processing system, a supermarket's point-of-sale (POS) checkout system, and the automated system that computes JAMB UTME results.
- **Global Positioning System (GPS):** A satellite-based navigation system that provides accurate location, speed, and time information to a GPS receiver anywhere on Earth. GPS is widely used in smartphone map apps, vehicle tracking, and aviation.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Remember the exact acronym:** ICT stands for Information and **Communication** Technology. Do not choose options that say "Communications" (plural) or "Computer" instead of Communication, unless it is the only plausible choice.
- **One-way vs. Two-way:** Broadcasting (radio, TV) is one-way. Telecommunications (phones, internet chats) is two-way.
- **Network size order:** Always remember the scale: PAN (smallest) → LAN → MAN → WAN (largest).
- **Intranet vs. Internet:** Do not confuse the two. An **Intranet** is private (like a bank's internal staff network), while the **Internet** is public and global.
- **PSTN meaning:** JAMB frequently tests abbreviations. PSTN stands for Public Switched Telephone Network (traditional landlines).
- **GPS function:** Remember that GPS is considered a type of Information System used for determining physical locations using satellites.

## Quick summary

1. ICT (Information and Communication Technology) combines computing and telecommunications to process, store, and share information.
2. The main types of ICT are Broadcasting, Telecommunications, Data Networks, Information Systems, and Satellite Communication.
3. Broadcasting (like radio and television) sends one-way information to a wide audience.
4. Telecommunications provides two-way communication and includes PSTN (landlines), mobile phone systems, and satellite phones.
5. Data networks are classified by size: PAN (personal), LAN (local/building), MAN (city), and WAN (country/global).
6. The Internet is a public WAN, whereas an Intranet is a private network used securely by a single organisation.
7. Information systems, such as Data Processing Systems and GPS, involve hardware, software, and people working together to process data and provide specific services like location tracking.

## Practice questions

**1. The acronym ICT stands for:**
- A. Information and Computer Technology
- B. Information and Communication Technology
- C. Internal Communication Technology
- D. Integrated Computer Technology

**Answer: B.** ICT stands for Information and Communication Technology, which merges computing with telecommunications. Option A is a common mistake that uses "Computer" instead of "Communication".

**2. Which of the following is an example of broadcasting?**
- A. A phone call between two friends
- B. Sending an email
- C. FM Radio transmission
- D. Browsing a private Intranet

**Answer: C.** Broadcasting is the one-way distribution of audio or video to a large audience. FM Radio is a classic example of this. The other options involve two-way telecommunications or data networking.

**3. The traditional, wired landline telephone system is known by the acronym:**
- A. CSPT
- B. PSTN
- C. GSM
- D. PAN

**Answer: B.** PSTN stands for Public Switched Telephone Network, which refers to the traditional landline telephone infrastructure.

**4. A network that connects computers within a single building, such as a school laboratory, is called a:**
- A. Wide Area Network (WAN)
- B. Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)
- C. Personal Area Network (PAN)
- D. Local Area Network (LAN)

**Answer: D.** A Local Area Network (LAN) covers a small geographical area, such as a single room or building. WAN covers countries, MAN covers cities, and PAN covers a few metres around a person.

**5. Which type of Data Network is restricted exclusively to the staff or members of an organisation?**
- A. Internet
- B. Extranet
- C. Intranet
- D. MAN

**Answer: C.** An Intranet is a private network belonging to an organisation and is securely restricted to its own members, unlike the public Internet.

**6. Which of the following best describes the Global Positioning System (GPS)?**
- A. A system used to process a bank's daily financial data
- B. A terrestrial television broadcasting network
- C. A satellite-based information system used for navigation and tracking
- D. A traditional wired telephone system

**Answer: C.** GPS uses a network of satellites to provide location, navigation, and timing information to receivers on Earth.

**7. A mobile phone used for communication in the deep ocean, where regular cellular network towers cannot reach, is a:**
- A. Satellite Telephone System
- B. Fixed Wireless Telephone System
- C. PSTN Land Line
- D. Circuit Switched Packet Telephone System

**Answer: A.** A Satellite Telephone System connects directly to orbiting satellites, making it suitable for remote areas like deep oceans or deserts.

**8. According to the JAMB syllabus, which of the following is considered an Information System?**
- A. Data Processing System
- B. Local Area Network
- C. Mobile phone system
- D. Radio broadcasting

**Answer: A.** The Data Processing System (along with GPS) is listed as a type of Information System. A LAN is a data network, a mobile system is telecommunications, and radio is broadcasting.

**9. Connecting a wireless mouse or earbuds to a laptop via Bluetooth is an example of creating a:**
- A. LAN
- B. WAN
- C. MAN
- D. PAN

**Answer: D.** A Personal Area Network (PAN) connects personal devices over a very short distance (usually within a few metres).

**10. What differentiates broadcasting from standard telecommunications like a mobile phone system?**
- A. Broadcasting is two-way, while telecommunications is one-way
- B. Broadcasting is one-way, while telecommunications involves two-way signal transmission
- C. Broadcasting relies purely on wires, while telecommunications is entirely wireless
- D. There is no difference; they are the same thing

**Answer: B.** Broadcasting (like TV and radio) involves a central sender transmitting data outwards to receivers without a return channel (one-way). Telecommunications (like phone calls) allows for mutual data exchange (two-way).
