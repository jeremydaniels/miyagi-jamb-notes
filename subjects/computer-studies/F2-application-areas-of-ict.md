## Application areas of ICT

**Content a: Application areas**
**Objective i: Define Applications Areas of ICT**

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) has changed the way we live, work, and learn. The **application areas of ICT** refer to the various fields, industries, and everyday activities where technology is used to solve problems, improve efficiency, and connect people.

Below are the major application areas you need to know for your exam:

### 1. Teleconferencing
Teleconferencing is a live audio meeting between two or more people who are in different locations, using telephone networks or the internet. The prefix "tele-" means "at a distance." In a teleconference, people can hear and speak to each other, but they cannot see each other. It is often used by businesses for quick meetings without the need for travel.

### 2. Video conferencing
Video conferencing is a live meeting where two or more people in different locations can both **see and hear** each other using computers, cameras, microphones, and the internet.
- **Examples:** Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Skype, and Google Meet.
- **Difference from teleconferencing:** While teleconferencing is usually audio-only, video conferencing includes a live video feed, making it feel more like a face-to-face meeting.

### 3. Telecommuting
Telecommuting (also called remote work or working from home) is an arrangement where employees do their jobs from outside the traditional office, usually from home. They use ICT (like laptops, internet, and messaging apps) to communicate with their employer and complete their tasks.
- **Advantage:** Saves the time and cost of commuting (traveling) through traffic to a physical office.

### 4. Telecomputing
Telecomputing is the use of communication networks to access computing resources remotely. Instead of processing data on your local machine, you connect to a remote computer (or server) to run programs, store files, or process data.
- **Example:** Cloud computing (like Google Drive) or a researcher in Nigeria logging into a powerful supercomputer in another country to run complex calculations.

### 5. Messaging
Messaging is the electronic transmission of text, voice, or media between users over a network. It has largely replaced traditional paper letters.
- **Examples:** Short Message Service (SMS) on mobile phones, email, and instant messaging apps like WhatsApp and Telegram.

### 6. Information search, retrieval and archival systems
ICT allows us to manage massive amounts of information efficiently.
- **Information search:** Looking for specific data using search engines (like Google) or database queries.
- **Information retrieval:** The process of fetching the requested information and displaying it to the user.
- **Archival systems:** Systems designed to safely store important data and documents for a long time (archiving). Digital archives take up much less space than physical filing cabinets and protect data from fire or natural decay.

### 7. E-Learning
Electronic Learning (E-Learning) is the use of ICT to deliver education and training.
- **Examples:** Taking online courses, watching educational videos, using educational apps, and taking computer-based tests.
- **Nigerian example:** The JAMB UTME is a Computer-Based Test (CBT), which is a direct application of e-learning and e-assessment systems.

### 8. Telemedicine
Telemedicine is the use of ICT to provide healthcare services to patients who are in a different location from the doctor.
- **Examples:** A patient in a rural Nigerian village consulting a specialist in Lagos via a video call, or a doctor sending a patient's X-ray to another hospital for a second opinion over the internet.

### 9. E-Commerce
Electronic Commerce (E-Commerce) is the buying and selling of goods and services over the internet. It includes online shopping and electronic payments.
- **Examples in Nigeria:** Online marketplaces like Jumia and Konga, or buying data and airtime using a bank's mobile app.

### 10. E-Government
Electronic Government (E-Government) is the use of ICT by the government to provide services and information to citizens, businesses, and other government departments easily and transparently.
- **Examples in Nigeria:** Registering for a National Identification Number (NIN) through the NIMC database, applying for a Nigerian passport online, or paying government taxes via the Remita platform.

### 11. E-Library
An Electronic Library (E-Library) is a digital collection of books, academic journals, audio, and video files stored on computer systems and accessed over a network. Instead of going to a physical building to borrow a paper book, students can download or read materials online at any time.

## ICT based Devices

**Content b: ICT based Devices**
**Objective ii: List types of ICT Devices**

ICT devices are hardware machines and appliances that process, store, communicate, or display data and information electronically. Below are the common types of ICT devices named in the syllabus:

### 1. Mobile phones
Mobile phones are portable, wireless communication devices.
- **Feature phones:** Basic phones used mainly for making calls and sending SMS.
- **Smartphones:** Advanced phones with operating systems (like Android or iOS) that act as mini-computers, allowing internet access, video calls, e-commerce, and messaging apps.

### 2. Computers
Computers are the central devices of ICT. They accept raw data, process it, store it, and output information.
- **Types:** Desktop computers, laptops, tablets, and large network servers.

### 3. Automated Teller Machines (ATM)
An ATM is a specialized electronic banking outlet that allows customers to complete basic transactions without the aid of a branch representative or teller.
- **Uses:** Withdrawing cash, checking account balances, transferring money, and paying bills using a magnetic or chip-and-pin card.

### 4. Dispensing Machines (Vending Machines)
A dispensing machine (or vending machine) is an automated machine that provides items to customers after they insert money, a token, or a card.
- **Uses:** Dispensing canned drinks, snacks, or electronic tickets. Note that an ATM is a specialized type of dispensing machine that dispenses cash.

### 5. Point of Sale Machines (POS)
A POS machine is an electronic device used to process card payments at retail locations.
- **Uses:** In supermarkets, it allows customers to pay for goods with their ATM cards.
- **Nigerian example:** POS terminals are widely used by mobile money agents (like Moniepoint, OPay, and Paga) across Nigeria to help people withdraw cash, transfer money, and pay bills in areas where bank branches or ATMs are scarce.

### 6. Automated Cash Register (ACR)
An ACR is a machine used in shops and supermarkets to calculate and record sales. It is an electronic replacement for the old mechanical cash registers.
- **Features:** It usually has a keyboard, a display screen, a barcode scanner, a receipt printer, and a secure drawer for cash. When an item is scanned, it automatically looks up the price, adds it to the total, and prints a receipt.

### 7. Radio sets
A radio set is an electronic device that receives audio broadcasts transmitted via radio frequency waves.
- **Uses:** It is a traditional ICT device used for mass communication, broadcasting news, educational programmes, and music (e.g., Wazobia FM or Radio Nigeria) over wide areas. It provides one-way communication.

### 8. Television sets
A television (TV) set receives and displays both audio and moving video broadcasts.
- **Modern TVs:** Smart TVs are now common; they connect directly to the internet to stream e-learning content, movies, and news, merging traditional broadcasting with modern computing.

### 9. Scanners
A scanner is an input device that "reads" physical documents, photographs, or barcodes and converts them into digital computer files.
- **Document Scanners:** Digitize paper documents for e-libraries or archival systems.
- **Barcode Scanners:** Used with POS systems and ACRs to read product prices instantly.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ICT** | Information and Communication Technology; technologies used to handle and communicate information. |
| **Teleconferencing** | A live audio meeting between people in different locations over a network. |
| **Video conferencing** | A live meeting where participants can both see and hear each other using video and audio links. |
| **Telecommuting** | Working remotely (usually from home) using ICT to communicate with the office. |
| **Telecomputing** | Accessing computing resources and processing power remotely over a network. |
| **E-Learning** | Delivering education, training, or assessments (like CBT) using digital technologies. |
| **Telemedicine** | Providing medical consultations, diagnoses, and treatments remotely via ICT. |
| **E-Commerce** | Buying, selling, or paying for goods and services electronically over the internet. |
| **E-Government** | The use of ICT by the government to provide public services to citizens (e.g., online passport applications). |
| **ATM** | Automated Teller Machine; an electronic device for basic banking transactions like cash withdrawal. |
| **POS Machine** | Point of Sale Machine; a device used to process card payments or mobile money transactions. |
| **ACR** | Automated Cash Register; a device used in retail to calculate sales, open a cash drawer, and print receipts. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Telecommuting vs. Telecomputing:** These are easily confused. **Telecommuting** is about the *commute* (the journey to work); it means working from home. **Telecomputing** is about the *computer*; it means accessing remote computing systems.
- **Teleconferencing vs. Video conferencing:** Remember that teleconferencing is generally audio-only (like a group phone call), whereas video conferencing includes visual (video) communication.
- **ATMs vs. POS machines:** Both handle financial transactions. An ATM is a large machine primarily used by customers to *dispense cash* unattended. A POS machine is a small, handheld terminal used by a merchant or agent to process card payments or agent banking.
- **JAMB uses "E-" heavily:** "E-" stands for electronic. If a question asks about government services online, look for **E-Government**. If it asks about buying goods online, look for **E-Commerce**.

## Quick summary

1. ICT application areas cover the various ways technology is used to improve everyday life, business, health, and education.
2. **Telecommuting** allows people to work from home, while **telecomputing** involves connecting to remote computer systems.
3. **Teleconferencing** (audio) and **video conferencing** (audio and video) allow meetings without physical travel.
4. **E-Learning**, **Telemedicine**, **E-Commerce**, and **E-Government** represent the digital transformation of education, healthcare, business, and public services, respectively.
5. ICT devices process and transmit data electronically. Common examples include computers, mobile phones, radios, TVs, and scanners.
6. Financial and retail ICT devices include the **ATM** (for self-service banking), the **POS machine** (for card payments and agent banking), and the **Automated Cash Register** (for calculating retail sales).

## Practice questions

**1. The application of ICT that allows employees to work from their homes instead of traveling to a central office is called:**
- A. Telecomputing
- B. Telecommuting
- C. Teleconferencing
- D. Telemedicine

**Answer: B.** Telecommuting (or remote work) uses ICT to eliminate the physical commute to an office. Mistake to avoid: Option A (Telecomputing) refers to accessing remote computer networks, not necessarily the act of working from home instead of an office.

**2. Which of the following is a primary difference between teleconferencing and video conferencing?**
- A. Teleconferencing uses the internet, while video conferencing uses only radio waves.
- B. Teleconferencing includes live video, while video conferencing does not.
- C. Video conferencing allows participants to see and hear each other, while teleconferencing is generally audio-only.
- D. Teleconferencing is used for education, while video conferencing is only used in medicine.

**Answer: C.** Teleconferencing is typically a group phone call (audio), whereas video conferencing includes both visual and audio feeds.

**3. A student preparing for the JAMB UTME uses a Computer-Based Test (CBT) software to practice at home. This is an example of:**
- A. E-Commerce
- B. E-Government
- C. E-Learning
- D. Telemedicine

**Answer: C.** E-Learning involves using computers and digital tools for education, training, and testing.

**4. The use of ICT by government agencies to provide services, such as online passport applications or NIN registration, is termed:**
- A. E-Commerce
- B. E-Library
- C. E-Government
- D. Telecomputing

**Answer: C.** E-Government refers to the digital interaction between a government and its citizens.

**5. Which of the following ICT devices is primarily used by mobile money agents in Nigeria to help customers withdraw cash from their bank accounts using an ATM card?**
- A. Automated Cash Register (ACR)
- B. Point of Sale (POS) machine
- C. Dispensing Machine
- D. Scanner

**Answer: B.** The POS machine reads the ATM card and processes the transaction for the agent. The ATM itself is a self-service machine, and an ACR is for calculating shop sales.

**6. A doctor in a teaching hospital reviews the digital X-rays of a patient located in a remote village over a secure network. This application is best described as:**
- A. Telecommuting
- B. E-Commerce
- C. Information retrieval
- D. Telemedicine

**Answer: D.** Telemedicine is the remote diagnosis and treatment of patients by means of telecommunications technology.

**7. Which of the following devices is an automated machine that provides items like drinks, snacks, or tickets when money is inserted?**
- A. Automated Teller Machine
- B. Dispensing Machine
- C. Automated Cash Register
- D. Point of Sale Machine

**Answer: B.** A dispensing machine (vending machine) automatically dispenses physical items. An ATM specifically dispenses cash.

**8. Buying a pair of shoes from an online store like Jumia or Konga is an example of:**
- A. E-Library
- B. Telecomputing
- C. E-Commerce
- D. E-Government

**Answer: C.** E-Commerce involves trading (buying and selling) goods and services over the internet.

**9. What does the acronym ACR stand for in a modern supermarket?**
- A. Automated Cash Register
- B. Automatic Counting Record
- C. Advanced Computing Resource
- D. Automated Card Reader

**Answer: A.** ACR stands for Automated Cash Register, which is used to scan items, calculate the total cost, and print receipts.

**10. An electronic system used to safely store old, important documents for long-term future reference is called a/an:**
- A. Information search system
- B. Archival system
- C. Teleconferencing system
- D. Messaging system

**Answer: B.** Archival systems are used to store data, files, or documents securely over a long period for historical or backup purposes.
