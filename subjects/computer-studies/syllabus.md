# JAMB UTME Computer Studies Syllabus

This is an exact transcription of the `computer-studies.pdf` syllabus (pages 1-23). The wording, spelling, punctuation, and numbering have been kept exactly as printed in the original document, including any errors or inconsistencies.

## GENERAL OBJECTIVES

The aim of the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination syllabus in Computer Studies is to prepare the candidates for the Board’s examination. The objectives of the syllabus are designed to test candidates’ understanding, knowledge and acquisition of:

1. Evolution of Computing Systems
2. Basic concepts of computer and its operations
3. Problem solving skills, data processing and practical skills in Computing
4. System software and Application Software.
5. Operations of Basic computer hardware – Input, Output, Memory and Central Processing Unit 
6. Application of Online resources and Online skills 
7. Ethics and human issues in computing
8. Career Prospects in Computing

The syllabus is divided into ten sections as given below:

A. Evolution of Computing
B. Fundamentals of Computing 
C. Computer Hardware
D. Computer Software
E. Basic Computer Operations
F. Computer Applications
G. Managing Computer Files
H. Developing Problem Solving Skills
I. Basics and Applications of Information and Communication Technologies (ICT)
J. Computer Ethics and Computers in Society
K. Potentials for Higher Studies in Computer related areas
.

## Transcription notes

These are not part of the syllabus. They flag places where the original wording looks like a typing error, is unclear, or looks unofficial or out of date, so the notes can handle them correctly. The syllabus text above is left exactly as printed. Source: the 23-page "Computer Studies" PDF supplied from IBASS; we have not checked it against any other JAMB publication. The running header "Computer Studies" on every page and the page numbers were left out. Where one topic's objectives were printed in several separate "Candidates should be able to" blocks, each block is kept as its own block, with its own numbering (i, ii, ...).

**Structure of the document**

*   **Section letters do not match.** The General Objectives say the syllabus "is divided into ten sections" but list eleven (A to K): A Evolution of Computing, B Fundamentals of Computing, C Computer Hardware, D Computer Software, E Basic Computer Operations, F Computer Applications, G Managing Computer Files, H Developing Problem Solving Skills, I Basics and Applications of ICT, J Computer Ethics and Computers in Society, K Potentials for Higher Studies in Computer related areas. The detailed syllabus has only nine sections, lettered and titled differently: A Evolution of Computing, B Fundamentals of Computing, C Computer Application Packages, D Managing Computer Files, E Computer Maintenance and Safety Measures, F Information & Communication Technology (ICT), G Developing Problem-Solving Skills, H Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Robotics, I Computer Ethics and Human Issues. Hardware, software and basic operations are all inside Section B in the detailed syllabus. The detailed syllabus (the nine sections) is the one that lists the content, so the notes follow it; the introductory list looks like an older outline.
*   **Section A, Topic 1, objective ii**: it asks for "the contributions and uses of each of the founders of these devices" but then lists ENIAC, EDVAC, UNIVAC 1 and "Desktop Personal Computers, etc", which are machines, not people, and are not the devices listed in objective i. **Possibly out of date or unofficial**

*   The syllabus mentions products that are discontinued or old: Floppy disks, Blackberry OS, Internet Explorer, Cometbird, Phoenix, Macromedia Flash, WordStar, VisiCalc, Foxbase, Harvard Graphics, Windows Movie Maker, FrontPage. The CorelDraw and PowerPoint feature lists are lists of "new features" from particular older versions (for example "Animation Painter" and "Mask Feature"), not features of every version. The notes must say that menus and features differ between versions and between software vendors.
*   The recommended texts and "Accessed on August 6, 2021" dates show that this version was prepared around 2021. Newer technology (AI, robotics, cybersecurity) appears in Sections H and I. These are fast-changing fields; the notes must hedge statements about current products, tools and threats.
*   Sections C and F ask candidates to "perform" or "use" software (launch Excel, design in CorelDraw, use Dreamweaver, navigate websites). An objective written examination cannot test these directly; the notes will teach the steps in words and with described diagrams, and say that menus differ between versions.
*   Section B (Data representation) asks only for the "number bases with special reference to binary, decimal, hexadecimal"; the objectives do not name conversions or binary arithmetic. The plan includes them under this topic as a judgement call, because number-base questions are common in Computer Studies papers; this is flagged in PLAN.md for approval.
