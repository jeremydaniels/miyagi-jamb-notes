# JAMB Crk Notes: The Covenant

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 2 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: The Covenant. It covers the following content items:

- (a) The flood and God’s covenant with Noah (Gen. 6:1-22; 7:1-24; 9:1-17)
- (b) God’s covenant with Abraham (Gen. 11:31-32; 12:1-9; 17:1-21; 21:1-13; 25:19-26)
- (c) God’s covenant with Israel (Ex. 19; 20; 24:1-11) cf. Deut. 28:1-19
- (d) The New Covenant (Jer. 31:31-34; Ezek. 36:25-28)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Explain the concept of covenant.
2. Examine the importance and implication of the covenants.
3. Distinguish between God’s covenants with Noah, Abraham and Israel.
4. Distinguish between the old and the new covenants.

## The concept of covenant

**Objective i: explain the concept of covenant.**

In the Bible, a **covenant** is a binding agreement, treaty, or pact between two or more parties. In everyday life, people make covenants (like a marriage vow or a business contract), but in the Bible, the most important covenants are those made between God and human beings. 

Because God and humans are not equals, a divine covenant is not a negotiation. It is an arrangement initiated by God, in which He sets the terms. 

Biblical covenants usually contain:
1. **Promises:** What God commits to do.
2. **Conditions:** What humans are required to do (if it is a conditional covenant).
3. **A Sign or Seal:** A visible symbol to serve as a reminder of the agreement.

**Types of covenants:**
- **Conditional Covenants:** The agreement depends on humans keeping their part of the deal. If they disobey, the covenant is broken and they receive a penalty or curse (e.g., the covenant with Israel at Sinai).
- **Unconditional Covenants:** God promises to fulfil His word regardless of human behaviour. It depends entirely on God's faithfulness (e.g., the covenants with Noah and Abraham).

## The flood and God's covenant with Noah

**Content a: The flood and God’s covenant with Noah (Gen. 6:1-22; 7:1-24; 9:1-17)**

The first major covenant mentioned in the Bible was made with Noah after the Great Flood. 

### The wickedness of man and the Ark
In Genesis 6, the Bible states that human wickedness had become so great that "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." God regretted making mankind and decided to destroy the earth with a flood.

However, **Noah found favour in the eyes of the Lord** because he was a righteous and blameless man who walked with God.
1. God commanded Noah to build an **ark of gopher wood**, with rooms, coated inside and out with pitch.
2. God told Noah to bring his family (his wife, his three sons—Shem, Ham, and Japheth—and their wives, making eight people in total).
3. Noah was instructed to bring into the ark two of every living creature (male and female) to keep them alive, along with food for them all.

### The Flood
In Genesis 7, the floodwaters came. 
1. It rained heavily for **forty days and forty nights**, and the "fountains of the great deep" burst open.
2. The waters covered the highest mountains. Every living thing on the face of the earth died, except for Noah and those with him in the ark.
3. The waters prevailed on the earth for **150 days** before receding.

### The Covenant and its sign
After the flood, when Noah and his family left the ark, God established a covenant with them (Genesis 9):
- **The Command:** God blessed Noah and his sons, telling them to "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth."
- **The Rules:** God permitted humans to eat meat, but strictly forbade eating meat that still had its lifeblood in it. He also instituted a law against murder, stating that whoever sheds man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed, because humans are made in the image of God.
- **The Promise:** God promised unconditionally that He would **never again destroy all living creatures by a flood**.
- **The Sign:** God set the **rainbow** in the clouds as the sign of this everlasting covenant between Himself and all life on earth. Whenever the rainbow appeared, God said He would remember the covenant.

## God's covenant with Abraham

**Content b: God’s covenant with Abraham (Gen. 11:31-32; 12:1-9; 17:1-21; 21:1-13; 25:19-26)**

God later chose one man through whom He would eventually bless the whole world. This was the covenant with Abraham.

### Background and the Call
Abram (as he was originally known) was from Ur of the Chaldeans. His father, **Terah**, took Abram, Abram's wife Sarai, and Abram's nephew **Lot**, intending to go to Canaan, but they settled in Haran instead, where Terah died (Genesis 11).

In Genesis 12, God called Abram, who was then 75 years old. God commanded him: "Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you."
God made several promises:
1. He would make Abram a **great nation**.
2. He would bless him and make his name great.
3. He would bless those who bless him and curse those who curse him.
4. **In him, all the families of the earth would be blessed.**

Abram obeyed in faith. He travelled through Canaan, building altars to the Lord at Shechem and Bethel.

### The Covenant of Circumcision
When Abram was 99 years old, God appeared to him to establish His covenant formally (Genesis 17). 
- God commanded him: "Walk before me, and be blameless."
- God changed his name from **Abram** (exalted father) to **Abraham** (father of a multitude of nations), and his wife's name from **Sarai** to **Sarah**.
- God promised the whole land of Canaan as an everlasting possession for his descendants.
- **The Sign:** God commanded that **every male must be circumcised** when they are eight days old. This was the physical mark (sign) of the covenant. Any uncircumcised male would be cut off from his people for breaking the covenant.
- God promised that Sarah, despite being very old, would have a son named **Isaac**, and the everlasting covenant would be established specifically through him. (Abraham laughed at this, wondering how a 100-year-old man and a 90-year-old woman could have a child).

### The birth of Isaac and the line of promise
In Genesis 21, God fulfilled His promise. Sarah gave birth to Isaac. Abraham circumcised Isaac on the eighth day, as God commanded. Abraham was 100 years old.

Before Isaac was born, Abraham had fathered a son named **Ishmael** through Sarah's Egyptian maid, **Hagar**. 
1. During a feast to celebrate Isaac being weaned, Sarah saw Ishmael mocking (or playing with) Isaac.
2. Sarah demanded that Abraham cast out Hagar and Ishmael, declaring that Ishmael would not share the inheritance with Isaac.
3. Abraham was very distressed, but God told him not to be displeased. God told Abraham to do whatever Sarah said, because **"through Isaac shall your descendants be named."** 
4. God also promised to make a great nation of Ishmael because he was Abraham's offspring. Abraham then sent Hagar and Ishmael away with bread and a skin of water.

### The continuation of the covenant line (Esau and Jacob)
The covenant promise was passed from Abraham to Isaac. Genesis 25 shows how the promise passed to the next generation.
1. Isaac was 40 when he married **Rebekah**. She was barren, so Isaac prayed to the Lord, and she conceived twins.
2. The babies struggled together in her womb. God revealed to Rebekah that two nations were in her womb, and **"the elder shall serve the younger."**
3. The firstborn came out red and hairy; they named him **Esau**. His brother came out with his hand gripping Esau's heel; they named him **Jacob**.
4. Despite custom dictating that the firstborn (Esau) should inherit the blessing, God chose Jacob to carry on the covenant line, demonstrating that God's promises depend on His sovereign choice.

## God's covenant with Israel

**Content c: God’s covenant with Israel (Ex. 19; 20; 24:1-11) cf. Deut. 28:1-19**

Centuries later, Abraham's descendants (the Israelites) were slaves in Egypt. God rescued them through Moses. In the third month after leaving Egypt, they arrived at the wilderness of Mount Sinai. Here, God made a **national covenant** with them.

### Arrival at Sinai and the Proposal
In Exodus 19, God gave Moses a message for the Israelites. If they would obey His voice and keep His covenant, they would be:
1. His own **possession** among all peoples.
2. A **kingdom of priests**.
3. A **holy nation**.

The people unanimously agreed, saying, "All that the Lord has spoken we will do." God commanded them to consecrate themselves (purify themselves and wash their garments) for three days. On the third day, God descended on Mount Sinai in a thick cloud, with thunder, lightning, smoke like a kiln, an earthquake, and a loud trumpet blast. 

### The Ten Commandments (The Law)
In Exodus 20, God spoke the conditions of the covenant, known as the Ten Commandments (the Decalogue). These laws regulated their relationship with God (commandments 1–4, such as having no other gods, keeping the Sabbath) and their relationships with one another (commandments 5–10, such as honouring parents, and prohibitions against murder, adultery, theft, false witness, and coveting).

### Ratification of the Covenant
A covenant in the ancient world required a formal ceremony to seal it, usually involving blood. The ratification process (Exodus 24:1-11) happened step-by-step:
1. Moses told the people God's laws, and they promised to obey.
2. Moses wrote down all the words of the Lord.
3. He built an altar at the foot of the mountain and set up **twelve pillars** representing the twelve tribes of Israel.
4. Young men offered burnt offerings and peace offerings (oxen) to the Lord.
5. Moses took half of the animal **blood** and threw it against the altar (representing God).
6. He read the **Book of the Covenant** to the people, and they promised again to be obedient.
7. Moses took the remaining half of the blood and **sprinkled it on the people**, saying, *"Behold the blood of the covenant which the Lord has made with you."* The blood bound God and the people together in the agreement.
8. After this, Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders of Israel went up the mountain. They saw a vision of the God of Israel and ate a covenant meal together without being harmed.

He is holding a basin and tossing red blood toward a large crowd of Israelites who are raising their hands in agreement. Clean flat textbook style, white background.]

### The Implications: Blessings and Curses
Because the Sinai covenant was **conditional**, it had strict consequences, detailed in Deuteronomy 28:
- **Blessings for obedience:** If Israel faithfully obeyed God, they would be set high above all nations. They would be blessed in the city and the field, blessed in their offspring and crops. Their enemies would be defeated, and God would make them "the head and not the tail."
- **Curses for disobedience:** If they failed to obey, they would be cursed in the city and field. They would suffer from diseases, drought, and famine. They would be defeated by their enemies, struck with madness and blindness, and ultimately driven out of the Promised Land.

## Distinguishing between the covenants with Noah, Abraham and Israel

**Objective iii: distinguish between God’s covenants with Noah, Abraham and Israel.**

These three Old Testament covenants share the concept of God making promises, but they differ widely in their scope, conditions, and signs.

| Feature | Noahic Covenant (Gen 9) | Abrahamic Covenant (Gen 17) | Mosaic / Sinai Covenant (Ex 19-24) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parties involved** | God and **all living flesh** (humanity and animals). | God, Abraham, and his specific descendants. | God and the **Nation of Israel**. |
| **Scope** | Universal (entire earth). | Familial (one family line). | National (a chosen country). |
| **Conditionality** | **Unconditional.** God promised regardless of human behaviour. | **Unconditional** (largely), but required Abraham to walk blamelessly and exercise faith. | **Conditional.** Required strict obedience to the Law. |
| **Main Promise** | Never to destroy the earth with a flood again. | Land (Canaan), many descendants, and to be a blessing to all nations. | Israel would be God's treasured possession and a holy nation. |
| **Sign of the Covenant** | The **Rainbow**. | **Circumcision** of males on the 8th day. | **Blood sprinkled** on the altar and the people (also keeping the Sabbath). |

## The New Covenant

**Content d: The New Covenant (Jer. 31:31-34; Ezek. 36:25-28)**

The history of the Israelites showed that they could not keep the conditional Sinai (Old) Covenant. They repeatedly fell into idolatry and disobedience, which led to their defeat and exile. During this dark time, God spoke through the prophets Jeremiah and Ezekiel, promising a completely **New Covenant**.

### Jeremiah's Prophecy
Jeremiah prophesied (Jeremiah 31:31-34) that days were coming when God would make a new covenant with the house of Israel and Judah. 
- **Not like the old one:** It would not be like the covenant made at Sinai, which the people broke.
- **Internalised Law:** Instead of laws written on stone tablets, God said, *"I will put my law within them, and I will write it upon their hearts."* 
- **Personal relationship:** God declared, "I will be their God, and they shall be my people." People would no longer need to teach their neighbours saying, "Know the Lord," because from the least to the greatest, **they shall all know Him personally**.
- **Total forgiveness:** God promised, *"I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more."*

### Ezekiel's Prophecy
Ezekiel gave a similar prophecy focusing on spiritual cleansing and transformation (Ezekiel 36:25-28):
- **Cleansing:** God promised to sprinkle clean water on the people to cleanse them from all their uncleanness and their idols.
- **A New Heart:** God said, *"A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you."*
- **Heart of flesh:** God promised to remove their stubborn, unresponsive **"heart of stone"** and replace it with a tender, obedient **"heart of flesh."**
- **The Holy Spirit:** God promised to put His own Spirit within them to empower them, causing them to walk in His statutes and obey His rules. They would dwell safely in the land and be His people.

For Christians, this New Covenant was established by Jesus Christ through His death and resurrection (when Jesus said at the Last Supper, "This cup is the new covenant in my blood").

## Distinguishing between the Old and the New Covenants

**Objective iv: distinguish between the old and the new covenants.**

The Old Covenant (made at Sinai) and the New Covenant (prophesied by Jeremiah and Ezekiel, fulfilled in Christ) have distinct differences in how humans relate to God.

| Feature | The Old Covenant (Sinai) | The New Covenant (Jeremiah/Ezekiel) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Location of the Law** | Written externally on **tablets of stone**. | Written internally on **people's hearts**. |
| **Nature of Obedience** | Demanded obedience from the outside; the people had a stubborn "heart of stone". | Empowered from the inside by God's Spirit; people are given a responsive "heart of flesh". |
| **Knowledge of God** | Mediated by priests and prophets; people had to be taught to "know the Lord". | Direct and personal; all will know the Lord directly. |
| **Forgiveness of Sin** | Required repeated animal sacrifices that only temporarily covered sin. | Absolute and permanent forgiveness ("I will remember their sin no more"). |
| **Success / Failure** | Broken by the people because of human weakness and disobedience. | Unbreakable, because God provides both the rules and the internal power (His Spirit) to keep them. |

## Importance and implication of the covenants

**Objective ii: examine the importance and implication of the covenants.**

1. **God’s Initiative:** Covenants show that God takes the first step in desiring a relationship with humanity. He reaches out to save and bless people.
2. **The Necessity of Obedience and Faith:** In both Abraham's and Israel's covenants, God requires a response. Faith (believing God's promise) and obedience (keeping the conditions) are essential for experiencing God's blessings.
3. **God's Faithfulness:** Even when humans fail (as Israel broke the Sinai covenant), God remains faithful to His ultimate promises, paving the way for a better, New Covenant.
4. **Progressive Revelation:** The covenants show God's unfolding plan. He started with the whole world (Noah), focused on one family (Abraham), built a nation (Israel), and finally promised an internal, spiritual transformation for all people (the New Covenant).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Covenant** | A binding agreement or pact between God and humans, involving promises, conditions, and a sign. |
| **Conditional Covenant** | An agreement where God's fulfilment of promises depends on human obedience. |
| **Unconditional Covenant** | An agreement where God promises to act regardless of human actions. |
| **Decalogue** | The Ten Commandments given to Moses at Mount Sinai. |
| **Circumcision** | The removal of the foreskin on the 8th day; the physical sign of the Abrahamic covenant. |
| **Ratification** | The formal sealing or confirming of a covenant, often done with blood. |
| **Heart of stone / flesh** | Ezekiel's metaphor for a stubborn, disobedient heart (stone) versus a transformed, responsive heart (flesh). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Match the sign to the covenant:** JAMB frequently tests this. Rainbow = Noah. Circumcision = Abraham. Blood on altar/people = Israel (Sinai).
- **Know the specific Bible characters:** Abraham's covenant child was **Isaac** (not Ishmael). The promise passed to **Jacob** (not Esau), fulfilling "the elder shall serve the younger."
- **Jeremiah vs. Ezekiel:** Know which prophet said what. **Jeremiah** spoke about the law written on the *heart* and forgiving sin. **Ezekiel** specifically used the phrases *clean water*, *new spirit*, and replacing the *heart of stone* with a *heart of flesh*.
- **Conditional vs. Unconditional:** Remember that the Sinai (Mosaic) Covenant is the classic conditional covenant ("If you obey..."). Noah and Abraham's covenants are primarily unconditional. 
- **The Ratification blood:** In Exodus 24, Moses threw half the blood on the altar (representing God) and half on the people. This sealed the covenant.

## Quick summary

1. A biblical covenant is a binding agreement initiated by God, featuring promises, conditions, and a sign.
2. God made a universal, unconditional covenant with Noah never to destroy the earth by flood again, setting the rainbow as a sign.
3. God called Abraham, promising him land, descendants, and worldwide blessing, and instituted circumcision on the 8th day as the sign of this covenant.
4. The covenant line continued through Isaac (not Ishmael) and Jacob (not Esau) by God's sovereign choice.
5. At Mount Sinai, God made a conditional national covenant with Israel, giving them the Ten Commandments, which they promised to obey to receive blessings instead of curses.
6. Moses ratified the Sinai covenant by sprinkling the blood of sacrificed animals on the altar and on the people.
7. Because Israel broke the Old Covenant, Jeremiah prophesied a New Covenant where God's law would be written on people's hearts and sins completely forgiven.
8. Ezekiel prophesied that God would replace Israel's stubborn "heart of stone" with a "heart of flesh" and put His Spirit in them.

## Practice questions

**1. The sign of the covenant between God and Noah was the**

- A. rainbow in the cloud
- B. circumcision of males
- C. sprinkling of blood
- D. keeping of the Sabbath

**Answer: A.** The rainbow was given in Genesis 9 as the permanent sign that God would never again destroy the earth with a flood.

**2. In the covenant with Abraham, God commanded that every male child should be circumcised on the**

- A. first day
- B. third day
- C. seventh day
- D. eighth day

**Answer: D.** Genesis 17:12 specifies that every male must be circumcised at eight days old as a sign of the covenant.

**3. According to God's promise to Abraham, the everlasting covenant was established specifically through**

- A. Ishmael
- B. Lot
- C. Isaac
- D. Jacob

**Answer: C.** In Genesis 17:19 and 21:12, God explicitly states that the everlasting covenant would be established with Sarah's son, Isaac.

**4. During the ratification of the covenant at Mount Sinai, Moses threw half of the blood on the altar and the other half on the**

- A. twelve pillars
- B. priests
- C. people
- D. Book of the Covenant

**Answer: C.** Exodus 24:8 records Moses sprinkling the remaining blood on the people, saying, "Behold the blood of the covenant."

**5. According to Deuteronomy 28, a major consequence of Israel disobeying the covenant was that they would**

- A. be destroyed by a flood
- B. suffer diseases and defeat by enemies
- C. wander in the wilderness for forty years
- D. lose the Ark of the Covenant

**Answer: B.** Deuteronomy 28 lists curses for disobedience, which include diseases, drought, famine, madness, and defeat by enemies.

**6. Which prophet prophesied that in the New Covenant, God would write His law upon the hearts of the people?**

- A. Isaiah
- B. Ezekiel
- C. Jeremiah
- D. Hosea

**Answer: C.** Jeremiah 31:33 contains the famous prophecy: "I will put my law within them, and I will write it upon their hearts."

**7. In the promise of the New Covenant, God declared He would remove the people's "heart of stone" and give them a**

- A. heart of love
- B. heart of gold
- C. heart of obedience
- D. heart of flesh

**Answer: D.** Ezekiel 36:26 states, "I will take out of your flesh the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh."

**8. A major difference between the covenants with Noah and Israel is that**

- A. Noah's covenant involved blood, while Israel's did not
- B. Noah's covenant was conditional, while Israel's was unconditional
- C. Noah's covenant was universal, while Israel's was national
- D. Noah's covenant had no sign, while Israel's did

**Answer: C.** The Noahic covenant was made with all living flesh everywhere (universal), while the Sinai covenant was made strictly with the nation of Israel.

**9. When Rebekah was pregnant with twins, God told her that**

- A. the younger shall serve the elder
- B. they would become a single great nation
- C. the elder shall serve the younger
- D. she would give birth to kings and priests

**Answer: C.** Genesis 25:23 states that two nations were in her womb, and "the elder shall serve the younger," referring to Esau and Jacob.

**10. Which of the following best describes the fundamental difference between the Old and New Covenants?**

- A. The Old was made by God, the New by Jesus.
- B. The Old was written on stone tablets, the New is written on human hearts.
- C. The Old involved keeping the Sabbath, the New involves circumcision.
- D. The Old was unbreakable, the New can be broken.

**Answer: B.** The Old Covenant (Sinai) was external, written on tablets of stone, whereas the New Covenant is internal, written on the heart (Jeremiah 31:33).
