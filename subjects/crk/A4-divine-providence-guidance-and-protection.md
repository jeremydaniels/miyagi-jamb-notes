# JAMB Crk Notes: Divine Providence, Guidance and Protection

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 4 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Divine Providence, Guidance and Protection. It explores how God led, preserved, and supplied the needs of the Israelites, with lessons for believers today. 

It covers three main content areas:
* (a) Guidance (Ex. 13:17-22; Josh. 8:1-22; Heb. 1:1; Matt. 11:27-30)
* (b) Protection (Ex. 14:10-31; Dan. 6:16-23; Ps. 91)
* (c) Provision (Ex. 16:1-21; 17:1-7; 13:20-22; 1 Kgs. 17:1-16; Num. 20:1-13)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. identify the different ways by which God guided and protected the people of Israel;
2. specify how God provided for His people;
3. identify the different occasions when God provided for Israel;
4. recognize that God still provides for people today.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** The syllabus places Hebrews 1:1 and Matthew 11:27-30 under "Guidance". While these passages do not tell Old Testament stories of physical guidance, they are key verses showing how God guides people *spiritually* (through the prophets and through Jesus Christ). Also, Exodus 13:20-22 is listed under "Provision" although it repeats verses from the "Guidance" section; it highlights that God *provided* His continuous presence through the pillars of cloud and fire.

## Content a: Guidance

The Bible records several instances and ways in which God directed the path of His people.

### Guidance out of Egypt (Exodus 13:17-22)
When Pharaoh finally let the Israelites go, God did not lead them through the land of the Philistines. Even though that route was shorter, God knew that if the people faced war immediately, they might change their minds and return to Egypt. Instead, God guided them round by the way of the wilderness toward the Red Sea. 

To guide them day and night, God provided a visible sign of His presence:
* **By day:** A **pillar of cloud** to lead them on the way.
* **By night:** A **pillar of fire** to give them light. 
Because of this continuous guidance, they could travel by day and by night. 

### Military Guidance: The Capture of Ai (Joshua 8:1-22)
After the Israelites were initially defeated at Ai (due to Achan's sin), God gave Joshua clear military guidance on how to capture the city. God told Joshua not to be afraid and instructed him to take all his fighting men and set an **ambush** behind the city.

**The Strategy:**
1. Joshua chose 30,000 mighty men of valor and sent them by night to hide behind Ai.
2. The next morning, Joshua and the main army marched toward the city to draw the men of Ai out.
3. When the king of Ai saw them, he and his men rushed out to battle.
4. Joshua and his men feigned defeat and fled toward the wilderness, drawing the defenders far away from the city, leaving it completely open.
5. At God's command, Joshua stretched out his spear toward Ai. This was the signal for the men in ambush to rise, enter the city, and set it on fire.
6. When the men of Ai looked back and saw the smoke, they lost heart. Joshua's retreating army turned back to fight them, and the ambush squad came out of the city, trapping the men of Ai in the middle. The Israelites completely defeated them.

### Spiritual Guidance through Prophets and Jesus
The syllabus includes two New Testament passages to show how God guides humanity spiritually:
* **Hebrews 1:1:** *"God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets..."* This verse shows that historically, God guided His people by giving His word through chosen prophets.
* **Matthew 11:27-30:** Jesus says, *"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."* He invites people to take His yoke and learn from Him. Here, Jesus is presented as the ultimate guide who reveals God the Father and leads believers into spiritual rest and truth.

## Content b: Protection

God did not just guide the Israelites; He actively protected them from their enemies and natural dangers.

### Protection at the Red Sea (Exodus 14:10-31)
As the Israelites camped by the Red Sea, Pharaoh and the Egyptian army pursued them with chariots and horsemen. When the Israelites saw the Egyptians, they were terrified and cried out to God, blaming Moses for bringing them into the wilderness to die. 

Moses encouraged them: *"Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord..."* (Exodus 14:13). God then intervened to protect them:
1. **The Cloud moves:** The angel of God and the pillar of cloud moved from the front of the Israelites and stood behind them, coming between the camp of Israel and the camp of Egypt. It brought darkness to the Egyptians but light to the Israelites, preventing the Egyptians from coming near them all night.
2. **The Sea divides:** Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and God drove the sea back with a strong east wind, turning the seabed into dry land with walls of water on the right and the left.
3. **The Crossing and Deliverance:** The Israelites crossed safely. The Egyptians pursued them into the sea, but God jammed their chariot wheels, causing them to panic. Moses stretched his hand over the sea again at daybreak, and the waters returned, drowning the entire Egyptian army. The Israelites saw God's great power and believed the Lord and His servant Moses.

### Protection in the Lions' Den (Daniel 6:16-23)
During the reign of King Darius of Babylon (Medo-Persian empire), other officials were jealous of Daniel's success. They tricked the king into signing a decree that anyone who prayed to any god or man except the king for thirty days would be cast into the lions' den. 

Daniel continued to pray to God three times a day. He was arrested, and King Darius, though deeply sorrowful, was forced by the law to throw Daniel into the lions' den. The king fasted all night. In the morning, Darius rushed to the den and called out to Daniel. Daniel replied that God had sent an angel to shut the lions' mouths because he was found innocent before God and the king. Daniel was brought out without a single scratch, showing God's miraculous protection over those who trust Him.

### Protection in Psalm 91
Psalm 91 is a famous song describing God's comprehensive protection over believers. It begins: *"He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty."*
The psalm promises that God protects His people from various dangers, including:
* The snare of the fowler (hidden traps/enemies).
* Noisome pestilence (deadly diseases).
* The terror by night and the arrow that flies by day.
* It states that God will give His angels charge over the believer to keep them in all their ways, bearing them up so they do not even dash their foot against a stone.

## Objective i: Identify the different ways by which God guided and protected the people of Israel

Based on the biblical accounts, God guided and protected the people of Israel in several distinct ways:

| Ways God Guided | Ways God Protected |
| --- | --- |
| **Supernatural signs:** He used the physical presence of the pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night to lead them on the safest routes (Exodus 13). | **Manipulating nature:** He parted the Red Sea and brought it crashing back down to destroy the pursuing Egyptian army (Exodus 14). |
| **Strategic military instruction:** He gave specific tactical plans, such as the ambush strategy for defeating Ai, ensuring their victory (Joshua 8). | **Angelic intervention:** He sent angels to protect individuals from certain death, such as shutting the mouths of the lions for Daniel (Daniel 6). |
| **Through the Prophets:** In the Old Testament, God guided the nation's morals and actions through messages delivered by prophets (Hebrews 1:1). | **Spiritual covering:** He provided a "secret place" and a "shadow" of protection, assigning angels to guard individuals from disease, traps, and attacks (Psalm 91). |
| **Through Jesus Christ:** Ultimately, God provided a personal guide for humanity through the teachings, yoke, and rest offered by Jesus (Matthew 11). | **Physical barriers:** The pillar of cloud moved to block the Egyptian army from reaching the Israelites at the Red Sea (Exodus 14). |

## Content c: Provision

God supplied the physical needs of His people in miraculous ways when they were in desperate situations.

### The Pillars of Cloud and Fire (Exodus 13:20-22)
The syllabus re-emphasizes the pillars here. As the Israelites journeyed from **Succoth to Etham** (the edge of the wilderness), God continuously *provided* His presence. The pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire did not depart from before the people, ensuring they were never left without a guide or light.

### Manna and Quails in the Wilderness of Sin (Exodus 16:1-21)
About one month after leaving Egypt (Exodus 16:1 dates it to the fifteenth day of the second month), the Israelites arrived in the Wilderness of Sin. Their food ran out, and the whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron, wishing they had died in Egypt where they had pots of meat and bread. 

God told Moses He would rain bread from heaven. 
* **Quails:** That evening, quails came up and covered the camp, providing meat.
* **Manna:** The next morning, a fine, flake-like thing like hoarfrost appeared on the ground. The Israelites called it *Manna* (meaning "What is it?"). It tasted like wafers made with honey.

**God's instructions for the Manna:**
1. Gather a specific amount (an *omer*) per person each day.
2. Do not leave any of it until morning. Some disobeyed, and the leftover manna bred worms and stank.
3. When the sun grew hot, the ungathered manna melted.
4. On the sixth day, they were to gather twice as much because the seventh day was the Sabbath, and no manna would fall. The manna kept for the Sabbath did not rot.

### Elijah fed by Ravens and the Widow (1 Kings 17:1-16)
During the reign of King Ahab, the prophet Elijah announced a severe drought as judgment for Israel's idolatry. God provided for Elijah in two ways during this famine:

1. **At the Brook Cherith:** God told Elijah to hide by the Brook Cherith (east of the Jordan). God commanded **ravens** to bring him bread and meat every morning and evening, and he drank from the brook.
2. **At Zarephath:** When the brook dried up because there was no rain, God sent Elijah to Zarephath (in Sidon) to a widow. When Elijah arrived, the widow was gathering sticks to cook a final meal of a handful of meal (flour) and a little oil for herself and her son before they died of starvation. Elijah told her not to fear, but to make a small cake for him *first*, promising that according to God's word, her jar of meal and cruse of oil would not run out until the rain returned. She obeyed, and God miraculously multiplied the food; it did not fail for many days.

### Water from the Rock: Rephidim vs. Kadesh
God provided water from a rock on two different occasions, which students often confuse. The syllabus requires knowledge of both (Exodus 17 and Numbers 20).

| Feature | Rephidim (Exodus 17:1-7) | Kadesh in Wilderness of Zin (Numbers 20:1-13) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Context** | Early in the wilderness wandering; people had no water and murmured against Moses. | Late in the wandering; Miriam had just died; people murmured bitterly about having no water or grain. |
| **God's Command** | God told Moses to take his rod and **strike (smite)** the rock in Horeb. | God told Moses to take the rod, gather the assembly, and **speak** to the rock before their eyes. |
| **Moses' Action** | Moses struck the rock as commanded, in the sight of the elders. | Moses, angry with the "rebels," **struck the rock twice** instead of speaking to it. |
| **The Result** | Water came out abundantly for the people to drink. | Water came out abundantly for the people and their livestock to drink. |
| **The Consequence** | Moses called the place **Massah** (testing) and **Meribah** (quarreling). | God punished Moses and Aaron for their unbelief/disobedience, declaring **they would not enter the Promised Land**. The water was called the water of Meribah. |

## Objective ii: Specify how God provided for His people

God was not limited to one method of provision. He supplied the needs of the Israelites through various means:

1. **Through Nature and Natural Elements:** God used natural occurrences, timed perfectly, to provide. He sent a strong east wind to part the Red Sea, and He caused water to flow from solid rocks when struck or spoken to.
2. **Through Animals:** God used creatures to feed His people. He brought massive flocks of quails into the wilderness camp to provide meat, and He commanded ravens to bring bread and flesh to Elijah twice a day.
3. **Through Miraculous Sustenance:** God rained Manna directly from heaven for forty years in the wilderness. He also miraculously prevented a widow's small jar of flour and oil from running out during a long famine.
4. **Through Human Agents:** God used the widow of Zarephath, a poor Gentile woman, to sustain the great prophet Elijah.
5. **Through Continuous Supernatural Presence:** By manifesting as a pillar of cloud and fire, God provided the physical and spiritual comfort of His presence.

## Objective iii: Identify the different occasions when God provided for Israel

The syllabus focuses on several specific, critical occasions where God stepped in to provide:

* **The departure from Succoth to Etham:** When they first left Egypt and entered the wilderness, God provided the pillars of cloud and fire to reassure and guide them.
* **In the Wilderness of Sin:** When the people faced starvation shortly after leaving Egypt, God provided Manna (bread from heaven) and Quails (meat).
* **At Rephidim (Massah and Meribah):** When the people were dying of thirst, God provided water from the rock at Horeb after Moses struck it.
* **In the Wilderness of Zin (Kadesh):** When the new generation of Israelites faced thirst again near the end of their wanderings, God provided water from the rock, though Moses disobeyed the method.
* **During the National Famine (Ahab's reign):** God provided for His prophet Elijah first by the Brook Cherith, and later at Zarephath through a widow's unending supply of flour and oil.

## Objective iv: Recognize that God still provides for people today

The biblical accounts of God's providence, guidance, and protection are not just historical stories; Christians believe they demonstrate God's unchanging character. Believers in Nigeria today apply these teachings to their daily lives:

1. **Daily Provision:** Just as God gave the Israelites manna daily, Christians pray, *"Give us this day our daily bread,"* trusting God to provide jobs, business opportunities, and harvest for farmers, even during economic recessions or inflation.
2. **Guidance in Decision-Making:** Christians believe God no longer guides with a physical pillar of cloud, but He guides spiritually through the Bible, the Holy Spirit, prayer, and wise counsel from church leaders and parents.
3. **Protection from Danger:** Like the deliverance from the lions' den or the Red Sea, Christians trust God for protection against accidents on the road, sicknesses (like the "pestilence" in Psalm 91), and spiritual attacks. 
4. **Using Human Agents:** Just as God used the widow of Zarephath, Christians believe God often provides for people through the kindness of strangers, the support of family, or the generosity of church members.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Divine Providence | God's continuous involvement in managing, protecting, and providing for His creation. |
| Pillar of Cloud/Fire | The visible manifestation of God's presence that guided Israel out of Egypt and through the wilderness. |
| Manna | The miraculous food that God provided for the Israelites every morning in the wilderness; means "What is it?". |
| Omer | An ancient Israelite unit of dry measure; the daily portion of manna each person was allowed to gather. |
| Massah and Meribah | Names given to the places where Israel tested God and quarreled with Moses over water; meaning "testing" and "quarreling". |
| Ambush | A military strategy of hiding troops to attack the enemy by surprise, used by Joshua at Ai. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Distinguish between the two "Water from the Rock" stories:** Rephidim (Exodus 17) is where Moses was told to *strike* the rock. Kadesh (Numbers 20) is where Moses was told to *speak* to the rock but angrily struck it twice, costing him entry into the Promised Land.
* **Know the Manna rules:** Gather one omer per person, do not leave it overnight (except on the sixth day for the Sabbath).
* **Identify the locations:** Elijah was fed by ravens at the *Brook Cherith* and by the widow at *Zarephath*.
* **Understand the capture of Ai:** God gave Joshua a specific military tactic—an ambush behind the city, accompanied by a feigned retreat to draw the defenders out.
* **Do not confuse the kings:** Daniel was thrown into the lions' den under King *Darius* (Medo-Persian empire), not Nebuchadnezzar.

## Quick summary

1. God guided the Israelites out of Egypt away from the Philistine route to avoid war, leading them by a pillar of cloud by day and fire by night.
2. God gave Joshua specific military guidance to defeat Ai using a well-planned ambush and a feigned retreat.
3. God protected the Israelites at the Red Sea by moving the cloud behind them to block the Egyptians, parting the sea for Israel, and drowning the pursuing army.
4. God protected Daniel in the lions' den by sending an angel to shut the lions' mouths.
5. In the Wilderness of Sin, God provided Manna in the morning and Quails in the evening to save Israel from starvation.
6. God provided water from rocks on two occasions: at Rephidim (where Moses struck the rock as commanded) and Kadesh (where Moses struck the rock in anger instead of speaking to it).
7. During a drought, God provided for Elijah through ravens at the Brook Cherith and miraculously multiplied a widow's flour and oil at Zarephath.
8. Christians believe God's providence continues today, providing daily needs, spiritual guidance, and protection against dangers.

## Practice questions

**1. Why did God lead the Israelites through the wilderness of the Red Sea rather than the nearer land of the Philistines?**
- A. The Israelites needed to be tested by the lack of water.
- B. God wanted to avoid the Israelites seeing war and returning to Egypt.
- C. Moses did not know the way through the land of the Philistines.
- D. The Philistines had already formed an alliance with Pharaoh.

**Answer: B.** God knew that if the people faced immediate warfare with the Philistines, they might become afraid, change their minds, and return to Egypt (Exodus 13:17).

**2. What strategy did God instruct Joshua to use in order to conquer the city of Ai?**
- A. March around the city walls for seven days.
- B. Send spies to bribe the guards of the city.
- C. Set an ambush behind the city and feign a retreat.
- D. Attack the city at night with torches and trumpets.

**Answer: C.** God commanded Joshua to set an ambush behind Ai. The main army feigned a retreat to draw the men of Ai out, allowing the ambush to take the city (Joshua 8).

**3. Before the Red Sea parted, how did God prevent the Egyptian army from attacking the Israelites at night?**
- A. He sent a swarm of locusts to blind the Egyptians.
- B. The pillar of cloud moved and stood between the two camps.
- C. He caused a deep sleep to fall upon Pharaoh and his charioteers.
- D. He sent hail and lightning to destroy the Egyptian chariots.

**Answer: B.** The angel of God and the pillar of cloud moved from the front to the back, separating the Israelites from the Egyptians and bringing darkness to the Egyptians (Exodus 14).

**4. According to Hebrews 1:1, how did God primarily speak to and guide the fathers in time past?**
- A. Through dreams and visions.
- B. By the casting of lots.
- C. By the prophets.
- D. Through angels appearing as men.

**Answer: C.** Hebrews 1:1 states clearly that God "spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets."

**5. Which of the following was a strict instruction God gave the Israelites regarding the gathering of Manna?**
- A. They must gather enough for a whole week on the first day.
- B. They must leave some of it on the ground as an offering.
- C. They must gather twice as much on the Sabbath day.
- D. They must not leave any of it until the morning.

**Answer: D.** God instructed them to gather only what they needed for the day (one omer) and not leave any overnight. The only exception was gathering twice as much on the sixth day for the Sabbath (Exodus 16).

**6. At Kadesh in the Wilderness of Zin, why did God tell Moses that he would not bring the assembly into the Promised Land?**
- A. Moses struck the rock twice instead of speaking to it.
- B. Moses refused to strike the rock out of fear.
- C. Moses allowed the people to worship a golden calf.
- D. Moses complained to God that the burden of the people was too heavy.

**Answer: A.** At Kadesh, God told Moses to *speak* to the rock. In anger, Moses struck it twice. Because he disobeyed and failed to honor God before the people, he was forbidden from entering the Promised Land (Numbers 20).

**7. How did God provide for Elijah when the Brook Cherith dried up?**
- A. He sent an angel to bake him bread on hot coals.
- B. He directed him to go to a widow in Zarephath.
- C. He caused a new spring of water to gush from a rock.
- D. He sent him back to the palace of King Ahab.

**Answer: B.** When the brook dried up because of the drought, God sent Elijah to Zarephath, where a widow miraculously sustained him with a jar of flour and a cruse of oil that did not run out (1 Kings 17).

**8. Which Psalm extensively describes God's protection from the snare of the fowler, the noisome pestilence, and the terror by night?**
- A. Psalm 23
- B. Psalm 51
- C. Psalm 91
- D. Psalm 119

**Answer: C.** Psalm 91 is a profound declaration of God's protection, stating that whoever dwells in the secret place of the Most High will be delivered from these exact dangers.

**9. When King Darius came to the lions' den the morning after Daniel was thrown in, what did Daniel say had protected him?**
- A. The lions were full and refused to eat him.
- B. God sent an angel to shut the lions' mouths.
- C. Daniel used his wisdom to tame the lions.
- D. A bright fire burned in the den, keeping the lions away.

**Answer: B.** Daniel told King Darius, "My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me" (Daniel 6:22).

**10. What specific provision did God make for the Israelites as they journeyed from Succoth to Etham?**
- A. He provided Manna and Quails for the first time.
- B. He provided twelve wells of water and seventy palm trees.
- C. He provided the continuous presence of the pillar of cloud and fire.
- D. He provided them with swords and shields from the Egyptians.

**Answer: C.** The syllabus highlights Exodus 13:20-22, which records that as they journeyed from Succoth to Etham, God provided His presence through the pillars of cloud and fire, which did not depart from them.
