# JAMB Crk Notes: Decision - Making

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 8 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Decision - Making. It explores the different ways Israel’s early kings made critical choices, the sources they relied upon, and the profound consequences of their decisions. It covers three main content areas:

- **(a) Reliance on a medium** (I Sam. 28:3-25)
- **(b) The wisdom of Solomon** (I Kings 3:3-28; 4:29-34; 5:1-12; 8:1-53)
- **(c) Unwise policies of Solomon and Rehoboam** (I Kings 9:15-23; 11:1-40; 12:1-20)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Identify the source of Solomon’s wisdom.
2. Compare the different ways used by Saul and Solomon in making decisions.
3. Analyse the decisions made by Saul, Solomon and Rehoboam.
4. Assess the consequences of Solomon and Rehoboam’s unwise decisions.

## Content (a): Reliance on a medium (1 Sam. 28:3-25)

The story of Saul at Endor is one of the most tragic episodes in the Bible, showing a king who had completely lost his connection with God and resorted to forbidden practices in a moment of panic.

### The Background
By this time, the prophet **Samuel was dead**, and all Israel had mourned him and buried him in Ramah. Earlier in his reign, King Saul had made a righteous decision to banish all mediums (people who consult the dead) and wizards from the land of Israel, according to the Law of Moses.

When the Philistines gathered their army at **Shunem**, Saul gathered the Israelites at **Gilboa**. When Saul saw the massive Philistine army, he was terrified, and his heart trembled greatly.

### Saul's Desperation
Saul needed guidance on what to do. He inquired of the LORD, but **God did not answer him**—not by dreams, not by the Urim (sacred lots used by priests), and not by prophets. Because of Saul’s earlier disobedience (such as sparing the Amalekite king, covered in `A6-obedience-and-disobedience.md`), God's Spirit had departed from him.

### The Visit to the Medium at Endor
Desperate for answers, Saul made a terrible decision. He ordered his servants to find a woman who was a medium. They told him there was one at **Endor**. 

1. **The Disguise:** Saul disguised himself by putting on different clothes and went at night with two men to see the woman.
2. **The Request:** He asked her to divine for him by a spirit and bring up the person he would name. 
3. **The Woman's Fear:** The woman hesitated, reminding him that King Saul had cut off mediums from the land, and she feared this was a trap to kill her. Saul swore an oath by the LORD that no punishment would come to her.
4. **Calling up Samuel:** Saul asked her to bring up **Samuel**. When the woman saw the spirit, she cried out loudly, suddenly realising that her disguised client was King Saul himself. Saul calmed her and asked what she saw. She said she saw a divine being coming up out of the earth—an old man wrapped in a robe. Saul knew it was Samuel and bowed his face to the ground.

### Samuel's Final Prophecy
Samuel asked Saul, "Why have you disturbed me by bringing me up?" 
Saul explained his distress: the Philistines were warring against him, and God had turned away from him and refused to answer him.

Samuel's response offered no comfort. He delivered a harsh judgment:
- He asked why Saul was consulting him when the LORD had become Saul’s enemy.
- He confirmed that God had torn the kingdom from Saul's hand and given it to his neighbor, **David**.
- He stated the reason: Saul did not obey the voice of the LORD and did not carry out His fierce wrath against Amalek.
- **The Doom:** Samuel prophesied that God would give Israel into the hands of the Philistines, and by the next day, Saul and his sons would be dead and with Samuel in the afterlife.

### Saul's Reaction
Hearing this, Saul fell full length on the ground, completely paralyzed by fear and physical weakness (he had eaten nothing all day and night). The medium and Saul's servants urged him to eat so he would have the strength to travel. Initially, he refused, but they compelled him. The woman quickly killed a fattened calf, baked unleavened bread, and set it before them. They ate and left that same night.

## Content (b): The wisdom of Solomon (1 Kings 3:3-28; 4:29-34; 5:1-12; 8:1-53)

Solomon’s early reign was marked by brilliant, God-guided decision-making that brought peace and prosperity to Israel.

### Solomon's Request at Gibeon (1 Kings 3:3-28)
Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of his father David, though he sacrificed at the "high places" because the Temple was not yet built. The most important high place was at **Gibeon**, where Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings.

At Gibeon, the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night and said, **"Ask what I shall give you."**

Solomon's response showed great humility. He praised God for His steadfast love to David. He then confessed his own inexperience, calling himself "but a little child" who did not know how to go out or come in, yet he was tasked with leading a great and numerous people. 
Therefore, Solomon asked for **an understanding mind (heart) to govern the people and to discern between good and evil.**

God was highly pleased with this decision. Because Solomon did not ask for selfish things—like long life, riches, or the death of his enemies—God granted him unparalleled wisdom. Furthermore, God promised to give him the things he *did not* ask for: **riches and honor**, so that no king would compare with him. 

### The Wisdom of Solomon Tested: The Two Harlots
Solomon's new wisdom was immediately put to the test. Two prostitutes came before the king.
1. They lived in the same house and had both given birth to baby boys three days apart.
2. One night, one of the women accidentally lay on her baby and smothered it. Finding her child dead, she quietly swapped it with the other woman's living child while she slept.
3. In the morning, the second woman realised the dead baby was not hers. They argued bitterly before the king.

Solomon made a shocking decision to reveal the truth. He called for a sword and ordered that the living child be cut in two, with half given to each woman. 
- The **fake mother** greedily agreed: "It shall be neither mine nor yours; divide it."
- The **true mother**, moved by deep love for her son, begged the king to give the living child to the other woman, just to save its life.

Solomon immediately pointed to the woman who wanted to save the baby and said, "Give the living child to her, and by no means slay it; she is its mother." When all Israel heard of this judgment, they stood in awe of the king, perceiving that the wisdom of God was in him.

### The Extent of Solomon's Wisdom (1 Kings 4:29-34)
God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure, "like the sand on the seashore." 
- His wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the East and all the wisdom of Egypt.
- He was wiser than famously wise men of his time (Ethan the Ezrahite, Heman, Calcol, and Darda).
- He spoke **3,000 proverbs** and wrote **1,005 songs**.
- He was an expert in natural sciences: he spoke of trees (from the cedar of Lebanon to the hyssop on the wall), beasts, birds, reptiles, and fish.
- People of all nations, sent by kings who had heard of his wisdom, came to listen to him.

### The Treaty with Hiram of Tyre (1 Kings 5:1-12)
Solomon used his wisdom to make excellent diplomatic decisions. **Hiram, king of Tyre** (in modern-day Lebanon), had been a friend of David. When Hiram heard Solomon was king, he sent envoys.
Solomon sent a message back, declaring his intention to build a Temple for the LORD, a task David could not do because of constant wars. 

Solomon asked Hiram to command his servants to cut **cedars of Lebanon** for the Temple, as the Sidonians were the best lumberjacks. In exchange, Solomon offered to pay Hiram's servants whatever wages he set.
Hiram rejoiced greatly and agreed to send timber down to the sea, float it in rafts to Israel, and break it up there. In return, Solomon made a wise trade agreement, providing Hiram's household with massive amounts of wheat and beaten oil year by year. They made a treaty of peace.

### The Dedication of the Temple (1 Kings 8:1-53)
Solomon's greatest achievement was building the Temple. When it was finished, he made a major religious decision: assembling the elders of Israel to bring the Ark of the Covenant from the City of David (Zion) to the Temple.
When the priests brought the Ark into the inner sanctuary (the Holy of Holies), a thick cloud filled the house, showing that God's glory had taken residence there.

Solomon then offered a massive **prayer of dedication**. His decisions in this prayer set the spiritual direction for the nation:
- He praised God for keeping His covenant and fulfilling the promise made to David.
- He acknowledged that no building can contain God ("behold, heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain thee; how much less this house which I have built!").
- He asked God to hear the prayers of the people whenever they prayed toward the Temple, whether they faced defeat in battle, drought, famine, disease, or even exile because of sin, and to **forgive them**.

## Content (c): Unwise policies of Solomon and Rehoboam (1 Kings 9:15-23; 11:1-40; 12:1-20)

Despite his great wisdom, Solomon's later years were marked by foolish and oppressive decisions. These mistakes were compounded by his son, Rehoboam, leading to the fracture of the nation.

### Solomon's Forced Labour and Taxation (1 Kings 9:15-23)
To fund his massive building projects—the Temple, his own palace, the Millo (terraces), the wall of Jerusalem, and the cities of Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer—Solomon made a harsh economic decision. He instituted a **forced labor levy**. 
The Bible notes he conscripted the descendants of the Canaanites (Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites, Jebusites) as slave labor. While the text says he did not make slaves of the Israelites (they were his soldiers and officials), the heavy taxation and mandatory national service placed a crushing economic burden on all tribes, planting seeds of deep resentment.

### Solomon's Foreign Wives and Idolatry (1 Kings 11:1-40)
Solomon's most catastrophic decision was spiritual. For political alliances, he married many foreign women, explicitly breaking God’s command against intermarriage with nations that worshipped false gods.
- He had **700 wives** (princesses) and **300 concubines**, including Pharaoh's daughter, and women from Moab, Ammon, Edom, Sidon, and the Hittites.
- In his old age, his wives **turned away his heart after other gods**.
- He built high places (altars) for **Ashtoreth** (goddess of the Sidonians), **Milcom/Molech** (the abomination of the Ammonites), and **Chemosh** (the abomination of Moab).

God was angry with Solomon and pronounced judgment: the kingdom would be torn away from him. However, for the sake of David, God would not do it in Solomon's lifetime, but during his son's reign, and He would leave one tribe (Judah) for his son.

### God Raises Adversaries
As a consequence of his idolatry, God raised up enemies against Solomon:
1. **Hadad the Edomite:** A survivor of David's conquest of Edom who had fled to Egypt and now returned to cause trouble.
2. **Rezon:** A man who gathered a band of marauders, captured Damascus, and became king in Syria, remaining an adversary all Solomon's days.
3. **Jeroboam, son of Nebat:** An Ephraimite whom Solomon had put in charge of the forced labor of the house of Joseph because he was industrious.

**The Prophecy of Ahijah:** The prophet **Ahijah the Shilonite** met Jeroboam on the road. Ahijah took off his own new garment and tore it into **twelve pieces**. He told Jeroboam to take ten pieces, prophesying that God was going to tear the kingdom from Solomon's hand and give **ten tribes** to Jeroboam because of Solomon's idolatry. When Solomon heard of this, he tried to kill Jeroboam, who fled to Egypt to King Shishak until Solomon died.

### Rehoboam's Foolish Decision (1 Kings 12:1-20)
After Solomon's death, his son **Rehoboam** went to **Shechem** to be crowned king. The assembly of Israel, led by the returned Jeroboam, came with a petition: *"Your father made our yoke heavy. Now therefore lighten the hard service of your father and his heavy yoke upon us, and we will serve you."*

Rehoboam asked for three days to make a decision. 
1. **Advice of the Elders:** He first consulted the old men who had served Solomon. They advised diplomacy and servant-leadership: *"If you will be a servant to this people today and serve them, and speak good words to them when you answer them, then they will be your servants for ever."*
2. **Advice of the Youths:** Rehoboam rejected this wise counsel and turned to the young men he had grown up with. They advised harshness and arrogance, telling him to say: *"My little finger is thicker than my father's loins. And now, whereas my father laid upon you a heavy yoke, I will add to your yoke. My father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions."*

Rehoboam chose the foolish advice of the young men and answered the people roughly.

### The Division of the Kingdom
The consequence was immediate rebellion. When all Israel saw that the king refused to listen to them, they cried out:
*"What portion have we in David? We have no inheritance in the son of Jesse. To your tents, O Israel! Look now to your own house, David."*

The northern tribes departed. Rehoboam foolishly sent **Adoram**, his taskmaster in charge of forced labor, to command them, but all Israel stoned Adoram to death. Terrified, Rehoboam fled in his chariot to Jerusalem. The ten northern tribes made **Jeroboam** king over Israel, leaving only the tribe of Judah (and Benjamin) loyal to Rehoboam.

## Objective i: Identify the source of Solomon’s wisdom

The Bible makes it explicitly clear that the source of Solomon's wisdom was **God alone**. 

- It was not the result of human intelligence, intense schooling, or age.
- It was a **divine gift**, granted by God's grace in response to Solomon's humble prayer at Gibeon. 
- When God offered him anything, Solomon acknowledged his own lack of ability ("I am but a little child") and asked only for a discerning heart to serve the people well. 
- Because his motives were pure and selfless, God poured out supernatural understanding upon him.

## Objective ii: Compare the different ways used by Saul and Solomon in making decisions

Saul and Solomon (in his early reign) represented two completely different approaches to leadership and decision-making. 

| Feature | Saul's Decision-Making (at Endor) | Solomon's Early Decision-Making (at Gibeon/Jerusalem) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Motivation** | Fear, desperation, and panic over the Philistine threat. | Humility, love for God, and a desire to serve his people well. |
| **Source consulted** | Forbidden occult practices (a medium) after God refused to answer him due to his past sins. | God Himself, approached through worship (sacrifices) and prayer. |
| **Respect for Law** | Violated God's law and broke his own royal decree against mediums. | Sought to align with God's will and rule with divine justice. |
| **Outcome of decision** | Utter despair, spiritual doom, and a terrifying prophecy of death and defeat. | A supernatural gift of wisdom, immense prosperity, and the awe of his citizens (e.g., the two harlots). |

*Note: In his later years, Solomon's decision-making style shifted away from God and began to resemble the kings of other nations, relying on political marriages (human calculation) rather than divine trust, which led him into the same trap of disobedience as Saul.*

## Objective iii: Analyse the decisions made by Saul, Solomon and Rehoboam

### Analysis of Saul’s Decision (Endor)
- **The Decision:** Consulting a medium to speak to the dead Samuel.
- **Analysis:** This was a decision born out of spiritual bankruptcy. When God is silent because of unrepented sin, a righteous leader should repent. Instead, Saul sought a shortcut through dark, forbidden channels. It showed a complete lack of faith and resulted in psychological collapse before the battle even began.

### Analysis of Solomon’s Decisions
- **The Good (Gibeon & The Temple):** Asking for wisdom instead of wealth showed a leader who understood the weight of responsibility. His decision to build the Temple provided a central unifying point for national worship.
- **The Bad (Foreign Wives & Forced Labor):** Solomon decided to secure his borders through marriages to foreign princesses. Politically, this seemed smart, but spiritually it was disastrous, leading to state-sponsored idolatry. Economically, his decision to undertake massive building projects required crushing taxation and forced labor. He treated his citizens as resources for his own glory rather than people to be protected.

### Analysis of Rehoboam’s Decision
- **The Decision:** Rejecting the elders' advice to show compassion, and choosing the youths' advice to threaten the citizens.
- **Analysis:** Rehoboam’s decision is a classic example of foolish arrogance. He lacked empathy for the suffering of his people. He mistakenly believed that harshness and cruelty equaled strength. By failing to read the political mood and refusing to be a servant-leader, he destroyed the very kingdom he was trying to dominate.

## Objective iv: Assess the consequences of Solomon and Rehoboam’s unwise decisions

The unwise decisions of both kings had catastrophic, long-lasting effects on the history of Israel.

**Consequences of Solomon's decisions:**
1. **Spiritual corruption:** His marriages led to idolatry being established in Jerusalem, angering God and leading the nation away from pure worship.
2. **Rise of enemies:** His actions caused God to raise up adversaries (Hadad and Rezon) who plagued Israel, ending the era of total peace.
3. **Internal unrest:** The heavy burden of forced labor and taxation created deep anger and resentment among the northern tribes. Jeroboam was marked by God (through Ahijah) to lead a future rebellion.

**Consequences of Rehoboam's decisions:**
1. **The Division of the Kingdom:** Rehoboam's harsh words immediately triggered a revolt. The unified nation of Israel permanently split into two: the **Northern Kingdom (Israel)** with ten tribes under Jeroboam, and the **Southern Kingdom (Judah)** with two tribes under Rehoboam.
2. **Loss of Life and Authority:** The immediate stoning of Adoram showed that Rehoboam had completely lost control and respect.
3. **Vulnerability:** Divided, both kingdoms became weaker militarily and economically, making them easy targets for foreign empires (like Egypt, and later Assyria and Babylon).

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Who was the prophet that tore the garment?** It was **Ahijah**, not Samuel or Nathan. Make sure you connect Ahijah with Jeroboam.
- **Remember the pieces:** Ahijah tore the garment into 12 pieces and gave **10** to Jeroboam.
- **Identify the adversaries:** Know the names of the men God raised against Solomon: **Hadad** (the Edomite), **Rezon** (of Damascus), and **Jeroboam**.
- **Distinguish the kings:** Do not confuse Jeroboam (the rebel who took the North) with Rehoboam (Solomon's son who kept the South). 
- **The elders' vs youths' advice:** The elders advised Rehoboam to be a *servant* to the people. The youths advised him to use *scorpions* (harsh whips).
- **Saul's motive:** Saul went to Endor because God refused to answer him by dreams, Urim, or prophets.

## Quick summary

1. When God stopped answering Saul because of his disobedience, Saul panicked and unlawfully consulted a medium at Endor.
2. The medium summoned the spirit of Samuel, who prophesied Saul's defeat and death by the Philistines.
3. God was the source of Solomon's wisdom, giving it as a gift when Solomon humbly asked for a discerning heart to govern the people.
4. Solomon demonstrated this wisdom by correctly identifying the true mother of a baby, composing proverbs, and making a wise treaty with King Hiram for Temple building materials.
5. Despite his wisdom, Solomon foolishly married many foreign women who led him into idolatry, angering God.
6. Solomon also burdened the people with heavy taxation and forced labor to build his grand projects.
7. Solomon's son, Rehoboam, foolishly rejected the elders' advice to lighten the people's burden, choosing instead to threaten them with harsher treatment.
8. The consequence was the permanent division of the nation, with ten tribes breaking away to form the Northern Kingdom under Jeroboam.

## Practice questions

**1. King Saul disguised himself and visited the medium of Endor because**

- A. Samuel had instructed him to do so before he died.
- B. he wanted to find out where David was hiding.
- C. God had refused to answer his inquiries about the Philistine battle.
- D. he wanted to repeal his own law against wizards.

**Answer: C.** Saul was terrified of the Philistine army, and because God did not answer him by dreams, Urim, or prophets, he desperately sought a medium.

**2. What did Solomon ask for when God appeared to him in a dream at Gibeon?**

- A. Riches and honor to outshine all other kings.
- B. An understanding mind to govern the people.
- C. Long life and the death of his enemies.
- D. A blueprint to build the Temple of the LORD.

**Answer: B.** Solomon humbly asked for an understanding heart to discern between good and evil, so he could properly govern God's great people.

**3. The source of Solomon’s great wisdom was**

- A. the ancient books of the Egyptians.
- B. the teaching of his father, David.
- C. the elders of Israel.
- D. God.

**Answer: D.** God was the sole source, granting it as a divine gift in response to Solomon's selfless prayer.

**4. How did Solomon first demonstrate his divine wisdom to the nation of Israel?**

- A. By naming all the animals and plants in the kingdom.
- B. By defeating Hadad the Edomite in battle.
- C. By proposing to divide a living child to discover its true mother.
- D. By building the Millo and the wall of Jerusalem.

**Answer: C.** His judgment between the two prostitutes over the living baby caused all Israel to stand in awe of him.

**5. The prophet who told Jeroboam that God would give him ten tribes was**

- A. Nathan
- B. Samuel
- C. Ahijah
- D. Elijah

**Answer: C.** Ahijah the Shilonite met Jeroboam and tore a new garment into 12 pieces, giving him 10.

**6. Which of the following was a major cause of God's anger against Solomon in his later years?**

- A. He failed to build a palace for Pharaoh's daughter.
- B. He married foreign women who turned his heart to false gods.
- C. He refused to pay King Hiram for the cedar wood.
- D. He consulted the medium at Endor.

**Answer: B.** Solomon's many foreign marriages violated God's command and led to state-sponsored idolatry (worshipping Ashtoreth, Milcom, Chemosh).

**7. When the Israelites came to Rehoboam at Shechem, what did they request?**

- A. That he go to war against Egypt.
- B. That he lighten the heavy yoke of forced labor imposed by his father.
- C. That he allow them to worship at the high places.
- D. That he hand over Jeroboam to be executed.

**Answer: B.** They asked him to reduce the heavy taxation and hard service (forced labor) that Solomon had placed on them.

**8. What advice did the young men give to Rehoboam?**

- A. To speak good words to the people and be their servant.
- B. To ask Jeroboam to become his prime minister.
- C. To tell the people he would chastise them with scorpions.
- D. To flee in his chariot to Jerusalem.

**Answer: C.** The youths advised arrogance, telling Rehoboam to threaten the people with even harsher treatment than his father used ("my father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions").

**9. Who was stoned to death by the Israelites as a direct consequence of Rehoboam's foolish decision?**

- A. Ahijah
- B. Rezon
- C. Hadad
- D. Adoram

**Answer: D.** Adoram, the taskmaster in charge of forced labor, was sent by Rehoboam to the rebellious tribes, and they stoned him to death.

**10. A major difference between the decision-making of Saul at Endor and the early decision-making of Solomon is that**

- A. Saul sought divine guidance through forbidden means, while Solomon sought God in humble prayer.
- B. Saul relied on foreign kings, while Solomon relied on his military strength.
- C. Saul made decisions with the elders, while Solomon ignored them.
- D. Saul built altars to false gods, while Solomon destroyed them.

**Answer: A.** Saul acted out of panic and used a medium (which God forbade), whereas the young Solomon humbly worshipped and prayed to God for wisdom.
