# JAMB Crk Notes: Religious Reforms in Judah

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 3 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Religious reforms in Judah. It covers the following content areas:

* (a) Cleansing of the Temple (II Kings 22)
* (b) Renewal of the Covenant (II Kings 23:1-30)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. analyse Josiah’s religious reforms;
2. determine the reasons for the renewal of the covenant;
3. assess the significance of the reforms.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** The syllabus lists "Cleansing of the Temple" with the reference "II Kings 22". In the biblical text, 2 Kings 22 focuses on the *repair* of the Temple and the discovery of the Book of the Law. The actual *cleansing* of the Temple (removing idols) happens as part of his reforms in 2 Kings 23. These notes explain the complete story across both chapters as the exam requires.

## Background to the Reforms

Before Josiah became king, Judah was in a state of severe religious decline. His grandfather, King **Manasseh**, and his father, King **Amon**, had promoted the worship of foreign gods (idolatry), built altars for the host of heaven (astral deities) inside the Temple of the Lord, and shed innocent blood. Amon was assassinated by his servants, and the people of the land made his son **Josiah** king. 

Josiah was only **eight years old** when he began to reign, but unlike his father and grandfather, he "did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, and walked in all the way of David his father" (2 Kings 22:2).

## Cleansing of the Temple (II Kings 22)

**Content a: Cleansing of the Temple (II Kings 22)**

In the eighteenth year of his reign (when he was 26 years old), King Josiah decided to repair and restore the Temple in Jerusalem, which had fallen into ruin during the reigns of the wicked kings before him. 

### 1. Funding the Temple repairs
Josiah sent his secretary (scribe), **Shaphan**, to the Temple. He instructed Shaphan to tell **Hilkiah the high priest** to count the money that the doorkeepers had collected from the people. This money was to be given to the workmen—carpenters, builders, and masons—to buy timber and quarried stone to repair the Temple. The Bible notes that the workmen were so honest that no accounting was required of them.

### 2. The discovery of the Book of the Law
While they were clearing and repairing the Temple, Hilkiah the high priest made a massive discovery. He said to Shaphan the secretary, "I have found the book of the law in the house of the Lord." Hilkiah gave the scroll to Shaphan, who read it. 

*(Note: Most biblical scholars believe this "Book of the Law" was the core of the Book of Deuteronomy, which contains strict laws against idolatry and promises severe curses for disobedience).*

### 3. Josiah's reaction
Shaphan returned to King Josiah to report that the money had been paid to the workers. He then mentioned the book, saying, "Hilkiah the priest hath delivered me a book." 

Shaphan read the book aloud to the king. When King Josiah heard the words of the Book of the Law and realized how far the nation had strayed from God's commands, he immediately **tore his clothes** in a sign of deep mourning, distress, and repentance.

[IMAGE: A young King Josiah in royal robes, standing in a stone palace room, gripping the collar of his tunic and tearing it in distress. In front of him stands Shaphan the scribe, holding open a large, aged parchment scroll. In the background, stone pillars and a brazier. Style: clean flat textbook illustration, white background.]

### 4. Consulting Huldah the prophetess
Josiah commanded Hilkiah the priest, Shaphan, Ahikam, Achbor, and Asaiah (the king's servant) to go and inquire of the Lord concerning the words of the book. Josiah was terrified because he knew God's wrath was kindled against Judah for the sins of their ancestors.

The men went to speak with **Huldah the prophetess**, the wife of Shallum, who lived in the Second Quarter of Jerusalem. Huldah gave them a two-part prophecy from the Lord:
* **Concerning Judah:** God would indeed bring disaster upon Jerusalem and its inhabitants—all the curses written in the book—because they had forsaken Him and burned incense to other gods. This judgment was final and could not be quenched.
* **Concerning Josiah:** Because Josiah's heart was tender (penitent), and he humbled himself before the Lord, weeping and tearing his clothes, God heard him. He would die in peace and be gathered to his grave before the disaster struck Jerusalem.

## Reasons for the Renewal of the Covenant

**Objective ii: determine the reasons for the renewal of the covenant.**

A covenant is a binding agreement between two parties. In the Old Testament, God made a covenant with Israel to be their God, provided they obeyed His laws. Josiah realized that Judah had broken this covenant. The specific reasons Josiah decided to renew the covenant were:

1. **The discovery of the Book of the Law:** The physical discovery of God's written word exposed the severe religious corruption of the nation. It showed them the standard they were supposed to live by.
2. **Ignorance and ancestral sins:** Josiah realized that "our fathers have not hearkened unto the words of this book." The nation had been living in generational disobedience and ignorance of God's commands.
3. **Fear of divine judgment:** The book contained severe curses for idolatry. Josiah was terrified of God's impending wrath. 
4. **Huldah's prophecy:** The prophetess confirmed that God's anger was real and that destruction was coming. Josiah wanted to appease God, seek His mercy, and delay the judgment by bringing the nation back to obedience.
5. **Personal conviction:** Josiah's personal repentance (tearing his clothes and humbling himself) naturally led to a desire to lead his entire nation into the same state of repentance.

## Renewal of the Covenant (II Kings 23:1-30)

**Content b: Renewal of the Covenant (II Kings 23:1-30)**

After receiving the message from Huldah, Josiah took immediate action to bring the nation back to God. 

### The Covenant Ceremony
1. **The Gathering:** Josiah sent messengers and gathered all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem. He went up to the Temple of the Lord, accompanied by all the men of Judah, the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the priests, the prophets, and all the people, both small and great.
2. **The Reading:** Josiah personally read in their hearing all the words of the Book of the Covenant which had been found in the Temple.
3. **The Vow:** The king stood **by the pillar** (the traditional royal place in the Temple courtyard) and made a covenant before the Lord. He promised:
   * To walk after the Lord.
   * To keep His commandments, testimonies, and statutes with all his heart and soul.
   * To perform the words of the covenant written in the book.
4. **The People's Agreement:** After the king made his vow, all the people present "stood to the covenant," meaning they agreed to it and pledged to keep it as well.

## Josiah’s Religious Reforms

**Objective i: analyse Josiah’s religious reforms.**

Having renewed the covenant, Josiah launched a massive, sweeping campaign to destroy idolatry and purify the religion of Judah. His reforms were thorough, violent against false religion, and extended even beyond the borders of Judah into the former northern kingdom of Israel.

His key reform actions included:

### 1. Cleansing the Temple of Idols
Josiah commanded Hilkiah and the priests to bring out of the Temple all the vessels and objects made for **Baal**, for **Asherah** (the fertility goddess), and for the **host of heaven** (sun, moon, and stars). He burned them outside Jerusalem in the fields of the Kidron valley and carried their ashes to Bethel.

### 2. Deposing Idolatrous Priests
He deposed (removed) the idolatrous priests whom the previous kings of Judah had ordained to burn incense in the high places. He also removed those who burned incense to Baal, the sun, the moon, the constellations, and all the host of heaven.

### 3. Defiling the High Places and Shrines
* He brought the Asherah image out of the Temple, burned it at the brook Kidron, ground it to powder, and scattered the dust over the graves of the common people.
* He broke down the houses of the male cult prostitutes (sodomites) that were near the Temple, where women wove hangings for the Asherah.
* He defiled the high places (local shrines) across Judah, from Geba to Beersheba, so they could no longer be used.
* He removed the horses that former kings had dedicated to the sun and burned the chariots of the sun with fire.

### 4. Eradicating Child Sacrifice
Josiah defiled **Topheth**, which was in the Valley of the Son of Hinnom. This was a notorious site where people made their sons and daughters pass through the fire as human sacrifices to the Ammonite god **Molech**. By defiling it, Josiah ensured no one would ever use it for child sacrifice again.

### 5. Destroying Jeroboam's Altar at Bethel
Josiah's reforms reached into the northern territory. He went to **Bethel** and destroyed the altar and the high place built centuries earlier by **Jeroboam the son of Nebat** (who had caused Israel to sin). 
* He burned the high place, crushed it to dust, and burned the Asherah.
* Seeing tombs on the mountain, he took human bones from them and burned them on the altar to defile it. 
* This specifically fulfilled a prophecy made hundreds of years earlier by a "man of God from Judah" who had prophesied against Jeroboam's altar by name (1 Kings 13:2). 
* Josiah saw the monument of the prophet who had foretold this and ordered his bones to be left in peace.

### 6. Restoration of the Passover
Josiah commanded all the people to keep the **Passover** to the Lord, as it was written in the Book of the Covenant. The Bible records that no such Passover had been kept since the days of the judges who judged Israel, nor during the times of the kings of Israel or Judah. It was celebrated in the eighteenth year of his reign.

### The end of Josiah's reign
Despite his righteousness, God's anger against Judah for Manasseh's sins remained. In 609 BC, **Pharaoh Neco** of Egypt went up to assist the king of Assyria. Josiah went out to fight Pharaoh Neco at **Megiddo**. Pharaoh Neco killed Josiah there. His servants brought his body in a chariot to Jerusalem, and he was buried in his own tomb.

| State of Religion Before Reforms (Manasseh/Amon) | State of Religion After Josiah's Reforms |
| --- | --- |
| Idols of Baal and Asherah in the Temple | Temple cleansed; idols burned at Kidron |
| Altars for astral deities in the Temple courts | Altars destroyed and crushed to dust |
| Child sacrifice practiced at Topheth (Molech) | Topheth defiled; child sacrifice stopped |
| Worship scattered across high places | High places defiled; worship centralized in Jerusalem |
| Book of the Law lost and ignored | Book of the Law found, read, and established as the rule of life |
| Passover neglected | The greatest Passover celebration since the Judges |

## Significance of the Reforms

**Objective iii: assess the significance of the reforms.**

Josiah's reforms were a turning point in the religious history of Israel, even though their political effects did not last long after his death. Their main significance includes:

1. **Centralization of Worship:** By destroying the local shrines (high places) and the altar at Bethel, Josiah ensured that the Temple in Jerusalem was the only legitimate place for sacrifice and national worship. This unified the people religiously.
2. **Supremacy of the Written Word:** The reforms marked a transition from a religion guided mostly by oral traditions and kings' decrees to a "religion of the book." The written scripture (the Book of the Law) became the ultimate authority for Jewish religious life.
3. **Eradication of Syncretism:** Josiah decisively separated the worship of Yahweh from the pagan practices that had crept in over centuries (like male cult prostitution, child sacrifice, and sun worship).
4. **Delay of Divine Judgment:** Though the Babylonian exile still happened later, God honored Josiah's repentance. The judgment was delayed, and Judah experienced a period of spiritual revival and peace during Josiah's reign.
5. **Fulfillment of Prophecy:** The destruction of the altar at Bethel fulfilled the specific centuries-old prophecy made against Jeroboam, proving the accuracy and power of the word of God.
6. **A Model of Righteous Leadership:** The Bible summarizes Josiah's significance with high praise: *"And like unto him was there no king before him, that turned to the Lord with all his heart, and with all his soul, and with all his might, according to all the law of Moses; neither after him arose there any like him"* (2 Kings 23:25). He serves as an enduring example that leaders should take responsibility for correcting corruption and returning their communities to God's standards.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Know the key names:** JAMB frequently tests who performed which action. **Hilkiah** *found* the book; **Shaphan** *read* the book; **Huldah** *prophesied* about it. 
* **Do not confuse the locations:** The idols were burned at the brook **Kidron**. Child sacrifice happened at **Topheth** (to the god Molech). The false altar destroyed in the north was at **Bethel** (built by Jeroboam).
* **Understand the immediate reaction:** Josiah's first reaction to hearing the book was **tearing his clothes** in repentance, not immediately attacking idols. The reforms came *after* renewing the covenant.
* **Remember the festival:** The major Jewish festival restored by Josiah, celebrated on a scale not seen since the days of the Judges, was the **Passover**.
* **Death of Josiah:** Josiah did not die peacefully in his bed; he died in battle at **Megiddo** against Pharaoh Neco. The prophecy of dying "in peace" meant he would die before the catastrophic destruction of Jerusalem by Babylon.

## Quick summary

1. King Josiah ordered the repair of the ruined Temple in the eighteenth year of his reign.
2. During the repairs, Hilkiah the high priest found the Book of the Law, which Shaphan the scribe read to the king.
3. Realizing Judah's sin, Josiah tore his clothes and sent messengers to Huldah the prophetess, who confirmed that God's judgment would fall on Judah, but not during Josiah's lifetime.
4. Josiah gathered all the people at the Temple, stood by the pillar, and renewed the covenant to obey the Lord.
5. He launched massive reforms: cleansing the Temple of idols (Baal, Asherah), destroying high places, and stopping child sacrifice to Molech at Topheth.
6. He destroyed Jeroboam's altar at Bethel, fulfilling an old prophecy, and ordered a grand celebration of the Passover.
7. The reforms centralized worship in Jerusalem and established the written word of God as the supreme authority in Judah.

## Practice questions

**1. Who was the high priest that found the Book of the Law during King Josiah's reign?**
- A. Shaphan
- B. Hilkiah
- C. Ahikam
- D. Shallum

**Answer: B.** Hilkiah was the high priest who found the book while the Temple was being repaired (2 Kings 22:8).

**2. King Josiah's immediate reaction upon hearing the words of the Book of the Law was to**
- A. execute the idolatrous priests
- B. destroy the altar at Bethel
- C. tear his clothes in repentance
- D. declare a national fast

**Answer: C.** The Bible records that as soon as the king heard the words of the book, he tore his clothes (2 Kings 22:11) as a sign of grief and repentance.

**3. The prophetess consulted by Josiah after the discovery of the Book of the Law was**
- A. Deborah
- B. Miriam
- C. Huldah
- D. Anna

**Answer: C.** Josiah's men went to Huldah the prophetess, who lived in Jerusalem, to inquire of the Lord (2 Kings 22:14).

**4. During the covenant renewal ceremony, King Josiah stood by the**
- A. altar of burnt offering
- B. pillar in the Temple
- C. gates of Jerusalem
- D. Ark of the Covenant

**Answer: B.** The king stood by the pillar (the traditional royal place) and made a covenant before the Lord (2 Kings 23:3).

**5. Which abhorrent practice was eradicated when King Josiah defiled Topheth in the Valley of Hinnom?**
- A. Temple prostitution
- B. Worship of the sun god
- C. Child sacrifice to Molech
- D. Baking cakes for the Queen of Heaven

**Answer: C.** Topheth was defiled by Josiah specifically so that no one might make his son or daughter pass through the fire to Molech (2 Kings 23:10).

**6. Which of the following best explains why Josiah decided to renew the covenant with God?**
- A. He wanted to secure a military alliance with Egypt
- B. He discovered the Book of the Law and feared God's impending wrath
- C. The priests demanded higher wages for Temple service
- D. He wanted to annex the northern kingdom of Israel

**Answer: B.** The discovery of the Book of the Law revealed Judah's disobedience, causing Josiah to fear God's wrath and seek to return the nation to obedience through a covenant.

**7. In his religious reforms, King Josiah destroyed the altar at Bethel which had originally been built by**
- A. Ahab
- B. Manasseh
- C. Jeroboam
- D. Omri

**Answer: C.** Josiah destroyed the altar and high place at Bethel that Jeroboam the son of Nebat had made, which caused Israel to sin (2 Kings 23:15).

**8. Which major religious festival did King Josiah restore, celebrating it on a scale not seen since the days of the Judges?**
- A. The Feast of Tabernacles
- B. The Passover
- C. The Feast of Weeks (Pentecost)
- D. The Day of Atonement

**Answer: B.** Josiah commanded the people to keep the Passover, and it was celebrated with a magnitude not seen since the days of the Judges (2 Kings 23:21-22).

**9. A significant outcome of Josiah's religious reforms was the**
- A. permanent defeat of the Assyrian army
- B. centralization of Yahweh's worship in Jerusalem
- C. total conversion of the surrounding nations
- D. immediate translation of the scriptures

**Answer: B.** By destroying all the local shrines and high places, Josiah ensured that the Temple in Jerusalem was the only legitimate place for sacrifice.

**10. How did King Josiah's reign come to an end?**
- A. He died of a severe disease in his old age
- B. He was assassinated by his own servants like his father Amon
- C. He was killed in battle at Megiddo by Pharaoh Neco
- D. He was taken into captivity by the Babylonians

**Answer: C.** Josiah went out to fight Pharaoh Neco of Egypt and was killed at Megiddo (2 Kings 23:29).
