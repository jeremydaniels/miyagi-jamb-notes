# JAMB Crk Notes: Concern for Judah

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 4 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Concern for Judah. It covers the historical period when the southern kingdom of Judah was conquered by the Babylonians, the people were taken into exile, and their later return to rebuild their nation and faith. 

It covers the following content items:
- **a.** The fall of Jerusalem (2 Kings 24; 25:1-17)
- **b.** Condition of Judah (Nehemiah 1:1-11; Ezra 1:1-11)
- **c.** Response to the state of Judah (Nehemiah 2; 4:1-23; Ezra 3:4; 5; 6; 7)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. identify the reasons for the fall of Jerusalem;
2. examine the condition of Judah during the exile;
3. analyse the people's response to the call of Nehemiah and Ezra to rebuild Jerusalem;
4. distinguish between Nehemiah and Ezra's responses to the opposition of their enemies.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** In the JAMB syllabus, the reference for content item (a) is printed with a line break as `(II kings 24; 25:1-` and then `17)` on the next line. This is a typographical error for `2 Kings 24; 25:1-17`, which is the correct continuous reference used in these notes. 

## The Fall of Jerusalem

**Content a: The fall of Jerusalem (2 Kings 24; 25:1-17)**
**Objective i: identify the reasons for the fall of Jerusalem**

The fall of Jerusalem and the Babylonian exile was a major turning point in Israel's history. It did not happen all at once, but in stages over several years under three successive kings of Judah: Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, and Zedekiah.

### The events leading to the fall (2 Kings 24)
1. **Jehoiakim's rebellion:** King Jehoiakim of Judah became a servant to Nebuchadnezzar, King of Babylon, for three years. However, he then rebelled against him.
2. **First waves of attack:** In response, the LORD sent bands of the Chaldeans (Babylonians), Syrians, Moabites, and Ammonites against Judah to destroy it. This was to fulfill the word of the LORD spoken by His prophets, because of the sins of King Manasseh, who had filled Jerusalem with innocent blood. 
3. **Jehoiachin's surrender:** Jehoiakim died, and his son Jehoiachin became king. Nebuchadnezzar personally besieged Jerusalem. Jehoiachin surrendered to the king of Babylon. 
4. **The first major deportation:** Nebuchadnezzar took Jehoiachin prisoner. He carried away all the treasures of the temple and the king's palace. He took 10,000 captives to Babylon, including all the princes, mighty men of valor, craftsmen, and smiths. Only the poorest of the land were left.
5. **Zedekiah installed:** Nebuchadnezzar made Jehoiachin's uncle, Mattaniah, king in his place and changed his name to Zedekiah. 

### The final destruction of Jerusalem (2 Kings 25:1-17)
1. **Zedekiah's rebellion:** Zedekiah also rebelled against the king of Babylon.
2. **The great siege:** In the ninth year of Zedekiah's reign, Nebuchadnezzar and his entire army came against Jerusalem. They built siege works around it, trapping everyone inside until the eleventh year of Zedekiah.
3. **The city is breached:** A severe famine struck the city, and there was no food. The city wall was finally breached by the Babylonians.
4. **The capture of Zedekiah:** King Zedekiah and his soldiers fled by night through a gate between two walls. The Chaldeans pursued them and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho, while his army scattered. 
5. **Zedekiah's punishment:** Zedekiah was brought to the king of Babylon at Riblah. They killed Zedekiah's sons before his eyes, then they put out Zedekiah's eyes, bound him in bronze fetters, and took him to Babylon.
6. **The temple and city destroyed:** Nebuzaradan, the captain of the Babylonian guard, came to Jerusalem. He burned the house of the LORD (the Temple), the king's house, and all the houses of Jerusalem. The Chaldean army broke down the walls around Jerusalem.
7. **The final deportation and looting:** Nebuzaradan carried the rest of the people into exile, leaving only some of the poorest to be vinedressers and plowmen. The Babylonians broke into pieces the bronze pillars, the bronze sea, and the stands in the temple, carrying the valuable metals back to Babylon.

### Reasons for the fall of Jerusalem
The biblical text clearly identifies the spiritual and political reasons for this disaster:
* **The sins of Manasseh:** 2 Kings 24:3-4 states that God removed Judah from His sight "for the sins of Manasseh, according to all that he had done, and also for the innocent blood that he had shed." God would not pardon this.
* **Persistent idolatry and disobedience:** The kings and the people continually turned away from God to worship false gods, ignoring the warnings of the prophets (such as Jeremiah).
* **Political rebellion and broken oaths:** Both Jehoiakim and Zedekiah broke their treaties and rebelled against Nebuchadnezzar, despite swearing loyalty to him. 

## Condition of Judah During the Exile

**Content b: Condition of Judah (Nehemiah 1:1-11; Ezra 1:1-11)**
**Objective ii: examine the condition of Judah during the exile**

While the best and brightest of Judah were taken to Babylon (the "Exile"), a remnant remained in the ruined land of Judah. The condition of Judah was a mixture of physical devastation and spiritual hope, which eventually led to their restoration.

### The physical and social condition (Nehemiah 1:1-11)
Many years after the destruction of Jerusalem, a Jew named **Nehemiah** was serving as the cupbearer to the Persian King Artaxerxes in the citadel of Susa. 
* **Hanani's report:** Nehemiah's brother, Hanani, and some men came from Judah. Nehemiah asked them about the surviving Jews who had escaped exile and about Jerusalem.
* **The desolate state:** They reported: "The survivors there in the province who escaped exile are in great trouble and shame; the wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates are destroyed by fire" (Nehemiah 1:3).
* **Nehemiah's reaction:** Hearing this, Nehemiah sat down and wept. He mourned, fasted, and prayed for days. 
* **Nehemiah's prayer:** He confessed the sins of the people of Israel, acknowledging that they had acted corruptly and broken God's commandments. He reminded God of His promise to Moses: that if the people were unfaithful, they would be scattered, but if they repented and kept the commandments, God would gather them back even from the uttermost parts of heaven. Nehemiah prayed for success and mercy before the king.

### The spiritual condition and the turning point (Ezra 1:1-11)
The condition of the Jews in exile changed dramatically when the Babylonian empire was conquered by the Persian empire.
* **Cyrus's Decree:** In the first year of Cyrus, king of Persia, the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus to fulfill the prophecy of Jeremiah. 
* **The proclamation:** Cyrus issued a written decree throughout his kingdom, stating that the LORD, the God of heaven, had given him all the kingdoms of the earth and charged him to build Him a house at Jerusalem in Judah.
* **Permission to return:** Cyrus commanded that any of God's people could go up to Jerusalem and rebuild the house of the LORD. 
* **Support from neighbors:** The decree ordered that the survivors be assisted by the people of their places with silver, gold, goods, and beasts, alongside freewill offerings for the temple.
* **Restoration of the Temple vessels:** Cyrus brought out the holy vessels of the house of the LORD, which Nebuchadnezzar had stolen. He counted them out to **Sheshbazzar**, the prince of Judah, who brought them back to Jerusalem along with the returning exiles.

## The Call to Rebuild Jerusalem

**Content c: Response to the state of Judah (Nehemiah 2; 4:1-23; Ezra 3:4; 5; 6; 7)**
**Objective iii: analyse the people’s response to the call of Nehemiah and Ezra to rebuild Jerusalem**

The rebuilding of Jerusalem happened in two main phases covered in the syllabus: the rebuilding of the Temple (led by Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and later supported by Ezra's reforms) and the rebuilding of the city walls (led by Nehemiah). The people's response was characterized by determination, unity, and a revival of faith, despite severe challenges.

### Nehemiah's call and the people's response (Nehemiah 2)
* **The King's favor:** Observing Nehemiah's sadness, King Artaxerxes asked what was wrong. Nehemiah explained that the city of his fathers' graves lay in ruins. The king granted him permission to go to Judah to rebuild it, giving him letters for safe passage and timber from the king's forest.
* **Night inspection:** Nehemiah arrived in Jerusalem and secretly inspected the ruined walls by night.
* **The Call:** He then revealed his plan to the priests, nobles, and officials: "You see the trouble we are in, how Jerusalem lies in ruins with its gates burned. Come, let us build the wall of Jerusalem, that we may no longer suffer disgrace." He told them how God's hand had been upon him and the words of the king.
* **The People's Response:** The people responded with great enthusiasm: **"Let us rise up and build."** They strengthened their hands for the good work, showing total unity and patriotic commitment to restoring their nation.

### The response regarding the Temple and Worship (Ezra 3:4; 5; 6)
The exiles who returned to Jerusalem focused first on restoring their religion.
* **Restoring Worship (Ezra 3:4):** Before the temple foundation was even laid, the people gathered as one man in Jerusalem. They built the altar of the God of Israel. **Ezra 3:4** notes that they kept the **Feast of Booths (Tabernacles)** as prescribed in the Law, offering the required number of daily burnt offerings. Their response was an immediate return to scriptural obedience.
* **Prophetic Encouragement (Ezra 5):** Work on the temple had been stopped due to opposition from neighboring peoples. However, the prophets **Haggai** and **Zechariah** prophesied to the Jews. In response, the leaders (Zerubbabel and Jeshua) immediately arose and began to rebuild the house of God, supported by the prophets.
* **Tattenai's inquiry:** Tattenai, the Persian governor of the province Beyond the River, came and asked them, "Who gave you a decree to build this house?" The elders responded confidently, relying on God. They explained their history, the destruction by Nebuchadnezzar, and the decree of Cyrus that authorized their work. 
* **Darius's support (Ezra 6):** Tattenai wrote to King Darius. Darius searched the archives, found Cyrus's original decree at Ecbatana, and ordered Tattenai to stay away and to fund the building work from royal revenues. 
* **Completion:** The people responded with diligent work. The temple was finished on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of King Darius. The people celebrated the dedication with joy and kept the Passover.

### Ezra's religious reforms (Ezra 7)
Years later, during the reign of King Artaxerxes, **Ezra** (a priest and a scribe skilled in the Law of Moses) traveled from Babylon to Jerusalem. 
* **Ezra's dedication:** Ezra had set his heart to study the Law of the LORD, to do it, and to teach His statutes and rules in Israel.
* **The King's commission:** Artaxerxes gave Ezra a decree allowing any Israelites to go with him. Ezra was given silver and gold to buy offerings for the temple. More importantly, Artaxerxes authorized Ezra to appoint magistrates and judges to judge the people according to God's law, and to teach the law to those who did not know it.
* **The People's Response:** Through Ezra, the people were called to spiritual renewal. They submitted to the teaching of the Law, establishing a community based on the word of God.

**Application to Nigerian life:** The patriotic responses of Nehemiah and the returned exiles serve as a powerful example of nation-building. When citizens are united, willing to work hard ("Let us rise up and build"), and rely on God, they can rebuild communities broken by corruption, conflict, or neglect, just as Judah was rebuilt.

## Nehemiah and Ezra's Responses to Opposition

**Objective iv: distinguish between Nehemiah and Ezra’s responses to the opposition of their enemies.**

Both Nehemiah (rebuilding the walls) and the temple builders described in Ezra's account faced severe opposition from surrounding governors and peoples who did not want Jerusalem to become strong again. However, the nature of the opposition and the leaders' responses were quite different.

### Nehemiah's response to opposition (Nehemiah 4:1-23)
**The Opposition:** Nehemiah faced intense, direct hostility from **Sanballat** (the Horonite), **Tobiah** (the Ammonite servant), and Geshem the Arab. 
* When Sanballat heard the wall was being built, he was angry and mocked the Jews, asking if they would restore things in a day.
* Tobiah famously mocked their work, saying: *"Yes, what they are building—if a fox goes up on it he will break down their stone wall!"*
* They conspired together to come and fight against Jerusalem and to cause confusion.

**Nehemiah's Response:**
1. **Prayer:** Nehemiah immediately turned to God, praying, *"Hear, O our God, for we are despised."*
2. **Vigilance and Defense:** He set a guard as a protection against them day and night. 
3. **Strategic Organization:** He stationed people behind the lowest parts of the wall by families, armed with swords, spears, and bows.
4. **Encouragement:** He told the nobles and people, "Do not be afraid of them. Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome, and fight for your brothers, your sons, your daughters, your wives, and your homes."
5. **Armed Labor:** From that day on, half of his servants worked on construction, and half held weapons. The builders worked with one hand and held a weapon with the other. Nehemiah kept a trumpeter beside him to sound the alarm if they were attacked. They worked continuously from dawn until the stars came out.

### Ezra's response to opposition (Ezra 5 and 6)
*(Note: The events in Ezra 5 and 6 technically involve the leaders Zerubbabel and Jeshua, but JAMB syllabus frames this as "Ezra's response" because it is recorded in the Book of Ezra and reflects the legal/prophetic approach of that book).*

**The Opposition:** The opposition here was political and legal, spearheaded by **Tattenai**, the governor of the province Beyond the River. He did not threaten immediate military attack but demanded legal authorization: *"Who gave you a decree to build this house and to finish this structure?"*

**The Response:**
1. **Refusal to stop:** The "eye of their God was on the elders of the Jews," and they refused to stop work until a report could reach King Darius.
2. **Legal and Historical Defense:** Instead of arming for battle, they provided a respectful, factual answer. They cited their history, God's discipline under Nebuchadnezzar, and crucially, they appealed to the legal decree issued by King Cyrus.
3. **Reliance on Prophetic Word:** They drew their courage to continue building not from weapons, but from the spiritual encouragement of the prophets Haggai and Zechariah.
4. **Trust in Divine Providence:** They trusted that the Persian king's search of the archives would vindicate them, which it did when Darius found Cyrus's decree and ordered Tattenai to fully support the Jews.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Know the Kings:** Do not confuse the kings! 
    *   *Nebuchadnezzar* (Babylon) destroyed Jerusalem. 
    *   *Cyrus* (Persia) issued the decree allowing the Jews to return. 
    *   *Darius* (Persia) confirmed Cyrus's decree for the temple.
    *   *Artaxerxes* (Persia) allowed Nehemiah to rebuild the walls and sent Ezra to teach the Law.
*   **Know the Kings of Judah:** Jehoiakim and Zedekiah both rebelled against Babylon; Zedekiah was the final king whose eyes were put out.
*   **Distinguish the Rebuilding:** Zerubbabel/Jeshua/Ezra's account focuses on the **Temple** and the Law. Nehemiah's account focuses on the **walls** and city gates.
*   **Feast of Booths:** Remember that *Ezra 3:4* specifically mentions the returnees keeping the Feast of Booths after building the altar.
*   **Opposition Tactics:** Remember Tobiah's exact insult: *"If a fox goes up on it he will break down their stone wall!"*
*   **Nehemiah's Strategy:** The key phrase for Nehemiah's defense is "working with one hand and holding a weapon with the other."

## Quick summary

1. Jerusalem fell to the Babylonians because of the sins of Manasseh, the people's idolatry, and the rebellions of Kings Jehoiakim and Zedekiah against Nebuchadnezzar.
2. King Zedekiah was captured, his sons were killed before him, he was blinded, and taken to Babylon, while the Temple and city walls were destroyed by Nebuzaradan.
3. During the exile, Jerusalem lay in ruins, and the remaining Jews were in great distress, which caused Nehemiah to weep, fast, and pray when he heard the news.
4. King Cyrus of Persia issued a decree allowing the Jews to return and rebuild the Temple, returning the stolen sacred vessels to them.
5. The people responded enthusiastically to Nehemiah's call to rebuild the walls, saying, "Let us rise up and build."
6. Under Zerubbabel and Jeshua, the returnees built the altar, celebrated the Feast of Booths (Ezra 3:4), and eventually completed the temple with the encouragement of prophets Haggai and Zechariah.
7. Nehemiah responded to the physical threats of Sanballat and Tobiah by praying, setting day and night guards, and having his men work with weapons in hand.
8. The temple builders in Ezra's time responded to Tattenai's legal opposition by continuing to work, appealing to Cyrus's legal decree, and trusting God to vindicate them through King Darius.

## Practice questions

**1. The final destruction of Jerusalem by the Babylonians occurred during the reign of King**
- A. Jehoiakim
- B. Jehoiachin
- C. Manasseh
- D. Zedekiah

**Answer: D.** Zedekiah was the last king of Judah. During his reign, the walls were breached, the temple was burned, and he was captured and blinded.

**2. According to 2 Kings, God brought judgment upon Judah primarily because of the innocent blood shed by**
- A. Zedekiah
- B. Jehoiakim
- C. Manasseh
- D. Ahab

**Answer: C.** 2 Kings 24:3-4 states that the LORD removed Judah from His sight for the sins of Manasseh and the innocent blood he had shed.

**3. When Nehemiah received the report about the ruined walls of Jerusalem, his immediate reaction was to**
- A. organize an army to fight the Babylonians
- B. weep, fast, and pray to God
- C. resign as the king's cupbearer
- D. write a letter to the survivors in Judah

**Answer: B.** Nehemiah 1:4 records that he sat down, wept, mourned for days, fasted, and prayed to the God of heaven.

**4. The Persian king whose decree officially permitted the Jews to return to Jerusalem to rebuild the Temple was**
- A. Darius
- B. Nebuchadnezzar
- C. Artaxerxes
- D. Cyrus

**Answer: D.** Ezra 1:1-4 details the decree of Cyrus, king of Persia, which allowed the exiles to return and rebuild the house of the LORD.

**5. According to Ezra 3:4, what festival did the returned exiles celebrate after building the altar?**
- A. The Passover
- B. The Feast of Weeks
- C. The Feast of Booths
- D. The Feast of Unleavened Bread

**Answer: C.** Ezra 3:4 states that they kept the Feast of Booths (Tabernacles) as it is written in the Law.

**6. Who mocked the Jews' rebuilding efforts by saying that if a fox went up on their wall, it would break it down?**
- A. Sanballat
- B. Tattenai
- C. Geshem
- D. Tobiah

**Answer: D.** Tobiah the Ammonite was beside Sanballat and made this mocking comment (Nehemiah 4:3).

**7. How did Nehemiah respond to the threat of physical attack from Sanballat and his allies?**
- A. He hired mercenaries from Egypt
- B. He stopped the work until the Persian army arrived
- C. He armed the workers and set a guard day and night
- D. He appealed to King Artaxerxes for a legal injunction

**Answer: C.** Nehemiah prayed, set a guard, and instructed his men to build with one hand and hold a weapon with the other (Nehemiah 4:16-17).

**8. Which two prophets encouraged the Jews to resume rebuilding the temple when they faced opposition in the Book of Ezra?**
- A. Isaiah and Jeremiah
- B. Elijah and Elisha
- C. Haggai and Zechariah
- D. Amos and Hosea

**Answer: C.** Ezra 5:1 records that the prophets Haggai and Zechariah prophesied to the Jews, prompting them to resume building the house of God.

**9. How did the Jewish elders respond to the opposition of Tattenai the governor?**
- A. They fought his soldiers at the city gates
- B. They cited the decree of King Cyrus as their authority
- C. They bribed him with silver and gold
- D. They abandoned the temple and built their own houses

**Answer: B.** They gave a historical and legal defense, explaining that Cyrus had issued a decree for them to build the temple, leading Tattenai to write to Darius to verify it.

**10. The Babylonian captain of the guard who burned the house of the LORD and broke down the walls of Jerusalem was**
- A. Nebuzaradan
- B. Tattenai
- C. Sheshbazzar
- D. Sanballat

**Answer: A.** 2 Kings 25:8-10 states that Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard and servant of the king of Babylon, burned the temple and broke down the city walls.
