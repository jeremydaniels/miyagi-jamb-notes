# JAMB Crk Notes: Faith, Courage and Protection

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 5 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Faith, Courage and Protection. It covers the biblical accounts of four young Jewish exiles in Babylon who faced the threat of death for their religious convictions. 

It has one content item:
- **Examples of Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego (Dan. 3:1-30; 6:1-28)**

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. analyse the stories of Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego and Daniel;
2. determine the occasions in which the four men demonstrated faith;
3. analyse the effects of the faith of the four men on the Babylonians;
4. identify the qualities of faith by four men worthy of emulation.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** In the syllabus, the Bible reference for this topic is printed with a minor typing error as `(Dan. 3:1-30: 6:1-28`. The colon is a misprint; the correct reference for the two distinct chapters is `(Dan. 3:1-30; 6:1-28)`. The notes below cover these exact passages.

## Examples of Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego

**Content item: Examples of Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego (Dan. 3:1-30; 6:1-28)**

The stories of Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego, and Daniel take place during the Jewish exile in Babylon. After the fall of Jerusalem, the brightest young men from Judah were taken to serve in the Babylonian royal court. They were given new Babylonian names (Daniel's friends, originally Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, were renamed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego). Despite living in a foreign, idol-worshipping culture, they remained completely devoted to the God of Israel.

### The Story of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego (Daniel 3:1-30)

1. **The Golden Image:** King **Nebuchadnezzar** of Babylon made a massive image of gold, set up on the plain of Dura.
2. **The Decree:** He commanded all officials of his empire to attend the dedication of the image. A herald proclaimed that as soon as the people heard the sound of musical instruments (the horn, pipe, lyre, trigon, harp, and bagpipe), they were to fall down and worship the golden image.
3. **The Penalty:** Anyone who did not fall down and worship would immediately be cast into a burning fiery furnace.
4. **The Accusation:** Certain Chaldeans (Babylonians) came forward and maliciously accused the Jews. They reported to the king that Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego paid no attention to his decree, neither serving his gods nor worshipping the golden image.
5. **The Confrontation:** Furious, Nebuchadnezzar summoned the three men. He offered them one more chance to bow when the music played, warning them of the furnace and asking, "who is the god that will deliver you out of my hands?" (Daniel 3:15).
6. **The Bold Reply:** They answered the king, saying they had no need to answer him on this matter. They declared that their God was able to deliver them from the furnace and from the king's hand. However, they added that **even if He did not deliver them**, they would still not serve his gods or worship the golden image.
7. **The Furnace:** Enraged by their defiance, Nebuchadnezzar ordered the furnace to be heated **seven times hotter** than usual. He commanded his strongest soldiers to bind the three men and throw them in. The fire was so intense that the flames killed the soldiers who threw them in.
8. **The Deliverance:** Nebuchadnezzar was astonished to see **four men** walking loose and unhurt in the fire. He noted that the appearance of the fourth was "like a son of the gods" (Daniel 3:25).
9. **The Outcome:** The king called them out of the furnace. The fire had no power over them—not a hair was singed, their cloaks were unharmed, and there was no smell of fire on them. Nebuchadnezzar praised their God, made a protective decree for their religion, and promoted the three men in the province of Babylon.

### The Story of Daniel in the Lions' Den (Daniel 6:1-28)

1. **Daniel's Success:** Under the reign of **Darius the Mede**, Daniel was made one of three presidents over 120 satraps (governors) who ruled the kingdom. Daniel distinguished himself above all others because "an excellent spirit was in him," and the king planned to set him over the whole kingdom.
2. **The Plot:** The other presidents and satraps were jealous and sought to find a ground for complaint against Daniel. Finding no corruption or negligence, they concluded they could only trap him regarding the law of his God.
3. **The Trap:** The officials tricked King Darius into establishing a royal statute: for **thirty days**, anyone who made a petition (prayed) to any god or man, except to King Darius, would be cast into the den of lions. Once signed by the king, the "law of the Medes and the Persians" could not be altered.
4. **Daniel's Defiance:** When Daniel knew the document had been signed, he went to his house. He had windows in his upper chamber that opened toward **Jerusalem**. He got down on his knees **three times a day**, prayed, and gave thanks to his God, just as he had done previously.
5. **The Arrest:** The conspirators found Daniel praying and immediately reported him to the king for breaking the decree.
6. **The Punishment:** King Darius was deeply distressed and tried all day to find a way to save Daniel. However, the men reminded the king that a Medo-Persian law could not be changed. Reluctantly, Darius commanded Daniel to be thrown into the lions' den, telling Daniel, "May your God, whom you serve continually, deliver you!" (Daniel 6:16). A stone was sealed over the mouth of the den.
7. **The Deliverance:** The king went to his palace, fasted all night, and could not sleep. At break of day, he hurried to the den and called out in a tone of anguish, asking if Daniel's God had delivered him. Daniel replied, "O king, live for ever! My God sent his angel and shut the lions' mouths..." (Daniel 6:21-22).
8. **The Outcome:** Daniel was taken up out of the den completely unharmed because he had trusted in his God. The king commanded that the men who had maliciously accused Daniel be thrown into the lions' den, along with their wives and children, and the lions overpowered them immediately. Darius then wrote a decree commanding all in his kingdom to tremble and fear before the God of Daniel.

## Analysing the stories of the four men

**Objective i: analyse the stories of Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego and Daniel.**

The narratives of Daniel and his three friends share a common theme: **the conflict between divine law and human law**. In both cases, the state demanded absolute allegiance, but the four men chose to obey God rather than man.

### Comparison of the two trials of faith

| Feature | Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego (Daniel 3) | Daniel (Daniel 6) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **King in power** | Nebuchadnezzar (Babylonian Empire) | Darius the Mede (Medo-Persian Empire) |
| **The conflict** | Forced to perform an act of idolatry (bowing to a golden image). | Forbidden from performing an act of true worship (praying to God). |
| **The accusers** | Chaldeans acting out of spite and loyalty to their gods. | Satraps and presidents acting out of political jealousy. |
| **The king's attitude** | Angry and prideful; personally challenged God's power. | Distressed and trapped by his own law; hoped for Daniel's deliverance. |
| **The punishment** | The fiery furnace (active execution). | The lions' den (passive execution). |
| **The deliverance** | A fourth man appeared in the fire; they were untouched by flames. | An angel was sent to shut the mouths of the lions; he was untouched by beasts. |
| **Result for accusers** | The soldiers who threw them in were killed by the heat. | The accusers and their families were fed to the lions. |

In both stories, the foreign rulers used religion as a tool for state control. For Nebuchadnezzar, worshipping the image was a test of loyalty to his empire. For Darius's officials, the prayer ban was a calculated political trap. The four men analysed the situation correctly: they saw that giving in to these demands would be a betrayal of their covenant with God. Their survival proved that God's authority is higher than any earthly king's authority.

## Occasions demonstrating faith

**Objective ii: determine the occasions in which the four men demonstrated faith.**

Faith is the absolute trust and confidence in God, regardless of the circumstances. The four men demonstrated this clearly at critical moments of decision:

1. **Refusal to bow to the image:** Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego demonstrated faith when the music played, and everyone else on the plain of Dura fell down. They alone remained standing, refusing to conform to idolatry despite being a minority.
2. **The bold declaration to the king:** When confronted by Nebuchadnezzar, they did not beg for mercy or try to compromise (for example, by pretending to tie their sandals). Their statement, *"Our God whom we serve is able to deliver us... But if not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods,"* is one of the greatest expressions of faith in the Bible. It showed faith that does not depend on a guaranteed rescue.
3. **Entering the furnace:** They demonstrated faith by willingly allowing themselves to be bound and thrown into a fire that was so hot it killed their guards.
4. **Daniel's open prayer:** Daniel demonstrated faith when he learned the decree was signed. He did not stop praying, nor did he pray secretly in the dark. He went to his upper room, opened his windows toward Jerusalem, and prayed three times a day exactly as he had always done, accepting the risk of death.
5. **Daniel in the lions' den:** When thrown into the pit of starved lions, the Bible notes that no harm was found on Daniel "because he had trusted in his God" (Daniel 6:23). He relied on God's protection through the night rather than panicking.

## Effects of their faith on the Babylonians

**Objective iii: analyse the effects of the faith of the four men on the Babylonians.**

The uncompromising faith of these four men had a profound impact on the pagan kings and the wider empire. 

### Effects in the time of Nebuchadnezzar (Daniel 3)
- **Acknowledgment of God's supremacy:** King Nebuchadnezzar publicly confessed that the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego had sent His angel and delivered His servants. He shifted from arrogant boasting ("who is the god that will deliver you?") to praising God.
- **Protection of the Jewish religion:** Nebuchadnezzar issued a royal decree to all peoples, nations, and languages. He commanded that anyone who spoke anything against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego would be torn limb from limb, and their houses laid in ruins.
- **Public witness:** He admitted that "there is no other god who is able to deliver in this way" (Daniel 3:29).
- **Promotion:** The Babylonians saw the king promote Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego to higher positions in the province of Babylon.

### Effects in the time of Darius (Daniel 6)
- **Acknowledgment of the Living God:** King Darius wrote a decree extending to all nations and languages on earth. He commanded that "in all my royal dominion men tremble and fear before the God of Daniel" (Daniel 6:26).
- **Theological declaration by a pagan king:** Darius publicly described Daniel's God as "the living God, enduring for ever; his kingdom shall never be destroyed." This exposed the Babylonians and Medo-Persians to the true nature of God.
- **Justice against the wicked:** The corrupt Babylonian and Medo-Persian officials who plotted against Daniel were destroyed.
- **Prosperity:** Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and the reign of Cyrus the Persian, serving as a powerful testimony of God's blessing in a foreign land.

## Qualities of faith worthy of emulation

**Objective iv: identify the qualities of faith by four men worthy of emulation.**

Nigerian students face their own pressures today—from peer pressure to join cults, to demands for bribes, to mockery for practising their religion. The four men in Babylon displayed several qualities that Christians today are encouraged to emulate:

1. **Courage:** They faced the most terrifying deaths (fire and wild beasts) without showing fear or giving in to panic. Courage is doing what is right even when you are afraid of the consequences.
2. **Uncompromising conviction:** They refused to find a "middle ground" with sin. Daniel did not close his windows to pray secretly; the three friends did not pretend to bow. They were absolute in their loyalty to God.
3. **Consistency:** Daniel's habit of praying three times a day was firmly established. His faith was not a sudden reaction to danger, but a steady, daily relationship with God.
4. **Selfless trust (Faith without conditions):** Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego trusted God to save them, but they also accepted that God might choose to let them die ("But if not..."). Their obedience was based on who God is, not just on what God could do for them.
5. **Excellent spirit and integrity:** Daniel was targeted because he was successful and honest. His enemies could find no fault or corruption in his work. A true quality of faith is living an honest, hardworking life in secular society.
6. **Peace under pressure:** Both Daniel and his friends showed remarkable calm. When faced with the enraged King Nebuchadnezzar, the three friends replied respectfully but firmly. When pulled from the den, Daniel greeted King Darius politely ("O king, live for ever!").

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego** | The Babylonian names given to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, three Jewish exiles. |
| **Nebuchadnezzar** | The king of the Babylonian Empire who built the golden image and threw the three friends into the furnace. |
| **Darius the Mede** | The king of the Medo-Persian Empire who was tricked into throwing Daniel into the lions' den. |
| **Satraps** | Provincial governors in the ancient Persian empire. Daniel was placed over them. |
| **Chaldeans** | People of southern Babylon; in this context, the native Babylonian officials who accused the Jews. |
| **Law of the Medes and Persians** | A legal principle in the empire stating that once a royal decree was signed, it could never be changed or revoked by anyone, not even the king. |
| **Dura** | The plain in the province of Babylon where Nebuchadnezzar set up his golden image. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Mix-ups:** Students often confuse which king goes with which story. Remember: **Nebuchadnezzar** = Golden Image and Fiery Furnace. **Darius** = Law against prayer and Lions' Den.
- **The specific threat:** Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego were commanded **to do** something sinful (bow). Daniel was commanded **to stop doing** something righteous (pray).
- **The statement of faith:** The phrase "Our God... is able to deliver us... But if not..." is frequently tested. Know that it was spoken by Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego to Nebuchadnezzar.
- **Daniel's accusers:** Daniel's enemies were jealous of his promotion and excellent spirit, not specifically angry about his religion. They just used his religion as a tool to trap him.
- **The furnace heat:** The furnace was heated **seven times** hotter than usual. (JAMB sometimes asks for this exact number).

## Quick summary

1. During the exile, King Nebuchadnezzar commanded everyone to bow to a golden image on the plain of Dura or be thrown into a fiery furnace.
2. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refused, demonstrating unconditional faith by declaring they would not bow even if God did not save them.
3. They were thrown into a furnace heated seven times hotter, but God protected them, and a fourth figure appeared with them in the flames.
4. Later, under King Darius, jealous officials tricked the king into signing an unalterable law forbidding prayer to anyone but the king for 30 days.
5. Daniel demonstrated courage and consistency by continuing to pray to God three times a day by his open window facing Jerusalem.
6. Daniel was thrown into the lions' den, but God sent an angel to shut the lions' mouths, keeping him completely safe.
7. Both pagan kings were profoundly affected, issuing decrees that protected the Jewish faith and commanded all nations to fear and honour the God of Israel.
8. The four men are perfect examples of courage, integrity, consistency in prayer, and uncompromising trust in God in the face of death.

## Practice questions

**1. Why were Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego thrown into the fiery furnace?**

- A. They plotted a rebellion against the Babylonian government.
- B. They refused to eat the king's rich food.
- C. They refused to fall down and worship the golden image.
- D. They destroyed the musical instruments of the Babylonians.

**Answer: C.** They refused to obey Nebuchadnezzar's decree to fall down and worship the golden image when the music played.

**2. Which king was forced by his own law to throw Daniel into the lions' den?**

- A. Nebuchadnezzar
- B. Cyrus
- C. Darius
- D. Belshazzar

**Answer: C.** Darius the Mede signed the decree that trapped Daniel and was distressed when he had to enforce it.

**3. "But if not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods..." This statement demonstrates the speakers'**

- A. fear of the burning fiery furnace.
- B. unconditional faith in God regardless of the outcome.
- C. desire to provoke the king to anger.
- D. knowledge that they would secretly escape.

**Answer: B.** Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego declared their loyalty to God was absolute, even if God chose not to save them from death.

**4. What trap did the satraps and presidents set for Daniel?**

- A. A law requiring everyone to bow to a golden image of Darius.
- B. A decree that anyone who prayed to any god or man except the king for 30 days would be thrown to the lions.
- C. A ban on eating any food prepared by Jews.
- D. A requirement that Daniel must collect taxes on the Sabbath.

**Answer: B.** Knowing Daniel prayed faithfully, they tricked Darius into signing a 30-day ban on petitions to any god or man other than the king.

**5. How did King Nebuchadnezzar react when he looked into the fiery furnace?**

- A. He was terrified and fled the province of Babylon.
- B. He ordered his soldiers to pour water on the flames.
- C. He was astonished to see four men walking unhurt in the fire.
- D. He commanded the musicians to play louder.

**Answer: C.** He was amazed to see four men unbound and unharmed, noting the fourth looked "like a son of the gods."

**6. What was the direct effect of Daniel's deliverance on King Darius?**

- A. He converted to Judaism and travelled to Jerusalem.
- B. He made a decree that everyone in his kingdom must tremble and fear before Daniel's God.
- C. He promoted Daniel's accusers out of fear of their power.
- D. He abolished the law of the Medes and Persians entirely.

**Answer: B.** After witnessing the miracle, Darius issued a decree acknowledging Daniel's God as the living God and commanding all to fear Him.

**7. How many times a day was Daniel known to pray in his upper chamber?**

- A. Once
- B. Twice
- C. Three times
- D. Seven times

**Answer: C.** The Bible specifically notes that Daniel got down on his knees three times a day to pray and give thanks to his God.

**8. When Nebuchadnezzar was enraged by the defiance of the three Jewish men, he ordered the furnace to be heated**

- A. twice as hot.
- B. three times as hot.
- C. seven times as hot.
- D. ten times as hot.

**Answer: C.** He ordered it to be heated seven times hotter than it was usually heated.

**9. What legal principle made King Darius unable to save Daniel from the lions' den?**

- A. The decree of the Chaldeans
- B. The Babylonian covenant
- C. The law of the Medes and Persians which cannot be altered
- D. The edict of Cyrus

**Answer: C.** According to the law of the Medes and Persians, any interdict or ordinance established by the king could not be changed.

**10. What happened to the men who maliciously accused Daniel?**

- A. They were banished from the kingdom of Babylon.
- B. They were forgiven by Daniel and kept their positions.
- C. They were thrown into the fiery furnace by the king.
- D. They were thrown into the lions' den along with their families.

**Answer: D.** King Darius commanded that the accusers, their wives, and their children be thrown into the lions' den, and the lions overpowered them.
