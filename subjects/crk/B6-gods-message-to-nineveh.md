## Jonah and his message (Jonah 1; 2; 3 and 4)

The book of Jonah tells the story of a Hebrew prophet who rebelled against God's command but was eventually forced to carry out his mission. Unlike other prophetic books that focus mainly on the prophet's sermons, the book of Jonah focuses on the prophet's personal attitude and God's dealings with him and the Gentile (non-Jewish) world. The story covers four chapters, detailing his call, his disobedience, his eventual preaching, and God's lesson on universal mercy.

## The story of Jonah's call

**Objective i: analyse the story of Jonah's call**

Jonah, the son of Amittai, was a Hebrew prophet. God spoke to him and gave him a clear and direct command. 

### The Command to Go to Nineveh
God told Jonah: *"Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it; for their wickedness is come up before me."* (Jonah 1:2). 

Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire. To the Israelites, the Assyrians were brutal and feared enemies who had frequently oppressed them. God wanted Jonah to go to the heart of enemy territory and deliver a message of impending judgment because of the city's extreme wickedness.

### Jonah's Rebellion and Flight
Instead of obeying God, Jonah decided to run away. He went down to the seaport city of **Joppa** and found a ship going to **Tarshish**. He paid the fare and boarded the ship, hoping to flee *"from the presence of the LORD"* (Jonah 1:3). 

Tarshish was in the completely opposite direction of Nineveh (believed by many scholars to be in ancient Spain). Jonah did not flee out of fear of the Assyrians. Later in the story (Jonah 4:2), he reveals his true reason for running: he knew that God was exceptionally gracious and merciful. Jonah hated the Assyrians and wanted them destroyed; he feared that if he preached to them, they might repent and God would forgive them, sparing Israel's greatest enemy.

## The consequences of Jonah's disobedience

**Objective ii: describe the consequences of Jonah's disobedience**

Jonah's attempt to run away from God had severe consequences, not only for him but also for the innocent sailors on the ship with him.

### The Great Storm
As the ship sailed towards Tarshish, God sent a great wind onto the sea, creating a violent storm that threatened to break the ship apart. The sailors, who were pagans, were terrified. They began crying out, each to his own god, and threw the ship's cargo into the sea to lighten it. 

Meanwhile, Jonah had gone down into the inner part of the ship and was fast asleep. The ship's captain found him, woke him up, and ordered him to pray to his God, hoping that Jonah's God might spare their lives.

### Casting Lots and Jonah's Confession
When the storm did not stop, the sailors decided to cast lots to find out whose fault it was that this evil had come upon them. The lot fell on Jonah. 

They questioned him about his occupation, his origin, and his people. Jonah answered honestly: *"I am an Hebrew; and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, which hath made the sea and the dry land."* (Jonah 1:9). He confessed that he was running away from his God.

Terrified by this, the sailors asked him what they should do to calm the sea. Jonah told them to pick him up and throw him into the sea, acknowledging that the great storm was his fault.

### Jonah Thrown Overboard
The sailors did not want to kill Jonah. They tried hard to row back to land, but the sea grew even more violent. Finally, they prayed to Jonah's God, begging Him not to hold them guilty of innocent blood. They then picked Jonah up and threw him into the sea. Immediately, the raging sea became totally calm. Witnessing this, the sailors feared the LORD exceedingly, offered a sacrifice to Him, and made vows.

### In the Belly of the Great Fish
God's punishment for Jonah was also His way of saving him. God had prepared a **great fish** to swallow Jonah. Jonah remained in the belly of the fish for **three days and three nights**.

While inside the fish, Jonah prayed to God (Jonah Chapter 2). His prayer was primarily a song of thanksgiving rather than just a plea for rescue. He acknowledged that God had cast him into the deep, yet he expressed faith that God would hear his voice from the depths of the grave (Sheol). He promised to pay his vows and declared that *"Salvation is of the LORD"* (Jonah 2:9). 

Following his prayer of repentance and submission, God commanded the fish, and it vomited Jonah out onto dry land.

## The effect of Jonah's message on the Ninevites

**Objective iii: assess the effect of Jonah's message on the Ninevites**

After Jonah was delivered from the fish, God spoke to him a second time, giving him the exact same command: "Arise, go unto Nineveh, that great city, and preach unto it the preaching that I bid thee." (Jonah 3:2). This time, Jonah obeyed.

### Jonah Preaches in Nineveh
Nineveh was described as an exceedingly great city, requiring a three days' journey to cross. Jonah entered the city, walking for a day, and delivered a short, urgent message: *"Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown."* (Jonah 3:4). He offered no conditions and mentioned no possibility of mercy; he simply declared total destruction in forty days.

### The Repentance of the Ninevites
The effect of Jonah's preaching was immediate and massive. Despite being pagans, the people of Nineveh **believed God**. 

1. **The People's Reaction:** They immediately proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth (rough clothing worn as a sign of mourning and repentance), from the greatest of them to the least.
2. **The King's Decree:** When the news reached the king of Nineveh, he stepped down from his throne, took off his royal robes, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in ashes. 
3. **Total National Repentance:** The king and his nobles issued a strict decree throughout the city. No human, and not even the animals (herds or flocks), were allowed to eat food or drink water. Both man and beast had to be covered with sackcloth.
4. **Turning from Evil:** Most importantly, the king ordered everyone to cry mightily to God and to specifically turn away from their evil ways and from the violence that was in their hands. The king hoped that God might change His mind and turn away from His fierce anger so that they would not perish.

### God's Forgiveness
When God saw their works—specifically that they had turned from their evil ways—He *"repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not."* (Jonah 3:10). God accepted their sincere repentance and spared the city.

## God's Universal Love: Jonah's Anger and God's Lesson

To fully understand Jonah's message (as the syllabus covers Jonah chapters 1 to 4), we must look at Jonah's reaction to God's forgiveness in Chapter 4. 

Jonah was completely displeased and became very angry that God had spared Nineveh. He prayed to God, admitting that this was exactly why he had fled to Tarshish in the first place: *"for I knew that thou art a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repentest thee of the evil."* (Jonah 4:2). Jonah was so upset that his enemies were forgiven that he asked God to take his life.

Jonah went outside the city, made a shelter, and sat under it to see what would happen to the city, perhaps hoping God might still destroy it. To teach Jonah a lesson about compassion, God did three things:
1. **The Gourd:** God made a plant (a gourd) grow up quickly to provide shade for Jonah. Jonah was very happy about the plant.
2. **The Worm:** The next morning, God sent a worm that attacked the plant, causing it to wither and die.
3. **The East Wind:** God then sent a scorching east wind, and the sun beat down on Jonah's head until he fainted and again wished to die.

God asked Jonah if it was right for him to be so angry about the plant. Jonah insisted he had a right to be angry enough to die. God then delivered the central message of the book: Jonah had pity on a simple plant that he did not create or make grow, which appeared in a night and died in a night. If Jonah could care about a plant, should God not have pity on Nineveh, a great city containing more than 120,000 innocent people (described as not knowing their right hand from their left) and many animals?

This concludes the book, leaving God's question hanging, teaching that **God's love, mercy, and salvation are universal**—they are for all nations, not just for the Israelites.

## Emulating the example of the Ninevites

**Objective iv: emulate the example of the Ninevites**

The story of the Ninevites serves as a powerful model of true repentance. Students and citizens in Nigeria can learn and apply the following lessons from their example:

1. **Taking God's Word Seriously:** The Ninevites did not argue with the prophet or demand miracles. They heard the warning of judgment and immediately believed God.
2. **True Humility:** Repentance requires humbling oneself. The King of Nineveh did not let his royal status stop him from showing remorse. He stepped off his throne, took off his royal robes, and sat in ashes. In Nigerian society, leaders and citizens alike must learn to swallow their pride and admit when they are wrong.
3. **Action, Not Just Words:** The king decreed that everyone must "turn every one from his evil way, and from the violence that is in their hands." Fasting and crying out to God are not enough if behaviour does not change. True repentance involves a practical turning away from sins such as corruption, violence, exam malpractice, and injustice.
4. **Collective Responsibility:** The whole city, from the king to the lowest servant, participated in the fast and repentance. When a community or nation faces crises, a collective return to godly values and righteousness can invite God's mercy and healing upon the land.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Nineveh** | The capital city of the ancient Assyrian Empire; Israel's enemy, known for its extreme wickedness. |
| **Tarshish** | The distant seaport city Jonah attempted to flee to instead of going to Nineveh. |
| **Joppa** | The port city where Jonah found the ship heading to Tarshish. |
| **Sackcloth** | A rough, uncomfortable fabric (usually made of goat's hair) worn as a symbol of deep sorrow, mourning, and repentance. |
| **Casting lots** | An ancient method of making a decision or finding out God's will by throwing objects (like stones or sticks), similar to drawing straws or rolling dice. |
| **Universalism** | The theological concept shown in Jonah that God's love, mercy, and salvation are available to all people and nations, not just Israel. |
| **Repentance** | A sincere change of mind and heart that results in turning away from sin and turning toward God. |
| **Gourd** | The fast-growing leafy plant that God prepared to give Jonah shade and teach him a lesson about compassion. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Jonah's motive:** A very common JAMB question asks *why* Jonah fled. Do not choose options saying he was afraid of the Ninevites or the sea. He fled because he knew God was merciful and he did not want Israel's enemies to be forgiven.
*   **The specific message:** Memorise Jonah's exact short sermon: *"Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown."*
*   **The King's decree:** Remember that the king included *animals* (beasts, herds, flocks) in the fast and the wearing of sackcloth. 
*   **Jonah's rescue:** He was swallowed by a "great fish" (the Bible uses this term, though it is commonly called a whale in popular culture) and was inside for exactly three days and three nights.
*   **The lesson of the plant:** God used the gourd (plant), the worm, and the east wind to teach Jonah about His universal compassion.
*   **Location:** Jonah went down to **Joppa** to board a ship to **Tarshish** to flee from **Nineveh**. Don't mix these three locations up.

## Quick summary

1. God commanded Jonah to go to Nineveh, the wicked capital of Assyria, and preach against it.
2. Jonah disobeyed and boarded a ship at Joppa bound for Tarshish because he did not want God to show mercy to Israel's enemies.
3. God sent a great storm; the pagan sailors cast lots, found out it was Jonah's fault, and threw him overboard at his own request.
4. God saved Jonah using a great fish, which swallowed him for three days and nights until Jonah prayed and was vomited onto dry land.
5. Given a second chance, Jonah went to Nineveh and preached that the city would be destroyed in forty days.
6. The Ninevites, led by their king, immediately believed God, proclaimed a strict fast for humans and animals, wore sackcloth, and turned from violence.
7. God saw their genuine repentance and spared the city, showing that His mercy is universal.
8. Jonah was angry at God's mercy, so God used a plant, a worm, and a harsh wind to teach him that the Creator rightly pities His creations, even ignorant Gentiles.

## Practice questions

**1. Where was Jonah told to go and cry against by God?**
- A. Joppa
- B. Tarshish
- C. Babylon
- D. Nineveh

**Answer: D.** God told him to arise and go to Nineveh, the great city, and cry against its wickedness.

**2. Jonah attempted to run away from God by boarding a ship heading to Tarshish from the seaport of**
- A. Tyre
- B. Joppa
- C. Sidon
- D. Caesarea

**Answer: B.** Jonah went down to Joppa, where he found a ship going to Tarshish to flee from the presence of the LORD.

**3. How did the sailors on the ship determine that Jonah was the cause of the terrible storm?**
- A. They asked him directly.
- B. God spoke to the captain in a dream.
- C. They cast lots.
- D. Jonah willingly confessed before they did anything.

**Answer: C.** The sailors said to one another, "Come, let us cast lots, that we may know for whose cause this evil is upon us." The lot fell on Jonah.

**4. What was the central theme of Jonah's message to the people of Nineveh?**
- A. "Repent or face a terrible plague."
- B. "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown."
- C. "The God of the Hebrews demands a sacrifice."
- D. "Turn from your violence and worship Yahweh."

**Answer: B.** Jonah preached a very short and direct message of absolute judgment: "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown."

**5. The true reason Jonah fled from his mission to Nineveh was that he**
- A. feared the cruel Assyrians would kill him.
- B. did not know the way to the city.
- C. knew God was merciful and might forgive them.
- D. felt he lacked the eloquence to preach.

**Answer: C.** In Jonah 4:2, Jonah prays and states that he fled to Tarshish because he knew God was gracious, merciful, and slow to anger, and would likely spare the city.

**6. Which of the following did the King of Nineveh NOT do when he heard Jonah's message?**
- A. He took off his royal robe and put on sackcloth.
- B. He sat in ashes.
- C. He ordered that only the adult males should fast.
- D. He decreed that men should turn from their evil ways.

**Answer: C.** The king decreed a fast for *everyone*, including the greatest to the least, and even extended the fast and the wearing of sackcloth to the herds and flocks (animals).

**7. How long was Jonah in the belly of the great fish?**
- A. Forty days and forty nights
- B. Three days and three nights
- C. Seven days
- D. One day and one night

**Answer: B.** God prepared a great fish to swallow Jonah, and he was in the belly of the fish for three days and three nights.

**8. What convinced God to turn away from the evil He had planned for Nineveh?**
- A. Jonah's intercessory prayer for them.
- B. The fact that they were a very large population.
- C. He saw their works, that they turned from their evil ways.
- D. The sacrifices they offered on the altar.

**Answer: C.** Jonah 3:10 says, "And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not."

**9. To teach Jonah a lesson about pity and compassion, God used a**
- A. fig tree, a locust, and rain.
- B. gourd, a worm, and an east wind.
- C. sheep, a wolf, and a storm.
- D. vineyard, a fox, and a drought.

**Answer: B.** God caused a gourd (plant) to grow over Jonah, then sent a worm to destroy it, and followed it with a vehement east wind to show Jonah how illogical his lack of pity for humans was compared to his pity for a plant.

**10. The story of Jonah is primarily used to illustrate which attribute of God?**
- A. God's universal love and mercy for all people.
- B. God's strict adherence to punishment.
- C. God's focus only on the nation of Israel.
- D. God's desire for animal sacrifices.

**Answer: A.** The book demonstrates that God's grace, mercy, and willingness to forgive are not limited to Israel, but extend universally to Gentile nations like Assyria when they repent.
