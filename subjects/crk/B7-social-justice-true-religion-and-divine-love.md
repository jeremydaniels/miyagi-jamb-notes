# JAMB Crk Notes: Social Justice, True Religion and Divine Love

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 7 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Social justice, True religion and Divine love. It focuses on the prophecies of Amos and Hosea to the Northern Kingdom of Israel, and the New Testament teaching of James on true religion. 

It has two content items:
- **a. Social justice and true religion (Amos 2:6-8; 4; 5:1-25; 6:1-14; 7:10-17; 8:4-14) cf James 1:19-27**
- **b. Divine love and human response (Hosea 1; 2; 3; 4; 6:1-11; 14)**

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. determine what true religion is;
2. identify the ills that led to the call for social justice in Amos' time;
3. identify the punishment meted for evil deeds;
4. examine the condition in Israel during Hosea's time;
5. analyse Hosea's portrayal of divine love and human response.

## Social justice and true religion (Amos and James)

**Content item a: Social justice and true religion.**

Amos was a prophet from **Tekoa**, a town in the Southern Kingdom (Judah), but God sent him to prophesy to the Northern Kingdom (Israel) during the reign of King **Jeroboam II** (around the 8th century BC). During this time, Israel was very wealthy and enjoyed a time of peace, but the society was deeply corrupt. The wealth was concentrated in the hands of a few who lived in extreme luxury by oppressing the poor.

### The ills that led to the call for social justice in Amos' time

**Objective ii: identify the ills that led to the call for social justice in Amos’ time.**

Amos fiercely condemned the rich and powerful for their lack of **social justice** (fair treatment of all people, especially the vulnerable). The specific social, economic and moral ills he identified included:

**1. Extreme oppression of the poor**
The wealthy exploited the vulnerable without mercy. Amos declared that they sold the righteous for silver, and **"the needy for a pair of shoes"** (Amos 2:6). They trampled the head of the poor into the dust of the earth and took their garments as a pledge (collateral for a debt), which they then sinfully slept on beside their altars.

**2. Exploitation by wealthy women ("Cows of Bashan")**
In Amos 4, the prophet specifically called out the wealthy women of Samaria, referring to them as the **"cows of Bashan"** (Bashan was a region known for its fat, well-fed cattle). These women oppressed the poor and crushed the needy, simply to maintain their luxurious lifestyles, demanding of their husbands, "Bring, that we may drink!"

**3. Corruption and bribery in the courts**
There was no justice in the judicial system. Amos stated that the leaders turned justice to **wormwood** (a bitter plant, meaning they made justice a bitter experience). In the "gate" (the place where court cases were heard), they took bribes, afflicted the just, and turned aside the rights of the poor (Amos 5:12).

**4. Dishonest business practices**
The merchants were greedy and dishonest. They could not wait for the Sabbath and the New Moon festivals to be over so they could go back to making money. They cheated their customers by **"making the ephah small and the shekel great"** (using a small basket to measure the grain they sold, but a heavy weight to measure the silver they received). They used false balances and even sold the sweepings (refuse) of the wheat to the desperate poor (Amos 8:4-6).

**5. Selfish and luxurious living**
While the poor suffered, the rich lived in extreme comfort and ignored the moral ruin of the nation. Amos 6 pronounces woe on "those at ease in Zion and secure in mount Samaria." They lay on **beds of ivory**, ate the best lambs and calves, sang idle songs to the harp, drank wine from large bowls, and anointed themselves with the finest oils. Yet, they were "not grieved for the ruin of Joseph."

### True religion: Amos and James

**Objective i: determine what true religion is.**

**False religion in Amos' time**
The Israelites believed they were highly religious and pleasing to God because they strictly observed the religious rituals. They offered burnt offerings, grain offerings and peace offerings. They held solemn assemblies and sang loud hymns. However, God rejected their hollow ritualism. Because their hearts were wicked and their society was full of injustice, their worship was an insult to God. 

**True religion according to Amos**
God declared through Amos: *"I hate, I despise your feast days, and I will not smell in your solemn assemblies... Take thou away from me the noise of thy songs; for I will not hear the melody of thy viols"* (Amos 5:21-23). 
Instead of empty rituals, Amos declared that true religion is the practice of justice and righteousness. He summarised God's demand in a famous verse:
> **"But let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."** (Amos 5:24)

**True religion according to James**
In the New Testament, the Apostle James agrees with Amos that true religion is not just about words or rituals, but about practical action and personal holiness. In James 1:19-27, he teaches that believers must be:
- **"Swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath"**, because human anger does not produce the righteousness of God.
- **"Doers of the word, and not hearers only."** A person who only listens to the word without doing it is like a man who looks at his natural face in a mirror and immediately forgets what he looks like.
- Able to **"bridle his tongue"** (control what they say). If someone claims to be religious but cannot control their tongue, their religion is vain (useless).

James gives the ultimate New Testament definition of true religion:
> **"Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world."** (James 1:27)

*Application:* In modern Nigeria, this means that going to church, praying loudly and giving offerings are not enough if a person is corrupt at work, oppresses their subordinates, or fails to help the needy.

### Conflict at Bethel: Amos and Amaziah

Because Amos preached boldly against the sins of Israel, he faced strong opposition from the religious establishment. **Amaziah**, the chief priest at the royal sanctuary in Bethel, was angered by Amos' prophecies. 

1. **The Accusation:** Amaziah sent a message to King Jeroboam II, accusing Amos of conspiracy. He claimed, "The land is not able to bear all his words," because Amos had prophesied that Jeroboam would die by the sword and Israel would be exiled (Amos 7:10-11).
2. **The Command to Flee:** Amaziah then confronted Amos directly, telling him to flee back to the land of Judah, eat bread there, and prophesy there. He warned Amos never to prophesy at Bethel again, because it was the king's sanctuary and a temple of the kingdom.
3. **Amos' Defence:** Amos replied that he was not a professional prophet ("neither was I a prophet's son"), but a simple **herdsman and a dresser of sycamore trees**. He only prophesied because God specifically called him from following the flock and commanded him, "Go, prophesy to my people Israel."
4. **The Prophetic Judgment on Amaziah:** Because Amaziah tried to stop the word of God, Amos pronounced a severe punishment on him: his wife would become a harlot in the city, his children would fall by the sword, his land would be divided, and Amaziah himself would die in a polluted (foreign) land.

### The punishment meted out for evil deeds

**Objective iii: identify the punishment meted for evil deeds.**

God is patient, but He eventually punishes unrepentant sin. Amos outlined the punishments God had already sent as warnings, and the final doom that was coming.

**Past unheeded warnings (Amos 4)**
God had sent several natural disasters to wake the Israelites up and lead them to repentance, but they ignored them. God said, "Yet you did not return to me." These past punishments included:
- **Famine:** God gave them "cleanness of teeth" (a Hebrew idiom for having no food to eat).
- **Drought:** God withheld the rain when there were still three months to the harvest.
- **Agricultural disasters:** God struck their crops with blight, mildew, and locusts.
- **Plague and war:** God sent pestilence among them "after the manner of Egypt" and allowed their young men to be killed by the sword.
- **Destruction:** God overthrew some of their cities as He overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah.

Because they ignored all these warnings, Amos declared: **"Prepare to meet your God, O Israel!"** (Amos 4:12).

**Impending judgment and exile (Amos 5, 6, 8)**
1. **The Day of the Lord as darkness:** The Israelites were eagerly waiting for the "Day of the Lord," thinking it would be a day God would destroy their enemies and bless them. Amos shocked them by declaring that for them, the Day of the Lord would be **darkness, and not light**. It would be inescapable, like a man who runs away from a lion only to meet a bear, or goes into his house and leans his hand on the wall, only for a snake to bite him (Amos 5:18-20).
2. **Exile:** The luxurious elite who lounged on ivory beds would be the very first to go into exile as captives.
3. **Destruction of mansions:** The great summer and winter houses of the rich would be smashed to pieces. 
4. **Famine of the Word of God:** Amos prophesied a terrifying future where God would stop speaking entirely. People would wander from sea to sea, running to and fro to seek the word of the Lord, but they would not find it (Amos 8:11-12).

## Divine love and human response (Hosea)

**Content item b: Divine love and human response.**

Hosea was a native of the Northern Kingdom (Israel) who prophesied to his own people shortly after Amos, during the final years before the nation was destroyed by the Assyrians (722 BC). While Amos emphasised God's justice, Hosea's message was a profound demonstration of God's **unconditional, redeeming love** in the face of human unfaithfulness. (Note: In the book of Hosea, the Northern Kingdom is often called **Ephraim**).

### The condition in Israel during Hosea's time

**Objective iv: examine the condition in Israel during Hosea’s time.**

By Hosea's time, the spiritual and moral condition of Israel had completely collapsed. Hosea 4 describes a nation that had entirely abandoned God's laws:

1. **Total moral decay:** God announced that He had a controversy (a case) against the land because there was **"no truth, nor mercy, nor knowledge of God in the land."** Instead, the society was full of swearing, lying, killing, stealing, and committing adultery. Bloodshed followed bloodshed.
2. **Spiritual adultery (Baal worship):** The people had abandoned the true God to worship the Canaanite fertility god, Baal. This was spiritual harlotry (prostitution). They foolishly believed that Baal was the one giving them their grain, wine, and oil, forgetting that it was the Lord who provided for them.
3. **Corrupt priesthood:** The priests, who were supposed to teach the law, were completely corrupt. God declared, **"My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge"** (Hosea 4:6). Because the priests had rejected knowledge and forgotten the law of God, God rejected them from being priests.

### Hosea's portrayal of divine love and human response

**Objective v: analyse Hosea’s portrayal of divine love and human response.**

To make the people understand how their spiritual adultery broke God's heart, God told Hosea to act out a living object lesson through his own marriage. 

#### 1. Hosea's marriage to Gomer (Hosea 1)
God commanded Hosea: *"Go, take unto thee a wife of whoredoms and children of whoredoms: for the land hath committed great whoredom, departing from the LORD"* (Hosea 1:2). Hosea married a woman named **Gomer**, the daughter of Diblaim. She symbolised the unfaithful nation of Israel, while Hosea symbolised the loving, faithful God.

Gomer bore three children, and God gave them specific prophetic names to show His judgment on Israel:
- **Jezreel (God scatters/punishes):** A reminder that God would punish the royal house of Jehu for the bloodshed committed at Jezreel, and bring an end to the kingdom of Israel.
- **Lo-ruhamah (Not pitied / No mercy):** God declared that He would no longer have pity on the house of Israel or forgive them.
- **Lo-ammi (Not my people):** The ultimate rejection. God said, "For ye are not my people, and I will not be your God."

#### 2. God's discipline and redeeming love (Hosea 2 and 3)
Gomer eventually left Hosea and went after her lovers, falling into destitution and slavery. This mirrored Israel going after false gods. 

In Hosea 2, God explained how He would deal with Israel. He would strip her naked and **hedge her way with thorns** (bring hardship upon the nation) so that she could not find her false lovers (idols). The hardship was meant to bring her to her senses, so she would say, *"I will go and return to my first husband; for then was it better with me than now."* Following this discipline, God promised to lovingly take her back and betroth her to Himself forever in righteousness, justice, steadfast love, and mercy.

To illustrate this, God gave Hosea an incredibly difficult command in Hosea 3: *"Go yet, love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress..."* 
Hosea went to the slave market and **bought Gomer back** for **fifteen shekels of silver, and a homer and a lethech of barley**. This powerful act of buying back a completely unfaithful spouse portrays God's enduring, redemptive love. Despite Israel's terrible sins, God was willing to pay the price to bring them back.

#### 3. The call to repentance and human response (Hosea 6 and 14)
**The superficial response (Hosea 6:1-11):** 
The people made a show of repentance, saying, *"Come, and let us return unto the LORD: for he hath torn, and he will heal us."* However, God saw that their repentance was shallow. He lamented, *"O Ephraim, what shall I do unto thee?... for your goodness is as a morning cloud, and as the early dew it goeth away."* Their love evaporated as quickly as morning mist. God told them what He truly wanted:
> **"For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings."** (Hosea 6:6)

**The final promise of restoration (Hosea 14):**
The book of Hosea ends with a beautiful, heartfelt plea from God. He begs the nation: *"O Israel, return unto the LORD thy God; for thou hast fallen by thine iniquity."* He urges them to bring words of true repentance and completely reject their idols.
If they do this, God promises a full and loving restoration:
> **"I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely: for mine anger is turned away from him."** (Hosea 14:4)

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Social justice | Fair and equitable treatment of all individuals in a society, without oppressing the poor or vulnerable. |
| True religion | A genuine relationship with God expressed through practical care for others (e.g. orphans, widows) and a holy life, not just empty rituals. |
| Cows of Bashan | Amos' descriptive term for the wealthy, oppressive women of Samaria who crushed the poor to fund their drinking. |
| Day of the Lord | A future day of divine intervention; Amos warned it would be a day of darkness and judgment for unrepentant Israel. |
| Ephah and Shekel | Ancient units of measurement. Dishonest merchants made the ephah (volume basket) small and the shekel (weight for money) heavy to cheat buyers. |
| Sycamore | A type of fig tree. Amos was a dresser (tender) of sycamore trees before his prophetic call. |
| Gomer | Hosea's unfaithful wife, symbolising the spiritual adultery of the nation of Israel. |
| Jezreel | Hosea's first child. The name means "God sows" or "God scatters", prophesying punishment. |
| Lo-ruhamah | Hosea's second child. The name means "Not pitied" or "No mercy". |
| Lo-ammi | Hosea's third child. The name means "Not my people". |
| Ephraim | The name of a dominant tribe in the Northern Kingdom, frequently used by Hosea to refer to the entire nation of Israel. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Do not confuse Amos and Hosea.** Both were prophets to the Northern Kingdom (Israel) around the same time, but their core messages differ. Amos is the prophet of **social justice**; Hosea is the prophet of **divine love** and forgiveness.
- **Remember Amos' background:** He was a herdsman and a dresser of sycamore trees from Tekoa (in Judah), but he was sent to prophesy in Israel.
- **Learn the exact social ills:** Selling the righteous for silver and the needy for a pair of shoes; the "cows of Bashan"; cheating with false balances (small ephah, great shekel).
- **The "Day of the Lord" in Amos:** Know that Amos described it as a day of **darkness and not light**.
- **Know the names of Hosea's children in order:** Jezreel, Lo-ruhamah (Not pitied), Lo-ammi (Not my people). JAMB often asks for their meanings.
- **The redemption price:** Hosea bought Gomer back for fifteen shekels of silver, and a homer and a lethech of barley.
- **James' definition:** James 1:27 defines pure religion as visiting orphans and widows in their affliction, and keeping oneself unspotted from the world.

## Quick summary

1. Amos prophesied against the extreme social injustice in Israel during the reign of Jeroboam II, where the rich lived in luxury by oppressing the poor and corrupting the courts.
2. God rejected their religious rituals because they lacked justice; Amos taught that true religion requires justice to "roll down like waters."
3. James taught that pure religion involves practically caring for the vulnerable (orphans and widows) and living a holy life, rather than just hearing the word without doing it.
4. Amaziah, the priest of Bethel, tried to banish Amos to Judah, but Amos declared his divine calling and prophesied severe judgment on Amaziah and his family.
5. Because Israel ignored God's past warnings (famine, drought, plagues), Amos warned that they would face utter destruction, exile, and a "famine of the word of the Lord."
6. Hosea ministered in a time of total moral collapse and spiritual adultery, where the people worshipped Baal instead of God.
7. God used Hosea's painful marriage to the unfaithful Gomer to illustrate Israel's spiritual unfaithfulness and God's unconditional, redeeming love.
8. Despite Israel's deep sins, God promised to "heal their backsliding" and "love them freely" if they would truly return to Him.

## Practice questions

**1. The Prophet Amos declared that the true worship of God must be accompanied by**
- A. numerous burnt offerings
- B. regular fasting and prayer
- C. justice and righteousness
- D. obedience to the king's commands

**Answer: C.** God rejected their empty rituals and feasts, commanding instead, "let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream" (Amos 5:24).

**2. According to James, which of the following describes pure and undefiled religion?**
- A. Preaching the gospel to the poor
- B. Visiting orphans and widows in their affliction
- C. Paying tithes and offerings regularly
- D. Attending synagogue every Sabbath

**Answer: B.** James 1:27 states that pure religion is to visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unspotted from the world.

**3. In the book of Amos, the wealthy women of Samaria who oppressed the poor were referred to as**
- A. daughters of Zion
- B. cows of Bashan
- C. wives of harlotry
- D. queens of the land

**Answer: B.** Amos 4:1 refers to the wealthy, oppressive women of Samaria as the "cows of Bashan" who crushed the needy to support their drinking.

**4. Amaziah the priest of Bethel ordered Amos to return to Judah because**
- A. Amos was not a certified prophet
- B. Bethel was the king's sanctuary and Amos was prophesying against the king
- C. the people of Judah needed to hear the message
- D. Amos was asking for money

**Answer: B.** Amaziah accused Amos of conspiracy against King Jeroboam II and told him to flee, because Bethel was the king's sanctuary (Amos 7:10-13).

**5. God commanded Hosea to marry a woman of harlotry to symbolise**
- A. the unfaithfulness of the nation of Israel to God
- B. God's rejection of the Northern Kingdom forever
- C. the corruption of the priests of Bethel
- D. the destruction of the house of Jehu

**Answer: A.** Gomer's unfaithfulness represented the spiritual harlotry of Israel, who had abandoned the Lord to worship Baal.

**6. What is the meaning of the name of Hosea's second child, Lo-ruhamah?**
- A. God scatters
- B. Not my people
- C. Not pitied
- D. God has remembered

**Answer: C.** Lo-ruhamah means "Not pitied" or "No mercy", signifying that God would no longer have pity on the house of Israel. (Lo-ammi means "Not my people").

**7. What did Amos say the "Day of the Lord" would be like for the Israelites?**
- A. A day of joyful celebration and feast
- B. A day of victory over the Assyrians
- C. A day of darkness, gloom and not light
- D. A day when all the nations would serve Israel

**Answer: C.** Amos 5:18 warns that for the unrepentant Israelites, the Day of the Lord would be a day of judgment and darkness, not light.

**8. To demonstrate God's redeeming love, Hosea bought back his unfaithful wife for**
- A. thirty pieces of silver
- B. fifteen shekels of silver and some barley
- C. twenty shekels of gold and some wheat
- D. fifty shekels of silver and a pair of shoes

**Answer: B.** Hosea 3:2 states that he bought her for fifteen shekels of silver, and a homer and a lethech of barley.

**9. Which of the following was one of the social ills condemned by Amos?**
- A. Refusing to pay taxes to the king
- B. Making the ephah small and the shekel great to cheat buyers
- C. Stealing horses from the royal stables
- D. Marrying foreign women

**Answer: B.** Amos 8:5 condemns the merchants who used false balances, making the ephah (measure of grain) small and the shekel (weight for money) great to cheat the poor.

**10. In Hosea 6, God declared that He desired**
- A. sacrifices and burnt offerings
- B. steadfast love and the knowledge of God
- C. the building of a new temple
- D. the destruction of all altars in Samaria

**Answer: B.** Hosea 6:6 says, "For I desired mercy (steadfast love), and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings."
