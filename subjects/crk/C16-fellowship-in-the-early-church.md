## Communal living

**Content a: Communal living (Acts 1:15-26; 2:41-47; 4:32-37)**

Communal living means living together as a close community, sharing resources, responsibilities, and fellowship. The Early Church set a powerful example of this lifestyle, which began even before the Holy Spirit was poured out at Pentecost and grew rapidly afterwards.

### The Foundation of the Fellowship: Choosing Matthias (Acts 1:15-26)

Before the day of Pentecost, about one hundred and twenty believers were gathered in an upper room in Jerusalem, praying together. This was their first experience of continuous fellowship after Jesus' ascension. 

During this time, Peter stood up and addressed the brethren about Judas Iscariot. He explained that Judas, who had betrayed Jesus, bought a field with the reward of his wickedness, fell headlong, burst open, and died (the field was called *Akeldama*, meaning Field of Blood). To fulfill the Scriptures (Psalms), Peter said his office of leadership needed to be taken by another.

Peter laid down the **criteria for the replacement**:
1. The person must be one of the men who had accompanied them during all the time Jesus went in and out among them.
2. This period had to be from the baptism of John until the day Jesus was taken up to heaven (the Ascension).
3. The person would become a witness with them to Jesus' resurrection.

The community put forward two candidates:
- **Joseph called Barsabbas** (also known as Justus).
- **Matthias**.

The community then prayed, asking God, who knows the hearts of all men, to show which one He had chosen. They cast lots, and the lot fell on **Matthias**, and he was enrolled with the eleven apostles, making them twelve again. This event shows how the early believers lived in unity, making decisions together through prayer.

### The Practice of Communal Living (Acts 2:41-47)

Following Peter's sermon on the day of Pentecost, about three thousand souls were baptised and added to the church. The Bible describes the lifestyle of this newly formed, large community:

1. **Devotion:** They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching, to fellowship, to the breaking of bread, and to prayers.
2. **Awe and Miracles:** A sense of awe came upon every soul, and many wonders and signs were done through the apostles.
3. **Sharing of Possessions:** All who believed were together and had all things in common. They sold their possessions and goods, and distributed the proceeds to all, as any had need.
4. **Daily Fellowship:** Day by day, they attended the temple together. They broke bread in their homes, sharing food with glad and generous hearts, praising God.
5. **Favour and Growth:** They had favour with all the people, and the Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved.

### Absolute Unity and the Example of Barnabas (Acts 4:32-37)

As the church grew, their communal living deepened. The Bible says that the full number of those who believed were of "one heart and soul." No one claimed that any of the things they possessed was their own; instead, they kept everything in common. 

Because of this intense love and sharing, **there was not a needy person among them**. Those who owned lands or houses would sell them, bring the proceeds of what was sold, and lay it at the apostles' feet. The apostles would then distribute the money to each person according to their need.

The Bible highlights one specific man as a perfect example of this spirit:
- **Joseph**, a Levite, a native of Cyprus.
- The apostles surnamed him **Barnabas** (which means "Son of encouragement").
- He sold a field which belonged to him, brought the money, and laid it at the apostles' feet.

## Reasons for communal living in the Early Church

**Objective i: identify the reasons for communal living in the Early Church.**

The early Christians did not adopt this lifestyle by accident. Several key reasons drove them to live and share communally:

1. **The Influence of the Holy Spirit:** The outpouring of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost filled the believers with divine love and a deep sense of unity. They saw each other as true brothers and sisters in Christ.
2. **Jesus' Teaching on Love:** Jesus had taught them to love their neighbours as themselves and to care for the poor. Communal living was the practical application of this teaching.
3. **Expectation of the Second Coming (Parousia):** The early believers expected Jesus to return very soon. Because they believed the end of the world was near, material wealth and property lost their value. They preferred to sell their lands to help others now, rather than hoard wealth for a future they did not expect to see.
4. **Practical Necessity for Pilgrims:** Many of the three thousand converts on the day of Pentecost were Jews from the Diaspora (other nations) who had travelled to Jerusalem for the feast. When they converted, they stayed back in Jerusalem to learn from the apostles instead of returning home. They had no jobs or homes in Jerusalem, so the local believers had to support them with food and shelter.
5. **Eradicating Poverty:** The community wanted to ensure social justice among themselves so that, as the Bible says, "there was not a needy person among them."

## Problems of communal living and solutions

**Content b: Problems of communal living and solutions (Acts 5:1-11, 6:1-6)**
**Objective ii: identify the problems of communal living and their solutions.**

Wherever humans gather, problems eventually arise. In the Early Church, two major internal problems threatened the fellowship: hypocrisy (deceit) and administrative discrimination (nepotism). 

### Problem 1: Deceit and Hypocrisy – Ananias and Sapphira (Acts 5:1-11)

While Barnabas was a genuine example of generosity, a couple named **Ananias and Sapphira** tried to fake it. 
1. Ananias sold a piece of property but, with his wife's knowledge, kept back some of the proceeds for himself.
2. He brought only a part of the money and laid it at the apostles' feet, pretending it was the full amount.
3. Peter, guided by the Holy Spirit, confronted him, saying: *"Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit and to keep back part of the proceeds of the land?"* Peter reminded him that the land was his to keep or sell, and the money was his to manage; his sin was not holding back money, but lying to God.
4. Upon hearing these words, Ananias fell down and died. Great fear fell upon all who heard of it. Young men came, wrapped him up, and buried him.
5. About three hours later, his wife **Sapphira** came in, not knowing what had happened.
6. Peter asked her if they had sold the land for the specific amount Ananias had brought. She lied and said yes.
7. Peter rebuked her for agreeing together to *"tempt the Spirit of the Lord."* He told her the feet of those who buried her husband were at the door and would carry her out too.
8. She immediately fell down at his feet and died. The young men buried her beside her husband.

**The Solution/Result:** Divine judgment was the solution. It completely rooted out the hypocrisy. The result was that **great fear** fell upon the whole church and upon all who heard of these things. It maintained the purity and reverence of the fellowship. Today, this teaches Nigerian Christians that God hates corruption, deceit, and doing good things merely to seek human praise.

### Problem 2: Discrimination and Nepotism – The Hellenist Dispute (Acts 6:1-6)

As the number of disciples multiplied, a serious administrative and ethnic problem arose concerning the distribution of food.
1. The **Hellenists** (Greek-speaking Jews who had lived outside Palestine) murmured against the **Hebrews** (Aramaic/Hebrew-speaking Jews native to Palestine).
2. The Hellenists complained that their widows were being neglected in the daily distribution of food, while the Hebrew widows were favoured.

**The Solution:**
The twelve apostles summoned the body of disciples. They stated that it was not right for them to give up preaching the word of God to "serve tables" (manage food distribution). 
They commanded the believers to pick out **seven men** with specific qualities to handle this duty. 
The criteria for the seven men were:
1. Of good repute (good reputation).
2. Full of the Spirit.
3. Full of wisdom.

The community chose seven men: **Stephen** (a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit), **Philip**, **Prochorus**, **Nicanor**, **Timon**, **Parmenas**, and **Nicolaus** (a proselyte of Antioch). Interestingly, all seven had Greek names, which suggests the church wisely chose leaders from the aggrieved group (the Hellenists) to ensure fairness.
The chosen men were brought to the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands on them to commission them (these men are traditionally considered the first *deacons*).

**The Result:** The problem was solved peacefully. The word of God increased, the number of disciples multiplied greatly in Jerusalem, and even a great many of the Jewish priests became obedient to the faith.

## How communal living helped the growth of the Early Church

**Objective iii: examine how communal living helped the growth of the Early Church.**

The practice of communal living was not just a welfare system; it was the engine of the early church's massive growth. It helped the church grow in several ways:

1. **It created total unity:** Because believers were of "one heart and soul," the church was strong. This unity made their prayers powerful and their fellowship unbroken by class or wealth divisions.
2. **It provided for the needy and vulnerable:** By pooling resources, the physical survival of the poorest members (especially widows and foreign pilgrims) was guaranteed. No one starved, which made the Christian community deeply attractive.
3. **It freed the leadership for ministry:** Because the community provided for everyone and later appointed deacons to handle administration, the apostles could focus entirely on "prayer and to the ministry of the word." This led to better teaching and faster spread of the gospel.
4. **It served as a powerful witness to outsiders:** The unselfish love, generosity, and joy the Christians displayed earned them "favour with all the people." Non-believers were amazed by how these Christians loved one another, leading the Lord to add to their number daily.
5. **It maintained spiritual purity:** The strict discipline within the community (like the death of Ananias and Sapphira) ensured that people did not join the church for selfish financial gain, keeping the church spiritually healthy and highly respected.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Criteria for replacing Judas:** Remember the specific requirement Peter gave—the person must have been with them from John's baptism to Jesus' ascension.
- **Know the two candidates:** Joseph called Barsabbas (Justus) and Matthias. Matthias won.
- **The meaning of Barnabas:** The apostles gave Joseph the name Barnabas, which means "Son of encouragement." JAMB frequently asks this.
- **The sin of Ananias:** It was not that he didn't give everything. Peter made it clear the money was his to keep. His sin was **hypocrisy and lying to the Holy Spirit**. 
- **The Seven Deacons:** Memorise the criteria for their selection: men of good repute, full of the Spirit, and full of wisdom. Stephen and Philip are the most commonly tested names among the seven.
- **Hellenists vs. Hebrews:** Ensure you know *who* murmured against *whom*. The Hellenists murmured against the Hebrews because the Hellenist widows were neglected.

## Quick summary

1. Before Pentecost, the 120 believers gathered in fellowship and replaced Judas Iscariot with Matthias, choosing him by prayer and casting lots.
2. After Pentecost, the believers lived communally, sharing all things, breaking bread, and praying together daily, which brought them favour with all people.
3. Barnabas was an outstanding example of this fellowship; he sold a field and gave the money to the apostles.
4. Reasons for communal living included the Holy Spirit's influence, Christ's teachings on love, the expectation of the Second Coming, and the need to care for poor pilgrims.
5. The first problem was deceit by Ananias and Sapphira, who lied about their property sale and were struck dead, bringing fear and purity to the church.
6. The second problem was the Hellenists complaining that their widows were neglected in the food distribution by the Hebrews.
7. The apostles solved this by appointing seven men (including Stephen and Philip) of good repute, full of the Spirit and wisdom, to handle welfare.
8. Communal living drove the growth of the early church by creating unity, ensuring no one lacked, freeing the apostles to preach, and attracting outsiders through incredible love.

## Practice questions

**1. The main criterion for choosing a replacement for Judas Iscariot was that the person must**

- A. be a Greek-speaking Jew of good reputation
- B. have been with the disciples from the time of John's baptism until Christ's ascension
- C. be full of the Holy Spirit and wisdom
- D. have sold his property and laid the money at the apostles' feet

**Answer: B.** Peter stated that the replacement must have accompanied them during the entire earthly ministry of Jesus, from John's baptism to the ascension (Acts 1:21-22).

**2. Which of the following men was chosen to replace Judas Iscariot?**

- A. Joseph called Barsabbas
- B. Justus
- C. Matthias
- D. Barnabas

**Answer: C.** The lot fell on Matthias, and he was numbered with the eleven apostles (Acts 1:26).

**3. In the Early Church, Barnabas distinguished himself by**

- A. being the first martyr for Christ
- B. selling his field and bringing the money to the apostles
- C. serving tables for the Hellenist widows
- D. replacing Judas Iscariot as an apostle

**Answer: B.** Barnabas (Joseph from Cyprus) sold a field and laid the money at the apostles' feet as an act of communal sharing (Acts 4:36-37).

**4. A major reason for communal living in the Early Church was the**

- A. fear of persecution by the Romans
- B. desire of the apostles to become wealthy
- C. command given by the Roman Emperor
- D. expectation of the imminent return of Jesus Christ

**Answer: D.** The early Christians expected the Second Coming (Parousia) very soon, which made earthly possessions less important and encouraged radical sharing.

**5. The sin committed by Ananias and Sapphira which led to their death was**

- A. refusing to sell their land
- B. giving all their money to the Hellenists
- C. lying to the Holy Spirit
- D. speaking against the apostles

**Answer: C.** Peter specifically rebuked Ananias for allowing Satan to fill his heart to "lie to the Holy Spirit" (Acts 5:3).

**6. What was the immediate result of the death of Ananias and Sapphira?**

- A. The believers stopped sharing their goods
- B. The Romans arrested Peter
- C. Great fear came upon the whole church
- D. The Hellenists murmured against the Hebrews

**Answer: C.** The Bible records that as a result of their sudden death, great fear fell upon the whole church and all who heard of it (Acts 5:11).

**7. The murmuring of the Hellenists against the Hebrews was caused by**

- A. the Hebrews refusing to let them preach in the temple
- B. the neglect of their widows in the daily distribution
- C. the unfair collection of temple taxes
- D. the fact that all the apostles were Hebrews

**Answer: B.** The Hellenists complained because their widows were neglected in the daily food distribution compared to the Hebrew widows (Acts 6:1).

**8. To solve the problem of the daily distribution, the apostles instructed the disciples to select seven men who were**

- A. wealthy, brave, and educated
- B. of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom
- C. able to perform miracles and speak in tongues
- D. from the tribe of Levi

**Answer: B.** The apostles said to pick out "seven men of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom, whom we may appoint to this duty" (Acts 6:3).

**9. Which of the following was among the seven deacons appointed to serve tables?**

- A. Barnabas
- B. Matthias
- C. Stephen
- D. Justus

**Answer: C.** Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit, was the first named among the seven appointed men (Acts 6:5).

**10. How did the appointment of the seven deacons immediately help the growth of the Early Church?**

- A. It caused the Roman soldiers to convert
- B. It freed the apostles to focus fully on the ministry of the word and prayer
- C. It allowed the widows to begin preaching in the streets
- D. It stopped all future persecution of the church

**Answer: B.** By delegating the welfare administration to the seven, the apostles could dedicate themselves to prayer and preaching, which led to the word of God increasing and disciples multiplying greatly (Acts 6:4, 7).
