# JAMB Crk Notes: The Holy Spirit and the Mission of the Church

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 17 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: The Holy Spirit and the mission of the Church. It covers two main passages of scripture:

*   **(a) The Pentecost** (Acts 1:8; 2:1-41)
*   **(b) The mission of the Church** (Acts 8:4-40)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. trace the story of the Pentecost;
2. identify the signs associated with the coming of the Holy Spirit;
3. examine the significance of the Pentecost experience;
4. analyse the mission of the Church.

## The Pentecost (Acts 1:8; 2:1-41)

**Content a: The Pentecost (Acts 1:8; 2:1-41)**

Before His ascension into heaven, Jesus commanded His disciples not to leave Jerusalem but to wait for the promise of the Father. He outlined the blueprint for the expansion of the early Church in **Acts 1:8**, stating: 
> "But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth."

This verse sets the stage for the outpouring of the Holy Spirit and the subsequent missionary work of the early Church, which moved outwards from Jerusalem just as Jesus predicted.

### Tracing the story of the Pentecost

**Objective i: trace the story of the Pentecost.**

The word "Pentecost" means "fiftieth". It was a major Jewish harvest festival (the Feast of Weeks) celebrated fifty days after the Passover. Because it was a major festival, Jerusalem was filled with Jews and converts to Judaism (proselytes) from all over the known world.

The story of the Pentecost in Acts 2 follows a clear sequence of events:

1. **The Gathering:** On the day of Pentecost, the apostles and other believers (about 120 people in total) were gathered together in one place (traditionally known as the Upper Room) in Jerusalem, praying and waiting.
2. **The Outpouring:** Suddenly, the Holy Spirit descended upon them, accompanied by physical and audible signs (wind, fire, and speaking in tongues). 
3. **The Crowd's Reaction:** The loud sound attracted a large crowd of Jews from various nations who were in Jerusalem for the festival. The crowd was confused and amazed because every man heard the disciples speaking in his own native language. They asked, "Are not all these which speak Galilaeans? And how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?"
4. **The Mockery:** While many marvelled at this miracle, some mockers accused the disciples of being drunk on "new wine".
5. **Peter's Sermon:** Peter, now filled with boldness, stood up with the other eleven apostles to address the crowd. He explained that they were not drunk, as it was only the third hour of the day (9:00 AM). He boldly preached about the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ.
6. **The Response:** The people were "pricked in their heart" by Peter's words and asked, "Men and brethren, what shall we do?" Peter told them to repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and they would receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.
7. **The Result:** About **3,000 souls** gladly received his word, were baptized, and were added to the Church that very day.

### Signs associated with the coming of the Holy Spirit

**Objective ii: identify the signs associated with the coming of the Holy Spirit.**

When the Holy Spirit fell upon the believers in the Upper Room, the Bible records three specific, miraculous signs that proved His presence:

1. **The Sound of Wind:** There came a sudden sound from heaven "as of a rushing mighty wind", and it filled the whole house where they were sitting. This was an audible sign of the Spirit's power.
2. **Cloven Tongues of Fire:** "And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them." This visual sign represented the divine presence, purification, and the lighting of a spiritual fire within the believers.
3. **Speaking in Other Tongues:** "And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance." This was a vocal sign. The disciples miraculously spoke in foreign languages they had never learned, allowing the diverse crowd in Jerusalem to hear the "wonderful works of God" in their own native dialects.

### The significance of the Pentecost experience

**Objective iii: examine the significance of the Pentecost experience.**

The events of Pentecost are a turning point in the New Testament and hold massive significance for Christians today:

* **The Birth of the Church:** Pentecost is widely considered the birthday of the Christian Church. It marked the transition from a small, fearful group of followers into a bold, empowered, and organized body of believers.
* **Fulfillment of Prophecy:** Peter explained that the outpouring of the Spirit was the direct fulfillment of a prophecy given by the Old Testament prophet **Joel** (Joel 2:28-32), who said, "I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh..."
* **Empowerment for Witnessing:** As Jesus promised in Acts 1:8, the Holy Spirit provided the disciples with divine power and courage. Peter, who had denied Jesus out of fear just weeks earlier, now stood fearlessly before thousands to accuse the Jewish leaders of crucifying the Messiah.
* **Breaking of Language and Ethnic Barriers:** The miracle of tongues reversed the confusion of languages that occurred at the Tower of Babel (Genesis 11). By allowing everyone to hear the gospel in their own language, God showed that the message of salvation was for all nations and tribes.
* **Mass Conversion:** The immediate result was the conversion of 3,000 people. This mass salvation demonstrated the convicting power of the Holy Spirit through the preaching of the Word.
* **Application to Nigerian Life:** Today, the Pentecost experience reminds Christians in Nigeria of the importance of relying on the Holy Spirit for boldness in evangelism, and the need for unity among people of diverse languages and ethnic backgrounds in the church.

## The mission of the Church (Acts 8:4-40)

**Content b: The mission of the Church (Acts 8:4-40)**

Following the stoning of Stephen (the first Christian martyr), a severe persecution broke out against the church in Jerusalem. This forced the believers to scatter throughout the regions of Judaea and Samaria. Only the apostles remained in Jerusalem. 

However, instead of silencing the Church, the persecution fuelled its mission. Acts 8:4 states, "Therefore they that were scattered abroad went every where preaching the word."

### Analysing the mission of the Church

**Objective iv: analyse the mission of the Church.**

To analyse the mission of the early Church as seen in Acts 8, we look at the ministry of **Philip**, one of the seven deacons appointed alongside Stephen. Philip's ministry demonstrates how the Church expanded geographically and socially, exactly as Jesus commanded in Acts 1:8 (from Jerusalem, to Judaea, to Samaria, and to the uttermost parts of the earth).

#### 1. The Mission to Samaria (Acts 8:5-25)
Philip went down to the city of Samaria and preached Christ to them. The Jews and Samaritans historically hated each other, but the gospel bridged this bitter divide.
* **Miracles and Joy:** The Samaritans gave heed to Philip's preaching because they saw the miracles he performed. Unclean spirits crying with loud voices came out of many, and those who were paralyzed (palsies) and lame were healed. As a result, "there was great joy in that city."
* **Simon the Sorcerer:** A man named Simon, who had previously amazed the people of Samaria with his sorcery (magic), also believed and was baptized. 
* **The Apostles' Visit:** When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent **Peter and John**. They prayed for the new Samaritan believers, laid hands on them, and they received the Holy Ghost.
* **Simony:** Seeing that the Holy Spirit was given through the laying on of hands, Simon the Sorcerer offered the apostles money to buy this power. Peter sharply rebuked him, saying, "Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money." (The act of trying to buy spiritual power or church office is now known as *simony*).

#### 2. The Mission to the Ethiopian Eunuch (Acts 8:26-40)
After the success in Samaria, an angel of the Lord told Philip to leave the busy city and go south to a desert road connecting Jerusalem and Gaza.
* **The Encounter:** On this road, Philip met an Ethiopian eunuch. This man was a highly placed official, the treasurer under **Candace, queen of the Ethiopians**. He had come to Jerusalem to worship and was returning home in his chariot, reading from the scroll of the prophet **Isaiah**.
* **The Explanation:** The Spirit told Philip to approach the chariot. Philip heard the man reading Isaiah 53 (the prophecy of the suffering servant being led like a sheep to the slaughter). Philip asked, "Understandest thou what thou readest?" The eunuch honestly replied, "How can I, except some man should guide me?"
* **The Conversion and Baptism:** Philip used that exact scripture to preach Jesus to him. As they travelled, they came to some water. The eunuch asked what hindered him from being baptized. After declaring his belief that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, Philip baptized him in the water.
* **The Departure:** Upon coming out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord miraculously "caught away" Philip. The eunuch saw him no more but went on his way rejoicing, taking the gospel to Africa. Philip was later found preaching in Azotus and all the cities until he reached Caesarea.

#### Analysis of the Church's Mission Strategy
From Philip's activities in Acts 8, we can analyse several key aspects of the Church's mission:
1. **Divine Guidance:** The mission was not random; it was directed by the Holy Spirit and angels (e.g., Philip being sent to a desert road to meet just one man).
2. **Breaking Barriers:** The mission deliberately crossed major cultural borders. Philip preached to the Samaritans (half-Jews despised by strict Jews) and to a Gentile African eunuch, proving the gospel is universal.
3. **Word and Power:** The mission relied on the bold preaching of the scriptures (Philip explaining Isaiah) backed by the power of the Holy Spirit (miracles of healing and deliverance).
4. **Persecution as a Catalyst:** God used the tragedy of persecution to push the believers out of their comfort zone in Jerusalem, accelerating global evangelism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Pentecost** | A Jewish agricultural festival held 50 days after Passover; the day the Holy Spirit descended on the early Church. |
| **Cloven tongues** | Divided or parted flames resembling tongues of fire that rested on the believers at Pentecost. |
| **Proselyte** | A Gentile who has converted fully to Judaism. |
| **Samaritans** | A group of people living between Judaea and Galilee, despised by the Jews due to their mixed ancestry and differing religious practices. |
| **Sorcery** | The use of magic or witchcraft; practiced by Simon in Samaria. |
| **Simony** | The sin of attempting to buy spiritual gifts, influence, or church offices with money (named after Simon the Sorcerer). |
| **Eunuch** | A castrated man; historically often employed as high-ranking officials or guards in royal courts. |
| **Candace** | The traditional title given to the queens of Ethiopia (the Kingdom of Kush). |
| **Azotus** | The city where Philip miraculously appeared after baptizing the Ethiopian eunuch (ancient Ashdod). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Memorise the geographic progression:** JAMB often asks for the order of places mentioned in Acts 1:8. The correct order is: Jerusalem → Judaea → Samaria → the uttermost part of the earth.
* **Know the signs of Pentecost:** Be able to differentiate between what was heard (a rushing mighty wind, speaking in tongues) and what was seen (cloven tongues of fire). 
* **Do not confuse the numbers:** **3,000** people were saved on the day of Pentecost (Acts 2). (Later in Acts 4, the number of men grew to 5,000—stick to 3,000 for Pentecost).
* **Identify the prophet:** Peter quoted the prophet **Joel** to explain the outpouring of the Holy Spirit. Do not choose Isaiah or Jeremiah for the Pentecost sermon.
* **Know Philip's converts:** Philip preached to the Samaritans (where Simon the Sorcerer was) and the Ethiopian eunuch (who served Queen Candace).
* **Identify the scripture read by the Eunuch:** The Ethiopian eunuch was reading the book of the prophet **Isaiah** (specifically Isaiah 53).

## Quick summary

1. In Acts 1:8, Jesus promised the disciples power from the Holy Spirit to be witnesses in Jerusalem, Judaea, Samaria, and the ends of the earth.
2. On the day of Pentecost, the Holy Spirit descended on the believers with the sound of a rushing wind, cloven tongues of fire, and the ability to speak in other languages.
3. Peter preached a bold sermon explaining that this was the fulfillment of Joel's prophecy, resulting in the conversion of 3,000 souls and the birth of the Church.
4. Severe persecution following Stephen's death scattered the believers, forcing them to take the gospel outside Jerusalem.
5. Philip preached in Samaria, performing miracles, bringing great joy, and encountering Simon the Sorcerer.
6. Directed by an angel, Philip travelled to a desert road to explain the prophet Isaiah to an Ethiopian eunuch.
7. Philip baptized the eunuch, taking the gospel across ethnic lines into Africa, before the Spirit caught Philip away to Azotus.

## Practice questions

**1. According to Jesus in Acts 1:8, the disciples were to receive power to be witnesses first in**
- A. Samaria
- B. the uttermost part of the earth
- C. Judaea
- D. Jerusalem

**Answer: D.** Jesus instructed them to begin their witness in Jerusalem, then Judaea, then Samaria, and finally to the uttermost part of the earth.

**2. Which of the following was NOT a sign associated with the descent of the Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost?**
- A. Cloven tongues like fire
- B. A dove descending from heaven
- C. The sound of a rushing mighty wind
- D. Believers speaking in other tongues

**Answer: B.** The dove descending from heaven occurred at Jesus' baptism, not at Pentecost. The signs at Pentecost were wind, fire, and speaking in tongues.

**3. During his sermon on the day of Pentecost, Peter quoted a prophecy to explain the pouring out of the Holy Spirit. Which prophet did he quote?**
- A. Isaiah
- B. Jeremiah
- C. Joel
- D. Ezekiel

**Answer: C.** Peter quoted the prophet Joel (Joel 2:28-32), saying, "I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh."

**4. How many souls were added to the Church following Peter's sermon on the day of Pentecost?**
- A. 120
- B. 3,000
- C. 4,000
- D. 5,000

**Answer: B.** The Bible records that those who gladly received his word were baptized, and the same day there were added unto them about 3,000 souls (Acts 2:41).

**5. What event primarily caused the early believers to scatter from Jerusalem and begin preaching in other regions?**
- A. The day of Pentecost
- B. A great famine in Jerusalem
- C. The persecution that arose after the death of Stephen
- D. The conversion of Saul of Tarsus

**Answer: C.** A great persecution against the church in Jerusalem broke out following the stoning of Stephen, scattering the believers throughout Judaea and Samaria.

**6. Who went down to the city of Samaria and preached Christ to them with great success?**
- A. Peter
- B. Paul
- C. Philip
- D. John

**Answer: C.** Philip the evangelist (one of the seven deacons) went down to Samaria and preached, bringing great joy to the city.

**7. In Samaria, a man tried to buy the power to give the Holy Spirit with money. What was his name?**
- A. Ananias
- B. Bar-Jesus
- C. Elymas
- D. Simon

**Answer: D.** Simon the Sorcerer believed and was baptized, but later tried to buy the power of the Holy Spirit from Peter and John, leading to a sharp rebuke.

**8. The Ethiopian eunuch whom Philip met was an official under the authority of**
- A. Pharaoh, king of Egypt
- B. Candace, queen of the Ethiopians
- C. Herod the Great
- D. Caesar Augustus

**Answer: B.** He was a eunuch of great authority under Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, and had charge of all her treasure.

**9. When Philip approached the Ethiopian eunuch's chariot, from which Old Testament book was the eunuch reading?**
- A. Psalms
- B. Isaiah
- C. Jeremiah
- D. Genesis

**Answer: B.** The eunuch was reading from the scroll of the prophet Isaiah (specifically the passage about the suffering servant in Isaiah 53).

**10. "Understandest thou what thou readest?" What was the Ethiopian eunuch's reply to this question from Philip?**
- A. "I understand fully, for the Spirit teaches me."
- B. "How can I, except some man should guide me?"
- C. "Yes, I am reading of the Messiah to come."
- D. "I do not understand the Hebrew language."

**Answer: B.** The eunuch humbly acknowledged his need for instruction, replying, "How can I, except some man should guide me?", which prompted Philip to preach Jesus to him.
