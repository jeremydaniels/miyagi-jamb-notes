# JAMB Crk Notes: Miracles

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 4 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Miracles. It covers the extraordinary acts performed by Jesus during his earthly ministry. The syllabus groups these into four categories:

* **a. Nature miracles:**
  * (i) Stilling the storm
  * (ii) Feeding of the five thousand
  * (iii) Walking on the sea
  * (iv) Changing water to wine
* **b. Miracles of resuscitation:**
  * (i) The raising of Lazarus
  * (ii) The raising of Jairus’ daughter
  * (iii) The raising of the widow’s son at Nain
* **c. Healing miracles:**
  * (i) The lepers
  * (ii) The paralytic at the pool
  * (iii) The centurion’s servant
  * (iv) The blind
* **d. Exorcism:**
  * (i) The Gerasene (Gadarene) demoniac
  * (ii) The epileptic boy

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Classify the different miracles of Jesus.
2. Indicate the occasion of each of the miracles.
3. Examine the significance of each of the miracles.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** For the feeding of the five thousand, the syllabus lists Matthew 14:13–24, though the actual feeding narrative usually ends at verse 21. For walking on the sea, it lists Matthew 14:22–26, though the story continues to verse 33. The notes below cover the full story of each event so that you understand the complete occasion and significance, while acknowledging the references printed in the syllabus.

## Objective i: Classify the different miracles of Jesus

A **miracle** is an extraordinary event that cannot be explained by natural laws, showing divine intervention. Jesus performed miracles to demonstrate God’s power, to show compassion for suffering people, and to authenticate his claim as the Messiah.

To help us understand his divine authority over all aspects of human existence, Christians and Bible scholars classify Jesus' miracles into four main groups:

| Classification | Meaning | Examples in the Syllabus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nature miracles** | Miracles showing Jesus' absolute control and authority over physical laws, natural elements, and material things. | Stilling the storm, Feeding the 5,000, Walking on the sea, Changing water to wine |
| **Miracles of resuscitation** | Miracles where Jesus brought dead people back to physical life (note: they eventually died again, unlike Jesus' own *resurrection* to eternal life). | Raising of Lazarus, Raising of Jairus' daughter, Raising the widow's son at Nain |
| **Healing miracles** | Miracles curing physical diseases, disabilities, and ailments, restoring people to full health. | Healing lepers, the paralytic at the pool, the centurion's servant, the blind |
| **Exorcism** | Miracles involving the casting out of evil spirits (demons) from possessed individuals. | The Gerasene demoniac, The epileptic boy |

## Nature Miracles

**Objective ii and iii: indicate the occasion and examine the significance of each miracle.**

### Stilling the storm
*(Matthew 8:23-27; Mark 4:35-41; Luke 8:22-25)*

**The Occasion:**
After teaching the crowds in parables, Jesus and his disciples got into a boat to cross over to the other side of the Sea of Galilee. Jesus, exhausted from his ministry, fell asleep on a cushion in the back of the boat. Suddenly, a fierce windstorm arose. The waves beat into the boat, and it began to fill with water, putting them in great danger. 
The disciples woke Jesus up in a panic, crying out, "Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing?" (or as Matthew records, "Save, Lord; we are perishing"). 
Jesus awoke, rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, "Peace! Be still!" Immediately, the wind ceased, and there was a great calm. He then asked his disciples, "Why are you afraid? Have you no faith?" The disciples were filled with awe, asking one another, "Who then is this, that even wind and sea obey him?"

**The Significance:**
1. **Divine Authority:** In Jewish thought, only God had the power to control the chaotic forces of the sea (e.g., Psalm 89:9). By calming the storm with just a word, Jesus demonstrated his divine identity and power over nature.
2. **Faith over Fear:** Jesus' rebuke of the disciples highlights that true faith requires trusting in God's presence and protection even in life's most terrifying "storms."
3. **Jesus' Humanity:** The fact that Jesus was deeply asleep in the boat during a storm shows his genuine physical exhaustion and complete human nature, perfectly combined with his divine power.

### Feeding of the five thousand
*(Matthew 14:13-24; Mark 6:30-44; Luke 9:10-17; John 6:1-13)*

**The Occasion:**
Jesus withdrew by boat to a desolate place for some rest after hearing about the death of John the Baptist. However, a massive crowd from the towns followed him on foot along the shore. Seeing them, Jesus had compassion because they were "like sheep without a shepherd," and he healed their sick and taught them.
When evening came, the disciples asked Jesus to send the crowds away to buy food in the villages. Jesus replied, "You give them something to eat." The disciples pointed out they only had **five loaves and two fish** (which, according to John's Gospel, were provided by a young boy). 
Jesus ordered the crowds to sit down on the green grass in groups of hundreds and fifties. He took the five loaves and two fish, looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke the loaves, and gave them to the disciples to distribute. Everyone ate and was satisfied. Afterwards, they gathered **twelve baskets** full of broken pieces left over. The number of men who ate was about five thousand, not counting women and children.

**The Significance:**
1. **Jesus as the Ultimate Provider:** Jesus provided abundantly for physical needs, mirroring how God provided Manna for the Israelites in the wilderness. He is presented as the new and greater Moses.
2. **The Bread of Life:** In John's Gospel, this miracle leads directly into Jesus' teaching that he is the "Bread of Life," satisfying not just physical hunger, but spiritual hunger.
3. **Compassion and Order:** Jesus' compassion for the helpless crowd was profound. His instruction to organize the people into groups showed order and preparation for God's provision.
4. **Eucharistic Foreshadowing:** The actions of Jesus (taking, blessing, breaking, and giving the bread) foreshadow the Last Supper and the Christian Holy Communion.

### Walking on the sea
*(Matthew 14:22-26; Mark 6:45-52; John 6:16-21)*

**The Occasion:**
Immediately after feeding the five thousand, Jesus made his disciples get into the boat to go ahead of him to the other side (Bethsaida/Capernaum), while he dismissed the crowd and went up on a mountain by himself to pray. 
During the night (the "fourth watch", roughly 3:00 am to 6:00 am), the disciples' boat was far from land, beaten by the waves because the wind was against them. Jesus came toward them, walking directly on the sea. 
When the disciples saw him, they were terrified, thinking it was a ghost, and cried out in fear. Jesus immediately spoke to them, saying, "Take heart, it is I; have no fear." *(Note: Matthew's account records that Peter asked to walk to Jesus on the water. Peter started well but saw the wind, became afraid, began to sink, and cried out to be saved. Jesus caught him, saying, "O man of little faith, why did you doubt?")* When Jesus got into the boat, the wind ceased.

**The Significance:**
1. **The Divine "I AM":** Jesus' words translated "It is I" (Greek: *ego eimi*) echo the divine name of God revealed to Moses ("I AM"). Walking on the sea proved he possesses the very nature of God, who "tramples the waves of the sea" (Job 9:8).
2. **Faith vs. Doubt (Peter's lesson):** Looking at Jesus brings miraculous power and safety; looking at the surrounding problems (the wind and waves) brings fear and failure. 
3. **Constant Care:** Even when Jesus was physically absent on the mountain, he saw their struggle and came to their rescue at the darkest hour.

### Changing water to wine
*(John 2:1-11)*

**The Occasion:**
Jesus, his mother Mary, and his disciples attended a wedding feast in Cana of Galilee. During the celebration, the wine ran out—a situation that would have caused immense social shame for the hosting family. Mary told Jesus, "They have no wine." Jesus responded, "O woman, what have you to do with me? My hour has not yet come." However, Mary told the servants to "Do whatever he tells you."
Nearby were **six stone water jars**, used for the Jewish rites of purification, each holding twenty to thirty gallons. Jesus told the servants to fill them with water. They filled them to the brim. He then told them to draw some out and take it to the master of the feast. 
When the master tasted it, not knowing where it came from, he called the bridegroom and praised him, saying, "Every man serves the good wine first... but you have kept the good wine until now."

**The Significance:**
1. **The First Sign:** John's Gospel calls this the first of Jesus' "signs," through which he manifested his glory, causing his disciples to believe in him.
2. **The New Covenant:** The stone jars represented the old Jewish law and rituals of purification (water). Jesus replacing the water with abundant, joyful wine symbolizes the New Covenant of grace replacing the Old Covenant.
3. **God's Abundance and Joy:** The vast quantity (up to 180 gallons) and superior quality of the wine demonstrate that when Christ gives, he gives abundantly. It also shows his approval of human joy and the institution of marriage.
4. **Mary's Intercession and Faith:** Mary's quiet confidence ("Do whatever he tells you") serves as a model of faith.

## Miracles of Resuscitation

### The raising of Lazarus
*(John 11:1-45)*

**The Occasion:**
Lazarus of Bethany, the brother of Mary and Martha, was very ill. The sisters sent word to Jesus, saying, "Lord, he whom you love is ill." Instead of rushing to Bethany, Jesus intentionally stayed where he was for two more days, stating that the illness was for the glory of God. 
By the time Jesus and the disciples arrived in Bethany, Lazarus had been in the tomb for **four days**. Martha went to meet Jesus, expressing faith that God would still give him whatever he asked. Jesus declared to her, "I am the resurrection and the life." Mary also came, weeping, along with Jewish mourners.
Seeing their sorrow, Jesus was deeply moved in his spirit and **wept**. He went to the cave-tomb and ordered the stone to be rolled away, despite Martha's warning of the terrible smell of decay. After praying aloud to the Father so the crowd would believe, Jesus cried out with a loud voice, "Lazarus, come out." The dead man came out, his hands, feet, and face still wrapped in burial cloths. Jesus told them, "Unbind him, and let him go."

**The Significance:**
1. **Conquest of Death:** This is the most dramatic resuscitation miracle, proving Jesus' claim that he is "the resurrection and the life." The detail of "four days" proved Lazarus was irreversibly dead according to Jewish belief (which held the soul lingered near the body for only three days).
2. **Foreshadowing the Resurrection:** This miracle vividly anticipated Jesus' own death and resurrection. 
3. **The Catalyst for the Cross:** Because of this spectacular public miracle, many Jews believed in him. Consequently, the Sanhedrin (led by Caiaphas) officially plotted to put Jesus to death to prevent a Roman crackdown (John 11:45-53).
4. **The Humanity of Jesus:** "Jesus wept" (John 11:35) perfectly illustrates his deep emotional empathy and human grief over the pain death causes.

### The raising of Jairus’ daughter
*(Luke 8:41-42, 49-56; Mark 5:21-43)*

**The Occasion:**
Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue, fell at Jesus' feet, begging him to come to his house because his only daughter, about twelve years old, was dying. As Jesus went, a large crowd pressed around him (this is when a woman with a bleeding issue touched his garment and was healed, causing a delay).
While Jesus was still speaking to the woman, messengers arrived from Jairus' house, saying, "Your daughter is dead; do not trouble the Teacher any more." Jesus turned to Jairus and said, "Do not fear; only believe, and she will be well."
When they arrived at the house, people were weeping and wailing loudly. Jesus told them to stop, saying, "She is not dead but sleeping," and they laughed at him. He put the mourners outside, took only Peter, James, John, and the girl's parents into the room. He took her by the hand and called out, "Child, arise" (Mark records the Aramaic: *Talitha cumi*). Her spirit returned, she got up at once, and Jesus ordered that she be given something to eat.

**The Significance:**
1. **Faith in the Face of Despair:** Jesus demands faith ("only believe") even when the situation appears absolutely hopeless (she was already dead).
2. **Spiritual vs. Physical Reality:** Jesus referred to her death as "sleeping." For the Lord of Life, physical death is as temporary and reversible as sleep.
3. **Compassionate Care for All:** Sandwiched together, the healing of the bleeding woman (a social outcast) and Jairus' daughter (child of a prominent religious leader) shows Jesus' grace is for all classes of people.
4. **Practical Care:** Jesus asking for her to be given food showed his attention to practical, physical realities alongside immense spiritual power.

### The raising of the widow’s son at Nain
*(Luke 7:11-17)*

**The Occasion:**
As Jesus approached the gate of a town called Nain, accompanied by his disciples and a large crowd, a funeral procession was coming out. The dead man was the **only son of his mother, and she was a widow**. A large crowd from the town was with her.
When Jesus saw her, he had compassion on her and said, "Do not weep." He stepped forward and touched the bier (the open coffin/stretcher), causing the pallbearers to stop. Jesus then said, "Young man, I say to you, arise." The dead man sat up and began to speak, and Jesus gave him back to his mother.
Fear seized all the people, and they glorified God, saying, "A great prophet has arisen among us!" and "God has visited his people!"

**The Significance:**
1. **Unprompted Compassion:** Unlike Jairus or Martha who asked for help, the widow asked for nothing. Jesus acted entirely out of pure pity for her severe social and economic vulnerability (losing a husband, and now an only son, meant destitution in ancient Israel).
2. **Jesus as the Great Prophet:** The people correctly connected this miracle to the prophets Elijah and Elisha, who had also raised the sons of widows (1 Kings 17, 2 Kings 4). By doing so by his *own* command ("I say to you"), Jesus showed he was greater than them.

## Healing Miracles

### The lepers
*(Matthew 8:1-4; Mark 1:40-45; Luke 5:12-16; 17:11-19)*

**The Occasion (The Single Leper - Matt/Mark/Luke 5):**
A man full of leprosy came to Jesus, fell on his face, and begged, "Lord, if you will, you can make me clean." Moved with pity, Jesus reached out and **touched him**, saying, "I will; be clean." The leprosy left him immediately. Jesus strictly charged him to tell no one, but to go show himself to the priest and offer the sacrifice commanded by Moses for his cleansing, as a proof to the authorities. Instead, the man went out and proclaimed it freely.

**The Occasion (The Ten Lepers - Luke 17):**
On his way to Jerusalem, passing between Samaria and Galilee, Jesus was met by ten lepers who stood at a distance (as the law required). They called out, "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." Jesus told them, "Go and show yourselves to the priests." 
As they went, they were cleansed. **Only one of them**, realizing he was healed, turned back, praising God with a loud voice. He fell on his face at Jesus' feet, giving him thanks. **He was a Samaritan.** Jesus asked, "Were not ten cleansed? Where are the nine? Was no one found to return and give praise to God except this foreigner?" He then told the man, "Rise and go your way; your faith has made you well."

**The Significance:**
1. **Defeating Stigma and Taboo:** Leprosy was a terrifying, highly contagious skin disease. Lepers were social outcasts, considered ceremonially "unclean." By *touching* the single leper, Jesus showed that his purity and healing power defeated the defilement of disease.
2. **Faith and Obedience:** The ten lepers had to show faith by acting on Jesus' word (heading to the priests) before the healing actually took place.
3. **Gratitude and Salvation:** In Luke 17, while all ten received physical healing, only the grateful Samaritan received Jesus' commendation ("your faith has made you well" / "saved you"). Gratitude opens the door to complete spiritual restoration, not just physical relief.
4. **Grace for Outsiders:** The fact that the only grateful man was a Samaritan (despised by Jews) emphasizes Luke’s theme of God’s grace extending to non-Jews.

### The paralytic at the pool
*(John 5:1-17)*

**The Occasion:**
During a Jewish feast, Jesus went to Jerusalem to the **Pool of Bethesda**, which had five roofed colonnades. A multitude of invalids, blind, lame, and paralyzed people lay there. According to popular belief, an angel occasionally stirred the water, and the first to step in would be healed.
Jesus saw a man who had been paralyzed for **thirty-eight years**. Jesus asked him, "Do you want to be healed?" The sick man answered, "Sir, I have no man to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up..." 
Jesus said to him, "Rise, take up your bed and walk." At once, the man was healed, took up his bed, and walked. 
However, **that day was the Sabbath**. The Jewish leaders rebuked the man for carrying his bed. Later, Jesus found the man in the temple and warned him, "See, you are well! Sin no more, that nothing worse may happen to you." The man told the Jews it was Jesus who healed him. The Jews began to persecute Jesus for working on the Sabbath. Jesus answered them, "My Father is working until now, and I am working."

**The Significance:**
1. **Sovereign Grace:** Jesus chose to heal one man out of a multitude, bypassing the superstitious belief about the stirring water. His word alone had the power to heal.
2. **Lord of the Sabbath:** This miracle provoked intense controversy. Jesus proved that doing good and saving life is the true purpose of the Sabbath. His claim that he was working just as his Father works was understood by the Jews as making himself equal with God (John 5:18).
3. **Sin and Sickness:** Jesus' warning to "sin no more" suggests that in this specific case (though not in all cases, as seen in John 9), the man's long illness may have been the consequence of a past sin.

### The centurion’s servant
*(Matthew 8:5-13; Luke 7:1-10)*

**The Occasion:**
When Jesus entered Capernaum, a **Roman centurion** (a military officer in charge of 100 soldiers) sent Jewish elders to Jesus. The centurion’s servant, who was highly valued by him, was sick and at the point of death (Matthew notes he was paralyzed and suffering terribly). The Jewish elders pleaded earnestly, saying the centurion was worthy because he loved their nation and built their synagogue.
Jesus went with them, but when he was not far from the house, the centurion sent friends (and came out himself) saying, "Lord, do not trouble yourself, for I am not worthy to have you come under my roof... **But say the word, and let my servant be healed.** For I too am a man set under authority, with soldiers under me..."
When Jesus heard this, he marvelled at him, turned to the crowd and said, "I tell you, not even in Israel have I found such faith." Jesus declared that many from the East and West (Gentiles) would sit at the table in the kingdom of heaven. He then said to the centurion, "Go; let it be done for you as you have believed." The servant was healed at that very moment.

**The Significance:**
1. **Unprecedented Faith:** The centurion understood authority perfectly. Just as his soldiers obeyed his orders without question, he knew sickness would obey Jesus' command instantly, without Jesus needing to be physically present.
2. **Humility:** Despite his high social and military status, and his wealth (building a synagogue), the centurion recognized his spiritual unworthiness before Jesus ("not worthy to have you come under my roof").
3. **Universal Kingdom:** This miracle is a major turning point indicating that the Gospel is for Gentiles too. Faith, not Jewish lineage, is the requirement for entering God's kingdom.

### The blind
*(John 9:1-12; Mark 10:46-52; Luke 18:35-43)*

The syllabus covers two major healing stories of blindness.

**1. The Man Born Blind (John 9:1-12):**
Passing by in Jerusalem, Jesus saw a man blind from birth. The disciples asked, "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" Jesus answered, "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents, but that the works of God might be displayed in him." 
Jesus spat on the ground, made mud with the saliva, anointed the man's eyes with it, and told him to go wash in the **Pool of Siloam** (which means "Sent"). The man went, washed, and came back seeing.

*Significance:* It corrects the false belief that all suffering is a direct punishment for personal sin. Instead, God can use suffering to display His glory. It also proves Jesus' claim in the same chapter to be the "Light of the World," who brings spiritual sight to those in darkness.

**2. Blind Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46-52 / Luke 18:35-43):**
As Jesus was leaving Jericho with a large crowd, a blind beggar named Bartimaeus (son of Timaeus) was sitting by the roadside. Hearing it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!" Many in the crowd sternly rebuked him and told him to be quiet, but he cried out all the more.
Jesus stopped and commanded him to be called. Throwing off his cloak, Bartimaeus sprang up and came to Jesus. "What do you want me to do for you?" Jesus asked. "Rabbi, let me recover my sight." Jesus said, "Go your way; your faith has made you well." He recovered his sight immediately and followed Jesus on the way.

*Significance:* 
*   **Spiritual Insight:** Though physically blind, Bartimaeus had sharp spiritual vision—he was one of the first to publicly acknowledge Jesus as the "Son of David" (the promised Messiah).
*   **Persistence in Prayer:** Despite opposition from the crowd, Bartimaeus persevered in his cries for mercy, representing aggressive, persistent faith.

## Exorcism

**Objective ii and iii: indicate the occasion and examine the significance of each miracle.**

### The Gerasene (Gadarene) demoniac
*(Matthew 8:28-34; Mark 5:1-20; Luke 8:26-39)*

**The Occasion:**
Jesus and his disciples crossed the Sea of Galilee to the country of the Gerasenes (a Gentile region). As soon as Jesus stepped out of the boat, he was met by a man possessed by demons. The man lived among the tombs, wore no clothes, and was incredibly violent—he had been bound with chains and shackles, but he broke them apart. No one had the strength to subdue him. He cried out day and night and cut himself with stones.
Seeing Jesus from afar, he ran and fell down before him, crying out with a loud voice, "What have you to do with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I adjure you by God, do not torment me." 
Jesus asked his name. The demon replied, "My name is **Legion**, for we are many." (A Roman legion was about 6,000 soldiers). The demons begged Jesus not to send them out of the country (or into the abyss), but instead into a large herd of **about 2,000 pigs** feeding nearby. Jesus gave them permission. The demons entered the pigs, and the entire herd rushed down a steep bank into the sea and drowned.
The herdsmen fled and told the city. The townspeople came and found the previously possessed man clothed and in his right mind. Terrified, and upset over the economic loss of the pigs, they begged Jesus to leave their region. The healed man wanted to follow Jesus, but Jesus sent him back, saying, "Go home to your friends and tell them how much the Lord has done for you." The man went away and proclaimed Jesus' works throughout the Decapolis (the Ten Cities).

**The Significance:**
1. **Ultimate Spiritual Authority:** Jesus demonstrated total dominance over massed, violent forces of evil. Even a "Legion" of demons trembled before the "Son of the Most High God."
2. **The Value of a Human Soul:** By permitting the destruction of a massive herd of valuable livestock to save one man, Jesus demonstrated that one human soul is worth more than immense material wealth.
3. **Misplaced Priorities:** The townspeople prioritized their economic loss over the miraculous restoration of a human life, asking the Saviour to leave.
4. **The First Missionary:** Jesus instructed the man to stay in his Gentile homeland as a witness. He became an effective early evangelist in the Decapolis.

### The epileptic boy
*(Mark 9:14-29; Luke 9:37-43a; Matthew 17:14-21)*

**The Occasion:**
Immediately after coming down from the Mount of Transfiguration, Jesus, Peter, James, and John found the other disciples arguing with the scribes, surrounded by a crowd. A man stepped forward, explaining that he had brought his son to the disciples, but they could not heal him. The boy was possessed by a mute and deaf spirit that caused severe epileptic-like seizures—dashing him to the ground, making him foam at the mouth, grind his teeth, and become rigid. Often it cast him into fire and water to destroy him.
Jesus lamented, "O faithless generation, how long am I to be with you?" They brought the boy to Jesus, and the spirit immediately convulsed him. The father pleaded, "If you can do anything, have compassion on us and help us."
Jesus replied, "**'If you can'! All things are possible for one who believes.**" 
The father immediately cried out, "I believe; help my unbelief!" 
Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, commanding it to come out and never enter him again. After crying out and convulsing the boy terribly, it came out. The boy looked like a corpse, but Jesus took him by the hand and lifted him up.
Later, indoors, the disciples asked Jesus why they could not cast it out. Jesus answered, "This kind cannot be driven out by anything but prayer" (and fasting, as some manuscripts add).

**The Significance:**
1. **The Necessity of Active Faith:** The father's desperate plea ("I believe; help my unbelief") is a profound model of honesty in prayer. Jesus responds to even imperfect faith when it is directed toward him.
2. **Spiritual Preparedness:** The disciples failed because they attempted to perform an exorcism based on past success or routine, neglecting deep, continuous dependence on God through prayer. Spiritual power requires constant fellowship with God.
3. **Contrast with the Mountaintop:** The miracle sharply contrasts the heavenly glory of the Transfiguration Jesus had just left, with the demonic suffering and human faithlessness waiting in the valley below.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Miracle** | An extraordinary event that goes beyond natural laws, demonstrating divine power and intervention. |
| **Resuscitation** | The act of bringing a dead person back to physical life. (They will eventually die again, unlike resurrection to eternal life). |
| **Exorcism** | The act of casting out evil spirits (demons) from a possessed person by divine authority. |
| **Legion** | A division of the Roman army (approx. 6,000 men). Used by the Gerasene demon to indicate they were many in number. |
| **Decapolis** | A group of ten Hellenistic (Greek-cultured) cities located primarily east of the Jordan River. |
| **Cana** | A village in Galilee where Jesus performed his first miracle (turning water to wine). |
| **Centurion** | A professional officer of the Roman army in command of a century (about 100 soldiers). |
| **Synagogue ruler** | A respected local official responsible for the physical care of the synagogue and ordering of its services (e.g., Jairus). |
| **Sabbath** | The Jewish day of rest (Saturday), during which the Pharisees enforced strict laws prohibiting any "work," including healing. |
| **Talitha cumi** | An Aramaic phrase meaning "Little girl, I say to you, arise," spoken by Jesus to Jairus' daughter. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Know the four categories perfectly:** JAMB frequently asks you to classify a specific miracle. E.g., "Raising Lazarus is an example of..." (Answer: resuscitation). "Stilling the storm is an example of..." (Answer: nature miracle).
* **Don't confuse resuscitation with resurrection:** Lazarus, Jairus' daughter, and the widow's son experienced *resuscitation* because they eventually died a physical death again later in life. 
* **Distinguish between the two blindness stories:** The *Man Born Blind* (John 9) was sent to wash in the Pool of Siloam to demonstrate God's works. *Blind Bartimaeus* (Mark 10) sat by the road in Jericho and cried out "Son of David".
* **Know the names and places:** 
  * The wedding was at **Cana**.
  * The paralytic was at the **Pool of Bethesda** (ill for 38 years).
  * The widow was from **Nain**.
  * The demoniac was in the country of the **Gerasenes (Gadarenes)**.
* **The Ten Lepers:** Remember that only *one* returned to give thanks, and he was a **Samaritan** (Luke 17).
* **Jesus' first miracle:** Turning water to wine at Cana is explicitly called the first of his signs in John's Gospel.

## Quick summary

1. Jesus performed miracles to demonstrate his divine authority, validate his messiahship, and show profound compassion for human suffering.
2. **Nature miracles** (stilling the storm, feeding 5,000, walking on water, water to wine) showed his sovereign control over physical elements and natural laws.
3. **Resuscitation miracles** (Lazarus, Jairus' daughter, the widow's son at Nain) proved his absolute power over death, foreshadowing his own resurrection. 
4. **Healing miracles** (lepers, paralytic, centurion's servant, the blind) restored the sick to physical health and social inclusion, demanding and rewarding faith.
5. **Exorcisms** (Gerasene demoniac, epileptic boy) demonstrated Jesus' ultimate authority over Satan and his demonic forces, freeing people from spiritual bondage.
6. The miracles often provoked two distinct reactions: deep faith and worship from those who believed, and severe opposition from religious leaders whose traditions were challenged.
7. Jesus often used miracles to teach spiritual truths, such as faith overcoming fear, spiritual vision vs. spiritual blindness, and the need for persistent prayer.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following is classified as a nature miracle?**
- A. Healing the centurion's servant
- B. Raising the widow's son at Nain
- C. The healing of the paralytic at the pool
- D. Changing water to wine

**Answer: D.** Changing water to wine is a nature miracle because it involves authority over material elements, unlike healing, resuscitation, or exorcism.

**2. When Jesus stilled the storm on the Sea of Galilee, he questioned the disciples about their**
- A. lack of fasting
- B. failure to pray
- C. lack of faith
- D. physical exhaustion

**Answer: C.** After calming the storm, Jesus asked them, "Why are you afraid? Have you no faith?" showing that faith overcomes the fear of physical circumstances.

**3. In the healing of the ten lepers, the only one who returned to thank Jesus was a**
- A. Roman centurion
- B. Samaritan
- C. Jew
- D. Syrophoenician

**Answer: B.** The only one who returned to praise God and thank Jesus was a Samaritan, highlighting the theme of grace extending to foreigners.

**4. According to John's Gospel, how long had Lazarus been in the tomb when Jesus arrived?**
- A. Two days
- B. Three days
- C. Four days
- D. Seven days

**Answer: C.** Lazarus had been in the tomb for four days, a detail that emphasized he was irreversibly dead according to Jewish customs, making the miracle undeniable.

**5. "I am not worthy to have you come under my roof; but only say the word, and let my servant be healed." Who made this statement?**
- A. Jairus, the synagogue ruler
- B. The centurion in Capernaum
- C. The nobleman from Cana
- D. Peter, on the Sea of Galilee

**Answer: B.** The Roman centurion said this, demonstrating his profound understanding of authority and faith, which Jesus praised highly.

**6. Why were the disciples unable to cast the demon out of the epileptic boy?**
- A. Because the demon was named Legion
- B. Because they did not have Jesus' physical cloak
- C. Because the boy did not have faith
- D. Because that kind of spirit only comes out through prayer

**Answer: D.** Jesus told his disciples in private that their failure was due to a lack of spiritual preparation, saying, "This kind cannot be driven out by anything but prayer."

**7. Jesus healed a man who had been paralyzed for thirty-eight years at the Pool of**
- A. Siloam
- B. Bethesda
- C. Jordan
- D. Galilee

**Answer: B.** The healing of the paralytic took place at the Pool of Bethesda (which had five porticoes). The Pool of Siloam is where the man born blind was sent to wash.

**8. The demons inside the Gerasene demoniac begged Jesus to send them into a herd of**
- A. sheep
- B. cattle
- C. pigs
- D. goats

**Answer: C.** The demons begged to be sent into a herd of about 2,000 pigs, which then rushed down a steep bank into the sea and drowned.

**9. When Jairus received news that his daughter was dead, what did Jesus tell him?**
- A. "Let the dead bury their own dead."
- B. "Do not fear, only believe."
- C. "Your faith has made her well."
- D. "Weep not, for she is sleeping."

**Answer: B.** Jesus told the terrified father, "Do not fear; only believe, and she will be well," demanding absolute faith in the face of death.

**10. What was the purpose of Jesus asking the blind man in John 9 to wash in the Pool of Siloam?**
- A. To wash away the man's personal sins
- B. To show the healing power of the angel that stirred the water
- C. To demonstrate obedience before his sight was restored
- D. To hide the miracle from the Pharisees

**Answer: C.** Jesus put mud on his eyes and told him to go wash. It was an act requiring the man's obedience and faith; when he washed, he came back seeing. (It had nothing to do with personal sins or an angel).
