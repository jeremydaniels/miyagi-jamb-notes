# JAMB Crk Notes: Sermon on the Mount

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 6 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Sermon on the Mount. It covers the core moral and spiritual teachings of Jesus Christ given to his disciples and the multitudes.

This file covers the following content item:
* **Mt. 5; 6** (The syllabus also references the parallel passage in **Lk. 6:17-26**, which is included in these notes).

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Analyse the teachings on the Mount (Objective i).
2. Identify the demands of the Kingdom (Objective ii).
3. Determine the consequences of placing worldly possessions above heavenly treasures (Objective iii).
4. Associate the rewards for obedience with the Sermon on the Mount (Objective iv).

## Mt. 5; 6: The Sermon on the Mount

The Sermon on the Mount is the longest continuous teaching of Jesus recorded in the New Testament. In Matthew's Gospel, Jesus goes up a mountain, sits down, and teaches his disciples and the gathered crowds. The sermon serves as a manifesto (a public declaration of policies) for the Kingdom of God, explaining how true followers of Christ are expected to live, think, and relate to God and others.

**Note on the Gospel accounts:** 
The syllabus lists Matthew 5 and 6, along with Luke 6:17-26. 
* **Matthew's account** (Matthew 5–7) is called the **Sermon on the Mount** because Jesus taught from a mountainside. 
* **Luke's account** (Luke 6:17-49) is often called the **Sermon on the Plain** because Luke states that Jesus came down and stood on a "level place" before teaching. 

## Objective i: Analyse the teachings on the Mount

Jesus' teachings in Matthew 5 and 6 can be broken down into several major sections: the Beatitudes, the believer's role in the world (Salt and Light), the fulfilment of the Law, and the correct motives for religious practices (piety).

### 1. The Beatitudes (Matthew 5:1-12)

"Beatitude" comes from a Latin word meaning "blessedness" or "supreme happiness." Jesus began his sermon with nine statements describing the character of true Kingdom citizens and the spiritual blessings (rewards) they receive.

| The Character (The Beatitude) | The Meaning | The Reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **"Blessed are the poor in spirit..."** | Those who recognize their spiritual emptiness and total dependence on God. | **"...for theirs is the kingdom of heaven."** |
| **"Blessed are those who mourn..."** | Those who express deep sorrow for sin and the evil in the world. | **"...for they shall be comforted."** |
| **"Blessed are the meek..."** | Those who are gentle, humble, and submit to God's will rather than seeking power. | **"...for they shall inherit the earth."** |
| **"Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness..."** | Those who have a passionate desire to do God's will and see justice done. | **"...for they shall be satisfied (filled)."** |
| **"Blessed are the merciful..."** | Those who show compassion, kindness, and forgiveness to others. | **"...for they shall obtain mercy."** |
| **"Blessed are the pure in heart..."** | Those whose motives are completely clean and who have undivided devotion to God. | **"...for they shall see God."** |
| **"Blessed are the peacemakers..."** | Those who actively work to resolve conflicts and bring people to God. | **"...for they shall be called sons of God."** |
| **"Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake..."** | Those who suffer unjustly because they do what is right. | **"...for theirs is the kingdom of heaven."** |
| **"Blessed are you when men revile you and persecute you..."** | Believers who face insults, false accusations, and attacks specifically because of their faith in Jesus. | **"...Rejoice and be glad, for your reward is great in heaven."** |

#### Matthew vs. Luke on the Beatitudes
JAMB frequently tests the differences between Matthew's and Luke's accounts of this teaching.

| Feature | Matthew 5 (Sermon on the Mount) | Luke 6:17-26 (Sermon on the Plain) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Location** | A mountain | A level place (plain) |
| **Number of Beatitudes** | Nine beatitudes | Four beatitudes |
| **Addition of Woes?** | No woes | Four woes (e.g., "Woe to you that are rich") |
| **Focus** | **Spiritual** (e.g., "poor *in spirit*", "hunger and thirst *for righteousness*") | **Physical/Social** (e.g., "you that are poor", "you that hunger now") |

### 2. Believers as Salt and Light (Matthew 5:13-16)

After describing their character, Jesus used two everyday items to describe how believers must influence the world.

* **"You are the salt of the earth":** In the ancient world, salt was used primarily to preserve food from decaying and to add flavour. Believers are meant to preserve society from moral decay and bring the "flavour" of God's goodness to the world. If salt loses its taste, it is useless and thrown out.
* **"You are the light of the world":** Light exposes darkness and guides people. Jesus said a city set on a hill cannot be hidden, and people do not light a lamp to put it under a basket. Believers must let their good works shine publicly, not to show off, but so that others will see their good deeds and **glorify their Father in heaven**.

### 3. Jesus and the Law (Matthew 5:17-48)

Jesus boldly declared his relationship to the Old Testament Law and the Prophets: *"Think not that I have come to abolish them but to fulfil them."* He taught that true righteousness must go deeper than the outward rule-keeping of the scribes and Pharisees; it must reach the heart. 

Jesus gave six examples (often called the Antitheses) where he quoted the old Law ("You have heard that it was said...") and revealed its deeper, true meaning ("But I say to you..."):

1. **Anger (Murder):** The Law forbade murder. Jesus taught that unjustified anger, insulting someone ("Raca" or empty-head), or calling someone a "fool" makes a person liable to the hell of fire. Believers must urgently seek reconciliation with an offended brother before offering gifts to God.
2. **Lust (Adultery):** The Law forbade adultery. Jesus taught that looking at a woman with lustful intent is committing adultery in the heart. He used hyperbole (extreme exaggeration), saying if your right eye or right hand causes you to sin, pluck it out or cut it off. It is better to lose a body part than to be thrown into hell.
3. **Divorce:** Under the old system, a man could easily write a certificate of divorce. Jesus taught that divorce is only permitted in cases of unchastity (sexual immorality). Divorcing for any other reason and remarrying causes adultery.
4. **Oaths:** People used complex oaths (swearing by heaven, earth, or Jerusalem) to try to prove they were telling the truth. Jesus taught that believers should simply say "Yes" or "No." Swearing oaths comes from evil, because a person's word should be completely trustworthy on its own.
5. **Retaliation:** The Old Testament allowed equal justice: "An eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth." Jesus taught non-retaliation. If someone slaps your right cheek, turn the other to him. If sued for your coat, give your cloak as well. If forced to walk one mile (a common demand by Roman soldiers), go two miles. 
6. **Love for Enemies:** The common teaching was to love your neighbour and hate your enemy. Jesus commanded: **"Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you."** Why? Because God makes the sun rise and the rain fall on both the evil and the good. By loving enemies, believers prove they are children of their heavenly Father. Jesus concluded: *"You, therefore, must be perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect."*

### 4. Practicing Piety: Motives Matter (Matthew 6:1-18)

Jesus warned against practicing religion to gain human praise. In Nigeria today, this is often called "eye-service"—doing things only when people are watching to look holy. Jesus addressed three common religious practices:

* **Almsgiving (Charity):** When giving to the poor, do not sound a trumpet like the hypocrites do in the synagogues and streets to be praised by men. They have received their reward in full (human applause). Instead, give in secret—do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing. God, who sees in secret, will reward you.
* **Prayer:** Do not stand on street corners to be seen praying, and do not use "vain repetitions" (empty phrases) like the Gentiles, who think they will be heard for their many words. Instead, go into a room, shut the door, and pray to God in secret. 
  * Jesus then taught them **The Lord's Prayer** ("Our Father who art in heaven..."), which focuses on God's glory, submitting to His will, asking for daily needs, and seeking forgiveness. 
  * Jesus added a strict condition: if you do not forgive others their trespasses, God will not forgive yours.
* **Fasting:** When fasting, do not look gloomy or disfigure your face to show others you are fasting. Instead, anoint your head and wash your face so that your fasting is only seen by God in secret. God will reward this secret devotion.

## Objective ii: Identify the demands of the Kingdom

The Sermon on the Mount sets out strict demands (requirements) for anyone who wishes to belong to the Kingdom of God. These demands go against standard human behaviour and societal norms:

1. **Inner Righteousness:** Jesus demanded that his followers possess a righteousness that "exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees." The Pharisees kept the outward rules perfectly, but their hearts were proud. The Kingdom demands pure motives and a transformed heart, not just good outward actions.
2. **Total Devotion to God (A Single Master):** Jesus demanded undivided loyalty. "No one can serve two masters... You cannot serve God and mammon (money/wealth)." The Kingdom demands that God occupies the absolute centre of a person's life.
3. **Rejection of Anxiety through Absolute Trust:** Jesus demanded that his followers refuse to worry about material needs. He pointed out that birds do not sow, reap, or store food, yet God feeds them. Lilies do not toil or spin clothes, yet they are dressed more beautifully than King Solomon. The Kingdom demands that believers trust God as their Provider.
4. **Seeking the Kingdom First:** The ultimate demand is found in Matthew 6:33: *"But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well."* A believer's primary life goal must be submitting to God's rule; material survival is secondary and will be handled by God.
5. **Perfection in Love:** Jesus demanded that believers show indiscriminate love, even to their enemies, to reflect the perfect love of God. 

## Objective iii: Determine the consequences of placing worldly possessions above heavenly treasures

In Matthew 6:19-24, Jesus addressed the human obsession with wealth and the severe spiritual consequences of prioritizing material things over God.

### Treasures on Earth vs. Treasures in Heaven
Jesus commanded: *"Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth... but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven."*

**The Consequences of Earthly Treasures:**
1. **Loss and Destruction:** Worldly possessions are temporary and insecure. On earth, **moth and rust consume** (clothes rot, metals corrode), and **thieves break in and steal**. If a person's life is built on wealth, they will eventually lose everything.
2. **Heart Misalignment:** Jesus stated a profound psychological truth: *"For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also."* If you prioritize money, your heart, emotions, and decisions will be chained to the earth and disconnected from God.
3. **Spiritual Darkness (The Evil Eye):** Jesus described the eye as the "lamp of the body." If your eye is sound (generous, focused on God), you are full of light. If your eye is not sound (greedy, envious, focused on money), your whole body will be full of darkness. Placing wealth first blinds a person to spiritual realities.
4. **Enslavement to Mammon:** You cannot be a slave to two masters. If you prioritize worldly possessions, wealth becomes your master ("Mammon"). Consequently, you will end up hating, despising, or neglecting God. You cannot serve both.

## Objective iv: Associate the rewards for obedience with the Sermon on the Mount

Throughout the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus motivated his followers not just with rules, but with the promise of divine rewards for those who obey Kingdom principles.

1. **Rewards for Inward Character (The Beatitudes):**
   * Those who humble themselves (poor in spirit) receive the highest reward: **the kingdom of heaven**.
   * Those who are meek do not have to fight for power; God promises they will **inherit the earth**.
   * Those who keep their hearts pure are rewarded with the ultimate privilege: they will **see God**.
   * Those who endure persecution for Christ are told to rejoice because **"great is your reward in heaven."**

2. **Rewards for Pure Motives (Secret Piety):**
   Jesus constantly repeated the phrase concerning giving, praying, and fasting: *"And your Father who sees in secret will reward you."* Obedience done purely for God's eyes, rather than for the applause of men (like the Pharisees), guarantees a reward from God Himself.

3. **Rewards for Trusting God over Anxiety:**
   For those who obey the command not to worry about food, drink, or clothing, and who choose to seek God's kingdom first, the reward is divine provision: *"All these things shall be yours as well"* (Matthew 6:33). God rewards absolute trust with total care.

4. **The Consequence of Disobedience (Loss of Reward):**
   Conversely, those who obey the law just to be seen by men (praying loudly on street corners, sounding trumpets for charity) receive a very brief reward: human applause. Jesus firmly stated, *"Truly, I say to you, they have received their reward."* They will receive absolutely nothing from God in heaven. 

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Beatitude** | Supreme blessedness; the declarations of blessing made by Jesus in the Sermon on the Mount. |
| **Sermon on the Mount** | Jesus' major teaching in Matthew 5–7, delivered on a mountainside. |
| **Sermon on the Plain** | The parallel teaching in Luke 6:17-49, delivered on a level place, focusing more on social and economic realities. |
| **Meek** | Humble, gentle, and submissive to God's will; not aggressive or arrogant. |
| **Almsgiving** | The act of giving money, food, or material help to the poor. |
| **Hypocrite** | A person who puts on a false appearance of virtue or religion; an actor. |
| **Mammon** | Wealth, riches, or material property, especially when it is worshipped or idolized as a false god. |
| **Anxiety** | Excessive worry, nervousness, or unease about life's needs, which Jesus commanded against. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Matthew vs. Luke:** This is a classic exam trap. Remember that Matthew has **9 spiritual beatitudes** on a **Mount**, while Luke has **4 physical/social beatitudes** (and 4 woes) on a **Plain**. 
* **Salt and Light:** Know the distinct purposes. Salt preserves and flavours; light illuminates and exposes. The goal of being light is so that men may see your good works and **glorify your Father in heaven**.
* **Fasting, Praying, Giving:** Memorize the common theme for all three in Matthew 6: do them in secret, not for the praise of men, and the Father who sees in secret will reward you.
* **The Lilies and the Birds:** Know the exact illustrations Jesus used for anxiety. He used **birds of the air** (they don't sow or reap) and **lilies of the field** (they don't toil or spin, yet are dressed better than Solomon). 
* **Eye for an Eye:** Remember that Jesus rejected the Old Testament law of retaliation. He taught turning the other cheek and loving enemies.
* **Treasures:** Know the specific things Jesus said destroy earthly treasures: **moth, rust, and thieves**. 

## Quick summary

1. The Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5 and 6) is the definitive guide to Kingdom living, demanding inner purity rather than just outward religious rule-keeping.
2. The Beatitudes list the character traits of true believers (humility, purity, mercy, peacemaking) and the specific heavenly and earthly rewards God promises them.
3. Believers are commanded to be the "salt of the earth" (preserving society) and the "light of the world" (showing good works that glorify God).
4. Jesus deepened the Law: murder includes angry insults, adultery includes lust, and the law of retaliation is replaced with turning the other cheek and loving enemies.
5. True piety (giving, praying, fasting) must be done in secret for God's eyes only, rejecting the hypocrisy of doing it to earn human praise.
6. Believers must not lay up treasures on earth (where moths, rust, and thieves destroy) or try to serve both God and Mammon, because where your treasure is, there your heart will be.
7. Jesus strictly commanded against anxiety, instructing followers to look at the birds and lilies, and to "seek first the kingdom of God" with the promise that all daily needs will be provided.

## Practice questions

**1. According to the Beatitudes, what is the reward for the meek?**
- A. They shall see God
- B. They shall inherit the earth
- C. They shall be called sons of God
- D. Theirs is the kingdom of heaven

**Answer: B.** Jesus said, "Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth."

**2. In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus taught that a believer's good works should shine before men so that they may**
- A. praise the believer in the church
- B. be converted to Christianity
- C. glorify their Father who is in heaven
- D. know that the believer is holy

**Answer: C.** Jesus said in Matthew 5:16, "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father who is in heaven."

**3. Which of the following is a major difference between the Beatitudes in Matthew and Luke?**
- A. Matthew contains four woes, while Luke contains nine woes
- B. Matthew was delivered on a plain, while Luke was delivered on a mountain
- C. Matthew focuses on spiritual qualities, while Luke focuses on physical/social realities
- D. Matthew is directed at the Pharisees, while Luke is directed at the Romans

**Answer: C.** Matthew spiritualizes the Beatitudes (e.g., "poor in spirit"), while Luke focuses on actual physical realities ("you that are poor", "you that hunger now") and includes four woes.

**4. According to Jesus' teaching on the Law, anyone who looks at a woman lustfully has**
- A. committed murder in his heart
- B. defiled his own body
- C. committed adultery in his heart
- D. broken the command against coveting

**Answer: C.** Jesus deepened the law against adultery by stating that the sin happens internally through lustful intent before any physical act takes place.

**5. How did Jesus say his followers should behave when fasting?**
- A. They should wear sackcloth and ashes
- B. They should anoint their heads and wash their faces
- C. They should pray loudly in the synagogues
- D. They should look gloomy so others know they are fasting

**Answer: B.** Jesus commanded believers to wash their faces and anoint their heads so their fasting is hidden from men and seen only by God, who will reward them in secret.

**6. Jesus taught that earthly treasures are insecure because they are subject to**
- A. inflation and economic crash
- B. floods and earthquakes
- C. moth, rust, and thieves
- D. fire, wind, and deceit

**Answer: C.** In Matthew 6:19, Jesus warned not to store up treasures on earth "where moth and rust consume and where thieves break in and steal."

**7. "No one can serve two masters..." In this teaching, Jesus identified the second master competing with God as**
- A. Satan
- B. the flesh
- C. the world
- D. mammon

**Answer: D.** Jesus concluded his teaching on wealth by stating, "You cannot serve God and mammon" (mammon being wealth/money).

**8. To illustrate that God will provide clothing for His children, Jesus pointed to the**
- A. birds of the air
- B. lilies of the field
- C. grass of the earth
- D. sheep of the pasture

**Answer: B.** While he used birds to talk about food, he specifically used the lilies of the field (which do not toil or spin but are dressed better than Solomon) to illustrate God's provision of clothing.

**9. According to the Beatitudes, who shall be called the sons of God?**
- A. The pure in heart
- B. The merciful
- C. The peacemakers
- D. Those who mourn

**Answer: C.** Jesus said, "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God" (Matthew 5:9). 

**10. What is the fundamental demand of the Kingdom regarding daily needs, as stated in Matthew 6:33?**
- A. Work hard and save money for the future
- B. Seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness
- C. Fast twice a week and give alms
- D. Pray without ceasing for all your needs

**Answer: B.** Jesus commanded his disciples to stop worrying and instead "seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well."
