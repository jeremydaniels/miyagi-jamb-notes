# JAMB Crk Notes: The Great Confession

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 8 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: The Great Confession. It covers Peter's declaration of Jesus as the Messiah at Caesarea Philippi, a major turning point in the Gospels. 

The syllabus specifies the following content items:
- Mt. 16:13-20
- Mk. 8:27-30
- Lk. 9:18-22

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. analyse the confession by Peter;
2. identify the occasion of the Great Confession;
3. examine the significance of the Great Confession.

## Identifying the occasion of the Great Confession

**Objective ii: identify the occasion of the Great Confession.**
**Content items: Mt. 16:13-20; Mk. 8:27-30; Lk. 9:18-22.**

The "Great Confession" refers to the moment when the Apostle Peter openly declared that Jesus was the promised Messiah (the Christ). This event is recorded in all three Synoptic Gospels (Matthew, Mark, and Luke) and marks the climax of Jesus' ministry in Galilee. 

### The Setting: Caesarea Philippi

The event took place in the region of **Caesarea Philippi** (mentioned in Matthew and Mark). 
*   Caesarea Philippi was a city in the far north of Israel, at the foot of Mount Hermon, outside the direct territory of Herod Antipas. 
*   It was a heavily Gentile area known for its Greek and Roman temples, particularly the worship of the Greek god Pan, and a massive rock formation. 
*   Jesus took His disciples to this quiet, remote area to be away from the Jewish crowds and the hostility of the religious leaders, giving Him time to instruct the Twelve privately.

### The Context of the Question

The Gospels give slightly different details about what Jesus was doing just before He questioned His disciples:
*   **Mark 8:27:** Jesus and His disciples were walking "on the way" to the villages of Caesarea Philippi.
*   **Luke 9:18:** Luke notes that Jesus was "praying alone" with only His disciples present before He asked the question. 

### The Question of Public Opinion

To test the disciples' understanding, Jesus first asked them about public opinion. 
*   In Matthew, He asked: "Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?" (Matthew 16:13).
*   In Mark and Luke, He simply asked: "Whom do men say that I am?"

The disciples reported the various rumours circulating among the Jewish people. The public recognised Jesus as a great man of God, but their answers fell short of identifying Him as the Messiah. They thought He was:
1.  **John the Baptist:** Some (including Herod Antipas) believed Jesus was John the Baptist raised from the dead, because of His preaching on repentance and His miraculous powers.
2.  **Elijah:** The prophet Malachi had prophesied that Elijah would return before the great and dreadful day of the Lord (Malachi 4:5). Because of Jesus' powerful miracles, many thought Elijah had returned.
3.  **Jeremiah:** Mentioned **only in Matthew's Gospel**. Jeremiah was known as the "weeping prophet" who suffered for his message, and some saw similarities in Jesus' ministry.
4.  **One of the prophets:** A general belief that Jesus was a resurrected prophet of the Old Testament.

## Analysing the confession by Peter

**Objective i: analyse the confession by Peter.**
**Content items: Mt. 16:13-20; Mk. 8:27-30; Lk. 9:18-22.**

After hearing the public's opinion, Jesus made the question intensely personal. He turned to the Twelve and asked, "But whom say ye that I am?" (Matthew 16:15).

Although the question was addressed to all the disciples, **Simon Peter** acted as their spokesman and boldly gave what is now known as the "Great Confession".

### Comparing the Gospel Accounts

While all three Synoptic Gospels record Peter's confession, they record his exact words slightly differently. JAMB often tests your knowledge of which phrase belongs to which Gospel.

| Feature | Matthew 16:16 | Mark 8:29 | Luke 9:20 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peter's words** | "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God." | "Thou art the Christ." | "The Christ of God." |
| **Jesus' blessing on Peter** | Yes (Detailed blessing and new name given) | No | No |
| **Mention of the "Rock" and "Keys"** | Yes | No | No |
| **Command to keep silent** | Yes | Yes | Yes |

### Jesus' Response to Peter (Matthew's Account)

Matthew's Gospel provides a much longer and more detailed response from Jesus following Peter's confession (Matthew 16:17-19). This response contains three major declarations:

**1. The Source of the Revelation**
Jesus blessed Peter, saying, "Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-jona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven." Jesus made it clear that Peter did not figure this out through human intelligence ("flesh and blood"); it was a spiritual truth revealed to him by God the Father.

**2. The Rock and the Church**
Jesus gave Simon a title based on his confession: "And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it."
*   The name **Peter** (Greek: *Petros*) means a stone or a piece of rock. 
*   Jesus said upon "this rock" (Greek: *petra*, meaning a massive bedrock or foundation) He would build His church. 
*   Christians hold different interpretations of "this rock." Some believe it refers to Peter himself as the leader of the early apostles. Others believe it refers to Peter's *confession of faith* (the truth that Jesus is the Christ), meaning the church is built on the reality of who Jesus is.
*   "The gates of hell (Hades)" means that the powers of death and evil will never be able to defeat or destroy the church.

**3. The Keys of the Kingdom**
Jesus said, "And I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven."
*   **Keys** are a symbol of authority. 
*   **Binding and loosing** was a Jewish phrase used by Rabbis to mean forbidding (binding) or permitting (loosing) certain actions, or declaring what is right and wrong. 
*   This signifies that Jesus was giving His apostles spiritual authority to preach the gospel, declare God's forgiveness, and guide the church.

### The Messianic Secret and the Prediction of the Passion

Following the confession, in all three Gospels, Jesus strictly commanded the disciples **to tell no one that He was the Christ** (Mt. 16:20; Mk. 8:30; Lk. 9:21). This command for silence is often called the "Messianic Secret".

Immediately after this command (as noted in Luke 9:22 and the parallel verses in Matthew and Mark), Jesus began to teach them that the Son of Man must **suffer many things, be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and on the third day be raised.**

## Examining the significance of the Great Confession

**Objective iii: examine the significance of the Great Confession.**

The Great Confession is one of the most important milestones in the New Testament. Its significance can be seen in several areas:

### 1. The True Identity of Jesus Revealed
The word **Christ** (Greek *Christos*) is the translation of the Hebrew word **Messiah**, which means "The Anointed One". By confessing Jesus as the Christ, Peter was stating that Jesus was the long-awaited deliverer and King promised in the Old Testament. This was the first time the disciples clearly and openly stated their belief in His divine mission.

### 2. A Turning Point in Jesus' Ministry
Before Caesarea Philippi, Jesus focused on proving His authority through miracles and teaching the crowds in Galilee. Once the disciples finally understood *who* He was, Jesus immediately changed His focus. The Great Confession marks the turning point where Jesus stopped focusing on the crowds and began heading toward Jerusalem, preparing His disciples for His upcoming death and resurrection.

### 3. Redefining the Messiah (The Reason for the Secret)
Why did Jesus tell them to keep it a secret? In first-century Israel, the people expected the "Messiah" to be a political and military leader who would raise an army, defeat the Romans, and make Israel a wealthy, independent nation. 
Jesus kept His identity secret to prevent a political uprising. He had to teach His disciples that He was a **Suffering Messiah**—one who had come to die for sins, not to fight the Romans. This is why Luke 9:22 immediately follows the confession with the prediction of His suffering and death.

### 4. The Foundation and Victory of the Church
The Great Confession is the very first time the word "church" (*ekklesia*) is used in the Bible. Jesus' promise that "the gates of hell shall not prevail against it" is highly significant. It assures Christians that despite persecution, opposition, and even death, the church of Christ will endure forever. 

### 5. Divine Revelation over Human Reason
The event highlights a major Christian teaching: spiritual truth cannot be fully understood through human logic or observation alone. The public saw Jesus do miracles but only saw a prophet. Peter recognised Jesus as the Son of God because, as Jesus said, God the Father revealed it to him.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The Great Confession** | Peter's declaration that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of the living God. |
| **Caesarea Philippi** | A Gentile region in northern Israel where the Great Confession took place. |
| **Christ / Messiah** | "The Anointed One"; the promised deliverer of Israel. |
| **Petros / Petra** | Greek words used by Jesus. *Petros* (Peter) means a stone; *petra* means a massive rock or bedrock. |
| **Keys of the kingdom** | A symbol of the spiritual authority given by Jesus to the apostles/church. |
| **Binding and loosing** | Jewish rabbinic terms meaning to forbid or permit, representing authority to declare God's will. |
| **Messianic Secret** | Jesus' practice of commanding His disciples and those He healed not to reveal His identity as the Messiah. |
| **Passion Prediction** | Jesus' prophecy regarding His upcoming suffering, rejection, death, and resurrection (Luke 9:22). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Know the differences between the Gospels:** JAMB frequently tests exact wording. 
    *   *Matthew:* "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God."
    *   *Mark:* "Thou art the Christ."
    *   *Luke:* "The Christ of God."
*   **Know what is unique to Matthew:** Only Matthew mentions **Jeremiah** in the public opinion list. Only Matthew records Jesus' blessing on Peter, the saying about the **rock**, and the **keys of the kingdom**.
*   **Common mistake:** Do not confuse the public's answer with the disciples' answer. The *public* said John the Baptist, Elijah, or Jeremiah. *Peter* said the Christ.
*   **Location:** Always remember the event took place at **Caesarea Philippi**, not Jerusalem, Capernaum, or Bethany.
*   **Immediate aftermath:** Remember that immediately after the confession, Jesus strictly charged them to **tell no one** (the Messianic Secret) and then began to predict His suffering and death.

## Quick summary

1.  At Caesarea Philippi, Jesus asked His disciples who the public thought He was.
2.  The public believed Jesus was a resurrected prophet: John the Baptist, Elijah, or Jeremiah.
3.  Jesus then asked the disciples for their own belief. Peter answered on their behalf, declaring Jesus to be the Christ.
4.  In Matthew's account, Jesus blessed Peter, stating this truth was revealed by God the Father, not human logic.
5.  Jesus declared He would build His church on "this rock," and gave the "keys of the kingdom" as a symbol of authority.
6.  Jesus commanded the disciples to tell no one about His identity as the Messiah, to avoid political misunderstandings.
7.  The Great Confession was a turning point; immediately after, Jesus began to teach them that He must suffer, die, and rise again.

## Practice questions

**1. The Great Confession of Peter took place in the region of**
- A. Capernaum
- B. Caesarea Philippi
- C. Jerusalem
- D. the Mount of Olives

**Answer: B.** The event occurred when Jesus and His disciples went to the villages of Caesarea Philippi, a region in northern Israel.

**2. According to the disciples, which of the following was NOT one of the public's opinions about who Jesus was?**
- A. John the Baptist
- B. Moses
- C. Elijah
- D. Jeremiah

**Answer: B.** The public thought Jesus was John the Baptist, Elijah, Jeremiah (in Matthew), or one of the prophets. Moses is not mentioned in this context.

**3. "Flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven." Jesus said this to Peter to show that**
- A. Peter was highly intelligent and observant.
- B. spiritual truth requires divine revelation.
- C. Peter was the most righteous of the disciples.
- D. the other disciples lacked faith.

**Answer: B.** Jesus emphasised that Peter did not come to this conclusion through human means ("flesh and blood"), but because God the Father revealed it to him.

**4. According to Luke's Gospel, Peter's exact confession was**
- A. "Thou art the Christ."
- B. "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God."
- C. "The Christ of God."
- D. "Truly this was the Son of God."

**Answer: C.** Luke 9:20 records Peter's words as "The Christ of God." Option A is Mark, and Option B is Matthew.

**5. Immediately after Peter's confession, Jesus commanded His disciples to**
- A. go and preach that He is the Christ to all nations.
- B. tell no one that He was the Christ.
- C. prepare for a journey to Rome.
- D. gather weapons to fight the religious leaders.

**Answer: B.** In all three Gospels, Jesus strictly charged them to keep His Messianic identity a secret at that time (the Messianic Secret).

**6. In Matthew's account of the confession, the "keys of the kingdom of heaven" given to Peter signify**
- A. the physical keys to the temple in Jerusalem.
- B. spiritual authority to bind and loose.
- C. wealth and prosperity for the early church.
- D. permission to overthrow the Roman government.

**Answer: B.** The keys represent spiritual authority to declare God's will, often described in rabbinic terms as the power to "bind" (forbid) and "loose" (permit).

**7. Which Gospel explicitly mentions that Jesus was "praying alone" with His disciples before asking them about His identity?**
- A. Matthew
- B. Mark
- C. Luke
- D. John

**Answer: C.** Luke 9:18 notes, "And it came to pass, as he was alone praying, his disciples were with him: and he asked them..."

**8. The title "Christ" used by Peter in his confession translates to**
- A. The Prophet
- B. The High Priest
- C. The Son of Man
- D. The Anointed One

**Answer: D.** "Christ" comes from the Greek *Christos*, which is the translation of the Hebrew *Messiah*, meaning "The Anointed One".

**9. What major teaching did Jesus introduce immediately following the Great Confession? (Luke 9:22)**
- A. The Sermon on the Mount
- B. The Parable of the Sower
- C. The prediction of His suffering, death, and resurrection
- D. The teaching on the Holy Spirit

**Answer: C.** Once the disciples understood He was the Christ, Jesus immediately began to explain that He was a Suffering Messiah who must be killed and raised on the third day.

**10. Only one Gospel includes the public opinion that Jesus might be the prophet Jeremiah. Which Gospel is it?**
- A. Matthew
- B. Mark
- C. Luke
- D. John

**Answer: A.** Matthew 16:14 uniquely includes Jeremiah in the list of prophets the public thought Jesus might be.
