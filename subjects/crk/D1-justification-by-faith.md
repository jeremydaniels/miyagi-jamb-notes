# JAMB Crk Notes: Justification by Faith

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section D, Topic 1 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Justification by Faith. It covers the Apostle Paul's teaching on how humans are made right with God, found primarily in his letters to the Romans and Galatians. 

The syllabus lists the following content items:
- Rom. 3:21-24
- Rom. 5:1-11
- Rom. 10:1-13
- Gal. 2:16-21

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Interpret the phrase 'justification by faith'.
2. Identify the basic conditions for justification.
3. Determine the fruits of justification.

## Interpreting the phrase 'justification by faith'

**Objective i: interpret the phrase 'justification by faith'.**

To understand Christian teaching on salvation, we must understand Paul's use of the term "justification". 

**Justification** is a legal term taken from the law courts. It means to be declared "not guilty" or to be acquitted. In Christian theology, justification is the act by which God legally declares a sinful person to be righteous, acceptable, and innocent in His sight, not because the person has never sinned, but because the person's sins have been forgiven through the sacrificial death of Jesus Christ.

**Faith** means total trust, belief, and reliance. In this context, it means trusting completely in the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ for salvation, rather than trusting in one's own good deeds or religious rituals.

Therefore, **"justification by faith"** is the central Christian doctrine that human beings cannot earn God's forgiveness or a place in heaven by trying to perfectly obey religious laws or by doing good works. Instead, people are declared righteous by God entirely as a free gift (grace), which they receive simply by putting their faith in Jesus Christ.

### Romans 3:21-24: Justification as a free gift

**Content: Rom. 3:21-24**

Paul explains justification clearly in his letter to the Romans. He argues that under the Law of Moses (the strict religious laws given to the Jews in the Old Testament), nobody could ever be justified because nobody can keep the law perfectly.

Paul writes that a new kind of righteousness from God has now been revealed, independent of the law, although the Law and the Prophets testify to it. This righteousness is given through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe. He states the core problem of humanity in Romans 3:23:
> *"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;"* (Romans 3:23, KJV)

Because everyone has sinned, no one can boast or claim to deserve heaven by their own merit. However, God provides a solution. Believers are *"justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus"* (Romans 3:24). 
- **Grace** means unmerited or unearned favour. God gives it freely because of His loving character, not because humans deserve it.
- **Redemption** means paying a price to buy someone's freedom (like paying a ransom to set a slave free). Jesus paid this price with His own life and blood on the cross.

### Galatians 2:16-21: Faith versus the works of the law

**Content: Gal. 2:16-21**

In his letter to the Galatians, Paul faced a major problem: false teachers (often called Judaizers) were telling early Gentile Christians that to be saved, a person had to believe in Jesus *and* perfectly obey the Jewish laws (such as circumcision, observing special days, and strict dietary rules). These rules and human efforts are what Paul calls the **"works of the law"**.

Paul strongly rejects this mixture. He teaches that faith and the works of the law cannot be mixed for salvation. He states clearly that *"a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Jesus Christ"* (Galatians 2:16). He argues that if a person could be made righteous by obeying the law, then Christ's death was completely useless and unnecessary. 

To describe his own justification and new life, Paul says he has died to the law so that he might live for God. He writes:
> *"I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me."* (Galatians 2:20, KJV)

By relying on Christ alone, Paul says he does not "frustrate" (or set aside) the grace of God. If humans could save themselves through their own efforts, grace would no longer be grace.

## Basic conditions for justification

**Objective ii: identify the basic conditions for justification.**

According to Paul, justification is a free gift, but it is not automatic for everyone in the world; it has specific conditions to be received. However, these conditions do not involve physical labour, wealth, or strict rule-keeping. The basic conditions are spiritual and involve a person's heart and mouth.

### Romans 10:1-13: The conditions for justification

**Content: Rom. 10:1-13**

In Romans 10, Paul expresses his deep sorrow and constant prayer for his fellow Israelites. He notes that the Jews had a zeal for God, but it was not based on knowledge. They were busy trying to establish their *own* righteousness by obeying the law, and as a result, they rejected the righteousness that comes from God through faith. Paul declares that **"Christ is the end of the law"** for righteousness to everyone who believes (Romans 10:4). This means Christ fulfilled all the requirements of the law, bringing it to an end as a way of trying to achieve righteousness.

Paul then outlines the basic, clear conditions for a person to be justified and saved:

1. **Confessing with the mouth:** A person must publicly declare and acknowledge that Jesus is Lord. This means submitting to Christ's authority over their life.
2. **Believing in the heart:** A person must genuinely believe in their inner being that God raised Jesus from the dead. This affirms faith in the resurrection and the power of God.

> *"That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."* (Romans 10:9-10, KJV)

Paul emphasizes that this condition applies to **everyone equally**. Under the Old Testament law, there was a strict division between Jews (who had the law of Moses) and Gentiles or Greeks (who did not). But under the new covenant of justification by faith, **there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek**. The same Lord is Lord of all and richly blesses all who call on Him. Paul quotes the Old Testament to prove this: *"Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved"* (Romans 10:13).

| The Old Way (Works of the Law) | The New Way (Justification by Faith) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Attempting to earn salvation through perfect obedience to rules and rituals | Receiving salvation as a free gift through the grace of God |
| Results in boasting (if successful) or condemnation (because all inevitably fail) | Results in deep humility and gratitude to God |
| Exclusive (favoured the Jews who had the Law of Moses) | Inclusive (available to all humanity, both Jews and Gentiles) |
| Condition: Do all the works of the law flawlessly | Condition: Believe in the heart and confess with the mouth |

## The fruits of justification

**Objective iii: determine the fruits of justification.**

When a person meets the conditions of justification by placing their faith in Christ, their life, their status, and their relationship with God change completely. These positive results and ongoing spiritual changes are called the **fruits of justification**. 

### Romans 5:1-11: The results of being justified

**Content: Rom. 5:1-11**

In Romans 5, Paul lists several direct benefits—or fruits—that come to a person who has been justified by faith:

1. **Peace with God:** Before justification, human sin makes people enemies of God, separated by a barrier of guilt. Justification removes this guilt, bringing total peace and harmony between the believer and God through Jesus Christ.
2. **Access to Grace:** Believers gain open access to stand in God's grace and favour continually. They are no longer kept at a distance.
3. **Rejoicing in Hope:** Believers can confidently rejoice in the hope of sharing in the glory of God in the future eternal life.
4. **Joy in Sufferings (Tribulations):** This is a unique fruit. Christians can rejoice even when they face hardship and persecution. Paul explains a spiritual chain reaction that occurs in a justified person's life: 
   - Suffering (tribulation) produces **endurance (patience)**.
   - Endurance produces **character (experience)**.
   - Character produces **hope**.
5. **The Gift of the Holy Spirit and God's Love:** This hope will not disappoint because God has poured out His love into the hearts of believers through the Holy Spirit, who is given to them as a seal and comforter.
6. **Salvation from God's Wrath:** Paul argues from the greater to the lesser: since Christ died for believers while they were still weak, ungodly sinners, how much more will they now be saved from the future wrath (judgment) of God since they have been justified by His blood.
7. **Reconciliation:** Believers, who were once God's enemies, are now fully reconciled (brought back into a friendly, loving relationship) with God through the death of His Son. Because they are reconciled, they will definitely be saved by Christ's resurrected life.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Justification** | The legal act by which God declares a sinner to be righteous and not guilty, based entirely on the sacrificial work of Jesus Christ. |
| **Grace** | God's unmerited, unearned favour and free gift to humanity. |
| **Faith** | Complete trust, belief, and reliance on Jesus Christ and His sacrifice, rather than on oneself. |
| **Works of the Law** | Attempting to gain God's favour and salvation by strictly obeying religious rules, laws, and rituals (like the Law of Moses). |
| **Reconciliation** | The restoration of a friendly relationship between God and humanity, ending the hostility caused by sin. |
| **Redemption** | The act of buying back or freeing someone from the slavery of sin by paying a ransom price (Christ's blood). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Understand the exact definition:** JAMB often asks for the exact meaning of justification. Remember it is a *legal declaration of righteousness* or being *acquitted* (a change in status before God), not the gradual process of becoming morally perfect over time (that is called sanctification).
- **Know Romans 3:23:** "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." This is the foundational reason why justification by works of the law is impossible for any human.
- **Know the conditions:** Romans 10 outlines two specific conditions: *believing in the heart* (that God raised Jesus from the dead) and *confessing with the mouth* (that Jesus is Lord).
- **Know the fruits:** Memorize the chain of fruits in Romans 5: tribulation -> endurance -> character -> hope. JAMB frequently tests this exact sequence.
- **Common mistake:** Confusing Paul's teaching on "justification by faith" with James' teaching on "faith without works is dead." In this specific topic, you must answer strictly according to Paul's letters to the Romans and Galatians, where Paul explicitly states we are justified by faith *apart from* the works of the law.
- **Christ is the end of the law:** Remember this phrase from Romans 10:4. It means that Christ fulfilled the purpose of the law, bringing righteousness to everyone who believes, and ending the law as a means of salvation.

## Quick summary

1. Justification is God's legal declaration that a sinner is righteous, received purely as a free gift (grace) through faith, not by human effort.
2. Paul teaches in Romans 3 that because every single person has sinned, no one can be justified by their own good works or by keeping the law. 
3. In Galatians 2, Paul argues strongly that justification comes exclusively by faith in Christ, not by the "works of the law" (Jewish religious rules like circumcision). 
4. If righteousness could be achieved by obeying the law, then Christ's death on the cross was meaningless.
5. Romans 10 gives the two basic conditions for justification: confessing with your mouth that Jesus is Lord, and believing in your heart that God raised Him from the dead.
6. This justification is available to everyone equally, with absolutely no distinction between Jews and Greeks (Gentiles).
7. Romans 5 lists the fruits of justification, which include peace with God, access to grace, the gift of the Holy Spirit, salvation from wrath, and reconciliation.
8. A justified person can even rejoice in suffering, knowing the spiritual chain reaction: suffering produces endurance, endurance produces character, and character produces hope.

## Practice questions

**1. According to Paul's teaching in Romans, justification is described as**
- A. a reward for keeping the Ten Commandments perfectly
- B. a process of doing good works to outweigh bad deeds
- C. a free gift of God's grace through redemption in Christ
- D. an exclusive privilege given only to the Jews

**Answer: C.** Romans 3:24 states that believers are "justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus."

**2. In Galatians 2, Paul states that if righteousness could be gained through the law, then**
- A. the Jews would be the only ones saved
- B. Christ died for nothing
- C. everyone must be circumcised
- D. faith would become stronger

**Answer: B.** Paul argues in Galatians 2:21 that he does not set aside the grace of God, because "if righteousness come by the law, then Christ is dead in vain" (meaning He died for nothing).

**3. According to Romans 10, the two basic conditions for salvation are**
- A. being baptized and paying tithes
- B. believing in the heart and confessing with the mouth
- C. keeping the Sabbath and doing good works
- D. fasting and praying daily

**Answer: B.** Romans 10:9-10 states that if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.

**4. According to Romans 3:23, the reason why justification by works of the law is impossible is that**
- A. the law was lost by the Israelites during the exile
- B. the law is too difficult to understand
- C. all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God
- D. the Gentiles do not have the law

**Answer: C.** Paul explains that no one can boast before God because "all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God." Human effort is always flawed by sin.

**5. Which of the following is one of the primary fruits of justification mentioned in Romans 5?**
- A. Material wealth and prosperity
- B. The ability to perform miracles
- C. Peace with God
- D. Exemption from all physical suffering

**Answer: C.** Romans 5:1 states, "Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ."

**6. According to Romans 5, Paul teaches that believers should rejoice in their sufferings because suffering directly produces**
- A. endurance (patience)
- B. faith
- C. wealth
- D. reconciliation

**Answer: A.** Romans 5:3 says that "tribulation worketh patience" (suffering produces endurance/patience), which then leads to character and hope.

**7. In Paul's teaching on justification, he emphasizes that "there is no distinction between Jew and Greek." What does this mean?**
- A. Jews and Greeks must worship in the exact same building
- B. Both Jews and Greeks speak the same spiritual language
- C. Salvation and justification are available equally to everyone who calls on the Lord
- D. The Law of Moses was originally given to the Greeks as well as the Jews

**Answer: C.** Romans 10:12 explains that there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek regarding salvation, because the same Lord is Lord of all and richly blesses all who call on Him.

**8. How is the love of God poured into the hearts of justified believers, according to Romans 5?**
- A. By the daily reading of the law
- B. Through the Holy Spirit given to them
- C. By the prophets of the Old Testament
- D. Through continuous good works and charity

**Answer: B.** Romans 5:5 states that hope does not make us ashamed, "because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us."

**9. In Galatians 2:20, Paul explains his justification by saying, "I have been crucified with Christ." What does he conclude from this?**
- A. He no longer lives, but Christ lives in him
- B. He must now suffer physically on a literal cross
- C. He is no longer required to love his neighbour
- D. He has become completely sinless in his flesh

**Answer: A.** Paul says, "I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me." He relies entirely on Christ's life within him, not his own legalistic efforts.

**10. In Romans 10:4, Paul declares that Christ is the ________ of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes.**
- A. beginning
- B. end
- C. destroyer
- D. teacher

**Answer: B.** Paul states that "Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth," meaning that faith in Christ replaces the need to try to earn righteousness by obeying the law.
