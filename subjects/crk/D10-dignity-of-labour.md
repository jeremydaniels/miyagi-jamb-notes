# JAMB Crk Notes: Dignity of Labour

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section D, Topic 10 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Dignity of Labour. It covers biblical teachings on the value of honest work, God's attitude towards labour, and the rejection of idleness. 

The syllabus lists the following passages:
- Matthew 20:1-16 (The Parable of the Labourers in the Vineyard)
- II Thessalonians 3:6-15 (Paul's warning against idleness)
- Colossians 3:23-25 (Working heartily as unto the Lord)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Interpret the concept of dignity of labour.
2. Analyse the benefits of labour.
3. Recognize that no particular kind of work leads to loss of respect for personal dignity.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** In the JAMB syllabus, the first reference is printed as `Matt. 20;1-16` using a semicolon. This is a typographical error for Matthew 20:1-16, which is the passage explained in these notes.

## Interpret the concept of dignity of labour

**Objective i: interpret the concept of dignity of labour.**

The word **dignity** refers to a state of being worthy of honour and respect. **Labour** means work, whether it is physical (manual) or mental (intellectual). 

The concept of the **dignity of labour** is the belief that all types of legitimate, honest work are respectable and honourable. It means that there is inherent value in working, and no honest job is inherently degrading or "too low" for a person to do.

In Christian teaching, work is not a punishment or a curse. God Himself is presented as a worker who laboured for six days to create the universe and rested on the seventh (Genesis 2). Because humans are made in the image of God, engaging in productive, honest labour is a way of reflecting God's nature. Therefore, the dignity of labour means that a person's worth is maintained and even enhanced when they earn their living through honest effort.

## Recognize that no particular kind of work leads to loss of respect for personal dignity

**Objective iii: recognize that no particular kind of work leads to loss of respect for personal dignity.**

In many societies, including Nigeria, there is a strong cultural preference for "white-collar" jobs (such as banking, medicine, engineering, and law). Conversely, society sometimes looks down on "blue-collar" or manual jobs (such as street sweeping, farming, mechanic work, vulcanizing, or domestic cleaning).

The biblical concept of the dignity of labour rejects this division. It teaches that **no particular kind of work leads to a loss of respect for personal dignity**, provided the work is honest and legitimate. 

- A Christian street sweeper who does their job well has exactly the same personal and spiritual dignity as a Christian bank manager. 
- Dignity comes from the **honesty** of the work and the **attitude** of the worker, not from the prestige of the job title or the size of the salary.
- What actually destroys personal dignity is not manual labour, but **idleness, begging, stealing, or engaging in corrupt practices** (such as fraud or "yahoo-yahoo") to survive.

## Biblical teachings on labour

### The Parable of the Labourers in the Vineyard (Matthew 20:1-16)

Jesus told this parable to explain the kingdom of heaven, focusing on God's grace, but it also provides a picture of daily labour, agreements, and fair wages.

1. Early in the morning, a householder (landowner) went out to hire labourers for his vineyard. He agreed to pay them a **penny** a day (a *denarius*, which was the standard daily wage for a worker at the time).
2. He went out again at the third hour (9:00 AM) and saw others standing idle in the marketplace. He told them to go into the vineyard, promising to pay them "whatsoever is right."
3. He did the same at the sixth hour (12:00 PM) and the ninth hour (3:00 PM).
4. Finally, at the eleventh hour (5:00 PM, just one hour before the workday ended), he found more men standing idle. They explained that no man had hired them. He sent them into the vineyard as well.
5. When evening came, the householder told his steward to call the labourers and pay them their hire, beginning from the last group to the first.
6. The workers hired at the eleventh hour came and received a penny. 
7. When the first group came, they supposed they would receive more, since they had worked all day, but they also received a penny.
8. They murmured against the householder, complaining: "These last have wrought but one hour, and thou hast made them equal unto us, which have borne the burden and heat of the day."
9. The householder answered one of them: "Friend, I do thee no wrong: didst not thou agree with me for a penny?" He told them to take their money and go, stating that it was his right to do what he wanted with his own money, and asked, "Is thine eye evil, because I am good?"

**Lessons on the dignity of labour:** 
- The householder respected all the workers, ensuring that none of them remained idle in the marketplace.
- All the labourers, whether they worked for one hour or twelve hours, contributed to the vineyard and were treated with dignity and paid a living wage. 
- While the primary theological lesson is about God's sovereign grace and the equality of salvation for all believers (whether they turn to God early or late in life), it also shows that employers must honour the wage agreements they make with their labourers.

### Warning against idleness (II Thessalonians 3:6-15)

In the early church at Thessalonica, some Christians misunderstood the teaching about the second coming of Jesus. Believing Jesus was returning immediately, they stopped working, became idle, and relied on others for food. Paul wrote strictly to correct this.

1. Paul commanded the church, in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, to withdraw from every brother who walks "disorderly" and not according to the tradition they had received. In this context, walking "disorderly" meant living in idleness.
2. Paul reminded them of his own example. When he was with them, he did not eat anyone's bread for free.
3. Instead, he worked with "labour and travail night and day" (Paul was a tentmaker) so that he would not be a financial burden to any of them.
4. Paul explained that as an apostle, he had the right to be supported by the church, but he chose to work manually to make himself an example for them to follow.
5. He reminded them of the strict rule he had given them when he was there: **"that if any would not work, neither should he eat."** (Note: Paul said if someone *would* not work, meaning they refused to work. He was not talking about those who *could* not work due to sickness or old age).
6. Paul noted that he heard some among them were not working at all, but had become "busybodies" (meddling in other people's affairs and spreading gossip).
7. He commanded and exhorted such people by the Lord Jesus Christ to work with quietness and eat their own bread.
8. He encouraged the rest of the believers not to be weary in doing good. If anyone disobeyed this letter, they were to note that person and have no company with him, so that he might be ashamed. However, they were not to treat him as an enemy, but admonish (correct) him as a brother.

### Working heartily as unto the Lord (Colossians 3:23-25)

Paul gave instructions to servants (slaves) regarding how they should approach their daily tasks. This teaching applies directly to all employees and workers today.

- **The command:** "And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men" (Colossians 3:23). Paul taught that believers should put their best effort into their work, treating it as if they are working directly for God, rather than just trying to please a human boss.
- **The reward:** They should do this "Knowing that of the Lord ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance: for ye serve the Lord Christ." The true employer is Jesus Christ, and He will reward honest labour.
- **The warning:** "But he that doeth wrong shall receive for the wrong which he hath done: and there is no respect of persons." God is impartial. If an earthly master treats a worker badly, or if a worker cheats their master, God will judge the wrongdoer fairly, regardless of their social status.

## Analyse the benefits of labour

**Objective ii: analyse the benefits of labour.**

Working honestly provides numerous benefits to the individual, the family, and society:

1. **Provision of basic needs:** Labour provides the income necessary to buy food, clothing, and shelter, allowing a person to "eat their own bread," as Paul instructed the Thessalonians.
2. **Maintenance of self-respect:** Earning a living independently protects a person from the shame of begging and preserves their personal dignity.
3. **Prevention of crime and social vices:** Idle hands are easily drawn into trouble. Working keeps people busy and away from the temptation to engage in stealing, fraud, or becoming "busybodies" and gossips.
4. **Ability to help others:** A person who works and earns money is in a position to give to the church, support extended family members, and help the poor. You cannot practice Christian charity if you produce nothing.
5. **Contribution to national development:** Every honest job, from farming to teaching to engineering, contributes to building the community and the nation's economy.
6. **Spiritual obedience:** Working hard and honestly is a direct obedience to God's command to subdue the earth, and it pleases the Lord, attracting His blessings.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Dignity | The state or quality of being worthy of honour and respect. |
| Labour | Work, especially hard physical or mental effort done for wages. |
| Dignity of labour | The belief that all legitimate and honest work is respectable and honourable. |
| Denarius / Penny | The standard daily wage for a labourer in first-century Israel (translated as "penny" in the King James Version). |
| Householder | A landowner or master of a house who hires workers. |
| Busybody | A person who meddles in the affairs of others, often resulting from idleness. |
| Disorderly | In the context of Thessalonians, living an undisciplined life of idleness and refusing to work. |
| Inheritance | The spiritual and eternal reward that God gives to those who serve Him faithfully in whatever work they do. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Understand Paul's rule:** "If any would not work, neither should he eat" (II Thess. 3:10). This applied to people who *refused* to work, not those who were unable to work.
- **Know the daily wage:** In the parable of the labourers, the agreed wage was a **penny** (or denarius). Remember this exact term for the exam.
- **Identify the problem in Thessalonica:** The specific problem Paul was addressing was **idleness** and people becoming **busybodies**, not just general disobedience.
- **Know the motivation for work:** According to Colossians 3, Christians are to work heartily "as to the Lord, and not unto men." The ultimate reward comes from the Lord.
- **Do not confuse parables:** The Parable of the Labourers in the Vineyard (Matthew 20) is about the equal reward of a penny regardless of hours worked. Do not confuse it with the Parable of the Talents, which is about investing what you are given.
- **All honest work is equal in dignity:** Be ready for application questions testing if you understand that a cleaner and a lawyer have equal personal dignity if their work is honest.

## Quick summary

1. The concept of the dignity of labour means that all honest, legitimate work has intrinsic value and is worthy of respect. 
2. No particular kind of honest work (whether manual or intellectual) causes a person to lose their personal dignity.
3. In the Parable of the Labourers in the Vineyard, the householder paid all workers the agreed wage of a penny, demonstrating respect for all workers and illustrating God's sovereign grace.
4. In Thessalonica, some Christians became idle busybodies. Paul commanded that anyone who refuses to work should not eat.
5. Paul worked night and day making tents so as not to be a burden, setting an example of hard work for the church.
6. According to Colossians, believers must do all work heartily as unto the Lord, knowing that their true reward (the inheritance) comes from Christ.
7. The benefits of labour include providing for personal needs, maintaining self-respect, preventing crime, enabling charity, and contributing to society.

## Practice questions

**1. The concept of the "dignity of labour" implies that:**
- A. only physical, manual work is acceptable to God
- B. white-collar jobs are spiritually superior to manual jobs
- C. all honest and legitimate work is honourable and worthy of respect
- D. a person must work without expecting a salary to be dignified

**Answer: C.** The dignity of labour is the principle that no honest work is degrading, and all legitimate jobs are worthy of respect.

**2. In the parable of the labourers in the vineyard, what was the agreed daily wage for the first group of workers?**
- A. A talent
- B. A piece of silver
- C. A penny (denarius)
- D. A shekel

**Answer: C.** The householder agreed with the early workers for a penny (a denarius) a day.

**3. What specific social problem resulted from idleness among the Thessalonian Christians?**
- A. They started rebelling against the Roman government
- B. They became busybodies meddling in other people's affairs
- C. They returned to worshipping idols
- D. They began stealing from the church treasury

**Answer: B.** Paul noted that because they were not working at all, they had become busybodies (II Thess. 3:11).

**4. Paul commanded the Thessalonians that if anyone would not work, he should not:**
- A. pray
- B. sleep
- C. speak
- D. eat

**Answer: D.** Paul's strict rule was: "that if any would not work, neither should he eat" (II Thess. 3:10).

**5. According to Colossians 3, Christians are instructed to do their work heartily as unto the:**
- A. church elders
- B. Lord
- C. earthly masters
- D. community

**Answer: B.** Believers are to work heartily "as to the Lord, and not unto men" (Colossians 3:23).

**6. Why did the householder in the parable pay the labourers who worked for only one hour the same wage as those who worked all day?**
- A. The first group did a poor job and damaged the vineyard
- B. The last group worked much faster than the others
- C. He chose to be generous and had the right to do what he wanted with his own money
- D. The workers who arrived late threatened him

**Answer: C.** He explained that he did the first workers no wrong since he paid the agreed amount, and asked, "Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own?"

**7. What did Paul do in Thessalonica to set an example for the believers?**
- A. He worked with labour and travail night and day so as not to be a burden
- B. He gave all his money to the poor and lived as a beggar
- C. He commanded the rich to give all their food to the church
- D. He spent his entire time preaching and accepted free food from everyone

**Answer: A.** Paul engaged in manual labour (tentmaking) to provide for himself, setting an example of hard work.

**8. Which of the following is NOT a benefit of labour discussed in Christian teaching?**
- A. Provision of basic daily needs
- B. Ability to give to those in need
- C. Avoidance of poverty and idleness
- D. Encouraging partiality and respect of persons

**Answer: D.** Labour does not encourage partiality. In fact, Colossians warns that with God "there is no respect of persons" (no partiality).

**9. According to Paul's letter to the Colossians, the ultimate reward for honest labour comes from:**
- A. the government
- B. the Lord
- C. the local church
- D. the employer

**Answer: B.** They are to work knowing that "of the Lord ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance" (Colossians 3:24).

**10. A Christian vulcanizer and a Christian hospital director both possess equal personal dignity because:**
- A. they both earn the same amount of money
- B. no honest and legitimate work leads to a loss of personal dignity
- C. manual work is spiritually superior to medical work
- D. they both work the exact same number of hours per week

**Answer: B.** Dignity comes from the honesty and integrity of the work itself, not from the prestige of the profession.
