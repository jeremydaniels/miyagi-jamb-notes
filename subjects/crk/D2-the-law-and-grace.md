## The Place of the Law Among the Jews

**Objective ii: identify the place of the Law among the Jews.**

To understand the Christian teaching on law and grace, you must first understand what "The Law" meant to the Jewish people. 

For the Jews, **the Law** (often called the Torah or the Law of Moses) was the absolute center of their religious, social, and political life. It consisted of the Ten Commandments and hundreds of other rules (traditionally 613 commandments) given by God to Moses at Mount Sinai. 

The Law had several important roles in Jewish life:
1. **The Mark of the Covenant:** The Law was the sign of their special relationship with God. Keeping it proved they were God's chosen people.
2. **The Standard of Righteousness:** The Jews believed that strict obedience to every detail of the Law was the only way a person could be considered righteous (justified) before God.
3. **A Guide for Daily Living:** The Law regulated everything they did, including what they could eat, how they should dress, how to conduct business, how to worship, and how to offer sacrifices for their sins.
4. **National Identity:** It separated the Jews from the Gentiles (non-Jews), whom they considered unclean because they did not have the Law.

Because the Law was so highly respected, many Jewish Christians in the early church believed that for a person to be saved, they had to believe in Jesus *and* obey the Law of Moses. The Apostle Paul wrote letters to the Romans and the Galatians to correct this misunderstanding and explain the true purpose of the Law and the superiority of God's grace.

## The Purpose and Significance of the Law and Grace

**Objective i: examine the purpose and significance of the law and grace.**

Paul taught that while the Law is holy and good, it can never save anyone because humans are naturally sinful and cannot keep it perfectly. Instead, God provided salvation through **Grace**—His unmerited (undeserved) favor and free gift of salvation through faith in Jesus Christ. 

The passages below outline Paul's teaching on this subject.

### Abraham's Promise Through Faith, Not Law (Rom. 4:13-25)

**Content item: Rom. 4:13-25**

To prove that salvation is by faith and not by the Law, Paul used the example of Abraham, the father of the Jewish nation. 

God promised Abraham that he and his descendants would inherit the world. Paul pointed out that this promise was given to Abraham long before the Law of Moses even existed. Therefore, the promise did not come because Abraham kept the Law, but through the **righteousness of faith**.

If people could only inherit God's promise by keeping the Law, then faith would be useless and the promise would be void. The Law brings God's wrath, because wherever there is a law, people will break it (transgression). Therefore, the promise rests entirely on grace, making it guaranteed to all of Abraham's spiritual descendants—both Jews and Gentiles who share his faith.

Abraham is the perfect example of faith. Even when he was nearly one hundred years old and his wife Sarah was well past the age of bearing children, he did not doubt God's promise that he would be the "father of many nations." 
> "And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform. And therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness." (Romans 4:21-22)

Paul explained that the words "it was imputed [credited] to him" were not written just for Abraham, but for Christians today. Righteousness will be credited to us if we believe in God, who raised Jesus from the dead to secure our justification.

### The Curse of the Law and Christ's Redemption (Gal. 3:10-14)

**Content item: Gal. 3:10-14**

Paul warned the Galatians that anyone who relies on the works of the Law to be saved is actually under a curse. The Law demands absolute perfection. The scripture says, "Cursed is every one that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the law to do them." Because no one can keep the whole law perfectly, everyone under it is cursed.

Furthermore, the Old Testament itself states that "The just shall live by faith" (Habakkuk 2:4). The Law is not based on faith, but on doing. 

To solve this problem, Christ took the curse upon Himself. 
> "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:" (Galatians 3:13)

By dying on the cross (a tree), Jesus absorbed the curse of the Law so that the blessing given to Abraham could come to the Gentiles through Jesus Christ, allowing everyone to receive the promise of the Holy Spirit through faith.

### The Law as a Custodian/Schoolmaster (Gal. 3:19-29)

**Content item: Gal. 3:19-29**

If the Law cannot save, then why did God give it in the first place? Paul answered this important question in Galatians 3:19-29.

1. **Added because of transgressions:** The Law was given to show people what sin is and to keep them in check until the "Seed" (Jesus Christ) arrived. 
2. **A temporary measure:** It was mediated by angels through a human mediator (Moses), but it was never meant to replace God's original promise to Abraham. 
3. **The Law as a Custodian:** The Law was not against God's promises, but it could not give life. Instead, it locked everyone up under the control of sin. Paul described the Law as our **schoolmaster** (in the King James Version) or **custodian/tutor** (in the Revised Standard Version). In the ancient world, a custodian was a strict servant who disciplined a child and led them to school. The Law acted as a strict disciplinarian, showing people their guilt and leading them to Christ so they could be justified by faith.
4. **Freedom in Christ:** Now that faith in Christ has come, we are no longer under the control of the schoolmaster (the Law). 

Through faith in Jesus, everyone becomes a child of God. In this new arrangement of grace, all human divisions are erased: "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus." If you belong to Christ, you are Abraham's seed and an heir to the promise.

### Adam, Christ, and the Abundance of Grace (Rom. 5:18-21)

**Content item: Rom. 5:18-21**

Paul compared two important historical figures to explain how grace works: **Adam** and **Jesus Christ**.

*   **Adam's Disobedience:** By one man's (Adam's) offense, judgment and condemnation came upon all human beings. Through his disobedience, many were made sinners.
*   **Christ's Obedience:** By one man's (Jesus Christ's) righteous act on the cross, the free gift of grace came to all men, bringing justification and life. By His obedience, many will be made righteous.

Paul explained that the Law was introduced so that the offense might abound—meaning the Law made people fully aware of how much they were sinning. However, "where sin abounded, grace did much more abound." God's grace is far greater than human sin. Just as sin reigned and brought death, grace now reigns through righteousness to bring eternal life through Jesus Christ.

### Justified Freely by Grace (Rom. 3:24)

**Content item: Rom. 3:24**

Paul summarized the core message of the Christian gospel in Romans 3:24:
> "Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus:" (Romans 3:24)

*   **Justified:** Declared righteous and innocent before God.
*   **Freely:** It is a free gift; it cannot be bought or earned by good works.
*   **By His grace:** Out of God's unmerited favor and love, not our own merit.
*   **Through redemption:** A ransom was paid to buy humanity back from the slavery of sin, and that price was the blood of Christ Jesus.

### Comparing the Law and Grace

| Feature | The Law | Grace |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Originator** | Given through Moses. | Came through Jesus Christ. |
| **Nature** | Demands perfect obedience (works). | Offered as a free, unmerited gift (faith). |
| **Effect on Sin** | Points out sin and makes offenses abound. | Forgives sin and removes guilt. |
| **Consequence** | Brings a curse and condemnation. | Brings justification and eternal life. |
| **Role** | Served as a temporary schoolmaster/custodian. | The permanent fulfillment of God's promise. |
| **Accessibility** | Originally given to the Jews. | Open to everyone (Jew and Greek, male and female). |

## Practical Application to Nigerian Life

In Nigeria today, some people still approach religion with a "Law" mindset. They believe that they can earn God's favor or buy their salvation by doing good works, going to church regularly, paying tithes, or giving alms. While good works are important for Christians, the teaching on Law and Grace shows that nobody can be perfect enough to earn salvation. 

Salvation is a free gift (grace). Understanding this should make Christians humble, knowing they were saved by God's mercy, not their own power. However, grace is not an excuse to live recklessly or commit crimes; rather, it is a transformation that empowers believers to live righteously out of love for God.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The Law** | The commands and rules given by God to Moses, which formed the basis of the Jewish religion. |
| **Grace** | God's unmerited (undeserved) favor and free gift of salvation to mankind. |
| **Justification** | The act by which God declares a sinner righteous and free from guilt. |
| **Custodian / Schoolmaster** | A strict servant or disciplinarian; Paul's description of the Law's role in leading people to Christ. |
| **Imputed** | Credited or assigned to someone's account (e.g., Abraham's faith was imputed to him as righteousness). |
| **Redemption** | The act of buying back or saving someone from bondage by paying a price (Christ's death). |
| **Transgression** | The breaking of a law or rule; a sin. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Remember the purpose of the Law:** The Law was *never* meant to save people. JAMB frequently asks this. Its purpose was to show people their sins and act as a **schoolmaster/custodian** to bring them to Christ (Galatians 3:24).
*   **Abraham's example:** Know that Abraham was justified by **faith**, not by the Law. The promise was given to him before the Law of Moses existed. 
*   **The Curse:** Know Galatians 3:13 well: Christ redeemed us from the curse of the Law by becoming a curse for us (by hanging on a tree/cross).
*   **Adam vs. Christ:** Understand the contrast in Romans 5. Adam brought sin and condemnation to all; Christ brought grace and justification to all.
*   **"Where sin abounded..."** Know how to complete this phrase: "...grace did much more abound."
*   **Common mistake:** Students sometimes confuse the Jewish view of the Law with Paul's teaching. The Jews believed the Law brought salvation; Paul taught that it brought a curse because no one could keep it perfectly.

## Quick summary

1. The Law was central to Jewish life and was seen as the only standard of righteousness and path to God's favor.
2. Paul taught that salvation comes by God's grace through faith, not by keeping the Law.
3. Abraham is the ultimate example of this; he believed God, and it was imputed to him for righteousness long before the Law existed.
4. Anyone who relies on the Law is under a curse because nobody can obey it perfectly. Christ broke this curse by dying on the cross.
5. The Law was added because of transgressions. It acted as a temporary "schoolmaster" or "custodian" to discipline people and point them to their need for Christ.
6. Through Adam's disobedience, sin and condemnation entered the world, but through Christ's obedience, grace and justification abound much more.
7. Under grace, all believers are justified freely, and traditional divisions (Jew/Greek, slave/free, male/female) are erased in Christ.

## Practice questions

**1. According to Paul in his letter to the Galatians, what was the primary purpose of the Law?**
- A. To grant eternal life to the Jews
- B. To act as a custodian to lead people to Christ
- C. To abolish the promise made to Abraham
- D. To separate the Jews from the Gentiles permanently

**Answer: B.** Paul describes the Law as a "schoolmaster" or "custodian" whose temporary role was to keep people in check and lead them to Christ so they could be justified by faith (Galatians 3:24).

**2. Which Old Testament figure did Paul use to prove that justification is by faith and not by the Law?**
- A. Moses
- B. David
- C. Abraham
- D. Isaac

**Answer: C.** Paul used Abraham as the prime example, pointing out that Abraham's faith was imputed to him for righteousness long before the Law was given (Romans 4).

**3. "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us..." How did Christ become a curse according to Galatians 3?**
- A. By being rejected by the Jewish leaders
- B. By hanging on a tree
- C. By breaking the Sabbath rules
- D. By descending into hell

**Answer: B.** Galatians 3:13 states that Christ became a curse for us, "for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree."

**4. According to Romans 5, what was the result of the one man's (Adam's) disobedience?**
- A. The Law was abolished
- B. Grace did much more abound
- C. Many were made sinners
- D. The Jews were chosen as God's people

**Answer: C.** By one man's disobedience, many were made sinners and judgment came upon all men to condemnation.

**5. What was the place of the Law among the Jews?**
- A. It was seen as an unnecessary tradition.
- B. It was the absolute standard of righteousness by which they sought to be justified.
- C. It was viewed as a curse they wanted to escape.
- D. It was a philosophy for the Gentiles to follow.

**Answer: B.** For the Jews, the Law was the center of their existence and the strict standard by which they believed a person could be justified before God.

**6. According to Paul in Romans 5:20, the Law entered so that the offense might abound, but where sin abounded...**
- A. God's anger was revealed.
- B. Grace did much more abound.
- C. The schoolmaster took control.
- D. Faith was made void.

**Answer: B.** Paul taught that although the Law made people fully aware of their sin, God's grace was far greater ("grace did much more abound").

**7. "Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus." This statement is found in...**
- A. Romans 3
- B. Galatians 5
- C. Romans 8
- D. Galatians 2

**Answer: A.** This is a direct quotation from Romans 3:24, which summarizes that justification is a free gift of grace.

**8. According to Galatians 3, why was the Law added?**
- A. Because Abraham lacked faith
- B. Because of transgressions
- C. To replace the covenant of grace
- D. To make the Jews superior to the Greeks

**Answer: B.** Galatians 3:19 states that the Law was "added because of transgressions, till the seed should come to whom the promise was made."

**9. Paul taught in Galatians 3:28 that because of faith in Christ, there is no longer any distinction between...**
- A. Sinners and saints
- B. Angels and demons
- C. Jew and Greek
- D. Law and prophets

**Answer: C.** Under the grace of Christ, human divisions are erased: "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female."

**10. According to Romans 4, what would happen if those who are of the Law are the only heirs of the promise?**
- A. Faith is made void and the promise is made of no effect.
- B. The whole world would become Jewish.
- C. Grace would increase.
- D. Moses would be glorified above Abraham.

**Answer: A.** Romans 4:14 states, "For if they which are of the law be heirs, faith is made void, and the promise made of none effect."
