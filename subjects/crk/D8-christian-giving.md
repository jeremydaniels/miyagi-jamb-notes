# JAMB Crk Notes: Christian Giving

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section D, Topic 8 of the JAMB CRK syllabus: Christian Giving. It covers the biblical teachings on how, why, and with what attitude Christians should give to others and to the work of God.

The syllabus lists the following content items to study:
- (Phil. 4:14-20; II Cor. 8:1-5; 9
  Cf. Matt 6:2-4)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. interpret the concept of Christian giving;
2. relate the teachings of Paul on Christian giving;
3. identify the importance of Christian giving.

*Note on the syllabus references: "Cf." stands for the Latin word "confer", meaning "compare with". The syllabus wants you to study Paul's letters to the Philippians and Corinthians, and compare them with Jesus' foundational teaching on giving in the Gospel of Matthew.*

## The concept of Christian giving

**Objective i: interpret the concept of Christian giving.**
**Content: Cf. Matt 6:2-4**

The concept of Christian giving is the belief that everything a person has comes from God, and that giving to others—especially the poor and the church—is an act of worship, love, and obedience to God. It is not just about donating money; it is about a heart attitude of generosity, selflessness, and surrender to God's will.

### Jesus' teaching on giving (Matthew 6:2-4)

To fully understand the concept of Christian giving, we must look at what Jesus taught during the Sermon on the Mount. In Jesus' time, some religious leaders gave to the poor (almsgiving) mainly to be seen and praised by others. 

Jesus condemned this attitude. He taught the following principles for giving:

1. **Do not be a hypocrite:** Jesus said, "Therefore when thou doest thine alms, do not sound a trumpet before thee, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may have glory of men" (Matthew 6:2). Those who give to show off have already received their full reward—the temporary praise of men.
2. **Give in secret:** Jesus taught that giving should be so quiet and secretive that "let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth" (Matthew 6:3). This is a figure of speech meaning that you should not even proudly congratulate yourself for your own generosity.
3. **God rewards secret giving:** When a Christian gives in secret out of genuine love, "thy Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly" (Matthew 6:4).

**Application to everyday life:** In many Nigerian communities, it is common to have public fund-raising events (launchings) where people announce their large donations to receive applause, titles, or political favour. Jesus' teaching warns Christians against this "show-off" mentality. True Christian giving is motivated by compassion and love for God, not by a desire for public recognition.

## Paul's teaching on Christian giving to the Corinthians

**Objective ii: relate the teachings of Paul on Christian giving.**
**Content: II Cor. 8:1-5; 9**

The Apostle Paul provided the most detailed teaching on Christian giving in his second letter to the Corinthians. At the time, Paul was collecting an offering from the Gentile (non-Jewish) churches to help the Jewish Christians in Jerusalem, who were suffering from severe poverty and famine. 

### The Example of the Macedonians (2 Corinthians 8:1-5)

To encourage the church in Corinth to give generously, Paul used the churches of Macedonia (such as Philippi, Thessalonica, and Berea) as an outstanding example of Christian giving.

1. **Giving out of poverty:** The Macedonians were experiencing a "great trial of affliction" and "deep poverty." However, their poverty did not stop them; instead, it overflowed in a wealth of rich generosity.
2. **Giving beyond their power:** Paul testified that they gave according to their means, and even beyond their means. 
3. **Giving willingly:** Nobody forced them to give. In fact, they earnestly begged Paul for the "favour and fellowship" of taking part in the relief offering for the saints in Jerusalem.
4. **Giving themselves first:** The secret to the Macedonians' incredible generosity was their spiritual surrender. Paul noted that they did not just give money; "they gave their own selves first to the Lord, and unto us by the will of God" (2 Corinthians 8:5). Because they had surrendered their lives entirely to God, surrendering their money was easy.

### Principles of giving (2 Corinthians 9)

In Chapter 9, Paul lays out several powerful spiritual rules regarding how and why Christians should give:

1. **The law of sowing and reaping:** Paul compared giving to farming. "He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully" (2 Corinthians 9:6). If a farmer plants only a few seeds, he gets a small harvest. If a Christian gives generously, God will bless them abundantly.
2. **Give intentionally and cheerfully:** Giving must be a personal decision made in the heart. It should not be done "grudgingly" (with regret) or "of necessity" (under pressure or force), "for God loveth a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7).
3. **God's abundant provision:** Paul assured the Corinthians that if they gave, they would not lack. God is able to make all grace abound toward them, so that they will always have enough for themselves and plenty left over for every good work.
4. **Producing thanksgiving to God:** When Christians give, it does more than just solve a physical problem. It causes the people who receive the gift to thank and glorify God. The poor Christians in Jerusalem would pray for the Corinthians and long for them with deep affection because of the grace of God shown through their giving.

## Paul's appreciation of the Philippians' gift

**Objective ii: relate the teachings of Paul on Christian giving.**
**Content: Phil. 4:14-20**

Paul's letter to the Philippians shows the practical result of Christian giving. Paul was in prison (most likely in Rome) and could not work to support himself. The church in Philippi (which was one of the Macedonian churches mentioned earlier) sent a man named **Epaphroditus** with a financial gift to care for Paul's needs.

In Philippians 4:14-20, Paul expresses his deep gratitude for their gift and explains its spiritual significance:

1. **Partnership in affliction:** Paul thanked them for sharing in his troubles. He reminded them that in the early days of his missionary work, after he left Macedonia, no other church entered into a partnership of "giving and receiving" with him except the Philippians. 
2. **Consistent support:** Even when Paul was ministering in Thessalonica, the Philippians sent help for his needs "once and again" (more than once).
3. **Seeking the spiritual fruit:** Paul made it clear that he was not greedy for their money. He said, "Not because I desire a gift: but I desire fruit that may abound to your account" (Philippians 4:17). He was happier about the spiritual reward that God would credit to the Philippians for their generosity than he was about the money itself.
4. **An acceptable sacrifice to God:** Paul declared that he was now fully supplied and had plenty because of the things Epaphroditus brought from them. He described their financial gift using Old Testament worship terms: it was "an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, wellpleasing to God" (Philippians 4:18). This shows that giving to God's servants is a direct act of worship to God.
5. **The promise of provision:** Because the Philippians had sacrificially supplied Paul's needs, Paul pronounced a famous blessing over them: "But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus" (Philippians 4:19).

## The importance of Christian giving

**Objective iii: identify the importance of Christian giving.**

Based on the teachings of Jesus and Paul, we can identify several vital reasons why giving is important in the Christian faith:

1. **It is an act of worship and obedience:** Giving is not just a social charity; it is a spiritual sacrifice that pleases God, described by Paul as a "sweet-smelling savour" (Philippians 4:18).
2. **It meets the physical needs of others:** Giving practically solves the problems of the poor, the needy, and those doing God's work (just as the Corinthians helped the poor in Jerusalem, and the Philippians helped Paul in prison).
3. **It proves our surrender to God:** Like the Macedonians who "gave themselves first to the Lord," giving our resources shows that God, not money, is the true master of our lives.
4. **It brings God's blessings to the giver:** The Bible promises that those who sow bountifully will reap bountifully, and that God will supply all the needs of those who give sacrificially (Philippians 4:19).
5. **It promotes love and unity:** Giving bridges the gap between different groups of people. The financial gift from the Gentile churches in Greece created a bond of love and unity with the Jewish Christians in Jerusalem.
6. **It produces thanksgiving to God:** When Christians give to those in need, the receivers are moved to thank and praise God, bringing glory to His name.
7. **It stores up eternal rewards:** Jesus promised that the Father will openly reward those who give in secret, and Paul spoke of the "fruit that increases to your account" (Philippians 4:17).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Alms/Almsgiving** | Giving money, food, or resources to the poor and needy. |
| **Hypocrite** | A person who pretends to be holy or righteous just to be praised by others, while their heart is wrong. |
| **Macedonia** | The region in northern Greece containing churches (like Philippi and Thessalonica) that gave generously despite deep poverty. |
| **Epaphroditus** | The messenger from the church in Philippi who travelled to Paul in prison to deliver their financial gift. |
| **Bountifully** | Giving generously, abundantly, and in large amounts. |
| **Cheerful giver** | Someone who gives happily and willingly from the heart, not out of regret or force. |
| **Sweet-smelling savour** | An Old Testament term for an animal sacrifice that pleased God, used by Paul to describe a financial gift. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Who were the example of extreme generosity?** JAMB frequently asks which group Paul used as an example to the Corinthians. The answer is the **Macedonians** (or the churches of Macedonia). 
- **What was the condition of the Macedonians?** Remember that they gave out of "deep poverty" and "severe affliction", not out of wealth.
- **What did they do first?** Before giving their money, the Macedonians "gave themselves first to the Lord." This is a very common exam question.
- **Who delivered the Philippians' gift?** Note that **Epaphroditus** was the one who brought the gift to Paul.
- **Jesus' teaching on secrecy:** Remember that Jesus said "do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing" to emphasize giving without seeking attention. 
- **Know the key quotes:** Be able to recognize the context of "God loves a cheerful giver" (Paul to the Corinthians) and "My God shall supply all your needs" (Paul to the Philippians).

## Quick summary

1. Jesus taught that giving must be done in secret, not to show off like hypocrites, and that God rewards those who give with pure motives.
2. Paul urged the Corinthians to give to the poor Christians in Jerusalem, using the extreme generosity of the poverty-stricken Macedonian churches as an example.
3. The secret to the Macedonians' generosity was that they first surrendered themselves to the Lord.
4. Paul taught the principle of sowing and reaping: those who sow bountifully will reap bountifully.
5. Giving must be done intentionally and happily, because "God loves a cheerful giver."
6. Paul thanked the Philippians for the gift they sent to him in prison through Epaphroditus.
7. He called their gift a fragrant offering and an acceptable sacrifice that pleases God, promising that God would in turn supply all their needs.

## Practice questions

**1. According to Jesus' teaching in Matthew, when a Christian gives alms, they should NOT**

- A. give to the Gentiles
- B. sound a trumpet to be seen by men
- C. expect a reward from God
- D. give more than they can afford

**Answer: B.** Jesus warned against sounding a trumpet in the synagogues and streets like the hypocrites do to get glory from men. Giving should be done in secret.

**2. Which churches did Paul use as an example of excellent giving to encourage the Corinthians?**

- A. The churches of Galatia
- B. The churches of Judea
- C. The churches of Macedonia
- D. The churches of Asia Minor

**Answer: C.** Paul used the churches of Macedonia as a model because they gave generously despite their severe affliction and deep poverty.

**3. According to Paul in 2 Corinthians 8, what was the primary reason the Macedonians were able to give so generously despite their poverty?**

- A. They expected Paul to pay them back.
- B. They wanted to prove they were better than the Corinthians.
- C. They gave themselves first to the Lord.
- D. They had a hidden store of wealth.

**Answer: C.** Paul wrote that "they gave their own selves first to the Lord, and unto us by the will of God," which was the foundation of their generous giving.

**4. "Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity..." What is the conclusion of this verse in 2 Corinthians 9?**

- A. for God will supply all his needs.
- B. for God loveth a cheerful giver.
- C. for giving is a sweet-smelling savour.
- D. for he that sows sparingly shall reap bountifully.

**Answer: B.** The verse concludes, "...for God loveth a cheerful giver." Giving should be done happily and willingly, not under pressure.

**5. In Paul's letter to the Philippians, who brought the financial gift to him?**

- A. Timothy
- B. Titus
- C. Silas
- D. Epaphroditus

**Answer: D.** Paul mentions in Philippians 4:18 that he had received from Epaphroditus the things which were sent from the Philippian church.

**6. Paul described the gift from the Philippians as**

- A. a loan that he would soon repay.
- B. a seed that would heal the sick.
- C. an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable to God.
- D. a small token of their vast wealth.

**Answer: C.** Paul viewed their financial support as a spiritual act of worship, calling it a sweet-smelling savour and a sacrifice well-pleasing to God.

**7. "But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus." To whom was this promise made?**

- A. The Corinthians, because they helped the poor in Jerusalem.
- B. The Philippians, because they supported Paul's ministry.
- C. The Romans, because of their great faith.
- D. The Macedonians, because of their extreme poverty.

**Answer: B.** This promise is found in Philippians 4:19. Paul declared this blessing over the Philippians immediately after thanking them for the financial gift they sent him.

**8. According to Paul's agricultural analogy in 2 Corinthians 9, a person who sows sparingly will**

- A. reap bountifully by God's grace.
- B. reap sparingly.
- C. lose all his seeds.
- D. harvest eternal life.

**Answer: B.** Paul taught the law of sowing and reaping: "He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully."

**9. When Paul was commending the Philippians, he noted that in the early days of the gospel, they were the only church that**

- A. sent missionaries to Rome.
- B. entered into a partnership of giving and receiving with him.
- C. refused to listen to false prophets.
- D. had wealthy members.

**Answer: B.** Paul noted that when he departed from Macedonia, no church communicated with him concerning giving and receiving except the Philippians.

**10. One of the major importance of Christian giving, as highlighted by Paul's collection for the saints, is that it**

- A. guarantees the giver will never fall sick.
- B. causes the receivers to offer thanksgiving and glory to God.
- C. allows the giver to boast in the church.
- D. replaces the need for daily prayer.

**Answer: B.** Paul explained in 2 Corinthians 9 that the administration of this service not only supplies the needs of the saints but is abundant also by many thanksgivings unto God.
