## The teachings of Romans 13 on civic responsibility

Civic responsibility refers to the duties and obligations a citizen owes to their country, community, and government. In his letter to the Romans, the Apostle Paul provided clear instructions on how Christians should behave as members of a human society, balancing their heavenly calling with their earthly duties. 

According to Paul, being a good Christian also means being a good citizen. A Christian must respect the laws of the land, contribute to societal development, and avoid destructive behaviours.

## The need for obedience to authority

**Objective i: identify the need for obedience to authority.**

In Romans 13:1-5, Paul strictly commands Christians to submit to civil authorities. He gives several theological and practical reasons why believers must not rebel against the government.

### 1. All authority comes from God
Paul writes, "Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God" (Romans 13:1). 
Christians believe that God is sovereign (supreme) over the whole universe. Therefore, any person in a position of political or civil leadership—whether a president, a governor, or a local chairman—is there because God has allowed it. 

### 2. Rebelling against authority is rebelling against God
Because God has instituted these authorities, whoever resists or disobeys them is actually resisting the ordinance (law or arrangement) of God. Paul warns that those who rebel will bring judgment (condemnation) upon themselves.

### 3. Rulers are God's ministers for good
Civil authorities exist to maintain order in society. Paul explains that rulers are not a terror to people who do good works, but to those who do evil. 
- If you do what is right, you will have no reason to fear the authorities; instead, you will receive their praise.
- The ruler is described as the "minister of God to thee for good." 

### 4. Rulers have the power to punish evil ("bearing the sword")
If a person does evil, they should be afraid. Paul says the ruler "beareth not the sword in vain" (Romans 13:4). The "sword" is a symbol of the government's power to punish criminals, enforce laws, and maintain peace. The ruler acts as God's servant to execute wrath and punishment on wrongdoers.

### 5. For the sake of conscience
Paul concludes that Christians must submit to authorities for two main reasons:
1. **To avoid wrath (punishment):** So they do not get arrested or penalized by the law.
2. **For conscience's sake:** So they maintain a clear, guilt-free conscience before God, knowing they have done the right thing.

## Requirements of good citizenship

**Objective ii: specify the requirements of good citizenship.**

Based on Romans 13 (and supported by 1 Timothy 2 and 1 Peter 2), good citizenship is not just about avoiding crime. It requires active participation in building the community and maintaining a moral lifestyle. The Bible specifies several key requirements for good citizenship:

### 1. Paying taxes and civic dues
Paul explicitly commands Christians to pay their taxes to support the government's work, because government officials are "God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing" (Romans 13:6). 
He breaks this down into four specific duties:
- **Tribute:** Direct taxes on individuals or property.
- **Custom:** Indirect taxes, tolls, or duties on goods and trade.
- **Fear (Reverence):** Showing deep respect to those in high authority.
- **Honour:** Giving appropriate respect to leaders and officials based on their position.

*Application to Nigeria:* A good citizen pays their taxes (e.g., to the Federal Inland Revenue Service) promptly. Tax evasion is both a civic crime and a sin against God's command.

### 2. Loving one's neighbour
Paul writes, "Owe no man any thing, but to love one another" (Romans 13:8). This means the only ongoing "debt" a Christian should have is the obligation to love others. 
He explains that all the commandments—such as not committing adultery, not killing, not stealing, and not coveting—are summed up in one rule: **"Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself."** Because love works no ill (harm) to a neighbour, love is the complete fulfillment of the law. A good citizen does not harm, cheat, or exploit others.

### 3. Living an upright, honest life (Walking in the light)
Paul urges Christians to wake up from spiritual sleep because the time of salvation is near. A good citizen must cast off the "works of darkness" and put on the "armour of light." This means living decently and honestly in the community. 

Paul specifically lists social vices that Christians must avoid:

| Works of darkness to avoid | Modern examples in society |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Rioting and drunkenness** | Cultism, violent protests, disturbing the public peace, and alcohol abuse. |
| **Chambering and wantonness** | Sexual immorality, promiscuity, prostitution, and indecent dressing. |
| **Strife and envying** | Fighting, ethnic or political violence, jealousy, and spreading harmful gossip. |

Instead of these vices, Christians are commanded to "put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof" (Romans 13:14).

### 4. Praying for political rulers (1 Timothy 2:1-4)
While Romans 13 focuses on submission and taxes, Paul adds another vital requirement in 1 Timothy 2:1-4: **prayer**. 
He urges that "supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men; For kings, and for all that are in authority." 
The goal of praying for leaders is so that citizens "may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty." This shows that good citizens actively pray for the success, wisdom, and peace of their national and local leaders, rather than merely cursing or complaining about them.

### 5. Submission to human institutions (1 Peter 2:13-17)
The Apostle Peter reinforces Paul's teachings, telling Christians to submit to every ordinance of man for the Lord's sake—whether it is to the king (as supreme) or to governors who punish evildoers and praise those who do well. Peter summarizes the Christian civic duty in four commands: "Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Civic responsibility** | The duties and obligations a citizen owes to their society and government. |
| **Ordinance** | An authoritative rule, law, or arrangement established by God or government. |
| **Higher powers** | The civil and political authorities that govern a society (e.g., presidents, governors, judges). |
| **Tribute / Custom** | Forms of taxes and dues paid to the government to support public administration. |
| **Conscience** | The inner sense of right and wrong; a reason given by Paul for obeying laws, beyond just fear of punishment. |
| **Bearing the sword** | A metaphor for the government's authority to use force to punish criminals and enforce the law. |
| **Armour of light** | A metaphor for living a morally pure, honest, and upright life in public. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Who gives authority?** JAMB often asks where civil authority comes from according to Romans 13. The answer is always **God**. Even bad rulers are in power because God permitted it.
- **"Bearing the sword":** Remember that this phrase refers to the government's right to **punish evildoers**, not to fight wars or persecute the church.
- **Why obey?** You must know the two reasons for obedience: **to avoid God's wrath (punishment)** and for **conscience's sake**.
- **The only acceptable debt:** Paul says we should owe no man anything except **love**. Love is the fulfillment of the law.
- **Purpose of prayer for leaders:** According to 1 Timothy 2, the primary reason we pray for leaders is so that we may live a **quiet and peaceable life**.
- **Do not confuse the lists:** Ensure you know the vices Paul says to cast off (rioting, drunkenness, chambering, wantonness, strife, envying). 

## Quick summary

1. Civic responsibility involves the moral duty of Christians to be law-abiding, contributing members of society.
2. Christians must submit to governing authorities because all power is ordained by God, and rebelling against government is rebelling against God's ordinance.
3. Rulers are established by God to praise those who do good and to execute punishment ("bear the sword") on evildoers.
4. Obedience to the law should be driven by the fear of punishment and the desire to maintain a clear conscience before God.
5. Good citizenship requires the faithful payment of taxes (tribute and custom) and showing respect (fear and honour) to officials.
6. The fundamental law of a community is to love one's neighbour, as love fulfills all the commandments and does no harm.
7. Good citizens must cast off works of darkness (violence, sexual immorality, jealousy) and live honest, upright lives.
8. Christians are commanded to pray and intercede for all those in authority so the nation can enjoy peace.

## Practice questions

**1. According to Paul in Romans 13, Christians must submit to governing authorities primarily because:**

- A. the rulers are always righteous and fair
- B. the authorities are ordained by God
- C. rulers have the power to kill citizens
- D. submission brings financial rewards

**Answer: B.** Paul teaches that "there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God."

**2. What does Paul mean when he says the ruler "beareth not the sword in vain"?**

- A. The ruler is equipped to fight external enemies
- B. The ruler uses weapons to oppress the poor
- C. The ruler has the divine authority to punish evildoers
- D. The ruler must force citizens to become Christians

**Answer: C.** The sword symbolizes the civil authority's God-given power to execute wrath and punishment upon those who do evil.

**3. In his teachings on civic responsibility, Paul states that Christians must be subject to authority not only to avoid wrath but also for the sake of:**

- A. public recognition
- B. eternal life
- C. conscience
- D. the church's reputation

**Answer: C.** Romans 13:5 states: "Wherefore ye must needs be subject, not only for wrath, but also for conscience sake."

**4. According to Romans 13, which of the following is an explicit requirement of good citizenship?**

- A. Paying tribute and custom
- B. Becoming a political leader
- C. Preaching on the streets
- D. Donating to the poor

**Answer: A.** Paul explicitly commands citizens to pay "tribute to whom tribute is due; custom to whom custom" (Romans 13:7).

**5. Paul teaches that the fulfillment of the law within a society is:**

- A. paying all taxes on time
- B. loving one's neighbour
- C. obeying traffic rules
- D. attending church regularly

**Answer: B.** Romans 13:10 says, "Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the fulfilling of the law."

**6. Which of the following is listed by Paul in Romans 13 as a "work of darkness" that citizens should cast off?**

- A. Paying custom
- B. Collecting tribute
- C. Rioting and drunkenness
- D. Praying for leaders

**Answer: C.** Paul urges Christians to walk honestly, not in "rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying" (Romans 13:13).

**7. According to Paul, what is the only debt a Christian is permitted to owe?**

- A. Tribute to the king
- B. Tithes to the church
- C. Love for one another
- D. Service to the community

**Answer: C.** Paul states in Romans 13:8, "Owe no man any thing, but to love one another."

**8. Based on the biblical teaching of civic responsibility, rulers are described as:**

- A. enemies of the cross
- B. ministers of God for good
- C. tempters of the faithful
- D. creators of the law

**Answer: B.** Romans 13:4 describes the civil ruler as "the minister of God to thee for good."

**9. According to 1 Timothy 2, what is the primary reason Christians are asked to pray for kings and all in authority?**

- A. So that the leaders will lower taxes
- B. So that the church may rule the nation
- C. So that citizens may lead a quiet and peaceable life
- D. So that the leaders will become pastors

**Answer: C.** We are commanded to pray for leaders "that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty" (1 Tim 2:2).

**10. What action does Paul say constitutes a rebellion against God's ordinance?**

- A. Refusing to bear arms in war
- B. Resisting the civil authorities
- C. Failing to fast and pray
- D. Criticizing a false prophet

**Answer: B.** Romans 13:2 warns that "whosoever therefore resisteth the power, resisteth the ordinance of God."
