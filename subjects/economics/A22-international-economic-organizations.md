## Roles and relevance of international organizations

No country in the modern world is an island. Complete self-sufficiency (known as **autarky**) is virtually impossible and economically inefficient. Because countries have different natural resources, capital, and skilled labour, they must interact with one another to survive and grow. 

**International Economic Organizations** are institutions created by treaties and agreements among several nations. Their primary role is to coordinate global and regional economic policies, promote international trade, provide financial assistance, and encourage sustainable economic growth. 

Countries join these organizations to achieve specific goals:
* **Financial assistance:** Securing short-term loans to stabilize their currencies or long-term loans for building infrastructure.
* **Economic integration:** Removing trade barriers (like tariffs and quotas) to create larger markets for their goods and services.
* **Rule-setting and dispute resolution:** Ensuring that international trade is fair and having a place to settle economic disagreements without resorting to trade wars.
* **Technical advice:** Gaining access to expert research, statistical data, and policy advice for national development.

## Economic organizations and their functions

**Objective i: identify the various economic organizations and their functions.**

To understand how these institutions work, we can divide them into four categories: global financial institutions, global trade and development organizations, African regional organizations, and European/developed world organizations.

### Global Financial Institutions

At the end of the Second World War in 1944, delegates from 44 nations met in Bretton Woods, USA, to design a new international monetary system. This conference birthed two major institutions, often called the "Bretton Woods twins": the IMF and the World Bank.

#### 1. The International Monetary Fund (IMF)
The **IMF**, headquartered in Washington, D.C., acts as the world's macroeconomic doctor. Its primary goal is to ensure the stability of the international monetary system—the system of exchange rates and international payments that enables countries to transact with each other.

**Functions of the IMF:**
* **Surveillance:** It constantly monitors the global economy and the economic policies of its 190 member countries, conducting regular "health checks" (known as Article IV consultations).
* **Financial Assistance:** It provides **short-term and medium-term loans** to countries experiencing **Balance of Payments (BOP) deficits**. A BOP deficit occurs when a country is spending much more foreign currency on imports and debt payments than it is earning from exports, leading to a foreign exchange crisis.
* **Capacity Development:** It provides technical assistance and training to member countries' central banks and finance ministries to help them manage their economies better.

#### 2. The World Bank and IBRD
While the IMF focuses on short-term stability, the **World Bank**, also headquartered in Washington, D.C., focuses on long-term development. The **International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD)** was created in 1944 specifically to rebuild Europe after WWII. Today, the IBRD is the primary lending arm of the broader **World Bank Group** (which also includes the International Development Association, IDA, which lends to the poorest countries).

**Functions of the World Bank / IBRD:**
* **Long-Term Capital Funding:** It provides long-term loans and guarantees for major capital projects, such as building dams, electrical grids, highways, and agricultural processing facilities.
* **Poverty Reduction:** Its overarching modern goal is to end extreme poverty. It does this by funding social sectors like education, healthcare, and rural water supply.
* **Policy Advice and Research:** It provides vast amounts of data, research, and expert advice on economic development and structural reforms.

| Feature | International Monetary Fund (IMF) | World Bank (IBRD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main Purpose** | Macroeconomic stability and stable exchange rates | Long-term economic development and extreme poverty reduction |
| **Type of Loans** | **Short-term** and medium-term loans | **Long-term** loans and grants |
| **Reason for Loan** | To fix **Balance of Payments (BOP)** problems | To fund **development projects** (infrastructure, agriculture, schools) |

### Global Trade and Development Organizations

#### 3. World Trade Organization (WTO)
Established in 1995 and headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, the **WTO** is the only global international organization dealing with the rules of trade between nations. It officially replaced the **General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT)**, which had been in place since 1947.

**Functions of the WTO:**
* **Administering Trade Agreements:** It sets the rules for global trade, ensuring trade flows as smoothly, predictably, and freely as possible.
* **Resolving Trade Disputes:** If a member country feels another is violating trade agreements (for example, by unfairly banning its products), the WTO acts as a neutral court to settle the dispute.
* **Trade Negotiations:** It provides a continuous forum for countries to negotiate the lowering of tariffs and the removal of quotas.
* **Enforcing Non-Discrimination:** It enforces the **Most-Favored-Nation (MFN)** principle, which states that a special trade favor granted to one WTO member must generally be granted to all.

#### 4. United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD)
**UNCTAD** is a permanent UN body established in 1964, also headquartered in Geneva. While the WTO enforces the general rules of trade for everyone, UNCTAD was created specifically to address the concerns of **developing countries**, helping them use trade, investment, and finance to develop their economies.

**Functions of UNCTAD:**
* **Integration into Global Trade:** It formulates policies to help developing countries maximize trade opportunities and integrate fairly into the world economy.
* **Stabilizing Commodity Prices:** It works to ensure that developing countries exporting primary commodities (raw agricultural and mineral products) get fair, stable prices in global markets.
* **Promoting the GSP:** It strongly advocates for the **Generalized System of Preferences (GSP)**, a system where developed countries allow exports from developing countries to enter their markets with zero or very low tariffs.

### African Regional Organizations

#### 5. Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)
Formed on May 28, 1975, with the signing of the Treaty of Lagos, **ECOWAS** is a regional political and economic union of 15 West African countries. Its headquarters is in Abuja, Nigeria. 

**Functions of ECOWAS:**
* **Economic Integration:** Its primary goal is to create a single, large trading block in West Africa through a Customs Union and a Common External Tariff (CET).
* **Free Movement:** Through the ECOWAS protocol, citizens of member states can travel, reside, and establish businesses in any other ECOWAS country without needing a visa.
* **Regional Security:** Economic growth requires peace. ECOWAS maintains regional stability through its military peacekeeping arm, **ECOMOG** (ECOWAS Monitoring Group).
* **Monetary Integration:** It coordinates efforts to eventually establish a single currency (proposed as the "Eco") for the region.

#### 6. African Union (AU)
The **AU** was officially launched in 2002, replacing the Organization of African Unity (OAU) which was founded in 1963. Headquartered in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, it covers the entire African continent.

**Functions of the AU:**
* **Continental Integration:** It drives the political and socioeconomic integration of the continent.
* **African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA):** A flagship project of the AU, AfCFTA aims to create a single continent-wide market for goods and services, eliminating tariffs on most goods across Africa.
* **Peace and Security:** The AU intervenes in conflicts, works to prevent coups, and promotes democratic principles.

#### 7. Economic Commission for Africa (ECA)
The **ECA** (often written as UNECA) is one of the UN's five regional commissions, established in 1958 and headquartered in Addis Ababa. 

**Functions of the ECA:**
* **Economic Research and Statistics:** It is the premier body for collecting macroeconomic data and conducting deep research on African economies.
* **Policy Advice:** It advises African governments on how to achieve sustainable economic development and structural transformation.
* **Capacity Building:** It trains public officials and national statisticians in economic planning.

#### 8. African Development Bank (ADB / AfDB)
Founded in 1964 and headquartered in Abidjan, Côte d'Ivoire, the **African Development Bank** is the continent's premier multilateral development finance institution. 

**Functions of the AfDB:**
* **Financing Development:** It provides long-term loans, equity investments, and grants to African governments and private companies for economic and social progress.
* **Infrastructure Focus:** It focuses heavily on funding "hard infrastructure" like cross-border highways, electricity grids, and Agro-Industrial Processing Zones.
* **Technical Assistance:** It helps member countries design and execute complex developmental projects.

### European and Developed World Organizations

#### 9. European Economic Community (EEC) and European Union (EU)
The **EEC** was created by the Treaty of Rome in 1957 by six European countries to establish a common market. As economic integration deepened into political union, the EEC evolved into the modern **European Union (EU)** following the Maastricht Treaty of 1993. The EU is headquartered in Brussels, Belgium.

**Functions of the EU:**
* **Single Market:** It guarantees the "four freedoms"—the free movement of goods, capital, services, and labor across member states.
* **Common Currency:** Many EU members share a single monetary policy and use the Euro.
* **Common Trade Policy:** Member countries operate a Common External Tariff, negotiating trade deals collectively rather than as individual nations.
* **International Aid:** Through the European Development Fund (EDF), the EU provides substantial development aid to African, Caribbean, and Pacific (ACP) countries.

#### 10. Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)
The **OECD** is an intergovernmental economic organization headquartered in Paris, France. Founded in 1961, it consists of 38 mostly high-income, developed nations (often colloquially called a "club of rich countries").

**Functions of the OECD:**
* **Global Standard Setting:** It researches and sets global standards on complex issues like corporate taxation, anti-corruption, and environmental policies.
* **Economic Data:** It publishes highly respected economic data, forecasts, and comparative statistics.
* **Coordinating Foreign Aid:** Through its Development Assistance Committee (DAC), the OECD tracks, coordinates, and sets guidelines for the foreign aid (Official Development Assistance) that rich countries give to the developing world.

## Relevance to the Nigerian economy

**Objective vii: evaluate their relevance to the Nigerian economy.**

International economic organizations heavily influence Nigeria's domestic policies, trade environment, and infrastructural development. 

### Relevance of the IMF
* **Balance of Payments Support:** Nigeria's economy is highly dependent on crude oil exports. When global oil prices crash, Nigeria suffers a severe shortage of foreign exchange. The IMF provides emergency funding (such as the Rapid Financing Instrument used during the 2020 COVID-19 crash) to help stabilize the Naira.
* **Policy Influence and Conditionalities:** The IMF's financial help often comes with strict conditions. In 1986, to secure IMF and World Bank support, Nigeria introduced the **Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP)**. This required Nigeria to devalue the Naira, privatize government-owned companies, and reduce public spending. Today, the IMF continues to strongly advise Nigeria on structural reforms, such as the removal of petrol subsidies.
* **Surveillance:** The IMF's regular reports on Nigeria's economy serve as a signal to foreign investors regarding whether it is safe to invest in the country.

### Relevance of the World Bank / IBRD
* **Infrastructural Funding:** The World Bank is one of Nigeria's largest creditors. It provides long-term loans for critical capital projects that the Nigerian budget cannot fully fund.
* **Agricultural and Social Development:** The World Bank heavily funded the **FADAMA agricultural projects**, which successfully provided resources, irrigation tools, and training directly to rural Nigerian farmers. It also funds massive immunization drives, rural electrification, and state-level educational improvements.
* **Debt Burden:** While the loans are developmental, the downside is that borrowing from the World Bank Group significantly adds to Nigeria's national external debt.

### Relevance of the WTO
* **Market Access:** As a WTO member, Nigeria's primary exports (like crude oil, natural gas, cocoa, and sesame seeds) are protected from unfair, discriminatory tariffs in other member countries.
* **Dispute Resolution:** If a foreign country arbitrarily blocks Nigerian goods without scientific justification, Nigeria can report them to the WTO for a fair hearing.
* **Protection against Dumping:** WTO rules allow Nigeria to enact anti-dumping measures if foreign companies attempt to flood the Nigerian market with goods priced below their cost of production to destroy local industries.
* **Challenge of Liberalization:** On the negative side, WTO rules limit how heavily Nigeria can protect its "infant industries." Being forced to open its markets means nascent Nigerian manufacturers sometimes struggle to compete with cheaper, mass-produced imports from Asia and the West.

### Relevance of UNCTAD
* **Attracting Foreign Direct Investment (FDI):** UNCTAD provides capacity building, research, and policy guidelines that help agencies like the Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission (NIPC) design better strategies to attract foreign investors.
* **Economic Diversification:** UNCTAD produces reports and frameworks specifically designed to help commodity-dependent countries like Nigeria diversify their economies away from crude oil.

### Relevance of ECOWAS
* **Expanded Market for Nigerian Goods:** Nigeria is the largest economy in West Africa. ECOWAS gives Nigerian manufacturers (e.g., cement producers like Dangote, and food and beverage companies) a vast regional market of hundreds of millions of people without high tariff barriers.
* **Cross-Border Business and Banking:** The free movement protocol allows Nigerian banks and businesses to easily establish branches in Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, and Senegal. It also allows Nigerians to trade freely across borders.
* **Regional Security (ECOMOG):** Instability in West Africa usually results in arms and refugees flowing into Nigeria. ECOWAS's security interventions keep the subregion relatively stable, which directly protects Nigeria's internal security.
* **Diplomatic Hegemony:** Hosting the ECOWAS Commission in Abuja allows Nigeria to project soft power and exercise leadership across the subregion.

### Relevance of the AU
* **The AfCFTA Opportunity:** Through the AU's African Continental Free Trade Area, Nigerian businesses are positioned to export freely not just to West Africa, but to massive markets in East, North, and Southern Africa. 
* **Collective Bargaining:** The AU allows Nigeria and the rest of Africa to negotiate collectively on the global stage (e.g., regarding climate change or global trade rules), giving them far more power than they would have individually.

### Relevance of the ECA
* **Data for National Planning:** The ECA provides the Nigerian government, specifically the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) and the Ministry of Finance, with vital macroeconomic frameworks, statistical tools, and demographic research needed to draft accurate national budgets.

### Relevance of the ADB (AfDB)
* **Massive Infrastructure Financing:** The AfDB provides billions of dollars in loans specifically tailored to African realities, funding Nigerian roads, water projects, and power generation without some of the strict political conditionalities of Western institutions.
* **Agro-Industrial Support:** It heavily funds Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones in Nigeria to help modernize agriculture and stop post-harvest losses.
* **Nigeria as a Donor (The NTF):** Uniquely, Nigeria acts as a leader within the AfDB. In 1976, Nigeria established the **Nigeria Trust Fund (NTF)** under the AfDB to provide concessional loans to poorer African countries, showcasing Nigeria's economic power and commitment to continental development.

### Relevance of the EEC / EU
* **Major Trade Partner:** The EU is one of the biggest buyers of Nigerian crude oil and Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG). In return, Nigeria imports heavily from the EU, particularly machinery, vehicles, and manufactured goods.
* **Economic Partnership Agreements (EPAs):** The EU negotiates complex trade agreements with African regions that dictate exactly how Nigerian goods enter the European single market.
* **Grants and Aid:** Through the European Development Fund, the EU provides massive financial grants to Nigeria. This money goes toward poverty alleviation, humanitarian aid (especially for internally displaced persons in North-East Nigeria), and supporting democratic institutions.

### Relevance of the OECD
* **Taxing Multinational Corporations:** Even though Nigeria is not a member of the OECD, it relies heavily on OECD policies. For example, Nigeria uses OECD guidelines on "transfer pricing" to prevent multinational oil and tech companies from shifting their profits out of Nigeria to avoid paying taxes to the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS).
* **Foreign Aid Coordination:** Much of the Official Development Assistance (ODA) flowing into Nigeria from rich countries is tracked and structured according to OECD standards.
* **Source of FDI:** The vast majority of foreign companies investing capital into Nigeria originate from OECD member countries.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Balance of Payments (BOP)** | A record of all financial transactions made between consumers, businesses, and the government in one country with the rest of the world. |
| **BOP Deficit** | A situation where a country pays out more money for imports and foreign debts than it receives from exports. |
| **Autarky** | Economic independence or self-sufficiency, where a country attempts to survive without international trade. |
| **Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP)** | Economic policies (often tied to IMF/World Bank loans) encouraging privatization, currency devaluation, and reduced government spending. |
| **Customs Union** | An agreement between countries to trade freely among themselves and adopt a Common External Tariff against non-members. |
| **Most-Favored-Nation (MFN)** | A core WTO rule stating that trade advantages given by a country to one member must be given to all members. |
| **AfCFTA** | African Continental Free Trade Area; an AU initiative aiming to create a single unified market across the African continent. |
| **Transfer Pricing** | The rules and methods for pricing transactions within and between enterprises under common ownership or control; heavily regulated by OECD guidelines to prevent tax evasion. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Mixing up the IMF and the World Bank:** This is the most frequently tested distinction in JAMB. **IMF** = Macroeconomic stability, short-term loans, fixing Balance of Payments (BOP) deficits. **World Bank / IBRD** = Long-term loans for physical infrastructure (roads, dams) and poverty reduction.
* **WTO vs. GATT:** GATT was a legal *agreement* from 1947; it was formally replaced by the **WTO** (a permanent *organization*) in 1995. The WTO handles global trade disputes.
* **UNCTAD's specific role:** While the WTO sets general trade rules for all nations, **UNCTAD** specifically focuses on integrating *developing countries* into the global economy and securing fair commodity prices.
* **ECOWAS main goals:** Know that ECOWAS's core economic objectives are establishing a customs union and ensuring the free movement of people across West Africa. ECOMOG is its ad-hoc military/peacekeeping wing, not its primary economic purpose.
* **EEC and EU:** The EEC (established in 1957) was the forerunner focused purely on a common market, which later transformed into the modern EU, a deeper economic and political union.
* **ADB meaning:** In JAMB questions with an African focus, ADB refers to the African Development Bank, which is now commonly styled as AfDB in modern literature.

## Quick summary

1. International economic organizations foster global cooperation, providing financial support, trade rules, data, and regional integration to member countries.
2. The IMF provides short-term loans and advice to cure Balance of Payments deficits, while the World Bank (IBRD) provides long-term capital for infrastructure and poverty reduction.
3. The WTO regulates global trade and acts as a court for trade disputes (replacing GATT), whereas UNCTAD focuses specifically on helping developing nations succeed in world trade.
4. In West Africa, ECOWAS promotes a customs union, regional security (ECOMOG), and the free movement of people, heavily benefiting Nigerian businesses.
5. The African Union (AU) drives continental integration, most notably through the creation of the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA).
6. The AfDB finances major African capital projects (with Nigeria actively participating via the NTF), and the ECA provides vital economic data for national planning.
7. The EU is a major trading partner and aid donor to Nigeria, while the OECD sets global standards, such as rules against corporate tax evasion, which Nigeria utilizes.

## Practice questions

**1. The international institution established primarily to grant long-term loans for infrastructural development is the**

- A. International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- B. World Bank (IBRD)
- C. World Trade Organization (WTO)
- D. Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)

**Answer: B.** The World Bank (specifically its IBRD arm) provides long-term capital for developmental projects like roads and dams. The most tempting wrong option is A (IMF), based on the common mistake of confusing the two Bretton Woods institutions; however, the IMF focuses on short-term macroeconomic stabilization rather than long-term infrastructural loans.

**2. Which of the following bodies was set up to assist member countries in correcting short-term Balance of Payments deficits?**

- A. International Finance Corporation
- B. African Development Bank
- C. International Monetary Fund
- D. United Nations Conference on Trade and Development

**Answer: C.** The International Monetary Fund (IMF) is the specific organization that acts as a financial backstop for countries facing severe BOP crises and foreign exchange shortages. The most tempting wrong option is A (International Finance Corporation), due to the word "Finance", but the IFC is part of the World Bank Group focused on private sector development, not government BOP issues.

**3. The fundamental objective of ECOWAS is to**

- A. create a unified military force in Africa
- B. establish a common language for West Africa
- C. promote economic integration and free trade in West Africa
- D. rival the European Union politically

**Answer: C.** ECOWAS was created to foster regional economic integration, establish a customs union, and allow the free movement of goods and citizens among member states. Option A is a tempting mistake because ECOWAS does have a peacekeeping force (ECOMOG), but military unification is a secondary, ad-hoc function, not its fundamental economic objective.

**4. The General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) was replaced by the**

- A. World Trade Organization (WTO)
- B. United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD)
- C. Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)
- D. European Union (EU)

**Answer: A.** GATT was replaced by the World Trade Organization (WTO) in 1995 to regulate international trade and resolve disputes. The most tempting wrong option is B (UNCTAD), as students often confuse the two global trade bodies, but UNCTAD was established alongside GATT specifically to focus on developing nations, rather than replacing it.

**5. Which of the following international organizations focuses primarily on the economic development of developing nations through trade?**

- A. OECD
- B. IMF
- C. UNCTAD
- D. EEC

**Answer: C.** The United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD) was established specifically to help developing countries maximize trade and development opportunities. Option A (OECD) is the most tempting wrong answer if one misreads the question, as the OECD focuses on economic development, but it is primarily a "club of rich countries" setting standards, not a body focused primarily on developing nations.

**6. A major economic relevance of the European Union (EU) to Nigeria is that the EU**

- A. sets Nigeria's domestic monetary policy
- B. is a major buyer of Nigeria's primary export commodities
- C. prints the currency used by the Central Bank of Nigeria
- D. strictly regulates internal trade between Nigerian states

**Answer: B.** The EU is one of Nigeria's most significant trading partners, importing a large share of Nigeria's crude oil and agricultural products. Option A is a typical misunderstanding for students who confuse international organizations with local sovereign powers; the EU only dictates monetary policy for its own Eurozone members, not for Nigeria.

**7. In the context of African development, the organization responsible for providing vital economic data and policy advice to governments is the**

- A. African Union (AU)
- B. Economic Commission for Africa (ECA)
- C. European Commission for Africa
- D. Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)

**Answer: B.** The United Nations Economic Commission for Africa (ECA) collects statistical data and advises African nations on development planning. Option A (AU) is a very tempting wrong answer because it is the supreme political body for Africa, but the specialized task of deep economic research and UN-level statistical data is the core mandate of the ECA.

**8. The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) was launched under the auspices of the**

- A. Economic Commission for Africa (ECA)
- B. African Union (AU)
- C. World Bank
- D. Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)

**Answer: B.** The African Union (AU) launched the AfCFTA to integrate all African economies into a single, continent-wide market. Option D (ECOWAS) is a common mistake because students know ECOWAS promotes free trade, but ECOWAS is restricted to West Africa, whereas AfCFTA covers the entire continent under the AU.

**9. Although Nigeria is not a member of the OECD, the organization is relevant to Nigeria because its guidelines**

- A. dictate the price of agricultural produce in Nigerian local markets
- B. help Nigeria regulate multinational tax avoidance through transfer pricing
- C. directly fund all local government infrastructural projects in Nigeria
- D. provide peacekeeping forces to secure Nigerian borders

**Answer: B.** The OECD sets global economic and tax standards. Nigeria adopts OECD guidelines on transfer pricing to ensure multinational corporations pay fair taxes. Option C is a tempting mistake because students associate international bodies with funding, but the OECD does not directly fund local Nigerian projects; it coordinates aid policies for its member states.

**10. In international economics, the IBRD is best known as the main lending arm of the**

- A. International Monetary Fund
- B. World Trade Organization
- C. World Bank Group
- D. African Development Bank

**Answer: C.** The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) is the founding and primary lending arm of the World Bank Group. Option A (IMF) is the most tempting wrong answer due to students confusing the twin Bretton Woods institutions, but the IBRD is strictly a World Bank entity for long-term development.
