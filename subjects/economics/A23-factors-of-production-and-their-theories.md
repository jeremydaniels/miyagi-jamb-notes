## Types, features and rewards of factors of production

**Objective i: identify the types, features and rewards of factors of production; Content a: Types, features and rewards**

Production is the process of creating goods and services to satisfy human wants. For production to take place, certain resources must be combined. These resources are called the **factors of production**.

In most modern textbooks there are four factors of production (some classical economists, such as Adam Smith and David Ricardo, listed only three: land, labour and capital): **Land, Labour, Capital, and the Entrepreneur (Enterprise)**. Each factor has unique features and receives a specific reward for its contribution to production.

### 1. Land
In Economics, "land" does not just mean the ground we walk on or build houses on. It refers to all **free gifts of nature** used in production. This includes agricultural soil, mineral resources (like crude oil in the Niger Delta, tin in Jos, limestone in Ogun State), forests, water bodies, and the climate.

**Features of Land:**
* **Free gift of nature:** It is not created by human effort.
* **Fixed in supply:** The total landmass of the earth or a country cannot be increased. Therefore, its supply is **perfectly inelastic**.
* **Geographically immobile:** You cannot move a piece of land from Kano to Lagos.
* **Subject to diminishing returns:** If you keep adding more workers or fertilizers to a fixed piece of land, the extra output (marginal product) will eventually start to fall.
* **Heterogeneous:** No two pieces of land are exactly the same in fertility, topography, or mineral content.

**Reward:** The reward for land is **Rent**.

### 2. Labour
Labour is all human effort—both mental and physical—directed towards the production of goods and services. A factory worker uses mainly physical labour, while an accountant or a teacher uses mainly mental labour.

**Features of Labour:**
* **Human element:** Labour is provided by human beings who have feelings, form trade unions, and can go on strike.
* **Mobile:** Labour is mobile. A worker can move from one location to another (geographical mobility) or from one profession to another (occupational mobility).
* **Cannot be stored:** If a worker misses a day of work, the labour for that day is lost forever.
* **Variable in quality:** Workers differ in skills, education, and efficiency.
* **Active factor of production:** Without labour, land and capital cannot produce anything by themselves.

**Reward:** The reward for labour is **Wages** (or salaries). 

### 3. Capital
Capital refers to all **man-made wealth** used to produce more wealth. Unlike land, which is a free gift of nature, capital is created by human beings. It includes machinery, factory buildings, tools, raw materials, and money. 

**Types of Capital:**
* **Fixed Capital:** Assets that are used continuously over a long time and do not change their form, e.g., buildings, tractors, heavy machinery.
* **Circulating (Working) Capital:** Items used up in the daily process of production, e.g., raw materials (like cocoa beans for making chocolate), fuel, and cash.
* **Social Capital (Infrastructure):** Facilities provided by the government that aid production, e.g., roads, electricity, and water supply.

**Features of Capital:**
* **Man-made:** It is produced through human effort and savings.
* **Depreciates:** Capital goods wear out over time (wear and tear).
* **Mobile:** Many forms of capital can be moved from place to place (e.g., vehicles, money).
* **Subject to creation and destruction:** Capital can be accumulated through savings or destroyed (e.g., a factory burning down).

**Reward:** The reward for capital is **Interest**.

### 4. The Entrepreneur (Enterprise)
The entrepreneur is the individual or group of individuals who organises, coordinates, and combines the other three factors of production. 

**Features of the Entrepreneur:**
* **Risk-taker:** They bear the financial risks of the business. If the business fails, they lose their investment.
* **Decision-maker:** They decide *what* to produce, *how* to produce, and *where* to produce.
* **Innovator:** They introduce new products, new technologies, or new ways of organizing business (innovation).
* **Controls the other factors:** The entrepreneur employs labour, hires land, and borrows capital.

**Reward:** The reward for the entrepreneur is **Profit** (or loss, if the business fails).

*Textbook variation:* Some modern textbooks group the entrepreneur as a special highly-skilled form of labour. However, for JAMB UTME, you must treat the Entrepreneur as a distinct, fourth factor of production.

| Factor of Production | Meaning | Reward | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Land** | Free gifts of nature | Rent | Fixed in supply (perfectly inelastic) |
| **Labour** | Human physical and mental effort | Wages / Salaries | Active factor; cannot be stored |
| **Capital** | Man-made wealth used for production | Interest | Depreciates over time |
| **Entrepreneur** | Organiser and risk-taker | Profit | Coordinates other factors |

## Determination of wages, interest and profits

**Objective ii: analyse the determination of wages, interest and profits; Content b: Determination of wages, interest and profits**

In a free market economy, the rewards for the factors of production are essentially prices. Like all prices, they are heavily influenced by the forces of demand and supply. 

### 1. Determination of Wages
Wages are the price of labour. The general level of wages in a market is determined by:

* **Demand and Supply of Labour (Market Forces):** The demand for labour is a **derived demand**; employers demand labour not for its own sake, but for the goods it can produce. If the demand for a product is high, the demand for workers to make it will be high, pushing wages up. Conversely, if there is an oversupply of workers (e.g., unskilled school leavers), wages will fall. The equilibrium wage is where the supply of labour equals the demand for labour.
* **Trade Unions (Collective Bargaining):** Organizations of workers (like the Nigeria Labour Congress - NLC, or Trade Union Congress - TUC) negotiate with employers for higher wages and better conditions. A strong union can push wages above the free-market equilibrium.
* **Government Policy (Minimum Wage):** The government can pass laws setting a price floor for labour. For example, setting the National Minimum Wage to ₦30,000 or ₦70,000 means no employer is legally allowed to pay less than that amount. 
* **Productivity and Skills:** Highly skilled workers (like neurosurgeons) receive higher wages than unskilled workers because their supply is scarce and their marginal productivity is high. This causes **wage differentials**.

### 2. Determination of Interest
Interest is the price paid for the use of borrowed capital (loanable funds). The interest rate is determined by:

* **Demand and Supply of Loanable Funds (Classical Theory):** The supply of funds comes from people's **savings**. The higher the interest rate, the more people are willing to save (supply slopes upward). The demand for funds comes from businesses wanting to **invest** in capital goods. The higher the interest rate, the less businesses want to borrow (demand slopes downward). The equilibrium interest rate is set where Savings equals Investment ($S = I$).
* **Time Preference:** People generally prefer to have money now rather than in the future. To convince someone to delay consumption and lend their money, they must be compensated with interest.
* **Risk:** The higher the risk that the borrower might not pay back (default), the higher the interest rate the lender will charge.
* **Government and Central Bank Policy:** In Nigeria, the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) heavily influences interest rates by setting the Monetary Policy Rate (MPR), which dictates the cost of borrowing for commercial banks.

### 3. Determination of Profits
Profit is the residual income left over for the entrepreneur after all other factors of production have been paid. 
$$\text{Profit} = \text{Total Revenue (TR)} - \text{Total Cost (TC)}$$

Profit is determined and justified by:
* **Risk-bearing:** Business is uncertain. Profit is the compensation the entrepreneur receives for taking the risk that consumers might not buy the product. 
* **Innovation:** According to economist Joseph Schumpeter, profit is the reward for successful innovation (introducing a new product, a cheaper production method, or finding a new market). 
* **Market Structure:** A firm in perfect competition may only make normal profit in the long run. A firm with a monopoly can restrict output, raise prices, and earn abnormal (supernormal) profit permanently.

## Marginal productivity and liquidity preference theories

**Objective iii: interpret the marginal productivity of liquidity preference theories; Content c: Theories: marginal productivity theory of wages and liquidity preference theory**

*(Remember the JAMB syllabus typo: these are two distinct theories, not one.)*

### 1. The Marginal Productivity Theory of Wages
Developed by neoclassical economists (like John Bates Clark), this theory explains how the demand for labour and the wage rate are determined in a competitive market.

**The core idea:** An employer will keep hiring workers up to the point where the extra revenue brought in by the last worker exactly equals the wage paid to that worker.

To understand this, we need two terms:
* **Marginal Physical Product (MPP):** The extra physical output produced by adding one more worker.
* **Marginal Revenue Product (MRP):** The extra revenue a firm gets by selling the extra output produced by one more worker. 
  $$\text{MRP} = \text{MPP} \times \text{Price (or Marginal Revenue)}$$

**How it determines wages:**
As a firm hires more workers, the law of diminishing returns sets in, meaning each additional worker adds less to total output than the previous one. Therefore, the MRP curve slopes downwards.
To maximize profit, the firm follows this rule: **Hire workers until Wage (W) = Marginal Revenue Product (MRP).**
* If MRP > Wage, the worker brings in more money than they cost. The firm should hire more workers.
* If MRP < Wage, the worker costs more than they bring in. The firm should reduce its workforce.
* Equilibrium is reached at **$W = MRP$**.

**Assumptions and Limitations of the Theory:**
* It assumes **perfect competition** in both the product and labour markets.
* It assumes all workers are **homogeneous** (identical in skills and effort), which is false in reality.
* It assumes perfect mobility of labour.
* In reality, trade unions and government minimum wage laws disrupt this pure market theory.

### 2. The Liquidity Preference Theory of Interest
Developed by the famous economist John Maynard Keynes in 1936, this theory explains how the rate of interest is determined by the demand for and supply of money.

**"Liquidity preference"** simply means the desire of people to hold their wealth in liquid cash rather than investing it in illiquid assets like bonds or property. According to Keynes, **interest is the reward for parting with liquidity.** If you lock your money away in a bond, you give up liquidity, so you must be paid interest.

Keynes identified **three motives** for demanding money (holding cash):
1. **The Transactions Motive:** People and firms hold cash for everyday purchases (buying food, paying transport fares). This depends on the level of income. Higher income = higher transaction demand.
2. **The Precautionary Motive:** People hold cash for unforeseen emergencies (sickness, unexpected car repairs). This also depends mainly on income.
3. **The Speculative Motive:** People hold cash to take advantage of future changes in the prices of financial assets (like bonds). **This motive depends inversely on the rate of interest.**
   * If interest rates are currently high, people expect them to fall (and bond prices to rise), so they use their cash to buy bonds. Speculative demand for money is low.
   * If interest rates are low, people expect them to rise, so they hold onto cash. Speculative demand for money is high.

**Determination of Interest:**
The total demand for money ($M_d$) is the sum of the transaction, precautionary, and speculative demands. This demand curve slopes downwards relative to the interest rate. 
The total supply of money ($M_s$) is fixed by the Central Bank (e.g., the CBN in Nigeria) and is represented by a vertical line. 
The equilibrium rate of interest is determined exactly where the Money Demand curve intersects the Money Supply curve ($M_d = M_s$).

## Factor mobility and efficiency

**Objective iv: examine factors mobility and efficiency; Content d: Factor mobility and efficiency**

### 1. Factor Mobility
Factor mobility is the ease with which a factor of production can be moved from one place to another, or from one use (occupation) to another. There are two main types:
* **Geographical Mobility:** The ease of moving a factor from one location to another (e.g., moving a worker from Enugu to Kaduna).
* **Occupational Mobility:** The ease of moving a factor from one job or industry to another (e.g., a teacher becoming a banker, or a piece of land changing from a cocoa farm to a housing estate).

**Mobility of specific factors:**
* **Land:** Completely **immobile geographically** (you cannot move land). However, it is **occupationally mobile** (a plot of land can be used for farming, then cleared and used to build a factory).
* **Labour:** Highly mobile both geographically and occupationally, but limited by family ties, language barriers, cost of relocation, trade union restrictions, and the need to acquire new skills.
* **Capital:** Varies widely. Liquid capital (money) is highly mobile everywhere. Fixed capital like a power plant is geographically immobile. Some capital is occupationally mobile (a delivery van can carry bread or spare parts), but highly specialized machinery (like an oil rig) is occupationally immobile.
* **Entrepreneur:** Highly mobile both geographically and occupationally.

### 2. Factor Efficiency
Factor efficiency refers to the ability of a factor of production to produce the maximum possible output from a given amount of input, without wasting resources. It is often measured as **productivity** (output per unit of input).

**Ways to improve the efficiency of Labour:**
1. **Education and Training:** Providing vocational training and higher education improves skills.
2. **Division of Labour and Specialization:** Breaking a task into smaller parts allows workers to become experts at their specific tasks, saving time and increasing output.
3. **Improved Working Conditions:** Good ventilation, safety equipment, and a healthy environment boost morale and energy.
4. **Adequate Compensation:** Fair wages, bonuses, and welfare packages motivate workers to put in their best.
5. **Provision of better Capital/Technology:** A worker using a modern computer is vastly more efficient than one using a manual typewriter.
6. **Good Health:** Access to quality healthcare means fewer sick days and stronger workers.

## Types and causes of unemployment in Nigeria

**Objective v: examine the types and causes of unemployment in Nigeria; Content e: Unemployment and its solutions (Types and Causes)**

**Unemployment** occurs when people within the labor force (generally accepted as ages 15 to 64) who are physically able, mentally willing, and actively seeking work at the current wage rate cannot find jobs. 

### Types of Unemployment
1. **Frictional Unemployment:** Temporary unemployment that occurs when people are moving between jobs or searching for their first job after graduation. It is a normal part of a dynamic economy.
2. **Structural Unemployment:** Occurs when there is a permanent change in the structure of the economy. It happens when workers' skills no longer match the jobs available, or when an entire industry declines (e.g., typists losing jobs due to the invention of computers).
3. **Cyclical (Keynesian or Deficient-Demand) Unemployment:** Caused by a general downturn in the economy, such as a recession. Consumers stop buying, so businesses cut back on production and lay off workers.
4. **Seasonal Unemployment:** Occurs in industries where demand or production is tied to a specific season. Common in Nigerian agriculture (farmers idle during the dry season) and construction.
5. **Disguised Unemployment (Underemployment):** A situation where more people are employed in a task than are actually needed. If some workers are removed, total output will not fall. The **marginal physical product of these extra workers is zero**. This is very common in Nigerian agriculture and the civil service.
6. **Voluntary Unemployment:** Occurs when people choose not to work because they are unwilling to accept the current market wage rate or are relying on other sources of income.
7. **Technological Unemployment:** Caused by the introduction of machinery or automation that replaces human labour (e.g., banking apps reducing the need for bank tellers).

| Type of Unemployment | Main Cause | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Frictional** | Transition time between jobs | A graduate looking for a first job |
| **Structural** | Skills mismatch / dying industry | A coal miner whose mine shut down permanently |
| **Cyclical** | Economic recession | Widespread layoffs during an economic crash |
| **Disguised** | Too many workers for one task | 5 people working a farm that only needs 2 |

### Causes of Unemployment in Nigeria
* **Defective Educational System:** Much of Nigeria's education is highly theoretical. Many graduates lack practical, technical, or vocational skills (skills mismatch), making them unemployable.
* **Poor Infrastructure:** Erratic electricity supply, bad roads, and high cost of transportation force many businesses to close down or relocate to other countries, leading to job losses.
* **Rural-Urban Migration:** The massive movement of youths from rural areas to cities (like Lagos and Abuja) causes severe overcrowding and urban unemployment, while leaving rural farmlands uncultivated.
* **Rapid Population Growth:** The population is growing faster than the economy can create jobs.
* **Lack of Capital and Credit:** High interest rates and tough collateral requirements make it hard for young entrepreneurs to borrow money to start small and medium enterprises (SMEs) that would create jobs.
* **Over-reliance on the Oil Sector:** Crude oil generates a lot of revenue for Nigeria but is highly capital-intensive and employs very few people compared to manufacturing or agriculture.

## Solutions to unemployment in Nigeria

**Objective vi: suggest solutions to unemployment in Nigeria; Content e: Unemployment and its solutions**

Solving unemployment requires a mix of government policy, educational reform, and private-sector support. 

1. **Reforming the Educational System:** The curriculum should emphasize Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM), as well as Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET). This will create graduates who are job creators rather than just job seekers.
2. **Infrastructural Development:** The government must invest heavily in power generation, roads, and security. A stable power supply will drastically lower the cost of doing business, encouraging factories to open and hire people.
3. **Agricultural Development:** Making agriculture attractive through mechanization, provision of high-yield seeds, and rural amenities will keep youths in rural areas and provide massive employment. Specialized banks like the Bank of Agriculture (BOA) should provide cheap credit to farmers.
4. **Government Intervention Programmes:** The government should actively create schemes to equip youths. A notable example in Nigeria is the **National Directorate of Employment (NDE)**, established in 1986 to provide skills acquisition and public works jobs. More recent examples include the N-Power scheme and various youth enterprise funds.
5. **Encouraging Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs):** Providing access to low-interest loans (microcredit) and tax holidays for start-ups will help small businesses grow and employ more labor.
6. **Population Control:** Educating citizens on family planning to reduce the high birth rate will ensure the labour force does not grow faster than job creation.
7. **Diversification of the Economy:** The government must diversify the economy away from crude oil and invest heavily in labour-intensive sectors like manufacturing, solid minerals, and tourism.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Common mistake:** Confusing *frictional* and *structural* unemployment. Remember: Frictional is temporary (just searching). Structural is permanent and severe (your skill is no longer needed in the economy).
* **Common mistake:** Thinking "land" only means soil. In Economics, land includes rivers, forests, sunlight, and crude oil. It is perfectly inelastic in supply.
* **Marginal Productivity Theory rule:** A firm maximizes profit when it hires labour up to the point where **Wage = Marginal Revenue Product (MRP)**.
* **Liquidity Preference Motives:** Memorize Keynes' three motives for holding money: Transaction, Precautionary, and Speculative. The *Speculative* motive is the only one that depends on the interest rate (inversely).
* **Factor Mobility:** Remember that land is completely *geographically* immobile, but it is *occupationally* mobile.
* **Disguised unemployment** is very popular in JAMB questions. Look for keywords like "marginal product is zero", "too many workers on a farm", or "underemployment".

## Quick summary

1. The four factors of production are Land (reward: rent), Labour (reward: wages), Capital (reward: interest), and Entrepreneur (reward: profit).
2. Land is a free gift of nature and perfectly inelastic in supply. Labour is the active human element. Capital is man-made wealth used to produce more wealth. The Entrepreneur organizes the others and bears the risk.
3. Wages are determined by demand and supply, trade unions, and government policy. Interest is determined by the supply of savings and the demand for investment. Profit is the reward for risk and innovation.
4. The Marginal Productivity Theory of Wages states that employers will hire workers until the Wage equals the Marginal Revenue Product ($W = MRP$).
5. The Liquidity Preference Theory of Interest (by Keynes) states that interest is the reward for giving up cash. People demand money for transaction, precautionary, and speculative motives.
6. Factor mobility can be geographical or occupational. Efficiency (productivity) can be improved through education, training, division of labour, and better technology.
7. Major types of unemployment include frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal, and disguised. In Nigeria, causes include poor education, infrastructural decay, and rural-urban migration, which can be solved through TVET, better infrastructure, and agricultural development.

## Practice questions

**1. The reward to the entrepreneur for organizing production and bearing risks is:**
- A. Rent
- B. Interest
- C. Profit
- D. Wages

**Answer: C.** Profit is the residual reward that goes to the entrepreneur for taking risks and organizing the other three factors of production. Rent is for land, interest for capital, and wages for labour.

**2. Which of the following features is unique to Land as a factor of production?**
- A. It depreciates rapidly over time
- B. Its supply is perfectly inelastic
- C. It is highly geographically mobile
- D. It is a man-made resource

**Answer: B.** The total amount of land on earth or within a country cannot be increased by human effort, making its supply perfectly inelastic. It is a free gift of nature, not man-made, and is geographically immobile.

**3. According to the Marginal Productivity Theory of Wages, a profit-maximizing firm will continue to employ labour up to the point where:**
- A. Wage is equal to Marginal Physical Product
- B. Wage is greater than Marginal Revenue Product
- C. Wage is equal to Marginal Revenue Product
- D. Wage is equal to Total Revenue

**Answer: C.** The firm maximizes profit by hiring workers until the cost of the last worker (Wage) exactly equals the revenue that worker brings in (Marginal Revenue Product, or MRP).

**4. A university graduate who has been actively looking for a job for three months since completing his youth service is experiencing:**
- A. Structural unemployment
- B. Cyclical unemployment
- C. Disguised unemployment
- D. Frictional unemployment

**Answer: D.** Frictional unemployment is the temporary period of joblessness experienced by people searching for their first job or moving between jobs. 

**5. According to Keynes' Liquidity Preference Theory, which motive for holding money is inversely related to the rate of interest?**
- A. Transaction motive
- B. Precautionary motive
- C. Speculative motive
- D. Profit motive

**Answer: C.** The speculative motive is the desire to hold cash to take advantage of future changes in asset prices (like bonds). When interest rates are high, people hold less cash and buy bonds, showing an inverse relationship. Transaction and precautionary motives depend on income, not interest rates.

**6. A piece of land used previously for cultivating cassava is cleared and used to build a shopping mall. This demonstrates that land is:**
- A. Geographically mobile
- B. Occupationally mobile
- C. Perfectly elastic in supply
- D. Not subject to diminishing returns

**Answer: B.** Land is occupationally mobile because it can be transferred from one use (farming) to another (building). It remains geographically immobile because the physical location did not change.

**7. In an agricultural community, ten people are working on a small family farm that can be effectively managed by four people. This situation is best described as:**
- A. Seasonal unemployment
- B. Voluntary unemployment
- C. Disguised unemployment
- D. Structural unemployment

**Answer: C.** Disguised unemployment (or underemployment) occurs when more people are doing a job than necessary. The extra workers add nothing to total output, meaning their marginal physical product is zero.

**8. Which of the following is a direct government intervention to solve the problem of unemployment in Nigeria?**
- A. Increasing the Monetary Policy Rate
- B. Establishment of the National Directorate of Employment (NDE)
- C. Privatization of public enterprises
- D. Increasing taxes on infant industries

**Answer: B.** The NDE was specifically established by the Nigerian government to provide skills acquisition and generate employment. Increasing interest rates or taxes would generally discourage business and increase unemployment.

**9. The interest rate in a free market economy is primarily determined by the:**
- A. Negotiation between trade unions and employers
- B. Interaction between the demand for and supply of loanable funds
- C. Total revenue of firms divided by their marginal cost
- D. Geographical mobility of fixed capital

**Answer: B.** According to classical economic theory, the interest rate is determined by the intersection of the supply of capital (savings) and the demand for capital (investment/borrowing).

**10. Structural unemployment is most likely to be caused by:**
- A. A temporary drop in consumer demand during a recession
- B. Workers taking time to find a new job after resigning
- C. A permanent change in technology that makes certain skills obsolete
- D. The dry season preventing farmers from planting crops

**Answer: C.** Structural unemployment is caused by fundamental changes in the economy, such as technological advancement making old skills (like manual typing) obsolete, creating a mismatch between available jobs and workers' skills. Option A is cyclical, B is frictional, and D is seasonal.
