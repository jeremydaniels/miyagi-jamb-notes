## The Earth in the Solar System, Rotation and Revolution

**Content item i: The earth in the solar system, rotation and revolution.**

The universe is made up of billions of galaxies. Our galaxy is called the **Milky Way**, and within it is our **Solar System**. The solar system consists of the Sun at the centre and the celestial bodies that revolve around it. These bodies include planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets. 

The Sun is a star, which means it produces its own light and heat. Planets do not produce their own light; they only reflect the light they receive from the Sun. 

### Relative Positions of the Planets in the Solar System

**Objective i: identify the relative positions of the planets in the solar system.**

There are currently **eight recognised planets** in our solar system. In order of their distance from the Sun, starting from the closest to the furthest, they are:

1. **Mercury**
2. **Venus**
3. **Earth** (our planet, the third from the Sun)
4. **Mars**
5. **Jupiter** (the largest planet)
6. **Saturn** (famous for its rings)
7. **Uranus**
8. **Neptune**

**Note:** Older textbooks might list nine planets, including Pluto. However, in 2006, the International Astronomical Union (IAU) reclassified Pluto as a "dwarf planet." For JAMB, remember that Earth is the **third planet** from the Sun and the fifth largest planet overall.

To easily remember the order of the planets, Nigerian students often use the mnemonic:
*"**M**y **V**ery **E**ducated **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**oodles"*
(Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune).

### Effects of the Rotation and Revolution of the Earth

**Objective ii: understand the effects of the rotation and revolution of the earth.**

The Earth experiences two major types of movement in space: **rotation** and **revolution**. It is very important not to confuse them, as JAMB often tests the differences between their effects.

#### 1. Earth's Rotation
**Rotation** is the spinning of the Earth on its axis. 
- **Direction:** The Earth rotates from **West to East** (anti-clockwise when viewed from the North Pole).
- **Time taken:** It takes exactly **24 hours** (one day) to complete one rotation.
- **Axis:** The Earth's axis is an imaginary line connecting the North and South Poles. It is tilted at an angle of $23\frac{1}{2}\text{°}$ to the vertical.

**Effects of Rotation:**
1. **Day and Night:** As the Earth spins, only the half facing the Sun experiences daylight. The half turned away from the Sun is in darkness.
2. **Time Difference:** Because the Earth turns West to East, places in the East see the sun first and their local time is ahead of places in the West. The Earth rotates $360\text{°}$ in 24 hours, meaning it turns $15\text{°}$ every hour.
3. **Apparent Movement of the Sun and Stars:** Because the Earth rotates from West to East, the Sun, Moon, and stars appear to rise in the East and set in the West.
4. **Deflection of Winds and Ocean Currents (Coriolis Force):** Rotation creates a force that deflects free-moving bodies (like winds and water). They are deflected to their **right** in the Northern Hemisphere and to their **left** in the Southern Hemisphere.
5. **Daily Rise and Fall of Tides:** The rotation of the Earth, combined with the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun, causes ocean tides twice a day.

#### 2. Earth's Revolution
**Revolution** is the movement of the Earth around the Sun on a fixed path called an **orbit**.
- **Orbit shape:** The orbit is elliptical (oval-shaped), not a perfect circle.
- **Time taken:** It takes **$365\frac{1}{4}\text{ days}$** (one year) for the Earth to complete one revolution. We group the $\frac{1}{4}$ days over four years to add one extra day to February, creating a **leap year** of 366 days.

**Effects of Revolution:**
1. **The Seasons:** The revolution of the tilted Earth causes the four distinct seasons: Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter. (In Nigeria and West Africa, we generally experience wet and dry seasons, but astronomically, the four seasons still occur globally).
2. **Varying Lengths of Day and Night:** Because of the Earth's tilted axis, as it revolves, the Northern and Southern Hemispheres alternately tilt towards or away from the Sun. During summer in the Northern Hemisphere, days are longer than nights. At the Equator, day and night are always 12 hours each.
3. **Changes in the Altitude of the Midday Sun:** The Sun's apparent position overhead shifts throughout the year between the Tropic of Cancer ($23\frac{1}{2}\text{°N}$) and the Tropic of Capricorn ($23\frac{1}{2}\text{°S}$).
4. **Eclipses:** The revolution of the Earth and the Moon leads to their alignment with the Sun, causing solar eclipses (Moon between Earth and Sun) and lunar eclipses (Earth between Sun and Moon).

**Key dates associated with Revolution:**
- **Equinoxes (March 21 and September 23):** The midday sun is directly overhead at the Equator. Day and night are exactly equal everywhere on Earth.
- **Summer Solstice (June 21):** The midday sun is directly overhead at the Tropic of Cancer. It is the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere.
- **Winter Solstice (December 22):** The midday sun is directly overhead at the Tropic of Capricorn. It is the shortest day (and longest night) in the Northern Hemisphere.

## The Shape and Size of the Earth

**Content item ii: The shape and size of the earth.**

People once believed the Earth was completely flat like a table. We now know that the Earth is spherical, but it is **not a perfect sphere**. 

Because the Earth rotates rapidly, it bulges slightly at the Equator and is flattened at the North and South Poles. This unique shape is called an **oblate spheroid** or a **geoid** (meaning "Earth-shaped").

### The Size of the Earth
The fact that the Earth is not perfectly round is proven by its measurements:
- **Equatorial Diameter:** $\approx 12,756\text{ km}$
- **Polar Diameter:** $\approx 12,714\text{ km}$
- **Equatorial Circumference:** $\approx 40,075\text{ km}$
- **Polar Circumference:** $\approx 40,008\text{ km}$

Notice that the measurements around the Equator are slightly larger than those around the poles. 

### Proofs of the Earth's Shape and Size

**Objective iii: provide proof for the shape and size of the earth.**

JAMB expects you to know several distinct proofs that the Earth is spherical (an oblate spheroid).

1. **Circumnavigation of the Earth:**
   If you travel continuously in one straight direction (for example, by aeroplane or ship) without turning back, you will eventually arrive at your starting point. The explorer Ferdinand Magellan first proved this by sailing around the world between 1519 and 1522. If the Earth were flat, a person would fall off the edge.
2. **The Ship's Visibility:**
   When a ship approaches a port, an observer on the shore sees the top of the ship's mast first, then the funnels, and finally the hull (the bottom body). If the Earth were flat, the entire ship would appear at once, just getting larger as it gets closer.
3. **Sunrise and Sunset:**
   The Sun rises and sets at different times in different places around the world. Places in the East experience sunrise before places in the West. If the Earth were flat, the whole world would experience sunrise at exactly the same moment.
4. **The Lunar Eclipse:**
   During a lunar eclipse, the Earth casts a shadow on the Moon. This shadow always appears as a **circular arc**. Only a spherical body can cast a circular shadow in all positions. 
5. **Observation of Other Planetary Bodies:**
   When observed through a telescope, the Sun, the Moon, and all other planets (like Mars and Jupiter) are completely spherical. Since Earth is a planet in the same solar system, it is logical that it is also spherical.
6. **Aerial and Satellite Photographs:**
   Pictures taken from high-altitude rockets, satellites, and the International Space Station provide the most direct and convincing visual proof that the Earth is spherical.
7. **The Bedford Level Experiment:**
   In 1870, three poles of exactly the same length were driven into the bed of a perfectly straight, flat canal in England at equal intervals. When viewed through a telescope from one end, the middle pole appeared slightly higher than the other two. This happens because the Earth's surface curves.

## Latitudes and Distances, Longitudes and Time

**Content item iii: Latitudes and distances, longitudes and time.**

To locate places accurately on the Earth's surface, geographers use a grid system made up of imaginary lines drawn on maps and globes. These lines are called **latitudes** and **longitudes**.

### Differences Between Latitudes and Longitudes

**Objective iv: differentiate between latitudes and longitudes.**

It is easy to mix these two up. 
- **Lines of Latitude** are horizontal lines that run from West to East but measure distance North and South of the Equator. 
- **Lines of Longitude** (also called **meridians**) are vertical lines that run from North to South but measure distance East and West of the Prime Meridian.

Here is a clear comparison to help you differentiate them:

| Feature | Lines of Latitude | Lines of Longitude (Meridians) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Direction drawn** | Drawn from West to East. | Drawn from North to South (connecting poles). |
| **Measurement** | Measure distance North (N) or South (S) of the Equator. | Measure distance East (E) or West (W) of the Prime Meridian. |
| **Reference line** | **The Equator** ($0\text{°}$). | **The Prime Meridian** or Greenwich Meridian ($0\text{°}$). |
| **Parallelism** | They are parallel to one another. They never meet. | They are not parallel. They converge (meet) at the North and South Poles. |
| **Length** | They vary in length. The Equator is the longest; they get shorter towards the poles. | They are all of equal length. |
| **Great Circles** | Only the Equator is a Great Circle; all others are Small Circles. | Every line of longitude forms half of a Great Circle. |
| **Main Uses** | Calculating distance, locating places, marking climate zones. | Calculating local time, locating places. |

**Important Lines of Latitude:**
- Equator ($0\text{°}$)
- Tropic of Cancer ($23\frac{1}{2}\text{°N}$)
- Tropic of Capricorn ($23\frac{1}{2}\text{°S}$)
- Arctic Circle ($66\frac{1}{2}\text{°N}$)
- Antarctic Circle ($66\frac{1}{2}\text{°S}$)

**Important Lines of Longitude:**
- Prime Meridian / Greenwich Meridian ($0\text{°}$)
- International Date Line ($180\text{°}$ East/West)

### Relating Lines of Latitude to Calculation of Distance

**Objective v: relate lines of latitude to calculation of distance.**

Because lines of latitude are parallel, the distance between them is roughly constant. The Earth's circumference is about $40,000\text{ km}$, and a full circle is $360\text{°}$.
Therefore, $40,000\text{ km} \div 360\text{°} \approx 111\text{ km}$.
**For JAMB calculations, $1\text{°}$ of latitude represents approximately $111\text{ km}$ on the Earth's surface.**

**General Steps for Calculating Distance Using Latitude:**
**Step 1:** Find the latitudinal difference ($\theta$) between the two places.
- If they are in the **same hemisphere** (both North or both South), **subtract** the smaller latitude from the larger one.
- If they are in **different hemispheres** (one North and one South), **add** the two latitudes together.
**Step 2:** Multiply the difference ($\theta$) by $111\text{ km}$.
**Formula:** $Distance = \theta \times 111\text{ km}$.

#### Worked Example 1: Same Hemisphere
*Find the approximate distance between Town A ($15\text{°N}$) and Town B ($35\text{°N}$) if they lie on the same longitude.*
**Step 1:** Note the hemispheres. Both are in the North (same hemisphere).
**Step 2:** Find the latitudinal difference ($\theta$) by subtracting.
$\theta = 35\text{°} - 15\text{°} = 20\text{°}$.
**Step 3:** Multiply the difference by $111\text{ km}$.
$Distance = 20 \times 111\text{ km} = 2,220\text{ km}$.
**Answer:** The distance between Town A and Town B is $2,220\text{ km}$.

#### Worked Example 2: Different Hemispheres
*Calculate the distance between City X ($40\text{°N}$) and City Y ($10\text{°S}$) on the same meridian.*
**Step 1:** Note the hemispheres. One is North, one is South (different hemispheres).
**Step 2:** Find the latitudinal difference ($\theta$) by adding.
$\theta = 40\text{°} + 10\text{°} = 50\text{°}$.
**Step 3:** Multiply the difference by $111\text{ km}$.
$Distance = 50 \times 111\text{ km} = 5,550\text{ km}$.
**Answer:** The distance between City X and City Y is $5,550\text{ km}$.

### Relating Lines of Longitude to Calculation of Time

**Objective vi: relate lines of longitude to calculation of time.**

The Earth rotates $360\text{°}$ in exactly 24 hours. This relationship helps us calculate local time anywhere in the world.
- $360\text{°} = 24\text{ hours}$
- $15\text{°} = 1\text{ hour}$ ($360 \div 24$)
- $1\text{°} = 4\text{ minutes}$ ($60\text{ minutes} \div 15$)

**Important Rule: East Gains, West Loses**
Because the Earth rotates from West to East, places to the East see the Sun first. Therefore:
- If a place is **East** of the known time, its time is **ahead** (you **add** the time difference).
- If a place is **West** of the known time, its time is **behind** (you **subtract** the time difference).
- A useful memory aid is **EGA (East Gain Add)** and **WLS (West Lose Subtract)**.

**General Steps for Calculating Time:**
**Step 1:** Find the longitudinal difference ($\theta$) between the two places.
- Same hemisphere (both East or both West) $\rightarrow$ **subtract**.
- Different hemispheres (one East, one West) $\rightarrow$ **add**.
*(If one place is on the Prime Meridian, $0\text{°}$, just take the value of the other place).*
**Step 2:** Convert the longitudinal difference to time.
- Divide the difference by $15\text{°}$ to get hours. 
- (If there is a remainder, multiply it by $4\text{ minutes}$).
**Step 3:** Determine if the unknown place is East (ahead) or West (behind) of the known place, and **add** or **subtract** the time calculated in Step 2.

#### Worked Example 3: Finding Local Time
*What is the local time in Town A ($60\text{°E}$) when it is 12:00 Noon in Town B ($15\text{°W}$)?*
**Step 1:** Note the hemispheres. Town A is East, Town B is West (different hemispheres).
Find the longitudinal difference: $\theta = 60\text{°} + 15\text{°} = 75\text{°}$.
**Step 2:** Convert difference to time. 
$\text{Time difference} = \frac{75\text{°}}{15\text{°}} = 5\text{ hours}$.
**Step 3:** Determine direction. You are looking for the time in Town A ($60\text{°E}$). Compared to Town B ($15\text{°W}$), Town A is further **East**. Therefore, Town A is ahead.
**Step 4:** Add the time difference.
$12:00\text{ Noon} + 5\text{ hours} = 5:00\text{ PM}$.
**Answer:** The local time in Town A is $5:00\text{ PM}$.

#### Worked Example 4: Finding Longitude from Time
*The time at Town Y is $8:00\text{ AM}$ when Greenwich Mean Time (GMT, $0\text{°}$) is $11:00\text{ AM}$. Find the longitude of Town Y.*
**Step 1:** Find the time difference between the two places.
From $8:00\text{ AM}$ to $11:00\text{ AM}$ is a difference of **3 hours**.
**Step 2:** Convert the time difference to degrees of longitude.
Since $1\text{ hour} = 15\text{°}$, then $3\text{ hours} = 3 \times 15\text{°} = 45\text{°}$.
**Step 3:** Determine the direction (East or West).
The time at Town Y ($8:00\text{ AM}$) is **behind** the time at Greenwich ($11:00\text{ AM}$). Since it is behind, it must be located in the **West**.
**Answer:** The longitude of Town Y is $45\text{°W}$.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Rotation** | The spinning of the Earth on its axis from West to East, completing a turn in 24 hours. |
| **Revolution** | The movement of the Earth around the Sun in an elliptical orbit, taking $365\frac{1}{4}$ days. |
| **Geoid / Oblate spheroid** | The true shape of the Earth; slightly bulging at the Equator and flattened at the poles. |
| **Equator** | The $0\text{°}$ line of latitude that divides the Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. It is the only Great Circle of latitude. |
| **Prime Meridian** | The $0\text{°}$ line of longitude that passes through Greenwich, London; used as the starting point for time calculation. |
| **Latitude** | Imaginary lines running East-West on a map, used to measure distance North or South of the Equator. |
| **Longitude** | Imaginary lines running North-South, connecting the poles, used to measure distance East or West of the Prime Meridian. |
| **Coriolis Force** | A force caused by Earth's rotation that deflects moving objects (like winds and currents) to the right in the North and left in the South. |
| **Solstice** | The time of year when the midday sun is directly overhead at one of the Tropics (Summer or Winter). |
| **Equinox** | The time of year when the sun is directly overhead at the Equator, resulting in equal day and night worldwide. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Mixing up rotation and revolution:** This is the most common JAMB trap. Remember: **Rotation** causes Day/Night, time differences, and Coriolis force. **Revolution** causes the Seasons and changes in day/night lengths over the year.
- **Longitude vs. Latitude:** Longitude lines are long and vertical (North-South) and meet at the poles. Latitude lines are parallel (like rungs on a ladder).
- **Time Calculation Error 1:** Forgetting to add longitudes when places are in *different* hemispheres (East and West), or failing to subtract when they are in the *same* hemisphere.
- **Time Calculation Error 2:** Knowing the time difference but doing the wrong math. Always ask: "Is the place I want to find East or West of the given time?" If East, ADD the hours. If West, SUBTRACT.
- **Distance Calculation Units:** Always remember $1\text{°} = 111\text{ km}$. Be careful not to confuse latitude addition/subtraction rules with longitude rules; they operate on the same logic (same side = subtract, opposite sides = add) but are applied differently (distance vs. time).
- **Proofs of shape:** Only the **lunar eclipse** (Earth's shadow on the Moon) proves Earth's shape. A *solar eclipse* does not.

## Quick summary

1. The Earth is the third planet from the Sun in our Solar System and is shaped as an oblate spheroid (geoid).
2. Proofs of Earth's spherical shape include aerial photographs, circumnavigation, ship's visibility, sunrise/sunset times, and the circular shadow during a lunar eclipse.
3. Earth's rotation on its axis takes 24 hours and causes day and night, time differences, and the Coriolis deflection of winds.
4. Earth's revolution around the Sun takes $365\frac{1}{4}$ days and causes the four seasons, varying lengths of day and night, and eclipses.
5. Lines of latitude run East-West, are parallel, and are used to calculate distance ($1\text{°} \approx 111\text{ km}$).
6. Lines of longitude run North-South, converge at the poles, and are used to calculate local time ($15\text{°} = 1\text{ hour}$).
7. When calculating distance or time, add the degrees if the locations are in different hemispheres, and subtract if they are in the same hemisphere.

## Practice questions

**1. The Earth is located in the Solar System as the**
- A. second planet from the Sun
- B. third planet from the Sun
- C. largest planet in the galaxy
- D. planet closest to Jupiter

**Answer: B.** Earth is the third planet from the Sun, falling between Venus (second) and Mars (fourth).

**2. Which of the following is a direct effect of the revolution of the Earth?**
- A. The occurrence of day and night
- B. The deflection of planetary winds
- C. The variation in the length of day and night
- D. The daily rising and setting of the sun

**Answer: C.** The varying length of day and night throughout the year (and the seasons) is caused by the Earth's revolution. The other options are caused by Earth's daily rotation.

**3. If the local time at Town P ($60\text{°W}$) is $8:00\text{ AM}$, what is the local time at Town Q ($30\text{°E}$)?**
- A. $2:00\text{ AM}$
- B. $2:00\text{ PM}$
- C. $10:00\text{ AM}$
- D. $6:00\text{ AM}$

**Answer: B.** 
Step 1: Different hemispheres, so add longitudes: $60\text{°} + 30\text{°} = 90\text{°}$.
Step 2: Convert to hours: $90\text{°} \div 15\text{°} = 6\text{ hours}$.
Step 3: Town Q ($30\text{°E}$) is East of Town P, so its time is ahead.
Step 4: $8:00\text{ AM} + 6\text{ hours} = 2:00\text{ PM}$.
*Option A is incorrect because it mistakenly subtracted the 6 hours instead of adding.*

**4. The equatorial diameter of the Earth is slightly longer than the polar diameter. This fact is a major proof that the Earth is**
- A. a perfect sphere
- B. an oblate spheroid
- C. completely flat
- D. undergoing a lunar eclipse

**Answer: B.** The difference between the equatorial and polar measurements proves the Earth bulges at the Equator and is flattened at the poles, making it an oblate spheroid (geoid).

**5. Calculate the approximate distance between City X ($15\text{°N}$) and City Y ($25\text{°N}$) on the same meridian.**
- A. $4,440\text{ km}$
- B. $1,110\text{ km}$
- C. $4,000\text{ km}$
- D. $1,665\text{ km}$

**Answer: B.** 
Step 1: Same hemisphere, so subtract latitudes: $25\text{°} - 15\text{°} = 10\text{°}$.
Step 2: Multiply by $111\text{ km}$: $10 \times 111\text{ km} = 1,110\text{ km}$.
*Option A is incorrect because it mistakenly added the latitudes ($40 \times 111$) instead of subtracting them.*

**6. The time at Town X is $12:00\text{ Noon}$ when Greenwich Mean Time ($0\text{°}$) is $9:00\text{ AM}$. What is the longitude of Town X?**
- A. $45\text{°E}$
- B. $45\text{°W}$
- C. $30\text{°E}$
- D. $15\text{°W}$

**Answer: A.** 
Step 1: Time difference is from $9:00\text{ AM}$ to $12:00\text{ Noon} = 3\text{ hours}$.
Step 2: Convert to degrees: $3 \times 15\text{°} = 45\text{°}$.
Step 3: Town X is ahead in time ($12:00\text{ Noon}$ is later than $9:00\text{ AM}$), so it must be in the East. Longitude is $45\text{°E}$.
*Option B is incorrect because it places the town in the West, which would mean it is behind GMT.*

**7. Which of the following is true about lines of longitude?**
- A. They run horizontally from West to East.
- B. They converge and meet at the poles.
- C. The Equator is the longest line of longitude.
- D. They are strictly parallel to one another.

**Answer: B.** Lines of longitude (meridians) run North-South and meet at the North and South Poles. Options A, C, and D describe properties of latitudes.

**8. Which of these phenomena provides evidence that the Earth is spherical?**
- A. The occurrence of a solar eclipse
- B. The circular shape of Earth's shadow during a lunar eclipse
- C. The changing altitude of the midday sun
- D. The deflection of ocean currents

**Answer: B.** The shadow cast by the Earth on the Moon during a lunar eclipse is always a circular arc, proving the Earth is spherical. A solar eclipse (Option A) does not cast a visible shadow of the Earth.

**9. When the midday sun is directly overhead at the Equator, making day and night equal all over the world, it is called an**
- A. Eclipse
- B. Equinox
- C. Solstice
- D. Orbit

**Answer: B.** An equinox (which occurs around March 21 and September 23) is when the sun is directly above the Equator, giving the whole Earth 12 hours of day and 12 hours of night.

**10. What is the approximate distance from the Equator ($0\text{°}$) to Town Z located at $20\text{°S}$?**
- A. $1,110\text{ km}$
- B. $2,220\text{ km}$
- C. $4,000\text{ km}$
- D. $2,000\text{ km}$

**Answer: B.** 
Step 1: The latitudinal difference between $0\text{°}$ and $20\text{°S}$ is $20\text{°}$.
Step 2: Distance $= 20 \times 111\text{ km} = 2,220\text{ km}$.
*Option D is incorrect because it likely multiplied by $100$ instead of the geographical standard $111\text{ km}$.*
