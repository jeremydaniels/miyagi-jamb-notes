# JAMB Geography Notes: Environmental Hazards

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section II (Physical Geography), Topic K of the JAMB Geography syllabus: Environmental hazards. 

The syllabus covers the following content items:
- **i. Natural hazards** (droughts, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, flooding)
- **ii. Man-induced** (soil erosion, deforestation, pollution, flooding and desertification)
- **iii. Effects, prevention and control of hazards.**

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Identify the natural hazards and their causes.
2. Relate the human-induced hazards to their causes.
3. Locate the major areas where environmental hazards are common and their effects.
4. Recommend possible methods of prevention and control.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** The syllabus lists **flooding** twice—once under natural hazards and once under man-induced (human-induced) hazards. This is deliberate, as flooding can be caused purely by forces of nature or triggered by human activities. The detailed geological processes of earthquakes and volcanoes are covered in `B3-volcanism-and-earthquakes.md`.

## What are Environmental Hazards?

An **environmental hazard** is any extreme event or substance in the environment that poses a danger to human life, property, and the natural ecosystem. They can occur suddenly (like an earthquake) or develop slowly over time (like desertification). Hazards are broadly divided into two categories based on their root causes: **natural hazards** and **man-induced (human-induced) hazards**.

---

## Natural Environmental Hazards and Their Causes

**Objective i: identify the natural hazards and their causes.**

Natural hazards are destructive events caused by the physical forces of nature, without human intervention. 

### 1. Droughts
A drought is a prolonged period of abnormally low rainfall leading to severe water shortages. 
- **Causes:** Droughts are caused by natural shifts in global weather patterns and climate systems. For example, if the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) fails to move far enough north during the West African rainy season, the northern regions receive little to no rain. Changes in ocean currents, such as the **El Niño** phenomenon, also disrupt rainfall patterns globally, leading to prolonged dry seasons.

### 2. Earthquakes
An earthquake is a sudden, violent shaking of the earth's crust.
- **Causes:** The earth's crust is broken into massive tectonic plates that are constantly moving. Earthquakes are caused when these plates collide (convergent boundary), pull apart (divergent boundary), or slide past one another (transform boundary). Friction causes the plates to lock, and when the stress becomes too great, they suddenly snap, releasing massive amounts of energy as seismic waves. They are also caused by volcanic activity and faulting. 

### 3. Volcanic Eruptions
A volcanic eruption is the ejection of molten rock (magma), ash, and gases from deep inside the earth onto the surface. Once magma reaches the surface, it is called **lava**.
- **Causes:** Like earthquakes, volcanoes are primarily caused by the movement of tectonic plates. At subduction zones, one plate is pushed under another and melts into magma. The extreme pressure forces the magma up through cracks in the crust. Eruptions are also caused by "hot spots" (plumes of exceptionally hot mantle beneath the crust).

### 4. Flooding (Natural Causes)
Flooding is the overflow of large amounts of water onto land that is normally dry. 
- **Causes:** Natural flooding occurs due to extreme, torrential rainfall that causes rivers to overflow their banks and submerge the surrounding floodplains. Coastal flooding is caused by high tides, storm surges (from hurricanes or cyclones), and **tsunamis** (massive ocean waves generated by underwater earthquakes). The melting of snow and glaciers in temperate regions also causes rivers to swell and flood naturally in spring.

## Human-Induced Hazards and Their Causes

**Objective ii: relate the human-induced hazards to their causes.**

Human-induced (or man-induced) hazards are environmental disasters caused or worsened by human activities, usually through the careless use of environmental resources.

### 1. Soil Erosion
Soil erosion is the physical removal of the fertile top layer of soil by agents such as water and wind. 
- **Process and Causes:** While erosion is a natural geological process, human activities severely accelerate it. 
  - **Deforestation and overgrazing:** Stripping the land of trees and grass leaves the soil loose and exposed to heavy rain and strong winds.
  - **Poor farming practices:** Ploughing up and down a slope (instead of across it), continuous cropping without fallow periods, and bush burning destroy soil structure.
  - When heavy rain falls on this exposed soil, the runoff creates small channels (rills) which eventually wash away deeply into massive, destructive **gullies**.

### 2. Deforestation
Deforestation is the large-scale clearing of forests and trees without replanting them.
- **Causes:** 
  - **Agriculture:** Vast areas of forest are cleared for farming and plantations.
  - **Fuelwood gathering:** In many parts of Nigeria and tropical Africa, trees are continuously cut down for firewood and charcoal production.
  - **Logging:** Felling trees for timber, paper, and furniture.
  - **Urbanisation and infrastructural development:** Clearing forests to build roads, industries, and new settlements.

### 3. Pollution
Pollution is the release of harmful substances (pollutants) into the environment, making it unsafe for living organisms.
- **Air Pollution Causes:** Emissions of harmful gases (like carbon (II) oxide and sulphur dioxide) from industrial smokestacks, vehicle exhausts, and power plants. In Nigeria, **gas flaring** in the Niger Delta is a major cause of air pollution.
- **Water Pollution Causes:** Discharge of untreated industrial chemical waste into rivers, large-scale **oil spillage**, indiscriminate dumping of solid refuse in water bodies, and agricultural runoff containing chemical fertilisers.
- **Land/Soil Pollution Causes:** Improper disposal of non-biodegradable solid waste (like plastics), oil spills contaminating farmlands, and excessive use of agrochemicals.
- **Noise Pollution Causes:** Heavy machinery in factories, aircraft, loud generators in residential areas, and heavy traffic.

### 4. Flooding (Human Causes)
Human activities frequently cause flooding in areas that might not naturally flood, especially in cities.
- **Causes:** 
  - **Poor urban planning:** Constructing buildings directly on floodplains and natural drainage paths blocks the flow of water.
  - **Blocked drainage channels:** Indiscriminate dumping of solid waste (like plastic bottles and nylon) blocks street drains.
  - **Paving over land:** Covering land with concrete and asphalt prevents rainwater from soaking into the ground (infiltration), massively increasing surface runoff.
  - **Dam failures:** The collapse of a dam, or the sudden, poorly timed release of water from a dam (such as the Lagdo Dam in Cameroon affecting the Benue River), causes catastrophic downstream flooding.

### 5. Desertification
Desertification is the process by which fertile, productive land degrades and turns into barren desert. It is the encroachment of desert conditions into previously arable regions.
- **Causes:**
  - **Overgrazing:** Large herds of livestock eat the vegetation faster than it can regrow, exposing the soil.
  - **Deforestation:** Cutting down trees for firewood removes the roots that hold the soil together and the canopy that shades the ground, causing the soil to dry out and blow away.
  - **Over-cultivation:** Farming the same plot of land continuously exhausts the soil's nutrients, turning it to dust.
  - These human activities, combined with natural droughts, cause the desert to expand southward in Africa.

## Major Areas and Effects of Environmental Hazards

**Objective iii: locate the major areas where environmental hazards are common and their effects.**

This section looks at exactly where these hazards occur globally and in Nigeria, and details their environmental and socio-economic effects.

### 1. Droughts and Desertification
- **Major Areas:** 
  - **Nigeria/Africa:** The Sahel region of Africa, which lies just south of the Sahara Desert. In Nigeria, the extreme northern states (such as Sokoto, Katsina, Kano, Jigawa, Yobe, and Borno) are highly prone to desertification and drought. Other African areas include the Horn of Africa (Somalia, Ethiopia) and the fringes of the Kalahari desert.
  - **Global:** The fringes of the Gobi desert in Asia, parts of Australia, and the south-western USA.
- **Effects:** 
  - **Socio-economic:** Severe crop failure leading to famine and starvation; mass death of livestock; migration of populations towards wetter regions (environmental refugees), which frequently sparks farmer-herder conflicts over scarce resources; deepening poverty.
  - **Environmental:** Drying up of surface water bodies (e.g., the shrinking of Lake Chad); loss of biodiversity; severe wind erosion.

### 2. Earthquakes and Volcanic Eruptions
- **Major Areas:**
  - **Global:** The **Circum-Pacific Belt** (often called the "Ring of Fire"), which borders the Pacific Ocean and includes Japan, the Philippines, and California. Another major area is the **Alpine-Himalayan belt** (running through the Middle East to India) and the **Mid-Atlantic Ridge** (Iceland). 
  - **Africa:** The East African Rift Valley (frequent volcanic activity, e.g., Mt. Kilimanjaro) and the Cameroon Line (Mt. Cameroon). Note that West Africa, including Nigeria, lies on a stable tectonic shield and generally does not experience major earthquakes or active volcanism.
- **Effects:**
  - **Socio-economic:** Earthquakes cause the immediate collapse of buildings, bridges, and dams, leading to massive loss of human life. They trigger fires from broken gas lines. Volcanic eruptions destroy settlements and farmlands with lava flows and ash.
  - **Environmental:** Earthquakes can trigger landslides and deadly tsunamis. Volcanic eruptions release massive amounts of sulphur dioxide and ash into the atmosphere, which can temporarily cool the global climate and cause acid rain. 
  - *Positive effect of volcanoes:* Volcanic ash and lava weather down over thousands of years to form extremely fertile soils for agriculture.

### 3. Flooding
- **Major Areas:**
  - **Nigeria:** The floodplains of the River Niger and River Benue; coastal regions like Lagos and the Niger Delta; major urban centres with poor drainage like Ibadan and Onitsha.
  - **Global:** The Ganges Delta in Bangladesh (highly vulnerable to coastal flooding and cyclones), the Nile Valley, and low-lying coastal cities worldwide.
- **Effects:**
  - **Socio-economic:** Drowning of humans and livestock; destruction of homes, roads, and bridges; washing away of agricultural crops resulting in food shortages.
  - **Environmental:** Outbreak of waterborne diseases (like cholera and typhoid) due to contaminated water supplies; severe soil erosion and the deposit of unwanted debris on farmlands.

### 4. Soil Erosion
- **Major Areas:**
  - **Nigeria:** **Gully erosion** is most severe in the South-eastern states (Anambra, Enugu, and Imo). The **Agulu-Nanka gully** in Anambra State is a classic textbook example. **Sheet and wind erosion** are widespread in the northern Savannah and Sahel belts.
- **Effects:**
  - **Socio-economic:** Deep gullies destroy roads, isolate communities, and swallow buildings. The loss of fertile topsoil drastically reduces agricultural crop yield, leading to poverty for farmers.
  - **Environmental:** Rivers and reservoirs become choked with washed-away sand (siltation), leading to water scarcity and localized flooding; massive degradation and fragmentation of the landscape.

### 5. Deforestation
- **Major Areas:**
  - **Global:** The Amazon Basin in South America, the Congo Basin in Central Africa, and the rainforests of Southeast Asia (e.g., Indonesia).
  - **Nigeria:** The southern high forest zones, particularly in Edo, Ondo, Cross River, and Ogun states.
- **Effects:**
  - **Environmental:** Massive loss of biodiversity as plant and animal species lose their habitats; disruption of the natural water cycle, leading to reduced rainfall; heavy soil erosion. Deforestation accelerates global warming because fewer trees are available to absorb carbon (IV) oxide (a major greenhouse gas).

### 6. Pollution
- **Major Areas:**
  - **Nigeria:** Industrial hubs like Lagos, Kano, and Port Harcourt. The **Niger Delta** region is globally notorious for severe oil spills and gas flaring.
  - **Global:** Heavily industrialized nations and regions (e.g., Eastern China, Northern India, Western Europe, North-eastern USA).
- **Effects:**
  - **Air Pollution:** Causes severe respiratory diseases (like asthma and bronchitis) in humans, causes acid rain which destroys vegetation, and drives global warming.
  - **Water Pollution:** Kills aquatic life (fish), makes water unsafe for human consumption, and ruins the livelihoods of fishing communities.
  - **Land Pollution:** Degrades soil fertility, making it useless for agriculture, and allows toxic chemicals to enter the human food chain.

## Prevention and Control of Environmental Hazards

**Objective iv: recommend possible methods of prevention and control.**

While natural tectonic events cannot be prevented, their impact can be controlled and mitigated. Human-induced hazards can be largely prevented through better environmental management.

| Environmental Hazard | Prevention and Control Methods |
| --- | --- |
| **Droughts & Desertification** | **Afforestation** (planting trees, e.g., the Great Green Wall project across Africa); practising controlled grazing; constructing dams and reservoirs to store water during the rainy season; promoting irrigation farming; providing rural people with alternative cooking fuels (like gas or biogas) to stop them from cutting down trees for firewood. |
| **Earthquakes** | Earthquakes cannot be prevented. **Control/Mitigation:** Enforcing strict building codes so that houses and bridges are earthquake-resistant; avoiding construction directly on known fault lines; establishing early warning systems; educating the public and holding earthquake drills. |
| **Volcanic Eruptions** | Eruptions cannot be prevented. **Control/Mitigation:** Monitoring volcanoes using tiltmeters and seismographs to detect rising magma; hazard mapping; safely evacuating vulnerable populations before an eruption occurs. |
| **Flooding** | Constructing **levees and dykes** along river banks; building dams to regulate river flow; regular dredging of rivers to deepen their channels; strictly prohibiting building on floodplains; clearing urban street drains of solid refuse; afforestation (trees slow down surface runoff). |
| **Soil Erosion** | **Afforestation** and cover cropping to bind the soil; practising **terracing** on steep slopes; **contour ploughing** (ploughing across the slope, not down it); preventing bush burning; creating proper concrete drainage channels in erosion-prone areas. |
| **Deforestation** | Enforcing strict government laws against illegal logging; creating and protecting designated forest reserves; aggressive reforestation (replanting trees that have been cut); public education on the importance of forests. |
| **Pollution** | Mandating factories to treat toxic industrial effluents before discharging them; proper disposal of solid waste using sanitary landfills and recycling; enforcing strict laws to stop gas flaring and oil spillage; using unleaded petrol and maintaining vehicles properly to reduce harmful exhaust emissions. |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Environmental hazard | Any extreme natural or human-induced event that threatens life, property, and the environment. |
| Drought | A prolonged period of abnormally low rainfall causing water scarcity. |
| Tsunami | A massive, destructive ocean wave usually caused by an underwater earthquake. |
| Soil erosion | The washing or blowing away of the top layer of soil by water or wind. |
| Gully | A deep, wide ditch cut into the earth by severe water erosion. |
| Deforestation | The large-scale clearing of forests without replanting trees. |
| Desertification | The process by which fertile land degrades into desert, driven by drought and human activities. |
| Afforestation | Planting trees on an area of land to create a forest where there was none recently. |
| Reforestation | Replanting trees in an area where the forest has been depleted. |
| Pollution | The introduction of harmful substances (pollutants) into the natural environment. |
| Gas flaring | The burning of unwanted natural gas associated with crude oil extraction, causing severe air pollution. |
| Overgrazing | Livestock eating vegetation too heavily and too fast, destroying the plant cover and exposing soil. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Understand Flooding:** Always remember that flooding is unique because JAMB classifies it as **both** a natural hazard (heavy rain, tsunamis) and a man-induced hazard (blocked drains, paved urban surfaces).
- **Location specifics:** Know your Nigerian geography. If a question asks about severe **gully erosion**, the answer is almost always the **South-Eastern states** (e.g., Anambra, Agulu-Nanka). If it asks about **desertification**, point to the **extreme North** (Sahel/Sudan savannah). If it asks about **oil pollution**, look to the **Niger Delta**.
- **Causes of earthquakes and volcanoes:** They are natural hazards driven by the movement of tectonic plates, not by human activities like mining (though humans can cause very minor tremors, JAMB focuses on the major natural causes).
- **Weathering vs. Erosion:** Do not confuse these two. Weathering is the breaking down of rocks *in their original place* (in situ). Erosion involves the *removal and transport* of the soil and rock materials.
- **Afforestation vs. Deforestation:** Be careful reading the options. Afforestation (planting trees) *prevents* hazards like erosion and desertification. Deforestation (cutting trees) *causes* them.

## Quick summary

1. Environmental hazards are destructive events that threaten life and the environment; they are classified as natural or man-induced.
2. Natural hazards include droughts, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and natural flooding. They are caused by climatic shifts and tectonic plate movements.
3. Man-induced hazards include soil erosion, deforestation, pollution, urban flooding, and desertification. They are driven by poor farming, overgrazing, logging, and poor waste management.
4. Earthquakes and volcanoes are globally concentrated along the Circum-Pacific Belt ("Ring of Fire") and the Alpine-Himalayan belt.
5. In Nigeria, the north is prone to drought and desertification, the south-east is plagued by severe gully erosion, and the Niger Delta suffers from oil pollution.
6. Environmental hazards cause massive socio-economic damage, including loss of life, famine, destruction of property, and displacement of populations.
7. While tectonic hazards cannot be prevented, their effects can be mitigated by strict building codes and early warning systems. Human-induced hazards are controlled through afforestation, proper waste disposal, sustainable agriculture, and environmental laws.

## Practice questions

**1. The Agulu-Nanka region in Anambra State is most famous for experiencing which of the following environmental hazards?**
- A. Severe coastal flooding
- B. Active volcanic eruptions
- C. Extreme wind erosion
- D. Severe gully erosion

**Answer: D.** Agulu-Nanka is the classic textbook example of severe gully erosion in the south-eastern part of Nigeria, caused by vulnerable soils, heavy rainfall, and poor land management.

**2. Which of the following is a primary man-induced cause of flooding in Nigerian urban centres like Lagos?**
- A. Prolonged torrential rainfall during the wet season
- B. Indiscriminate dumping of refuse in drainage channels
- C. The melting of glaciers and snow
- D. The occurrence of high tides from the Atlantic Ocean

**Answer: B.** While rainfall and high tides (options A, C, and D) are natural causes, blocking drainage channels with solid waste is a human action that prevents water from escaping, causing urban flooding.

**3. The continuous removal of fertile topsoil by the action of wind and water is known as:**
- A. desertification
- B. soil erosion
- C. weathering
- D. deforestation

**Answer: B.** Soil erosion is the physical removal and transport of topsoil. A common mistake is choosing weathering, but weathering breaks rocks down without moving them.

**4. The global region that experiences the highest frequency of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions is the:**
- A. Circum-Pacific Belt
- B. Amazon Basin
- C. Sahel region
- D. Congo Basin

**Answer: A.** The Circum-Pacific Belt, also known as the "Ring of Fire", is a highly active tectonic zone surrounding the Pacific Ocean where the majority of the world's earthquakes and volcanoes occur.

**5. Which of the following practices is highly recommended to prevent desertification and soil erosion?**
- A. Overgrazing
- B. Continuous cropping
- C. Afforestation
- D. Bush burning

**Answer: C.** Afforestation (the planting of trees) binds the soil with roots, acts as a windbreak, and prevents the encroachment of desert conditions. The other options are causes of land degradation.

**6. The release of carbon (II) oxide, sulphur dioxide, and soot from gas flaring in the Niger Delta primarily leads to:**
- A. noise pollution
- B. land degradation
- C. air pollution
- D. tectonic tremors

**Answer: C.** These substances are harmful gases and particulates that contaminate the atmosphere, causing severe air pollution and acid rain.

**7. Which of the following environmental hazards is most prevalent in the extreme northern states of Nigeria, such as Sokoto and Borno?**
- A. Gully erosion
- B. Coastal flooding
- C. Oil spillage
- D. Desertification

**Answer: D.** The extreme northern states lie in the Sahel and Sudan savannah belts, which border the Sahara Desert and suffer from low rainfall, making them highly prone to drought and desertification.

**8. One positive environmental effect of volcanic eruptions is the:**
- A. reduction of global warming by ash clouds
- B. formation of highly fertile soils for agriculture
- C. creation of deep reservoirs for dams
- D. permanent prevention of future earthquakes

**Answer: B.** Volcanic lava and ash break down over time to form incredibly rich, fertile soils that support intensive agriculture.

**9. Deforestation in tropical Africa is largely driven by all the following EXCEPT:**
- A. gathering of wood for fuel and charcoal
- B. clearing land for large-scale agriculture
- C. planting of cover crops
- D. commercial logging for timber

**Answer: C.** Planting cover crops is a soil conservation method. Gathering firewood, farming, and logging are the main reasons forests are cut down.

**10. Which of the following is the best method to control the impact of earthquakes?**
- A. Diverting magma flows with heavy machinery
- B. Enforcing strict building codes for earthquake-resistant structures
- C. Building levees and dykes along fault lines
- D. Practising contour ploughing in affected zones

**Answer: B.** Earthquakes cannot be stopped, so the best mitigation strategy is to build strong, flexible structures that will not collapse and kill people during a tremor.
