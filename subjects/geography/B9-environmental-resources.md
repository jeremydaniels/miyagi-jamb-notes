## The Concept of Environmental Resources

**Objective i: interpret the concept of environmental resources.**

To understand what environmental resources are, we need to break the term down into its two root words: **environment** and **resource**.

- The **environment** refers to the total surroundings of an organism in a given area. For humans, this includes the physical or natural surroundings (such as air, water, land, soil, plants, and animals) and the built environment (such as towns and cities).
- A **resource** is anything that is useful to mankind. It is a supply of something that has value, utility, and can be drawn upon when needed to create wealth, satisfy human wants, or improve the standard of living.

Therefore, **environmental resources** are all the useful materials, elements, and forces found in the natural environment that humans can harness and use to satisfy their needs and create wealth. 

If something exists in the environment but humans have no use for it (or do not yet have the technology to use it), it is just a "neutral stuff" or an element of the environment, not a resource. For example, crude oil in the Niger Delta was just a dark, sticky liquid in the ground for centuries until the invention of the internal combustion engine and oil-drilling technology turned it into a highly valuable resource.

## Types of Environmental Resources and Their Uses

**Content i: Types of resources (atmospheric, land, soil, vegetation and minerals)**
**Objective ii: relate environmental resources to their uses.**

Environmental resources are broadly classified into five main types based on where they are found and what they are made of. These are atmospheric, land, soil, vegetation, and mineral resources.

### 1. Atmospheric Resources

The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding the earth (see `B1-the-earth-as-a-planet.md`). It contains vital resources such as solar radiation, wind, precipitation (rainfall), and various gases.

**Uses of Atmospheric Resources:**
*   **Solar Energy (Sunlight):**
    *   **Agriculture:** Sunlight provides the energy that plants need to manufacture their food through photosynthesis. Without solar energy, crops cannot grow.
    *   **Drying:** In Nigeria, solar heat is heavily relied upon to dry agricultural produce like cocoa beans, groundnuts, cassava (for garri), and grains, as well as to dry clothes and preserve fish.
    *   **Power generation:** Solar panels are increasingly used to convert sunlight directly into electricity, providing a vital alternative power source in homes and industries.
*   **Wind (Air in motion):**
    *   **Energy generation:** Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of the wind into electricity (wind power).
    *   **Agriculture and Nature:** Wind aids in the pollination of flowers and the dispersal of seeds. It is also the major driver of ocean currents and the movement of rain-bearing clouds (such as the South-West Trade Winds that bring rain to West Africa).
    *   **Transport:** Wind is used to propel sailing boats and ships on water bodies.
*   **Rainfall (Precipitation):**
    *   **Water supply:** Rainfall is the primary source of fresh water for rivers, lakes, and underground aquifers, which humans use for drinking, washing, and industrial processes.
    *   **Agriculture:** Rain-fed agriculture depends entirely on atmospheric moisture.
*   **Atmospheric Gases:**
    *   **Oxygen (O₂):** Essential for the respiration of humans and animals. It is also required for combustion (burning fuels for energy).
    *   **Carbon (IV) oxide (CO₂):** Absorbed by green plants during photosynthesis to make food.
    *   **Nitrogen (N₂):** The most abundant gas in the atmosphere (about 78%). It is converted into nitrates by soil bacteria (nitrogen fixation) for plant growth, and is extracted industrially to manufacture chemical fertilizers.

### 2. Land Resources

While "land" and "soil" are often used interchangeably in everyday language, in geography, they mean different things. **Land resources** refer to the solid surface of the earth in terms of its area, space, topography (relief), and physical location.

**Uses of Land Resources:**
*   **Space for Settlement:** Land provides the physical space on which humans build their homes, towns, and cities.
*   **Infrastructure and Transport:** Land is needed to construct roads, railways, airports, pipelines, and telecommunication masts.
*   **Agriculture and Forestry:** Land provides the vast areas required for crop farming, grazing livestock, and establishing forest reserves.
*   **Recreation and Tourism:** Specific relief features of the land, such as mountains, valleys, and beaches, are major tourist attractions. Good examples in Nigeria include the Obudu Mountain Resort (formerly Obudu Cattle Ranch) in Cross River State, Olumo Rock in Ogun State, and the Idanre Hills in Ondo State.
*   **Wildlife Conservation:** Large tracts of land are set aside as game reserves and national parks to protect wildlife, such as the Yankari National Park in Bauchi State and the Cross River National Park.

### 3. Soil Resources

**Soil** is the loose, weathered, topmost layer of the earth's crust, mixed with organic matter, where plants grow. It is a distinct resource separate from the general space of "land".

**Uses of Soil Resources:**
*   **Agriculture (Crop Production):** This is the most important use of soil. Soil provides a medium for plant roots to anchor and supplies the water and essential nutrients (like nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium) that crops need. Different soils support different crops; for example, well-drained loamy soils support yams and maize, while clayey soils are suited for rice.
*   **Building and Construction:** 
    *   **Sand and gravel** are excavated from soils to mix with cement for making concrete and blocks.
    *   **Laterite** (a reddish soil rich in iron and aluminium) is widely used in Nigeria for road construction and rural building.
*   **Pottery and Ceramics:** **Clay soils** are highly plastic when wet and harden when baked. They are used to make pots, tiles, bricks, and ceramics. The Ladi Kwali Pottery Centre in Abuja is a famous historical example of using local clay resources.
*   **Mineral Habitat:** The soil harbours essential trace minerals and microorganisms that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients back into the ecosystem.

### 4. Vegetation Resources

Vegetation refers to the plant cover growing in a particular area. In Nigeria, vegetation ranges from the dense rainforests in the south to the vast savannas (grasslands) in the north.

**Uses of Vegetation Resources:**
*   **Timber/Lumbering:** Forests provide valuable hard woods such as Iroko, Obeche, Mahogany, and Sapele wood. These are used for furniture making, building construction, boat building, and paper production.
*   **Non-Timber Forest Products (NTFPs):** Vegetation provides a wealth of other products including rubber (for tyres), palm produce, ropes, bamboo, and shea butter.
*   **Food and Medicine:** Vegetation provides wild fruits, nuts, roots, and vegetables. Many barks, leaves, and roots of forest plants are used as traditional medicinal herbs (e.g., the bark of the *Cinchona* tree for quinine to treat malaria, or dogonyaro (neem) leaves in Nigeria).
*   **Grazing (Pasture):** The savanna grasslands provide natural pasture (grasses and shrubs) for rearing livestock such as cattle, sheep, and goats. This supports the pastoral farming practiced by Fulani herdsmen in northern Nigeria.
*   **Environmental Protection:** Vegetation protects the soil from direct rainfall and wind, preventing soil erosion. Plant roots bind the soil together, and the canopy reduces the speed of raindrops.
*   **Fuel:** In many rural areas, wood from vegetation is cut and used as firewood or processed into charcoal for cooking and heating.

### 5. Mineral Resources

Mineral resources are naturally occurring, inorganic, solid substances found in the earth's crust, as well as fossil fuels (which are organic). They are the foundation of modern industry. Minerals can be classified into three main groups: metallic, non-metallic, and mineral fuels (fossil fuels).

**Uses of Mineral Resources:**
*   **Generation of Revenue and Foreign Exchange:** The export of minerals is the major source of income for many nations. In Nigeria, crude oil (petroleum) accounts for the vast majority of government revenue and foreign exchange earnings.
*   **Provision of Energy:** Fossil fuels are the primary source of the world's energy.
    *   **Crude Oil and Natural Gas** (found abundantly in the Niger Delta) are refined into petrol, diesel, and aviation fuel to power transport and generate electricity.
    *   **Coal** (mined historically in Enugu) is burned to generate electricity and power locomotives, though its use has declined.
*   **Industrial Raw Materials:**
    *   **Limestone** (mined at Ewekoro in Ogun State, Obajana in Kogi State, and Gboko in Benue State) is the principal raw material for manufacturing cement.
    *   **Iron Ore** (mined at Itakpe in Kogi State) is smelted to produce iron and steel, which are essential for manufacturing vehicles, ships, and building frameworks.
*   **Jewellery and Store of Value:** Precious metals like **Gold** (found in Ilesha, Osun State, and parts of Zamfara) and diamonds are used to make expensive jewellery and are held by central banks as financial reserves.
*   **Employment and Infrastructure:** Mining operations create thousands of direct and indirect jobs and often lead to the development of roads, railways, and towns in mining areas (e.g., the growth of Jos due to tin mining).

## Renewable and Non-Renewable Resources

**Content ii: The concept of renewable and non-renewable resources**
**Objective iii: differentiate between the concepts of renewable and non-renewable resources.**

All environmental resources can be grouped into two broad categories based on their ability to regenerate or replenish themselves after they have been used.

### 1. Renewable Resources

**Renewable resources** are resources that can be replenished, replaced, or regenerated by natural processes within a relatively short period of human time after they are used. As long as they are managed properly and not used faster than they can regenerate, their supply is essentially endless.

**Examples of Renewable Resources:**
*   **Solar energy:** The sun shines every day.
*   **Wind energy:** The wind blows continuously.
*   **Water:** Water is constantly recycled through the hydrological (water) cycle.
*   **Vegetation and crops:** Trees can be replanted, and crops are grown every season.
*   **Animals and fish:** They reproduce and replace their populations.
*   **Soil:** Through weathering and organic decomposition, soil slowly renews itself, though it requires careful management.

**Important Note on Sustainability:** Just because a resource is renewable does not mean it is indestructible. If a renewable resource is exploited faster than it can naturally replace itself (a process known as **environmental degradation** or **unsustainable use**), it can be severely depleted. For example, if a forest is heavily logged and not replanted, or if fish are caught in massive numbers before they can breed, these renewable resources can be destroyed.

### 2. Non-Renewable Resources

**Non-renewable resources** are resources that exist in a fixed, limited quantity in the earth's crust. Once they are extracted and used up, they cannot be replaced by natural processes, or they take millions of years to form—meaning they cannot be replaced within a human lifespan. 

Because their supply is finite, continuous extraction will eventually lead to their complete exhaustion (depletion).

**Examples of Non-Renewable Resources:**
*   **Fossil fuels:** Crude oil, natural gas, and coal. These formed from the remains of ancient plants and animals over hundreds of millions of years under extreme heat and pressure. When we burn a litre of petrol, it is gone forever.
*   **Metallic minerals:** Iron ore, tin, columbite, gold, copper, and bauxite.
*   **Non-metallic minerals:** Limestone, marble, and granite.

*Note: While minerals themselves are non-renewable, many metallic products (like aluminum cans and iron steel) can be **recycled** and reused, which helps conserve the non-renewable raw mineral in the ground.*

### Comparison: Renewable vs. Non-Renewable Resources

The table below differentiates between the two concepts as required by JAMB.

| Feature | Renewable Resources | Non-Renewable Resources |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Resources that can naturally replenish themselves over a short period of time. | Resources that exist in fixed amounts and cannot be quickly replaced once used. |
| **Depletion** | Can last indefinitely if managed sustainably. | Will eventually be exhausted (run out) with continuous use. |
| **Rate of formation** | Fast (days, months, years, or continuous). | Extremely slow (takes millions of years to form). |
| **Examples** | Solar energy, wind, water, plants, animals, soil. | Crude oil, coal, natural gas, limestone, iron ore, gold, tin. |
| **Environmental Impact (General)** | Often have a lower environmental impact and are "cleaner" to use (e.g., solar, wind). | Extraction and use often cause significant pollution (e.g., oil spills, greenhouse gases from burning coal). |
| **Management strategy** | Focus is on **sustainable yield** (harvesting only what can grow back). | Focus is on **conservation**, **recycling**, and finding alternative substitutes. |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Environment** | The total physical, natural, and built surroundings of an organism in a given area. |
| **Resource** | Any material, element, or force in the environment that is useful to mankind. |
| **Atmospheric resources** | Useful elements of the atmosphere, such as solar energy, wind, rainfall, and gases. |
| **Land resources** | The physical space, surface area, and relief features of the earth used for settlement, agriculture, and infrastructure. |
| **Soil resources** | The weathered top layer of the earth's crust used for agriculture, pottery, and construction materials. |
| **Vegetation resources** | The plant cover of an area providing timber, food, medicine, and pasture. |
| **Mineral resources** | Solid inorganic substances and fossil fuels extracted from the earth for industrial and energy use. |
| **Renewable resource** | A resource that can naturally regenerate or replace itself within a short human timeframe (e.g., solar, wind, water). |
| **Non-renewable resource** | A resource that exists in a fixed, finite amount and takes millions of years to form (e.g., crude oil, coal, gold). |
| **Sustainable use** | Using renewable resources at a rate that allows them to naturally replenish without being degraded. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Land vs. Soil:** A very common mistake is treating land and soil as the exact same thing in geography. **Land** refers to the space/surface (used for building, relief for tourism). **Soil** refers to the dirt/top layer (used for planting crops, making bricks, pottery). Read the question carefully to see which one JAMB is asking about.
*   **Are all natural resources renewable?** No! Minerals and fossil fuels are natural, but they are non-renewable. Do not confuse "natural" with "renewable".
*   **Coal is a mineral fuel:** Even though coal is formed from ancient vegetation, it took millions of years to form. Therefore, it is a **non-renewable mineral resource**, not a vegetation resource.
*   **Recycling vs. Renewing:** Iron and aluminum can be recycled, but the ores they come from are non-renewable. Recycling delays the depletion of non-renewable resources, but it does not make them renewable.
*   **Gases and their uses:** Remember that nitrogen (N₂) is heavily tied to plant growth and fertilizers, while carbon dioxide (CO₂) is used by plants for photosynthesis. 
*   **Know your Nigerian examples:** JAMB loves testing local knowledge. Know that limestone is heavily tied to cement (Ewekoro, Obajana), tin/columbite to Jos, coal to Enugu, and iron ore to Itakpe.

## Quick summary

1. Environmental resources are useful materials and forces found in our natural surroundings that humans harness to satisfy needs and create wealth.
2. Atmospheric resources include solar energy, wind, rainfall, and gases (oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide). They are crucial for energy, agriculture, and respiration.
3. Land resources provide the physical space for settlements, transport infrastructure, agriculture, and tourist attractions (like mountains and rock outcrops).
4. Soil resources provide the medium for crop growth and raw materials for construction (sand, gravel, laterite) and pottery (clay).
5. Vegetation resources provide timber, herbs, food, and pasture for grazing animals.
6. Mineral resources include fossil fuels (crude oil, coal) and solid minerals (iron ore, limestone). They are primarily used for energy generation, industrial manufacturing, and earning foreign exchange.
7. Renewable resources (like sunlight, wind, water, and forests) can naturally replenish themselves over a short period.
8. Non-renewable resources (like crude oil, coal, and metallic minerals) exist in finite quantities and cannot be replaced once exhausted.

## Practice questions

**1. Useful materials and elements found in the natural environment that can be used to create wealth are best described as:**
- A. The atmosphere
- B. An environmental resource
- C. The biosphere
- D. A non-renewable asset

**Answer: B.** An environmental resource refers to anything in the total surroundings (environment) that has utility and can be used by man to create wealth or satisfy needs.

**2. Which of the following is NOT an atmospheric resource?**
- A. Solar radiation
- B. Wind
- C. Precipitation
- D. Laterite

**Answer: D.** Laterite is a type of reddish soil rich in iron and aluminium, making it a soil resource, not an atmospheric one. Solar radiation, wind, and precipitation are derived from the atmosphere.

**3. In geography, which of the following best describes a major use of land resources, as distinct from soil resources?**
- A. Cultivation of root crops like cassava
- B. Extraction of clay for pottery
- C. Construction of roads, airports, and settlements
- D. Absorption of nitrates by plant roots

**Answer: C.** Land resources refer to the physical space and relief surface used for infrastructure and settlement. Cultivation and clay extraction depend specifically on the soil (the weathered top layer).

**4. The atmospheric gas heavily utilized by soil bacteria for plant growth and industrial fertilizer production is:**
- A. Oxygen
- B. Carbon (IV) oxide
- C. Nitrogen
- D. Argon

**Answer: C.** Nitrogen is fixed by soil bacteria into nitrates, which are essential nutrients for plant growth, and is the primary element in artificial fertilizers.

**5. Which of the following pairs represents non-renewable environmental resources?**
- A. Wind and solar energy
- B. Crude oil and limestone
- C. Timber and water
- D. Soil and vegetation

**Answer: B.** Crude oil and limestone are minerals that take millions of years to form and exist in finite amounts, making them non-renewable. The other options contain renewable resources.

**6. The Obudu Cattle Ranch (Mountain Resort) in Cross River State serves as an environmental resource primarily for:**
- A. Heavy industrial manufacturing
- B. Generation of wind power
- C. Tourism and recreation
- D. Extraction of metallic minerals

**Answer: C.** The relief and pleasant climate of the Obudu highlands make it a major land resource used for tourism and recreation. 

**7. Which of the following best explains why vegetation is classified as a renewable resource?**
- A. It has a high market value in international trade.
- B. It can be naturally replaced or replanted over a short period of time.
- C. It takes millions of years to form under immense heat and pressure.
- D. It does not cause environmental pollution when burned.

**Answer: B.** A resource is classified as renewable if it has the capacity to regenerate, regrow, or be replaced by natural processes within a relatively short, human timeframe.

**8. Limestone is heavily mined at Ewekoro and Obajana. What is its primary industrial use?**
- A. Smelting iron ore
- B. Generating electricity
- C. Manufacturing cement
- D. Producing ceramics and pottery

**Answer: C.** Limestone is the principal raw material required for the industrial production of cement. Clay, not limestone, is used for ceramics and pottery. 

**9. Even though it is a renewable resource, vegetation can be severely depleted if:**
- A. It is used for medical herbs.
- B. It is exploited faster than it can naturally regenerate.
- C. Animals graze on it during the wet season.
- D. It is exposed to too much solar radiation.

**Answer: B.** Renewable resources like vegetation and soil can face degradation and depletion if they are harvested or used unsustainably—that is, faster than their natural rate of replacement. 

**10. Coal, previously mined in large quantities in Enugu, is classified as a:**
- A. Renewable vegetation resource
- B. Non-renewable mineral fuel
- C. Renewable atmospheric resource
- D. Non-renewable soil resource

**Answer: B.** Coal is a fossil fuel. Even though it originated from ancient plant matter, it is classified as a non-renewable mineral resource (mineral fuel) because it takes millions of years to form and cannot be replaced once mined.
