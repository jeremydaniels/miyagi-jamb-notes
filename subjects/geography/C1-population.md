## Different concepts of population

**Objective i: define different concepts of population.**

In geography, **population** refers to the total number of people living in a specific geographical area (such as a village, a country, or the world) at a given time. To understand population dynamics, you must master several key concepts:

* **Population size:** The actual total number of individuals living in a place. For example, the population of Nigeria was estimated to be over 200 million people at the time of writing.
* **Population distribution:** The way people are spread out across a given area. It tells us where people live.
* **Population density:** The ratio of the total number of people to the total land area they occupy. It tells us how crowded a place is, measured in persons per km².
* **Crude Birth Rate (CBR):** The total number of live births in a year per 1,000 people in the population.
* **Crude Death Rate (CDR):** The total number of deaths in a year per 1,000 people in the population.
* **Rate of Natural Increase:** The difference between the birth rate and the death rate. It shows how fast a population is growing naturally, excluding migration.
* **Migration:** The movement of people from one place to another with the intention of settling down.
* **Optimum population:** The ideal population size that, when combined with the country's available resources and technology, yields the highest possible standard of living for everyone.
* **Overpopulation:** A situation where the number of people is too large for the available resources, leading to poverty and a drop in living standards.
* **Underpopulation:** A situation where the population is too small to fully develop and utilise the available resources, meaning living standards could rise if the population grew.

### Important population formulas and worked examples

To calculate these concepts, we use specific formulas.

**1. Population Density**
The formula for population density is:
$$ \text{Population Density} = \frac{\text{Total Population}}{\text{Total Land Area}} $$

*Worked Example:*
A state in Nigeria has a total land area of 20,000 km² and a total population of 4,000,000 people. Calculate its population density.
* **Step 1:** State the formula: $\text{Population Density} = \frac{\text{Total Population}}{\text{Total Land Area}}$.
* **Step 2:** Substitute the values into the formula: $\frac{4,000,000}{20,000 \text{ km}^2}$.
* **Step 3:** Perform the division to get the answer. $4,000,000 \div 20,000 = 200$.
* **Answer:** The population density is 200 persons per km².

**2. Crude Birth Rate and Crude Death Rate**
$$ CBR = \frac{\text{Total live births in a year}}{\text{Total population}} \times 1000 $$
$$ CDR = \frac{\text{Total deaths in a year}}{\text{Total population}} \times 1000 $$

**3. Rate of Natural Increase (RNI)**
$$ RNI = CBR - CDR $$
*(This gives the rate per 1,000 people. To convert it to a percentage (%), divide the result by 10).*

*Worked Example:*
Country A has a population of 100,000 people. In one year, there were 4,000 live births and 1,000 deaths. Calculate the Crude Birth Rate, Crude Death Rate, and the Rate of Natural Increase as a percentage.
* **Step 1:** Calculate the CBR. $CBR = (\frac{4,000}{100,000}) \times 1000 = 40$ births per 1,000 people.
* **Step 2:** Calculate the CDR. $CDR = (\frac{1,000}{100,000}) \times 1000 = 10$ deaths per 1,000 people.
* **Step 3:** Calculate the RNI per 1,000 people by subtracting the death rate from the birth rate. $40 - 10 = 30$ per 1,000 people.
* **Step 4:** Convert the rate to a percentage by dividing by 10. $30 \div 10 = 3.0\%$.
* **Answer:** The rate of natural increase is 3.0%.

## Characteristics of population: growth rates and structure

**Objective ii: identify the characteristics of population (growth rates and structure).**

### Birth and death rates, ages/sex structure

**Content ii: Characteristics – birth and death rates, ages/sex structure.**

A population's characteristics are largely defined by its vital rates and how the population is divided by age and sex.

**1. Growth Rates**
Population growth is determined by the balance of births and deaths (natural increase), plus net migration (the difference between immigrants entering and emigrants leaving). 
* **Developing countries** (like Nigeria and many African nations) typically have high birth rates and falling death rates. This combination results in a very high population growth rate.
* **Developed countries** (like Japan and nations in Western Europe) typically have low birth rates and low death rates, resulting in slow, zero, or even negative population growth.

**2. Age and Sex Structure (Population Pyramids)**
The age and sex structure of a population is visually represented by a bar graph called a **population pyramid**. The vertical axis shows age groups (e.g., 0–4, 5–9, up to 80+), and the horizontal axis shows the percentage or number of the population. Males are traditionally shown on the left and females on the right.
* **Broad-based pyramid:** Has a wide base that narrows sharply towards the top. This indicates a high birth rate (many children) and a high death rate (few elderly people). It is typical of developing countries with a large youthful population.
* **Narrow-based (or barrel-shaped) pyramid:** Has a narrow base and a bulging middle. This indicates a low birth rate (fewer children) and a long life expectancy (many adults and elderly people). It is typical of developed countries with an aging population.

**3. The Dependency Ratio**
The age structure determines the **dependency ratio**, which measures the proportion of the population that relies on the working-age population for financial and social support. 
* **Dependent population:** Children (under 15 years) and the elderly (65 years and above).
* **Working population:** Adults (aged 15 to 64 years).

The formula is:
$$ \text{Dependency Ratio} = \frac{\text{Population under 15} + \text{Population 65 and above}}{\text{Population aged 15 to 64}} \times 100 $$

*Worked Example:*
A country has 45,000 children (0–14 years), 50,000 adults (15–64 years), and 5,000 elderly people (65 years and above). Calculate the dependency ratio.
* **Step 1:** State the formula.
* **Step 2:** Add up the dependent population (children + elderly). $45,000 + 5,000 = 50,000$.
* **Step 3:** Identify the working population (adults 15–64 years), which is 50,000.
* **Step 4:** Divide the dependents by the working population and multiply by 100. $(\frac{50,000}{50,000}) \times 100 = 1 \times 100 = 100\%$.
* **Answer:** The dependency ratio is 100% (meaning there is one dependent for every one worker).

## Factors and patterns of population distribution

**Objective iii and Content iii: determine the factors and the patterns of population distribution.**

The world's population is spread very unevenly. Some areas are packed with people, while others are virtually empty.

**Global Patterns:**
* **Densely populated areas:** South and East Asia (India, China, Japan), Western Europe, the North-Eastern U.S.A., and fertile river valleys (like the Nile Valley and the Niger Delta).
* **Sparsely populated areas:** Hot deserts (the Sahara), cold deserts and tundras (Antarctica, Northern Canada), high rugged mountains (the Himalayas), and dense tropical rainforests (the Amazon Basin).

[IMAGE: A simplified world map showing global population distribution. Dark shading indicates densely populated areas (e.g., North-Eastern USA, Western Europe, India, Eastern China). Light shading indicates sparsely populated areas (e.g., Sahara Desert, Amazon Basin in South America, Northern Canada, Australian Outback). Clean flat textbook style, white background.]

**Factors Influencing Population Distribution**
Population distribution is influenced by both physical (natural) and human (economic/social) factors.

| Factor | Encourages Dense Population | Causes Sparse Population |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Climate** | Moderate temperatures and reliable rainfall (temperate or monsoon climates) attract people for comfort and farming. | Extreme climates (too hot, too cold, or too dry) make survival and farming very difficult. |
| **Relief (Landform)** | Flat plains, river valleys, and coastal lowlands are easy to build on and farm. | High, rugged mountains and steep slopes are cold, inaccessible, and hard to build on. |
| **Soils** | Fertile soils (such as river alluvium or volcanic ash) support heavy agriculture to feed large populations. | Poor, thin, or heavily leached soils cannot support many crops. |
| **Water Supply** | Availability of fresh surface water or groundwater is essential for drinking and irrigation. | Lack of water (aridity) severely limits human settlement. |
| **Economic Activities** | Areas with factories, mining, trade centres, and jobs naturally attract migrants. | Areas with few job opportunities beyond subsistence farming lose people. |
| **Transport** | Good roads, railways, and natural harbours encourage trade and settlement. | Isolated and inaccessible areas deter settlement. |
| **History and Politics** | Historically peaceful and stable regions allow populations to grow steadily over centuries. | War, ethnic conflict, and forced resettlements scatter and reduce populations. |

## World population with reference to specific regions

**Content i: World population with particular reference to the Amazon Basin, N.E. U.S.A., India, Japan and the West Coast of Southern Africa.**

The syllabus requires you to understand how the factors above apply to five specific regions of the world.

### 1. The Amazon Basin (South America)
* **Pattern:** Very sparsely populated.
* **Reasons:** The region experiences a hot, wet Equatorial climate all year round, resulting in the growth of dense, impenetrable tropical rainforests (selvas). The soils (latosols) are heavily leached and poor for agriculture. The hot and humid conditions encourage tropical diseases like malaria. Transport is difficult, and most settlements (such as Manaus) are confined to the edges of rivers.

### 2. North-Eastern U.S.A.
* **Pattern:** Extremely densely populated (forming a continuous urban area called a "Megalopolis", stretching from Boston to Washington D.C., often called BosWash).
* **Reasons:** The area has a favourable temperate climate. Historically, it was the first major landing point for European immigrants. It has excellent natural harbours (like New York) that promote international trade. The region also has massive industrial development, supported by nearby mineral resources (such as coal in the Appalachian Mountains) and highly advanced transport networks.

### 3. India
* **Pattern:** Very densely populated (India is one of the most populous countries in the world).
* **Reasons:** India has a tropical monsoon climate that brings heavy summer rains, which are perfect for large-scale rice cultivation. The Indo-Gangetic plain is flat and covered in highly fertile alluvial soils deposited by major rivers like the Ganges. Culturally and historically, large families were encouraged, leading to massive natural population increase over the centuries.

### 4. Japan
* **Pattern:** Densely populated, but highly concentrated in specific areas.
* **Reasons:** Over 70% of Japan consists of rugged, uninhabitable mountains. Therefore, the massive population is forced to crowd onto narrow coastal plains (such as the Kanto Plain around Tokyo and Yokohama). Despite the lack of land, Japan supports its population through heavy industrialisation, high technological advancement, intensive agriculture on terraces, and a world-class fishing industry.

### 5. The West Coast of Southern Africa
* **Pattern:** Mostly sparsely populated, with a few dense pockets.
* **Reasons:** This region (mostly the coast of Namibia and western South Africa) is washed by the cold **Benguela Current**. The cold ocean current cools the air and prevents rainfall, creating the extremely dry **Namib Desert**. The arid climate, lack of fresh water, and poor soils make the region largely hostile to human settlement. However, there are pockets of dense population around valuable mining towns (for diamond extraction) and major fishing ports (like Walvis Bay).

## Factors and problems of population growth

**Objective iv and Content iv: identify the factors and problems of population growth.**

### Factors causing rapid population growth
In developing nations, population size is increasing rapidly due to several factors:
1. **High birth rates:** Driven by cultural traditions that value large families, early marriages, polygamy, and a lack of access to (or religious objections against) modern family planning.
2. **Declining death rates:** This is the most significant factor in recent decades. Better medical care (vaccinations, antibiotics), improved sanitation, cleaner water, and better maternal care mean people live much longer and infant mortality has dropped sharply.
3. **Improved agriculture:** Better farming techniques and food distribution have reduced the occurrence of famines.

### Problems of rapid population growth (Overpopulation)
When a population grows too fast for its resources, it faces severe challenges:
1. **Pressure on natural resources:** Land, water, and forests are overused, leading to deforestation, soil erosion, and environmental degradation.
2. **High unemployment:** The economy cannot create jobs fast enough for the millions of young people entering the workforce.
3. **Strain on social amenities:** Schools, hospitals, roads, and electricity grids become congested and fail.
4. **Poor housing and slums:** Rapid migration to cities leads to overcrowding and the expansion of shanty towns lacking basic sanitation.
5. **High crime rates:** Poverty and joblessness often drive youth into crime and social unrest.

### Problems of slow or negative population growth (Underpopulation)
While overpopulation is a problem in the developing world, many developed countries face issues because their population is shrinking or is too small:
1. **Shortage of labour:** Not enough young people are available to work in factories and services.
2. **Aging population:** A high dependency ratio occurs at the top of the population pyramid, meaning a shrinking workforce must pay high taxes to support millions of elderly pensioners.
3. **Underutilization of resources:** Land, minerals, and economic opportunities may remain unexploited due to a lack of manpower.

## Types, causes and effects of migration

**Objective v and Content v: relate the types of migration to their causes and effects.**

**Migration** is the movement of people from one place to another to settle. The place people leave is the **source region**, and the place they arrive at is the **destination region**.

### Types of migration
1. **Internal migration:** Movement within the borders of the same country.
   * **Rural-urban:** Moving from villages to cities (e.g., moving from a village in Kano to Lagos in search of work). This is the most common type of migration in Nigeria.
   * **Rural-rural:** Moving from one farming or rural area to another (e.g., nomadic Fulani herdsmen seeking fresh pasture for cattle, or farmers moving to find more fertile land).
   * **Urban-rural:** Moving from cities back to villages, often done by retirees or those seeking a quieter, cheaper life.
   * **Urban-urban:** Moving from one city to another (e.g., moving from Ibadan to Abuja).
2. **International (external) migration:** Movement across national borders.
   * **Emigration:** Leaving one's home country.
   * **Immigration:** Entering a new country.
3. **Forced migration:** People are forced to move against their will due to wars, terrorism, natural disasters, or historical events like the slave trade.
4. **Voluntary migration:** People choose to move to improve their standard of living, find better jobs, or get an education.

### Causes of migration (Push and Pull factors)

Migration is driven by forces that push people away from their homes and forces that pull them toward a new place.

| Push Factors (Why people leave the source) | Pull Factors (Why people choose the destination) |
| --- | --- |
| Poverty, lack of jobs, and low wages | Abundant employment opportunities and higher wages |
| Poor social amenities (no electricity, bad roads) | Excellent amenities (good schools, hospitals, power) |
| Natural disasters (floods, drought, soil exhaustion) | Favourable climate and fertile land |
| War, terrorism, or political instability | Peace, safety, and political stability |

### Effects of migration
Migration drastically changes both the source and the destination regions.

**Effects on the Source Area (e.g., the village):**
* **Loss of able-bodied youth:** The most active, educated, and skilled young people leave. When highly skilled professionals (like doctors and engineers) leave a country, it is called **brain drain**.
* **Decline in agriculture:** With fewer strong young people left behind to farm, food production drops.
* **Aging population:** Mostly children and the elderly remain in the village.
* **Remittances:** Migrants often send money back home, which boosts the local economy and helps family members survive.

**Effects on the Destination Area (e.g., the city):**
* **Pressure on amenities:** Housing, transport, and hospitals become overcrowded.
* **Creation of slums:** A lack of affordable housing forces poor migrants to build makeshift homes in shanty towns.
* **Abundant cheap labour:** The large influx of people provides industries and businesses with plenty of workers.
* **Unemployment and crime:** If the city cannot provide enough jobs, many migrants turn to crime.
* **Cultural mixing:** People from different ethnic and cultural backgrounds interact, which can enrich the society but also sometimes leads to ethnic or religious tension.

## How population constitutes a resource

**Objective vi: account for the ways population constitute a resource.**

A country's people are not just mouths to feed; they are its most vital economic asset, known as **human resources**. A population constitutes a resource in the following ways:

1. **Provision of Labour:** A large, active working-age population provides the physical manpower needed for agriculture, mining, manufacturing, and construction. Without human labour, natural resources like oil or fertile soil cannot be extracted or processed.
2. **Market for Goods and Services:** A large population creates a massive domestic market. High demand for food, clothes, housing, and technology stimulates industrial growth, encourages investment, and expands trade.
3. **Brain Power and Innovation:** Humans provide ideas, invention, and technological advancement. An educated and skilled population (human capital) drives modern economic development through research, engineering, and business management.
4. **Defence and Security:** A larger population provides the personnel needed for a nation's military and police forces, ensuring the country is protected from internal and external threats.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Mixing up CBR and RNI:** Remember that the Crude Birth Rate is just the number of births per 1,000 people. The Rate of Natural Increase is found by subtracting the death rate from the birth rate.
* **Dependency ratio calculation errors:** When calculating the dependency ratio, the denominator is **only** the working population (ages 15–64), not the total population. Always remember to multiply the final fraction by 100 to get a percentage.
* **Population density calculation slips:** Do not divide the area by the population; always divide the **total population** by the **total land area**.
* **Confusing the West Coast of Southern Africa:** The syllabus references the West Coast of Southern Africa. This is a cold, desert region (Namib Desert) influenced by the Benguela Current, making it sparsely populated, not a tropical or densely populated area.
* **Japan's geography:** Remember that Japan is densely populated *despite* being over 70% mountainous, because the people are highly concentrated on the few flat coastal plains.
* **Push vs. Pull factors:** "Push" means reasons to leave (poverty, war). "Pull" means reasons to go there (jobs, good schools). Don't mix them up.

## Quick summary

1. Population density measures how crowded a place is by dividing the total population by the land area.
2. The rate of natural increase is the difference between the birth rate and the death rate. 
3. A broad-based population pyramid indicates a high birth rate and a large youthful population, typical of developing countries. The dependency ratio compares the young and elderly to the working-age population.
4. Population is distributed unevenly based on climate, relief, soils, and economic factors. The Amazon Basin and Namib Desert are sparse, while N.E. U.S.A., India, and Japan are highly dense.
5. Rapid population growth causes overpopulation, leading to unemployment, slums, and resource pressure.
6. Migration can be internal or international, driven by push factors (poverty, lack of amenities) and pull factors (jobs, better living standards). It causes a "brain drain" and agricultural decline in source areas.
7. Population acts as an economic resource by providing a labour force, a market for goods, human intelligence for innovation, and manpower for national defence.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following defines the rate of natural increase of a population?**
- A. The total number of live births in a year
- B. The difference between the birth rate and the death rate
- C. The addition of immigrants to a country
- D. The movement of people from rural to urban areas

**Answer: B.** The rate of natural increase is calculated strictly by subtracting the Crude Death Rate (CDR) from the Crude Birth Rate (CBR). It does not include migration.

**2. In a country, the population under 15 years is 60,000, the population aged 15 to 64 years is 80,000, and the population aged 65 and above is 10,000. What is the dependency ratio?**
- A. 46.7%
- B. 75.0%
- C. 87.5%
- D. 114.3%

**Answer: C.** 
Step 1: Identify dependent population (under 15 + 65 and above) = $60,000 + 10,000 = 70,000$. 
Step 2: Identify working population (15–64) = $80,000$. 
Step 3: Divide dependents by working population and multiply by 100. $(\frac{70,000}{80,000}) \times 100 = 87.5\%$. 
The most tempting wrong option is A (46.7%), which comes from mistakenly dividing the dependents by the total population (150,000) instead of just the working population.

**3. A state has a total population of 2,500,000 people and a total land area of 50,000 km². What is its population density?**
- A. 0.02 persons per km²
- B. 5 persons per km²
- C. 50 persons per km²
- D. 500 persons per km²

**Answer: C.** 
Step 1: State the formula for population density (Total Population / Total Land Area). 
Step 2: Substitute the values: $2,500,000 \div 50,000$. 
Step 3: Calculate to get 50 persons per km². 
Option A (0.02) is wrong because it inverts the formula (Area / Population), which is a common calculation slip.

**4. In a town of 40,000 people, there were 1,200 live births and 400 deaths in one year. What is the rate of natural increase expressed as a percentage?**
- A. 2.0%
- B. 3.0%
- C. 20.0%
- D. 30.0%

**Answer: A.** 
Step 1: Calculate the CBR. $CBR = (\frac{1,200}{40,000}) \times 1,000 = 30$ births per 1,000. 
Step 2: Calculate the CDR. $CDR = (\frac{400}{40,000}) \times 1,000 = 10$ deaths per 1,000. 
Step 3: Find the Rate of Natural Increase per 1,000. $RNI = 30 - 10 = 20$ per 1,000. 
Step 4: Convert to a percentage by dividing by 10. $20 \div 10 = 2.0\%$. 
Option C (20.0%) is a common mistake where the student calculates the rate per thousand but forgets to divide by 10 to convert it to a percentage.

**5. Which of the following is the main reason for the dense population in the North-Eastern U.S.A.?**
- A. Extensive rice cultivation due to heavy monsoon rains
- B. High industrialisation and excellent natural harbours
- C. A hot, wet equatorial climate all year round
- D. The presence of the cold Benguela Current

**Answer: B.** The North-Eastern U.S.A. (Megalopolis) is highly populated due to its history of immigration, massive industrial growth, and deep natural harbours that support trade. Monsoon rains (Option A) apply to India.

**6. The Amazon Basin is sparsely populated mainly because of its**
- A. high mountains and steep slopes
- B. highly mechanised agricultural sector
- C. dense rainforest, poor soils and hot, wet climate
- D. frequent earthquakes and volcanic eruptions

**Answer: C.** The Amazon Basin features an Equatorial climate with impenetrable tropical rainforests, leached latosol soils that are poor for farming, and prevalent tropical diseases, making it hostile to heavy human settlement.

**7. A population pyramid that has a very broad base and narrows sharply towards the top indicates a**
- A. low birth rate and large elderly population
- B. negative population growth rate
- C. country with a severe shortage of labour
- D. high birth rate and a large youthful population

**Answer: D.** The broad base represents a large number of children (high birth rate), and the sharp narrowing means few people live to old age (high death rate). This is typical of a growing, youthful population in developing countries.

**8. Which of the following is a "pull factor" in rural-urban migration?**
- A. Soil exhaustion in the village
- B. Lack of social amenities in rural areas
- C. Better employment opportunities in the city
- D. Ethnic conflict and war in the village

**Answer: C.** Pull factors are positive attributes of a destination that attract migrants. Better jobs pull people to the city. Options A, B, and D are push factors that drive people away from their homes.

**9. Despite being over 70% mountainous, Japan is densely populated. This density is largely supported by its**
- A. vast inland deserts and oases
- B. high level of industrialisation and concentration on coastal plains
- C. tropical rainforest resources
- D. nomadic cattle rearing

**Answer: B.** Because the mountainous interior is hard to inhabit, Japan's population is heavily crowded onto its few flat coastal plains, sustained by advanced industry, technology, and fishing.

**10. Which of the following best explains how a large population constitutes an economic resource?**
- A. It causes traffic congestion and housing shortages in cities.
- B. It increases the dependency ratio and strains social amenities.
- C. It leads to the rapid depletion of natural resources and deforestation.
- D. It provides a large labour force and a massive market for goods and services.

**Answer: D.** A population is a human resource because it supplies the workers needed for industries and agriculture, and creates a large domestic market of consumers that drives economic growth. Options A, B, and C describe the problems of overpopulation, not its resource value.
