## Location, Size, and Political Divisions of Nigeria

**Objective i: describe the location, size and political divisions of Nigeria.**
**Content i (part): Location, position, size, political division (states).**

### Location and Position
Nigeria is located in **West Africa**, along the **Gulf of Guinea** (an arm of the Atlantic Ocean). 
- **Latitudes:** It lies between latitudes **4°N and 14°N** of the Equator. Because it is entirely north of the Equator, it is in the Northern Hemisphere and falls within the tropics.
- **Longitudes:** It lies between longitudes **3°E and 15°E** of the Greenwich Meridian. This places it entirely in the Eastern Hemisphere.

### Size
Nigeria is a large country with a total land area of approximately **923,768 square kilometres (km²)**. 
- In terms of landmass, it is about four times the size of Ghana and about one-tenth the size of the USA.
- The distance from the south (the Atlantic coast) to the north is about 1,050 km, and from east to west is about 1,150 km.

### Political Divisions
At independence in 1960, Nigeria had three regions (Northern, Western, and Eastern). Over the years, these have been divided for easier administration and to bring government closer to the people.
Today, Nigeria's political divisions consist of:
- **36 States**, plus the **Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Abuja**.
- **774 Local Government Areas (LGAs)**.
- For political and administrative convenience, the country is unofficially grouped into **six geopolitical zones**:
  1. **North-West:** Kano, Kaduna, Katsina, Sokoto, Kebbi, Zamfara, Jigawa.
  2. **North-East:** Borno, Yobe, Adamawa, Taraba, Bauchi, Gombe.
  3. **North-Central (Middle Belt):** Plateau, Nasarawa, Benue, Kogi, Kwara, Niger, FCT.
  4. **South-West:** Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Osun, Ondo, Ekiti.
  5. **South-East:** Enugu, Anambra, Imo, Abia, Ebonyi.
  6. **South-South (Niger Delta region):** Rivers, Delta, Bayelsa, Akwa Ibom, Cross River, Edo.

## Boundaries and Neighbours of Nigeria

**Objective ii: identify the boundaries and neighbours of Nigeria.**
**Content ii: Boundaries and neighbours.**

Nigeria is surrounded by Francophone (French-speaking) countries on its land borders. 

| Direction | Neighbouring Country / Boundary | Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **North** | **Niger Republic** | A long, mostly dry land boundary separating northern Nigerian states (like Sokoto, Katsina, Kano, Jigawa, Yobe) from Niger. |
| **North-East** | **Chad Republic** | A short boundary located in the Lake Chad basin. |
| **East** | **Cameroon** | A long, mountainous boundary running along the Eastern Highlands (Adamawa, Mandara, and Obudu mountains) down to the coast. |
| **West** | **Benin Republic** | A land boundary separating western states (like Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Kwara, Niger, Kebbi) from Benin. |
| **South** | **The Atlantic Ocean** | The southern boundary is formed by the **Gulf of Guinea** (specifically the Bight of Bonny and Bight of Benin), providing access to international shipping routes. |

## Ethnic Groups and Their Distribution

**Objective iii: identify the ethnic groups and their distributions.**
**Content i (part): peoples.**

Nigeria is a multi-ethnic nation with over **250 distinct ethnic groups**, each with its own language and culture. 

### Major Ethnic Groups
Three ethnic groups are considered the largest, accounting for nearly 60% of the population:
1. **Hausa/Fulani:** Mostly found in the **North-West** and **North-East** (e.g., Kano, Kaduna, Sokoto, Katsina). They are traditionally farmers, traders, and nomadic cattle rearers (the Fulani).
2. **Yoruba:** Predominantly located in the **South-West** (e.g., Lagos, Oyo, Ogun, Osun, Ondo, Ekiti) and parts of Kwara and Kogi states. They are known for early urbanization, trading, and agriculture (cocoa).
3. **Igbo:** Concentrated in the **South-East** (e.g., Enugu, Anambra, Imo, Abia, Ebonyi) and parts of Delta and Rivers. They are highly noted for trading, business enterprise, and manufacturing.

### Minority Ethnic Groups
The remaining groups, often referred to as minority groups, are spread across the Middle Belt and the southern coastal areas:
- **Tiv, Igala, Idoma, Nupe, Gbagyi (Gwari), Birom:** Mostly found in the **North-Central** (Middle Belt) region (Benue, Kogi, Niger, Plateau).
- **Kanuri:** Dominant in the **North-East**, specifically in Borno and Yobe states.
- **Ijaw, Edo, Urhobo, Itsekiri:** Found in the western part of the **South-South** (Rivers, Bayelsa, Delta, Edo).
- **Efik and Ibibio:** Located in the eastern part of the **South-South** (Cross River and Akwa Ibom).

## Physical Setting and Its Effects on Human Activities

**Objective iv: relate the components of physical settings to their effects on human activities.**
**Content iii: Physical setting: geology, relief, landform, climate and drainage, vegetation and soils.**

The physical environment directly dictates what people can do to survive, from what crops they plant to where they build cities and what minerals they mine.

### 1. Geology
Nigeria's rock structure is divided almost equally into two types:
*   **Basement Complex Rocks:** These are ancient, hard igneous and metamorphic rocks. They cover about 50% of Nigeria (mostly in the West, parts of the North, and the Eastern Highlands). 
    *   *Effect on human activities:* They are rich in solid minerals like **gold** (Zamfara, Ilesha) and **tin/columbite** (Jos Plateau). However, they have poor groundwater storage, making borehole drilling difficult.
*   **Sedimentary Basins:** These are younger, softer rocks laid down by water over millions of years. They cover the other 50% of the country (the Niger Delta, the Niger-Benue trough, the Chad Basin, and the Sokoto plains).
    *   *Effect on human activities:* They are rich in fossil fuels (**petroleum and natural gas** in the Niger Delta, **coal** in Enugu) and **limestone** (Ogun, Kogi, Sokoto). They are also excellent for groundwater (aquifers).

### 2. Relief and Landforms
Relief refers to the high and low lands. Nigeria has three main highland areas and three main lowland areas.
*   **Highlands:** 
    *   *North-Central Plateau* (including the Jos Plateau, rising up to 1,800 m).
    *   *Eastern Highlands* (Adamawa, Mandara, and Obudu mountains, rising over 2,000 m; highest point is Chappal Waddi at 2,419 m).
    *   *Western Highlands* (Idanre hills, Yoruba highlands).
    *   *Effect on human activities:* The cool climate of the Jos Plateau and Obudu makes them suitable for **tourism**, dairy farming, and cultivating temperate crops (like apples, tea, and strawberries). However, rugged terrain makes road construction difficult.
*   **Lowlands:** 
    *   *Coastal Plains* (south).
    *   *Niger-Benue River Trough* (middle).
    *   *Chad Basin* (north-east).
    *   *Effect on human activities:* Flat lands are excellent for **agriculture, settlement, and road construction**. However, coastal and river lowlands are heavily prone to **flooding**.

### 3. Climate
Nigeria has a tropical climate with two distinct seasons: the **wet season** (brought by the warm, moist South-West Trade Winds from the Atlantic) and the **dry season** (brought by the cold, dusty North-East Trade Winds, known as the **Harmattan**, from the Sahara Desert).

*   **South:** High temperatures, high rainfall (over 2,000 mm annually), and a long wet season. 
    *   *Effect:* Supports the cultivation of tree crops (cocoa, rubber, oil palm) and root crops (yam, cassava). High rainfall causes severe leaching of soils and coastal flooding.
*   **North:** High temperatures, low rainfall (under 1,000 mm annually), and a long dry season.
    *   *Effect:* Supports grain farming (millet, sorghum, maize), legumes (groundnuts, cowpeas), and nomadic pastoralism (cattle rearing) due to the absence of the tsetse fly. Very dry areas suffer from drought and desertification.

### 4. Drainage
Nigeria is well-drained by a network of rivers. The drainage pattern is dominated by the **River Niger** (enters from the north-west) and its main tributary, the **River Benue** (enters from the east). They meet at a confluence in **Lokoja** and flow southward into the Atlantic Ocean, forming a massive delta.
*   **Other rivers:** Cross River, Ogun, Osun, Sokoto, Kaduna, and Hadejia.
*   **Lake Chad:** A shallow inland drainage lake in the north-east where rivers like Yobe flow into, but no rivers flow out to the sea.
*   *Effect on human activities:* Rivers provide water for **domestic use, fishing, irrigation** (e.g., the Sokoto-Rima and Chad basins), and **hydro-electric power (HEP)** generation (e.g., Kainji and Jebba dams on the River Niger, Shiroro on the River Kaduna). The rivers also provide a cheap means of inland water transport, though rapids and seasonal low water levels can be problematic.

### 5. Vegetation and Soils
*   **Vegetation:** Moves in broad east-west belts from the wet south to the dry north:
    *   *Mangrove and Freshwater Swamps* (coast): Useful for timber (mahogany), fishing, and salt making.
    *   *Tropical Rainforest* (south): Supports massive lumbering activities (Iroko, Obeche, Mahogany) and tree crop farming.
    *   *Savanna (Guinea, Sudan, Sahel)* (middle and north): Grasslands dotted with trees. Highly suitable for grazing cattle and large-scale mechanized cereal farming.
*   **Soils:** 
    *   *Alluvial Soils:* Found along river valleys and the coast. Very fertile and great for rice cultivation.
    *   *Ferruginous/Lateritic Soils:* Found across much of the interior. They are highly leached (nutrients washed down by heavy rain) and often require fertilizers.
    *   *Sandy Soils:* Found in the far north. They drain too quickly and are prone to wind erosion.

## Population: Size, Distribution, and Patterns

**Objective v: account for the pattern of population distribution.**
**Content iv (part): Population: size, distribution.**

### Population Size
At the time of writing, Nigeria's population is estimated at **over 200 million people**, making it the most populous country in Africa and one of the most populous in the world. The population is characterized by a high birth rate, a high natural increase, and a large proportion of young people.

### Population Distribution
The population of Nigeria is not evenly distributed across the land. It is clustered in specific zones based on favorable physical and economic conditions.

| Density Zone | Description | Major Areas/Cities | Reasons for Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Densely Populated Areas** | More than 200 persons per km². Very crowded with high economic activity. | **Lagos, Ibadan, Kano, Onitsha, Aba, Port Harcourt, Enugu.** | **Historical:** Centres of early kingdoms (Kano, Ibadan). <br>**Economic:** Commercial hubs, industries, jobs, and ports (Lagos, Port Harcourt, Aba). <br>**Physical:** Fertile soils for agriculture. |
| **Moderately Populated Areas** | 50 to 200 persons per km². | Parts of the Middle Belt, Edo (Benin), Cross River (Calabar), Kaduna. | Moderate rainfall, decent soils, and growing administrative centers (state capitals). |
| **Sparsely Populated Areas** | Less than 50 persons per km². Vast areas of empty or lightly settled land. | **Borno, Yobe, Taraba, Niger, parts of Kwara and Kogi.** | **Physical challenges:** Extreme dryness, drought, and desertification (Borno/Yobe). <br>Rugged terrain or dense, inaccessible forests (parts of Taraba/Cross River). <br>Presence of pests like tsetse fly or river blindness (historically in the Middle Belt). |

## Migration: Types, Problems, and Effects

**Objective vi: examine the types of migration, their problems and effects.**
**Content iv (part): migration, (types, problems and effects).**

Migration is the movement of people from one geographical location to another with the intention of settling temporarily or permanently.

### Types of Migration
1. **Internal Migration:** Movement within the country.
   *   **Rural-Urban:** Moving from villages to cities (e.g., moving from a village in Osun to Lagos). This is the most common type in Nigeria, driven by the search for jobs, better education, and modern amenities.
   *   **Rural-Rural:** Moving from one village to another (e.g., nomadic Fulani herdsmen moving south, or migrant farmers looking for fertile land).
   *   **Urban-Rural:** Moving from a city back to a village (usually retirees, or people escaping city hardship).
   *   **Urban-Urban:** Moving from one city to a larger or more prosperous one (e.g., from Akure to Lagos).
2. **International Migration:** Movement across international borders.
   *   **Emigration:** Leaving Nigeria to live in another country (e.g., moving to the UK, USA, or Canada).
   *   **Immigration:** People from other countries moving into Nigeria (e.g., ECOWAS citizens).

### Problems and Effects of Migration

Migration affects both the source region (where people leave) and the destination region (where they go). 

**Effects of Rural-Urban Migration:**
*   **On the Urban Area (Destination):**
    *   *Problems:* Overpopulation and congestion, leading to traffic jams and high crime rates. Overstretching of social amenities (water, electricity, hospitals). Creation of slums (e.g., Makoko in Lagos). High unemployment.
    *   *Benefits:* Provides a large pool of cheap labour for urban industries. Cultural integration.
*   **On the Rural Area (Source):**
    *   *Problems:* Shortage of able-bodied workers, causing a decline in agricultural production (which leads to food scarcity). The rural population becomes an aging one (mostly the elderly and young children left behind).
    *   *Benefits:* Remittances (money sent back home by city workers helps develop the village). Reduces population pressure on rural farm land.

**Effects of International Migration:**
*   **Brain Drain:** The loss of highly skilled professionals (doctors, engineers, nurses) to developed countries severely damages Nigeria's healthcare and technical sectors.
*   **Remittances:** The money Nigerians abroad send home is a massive source of foreign exchange for the country.

## Natural Resources: Types and Distribution

**Objective vii: identify the types of natural resources and their distribution.**
**Content v (part): Natural resources: types (minerals, soils, water, vegetation, etc.) distribution.**

A natural resource is anything provided by nature that is useful to humans. Nigeria is heavily endowed with these.

### 1. Mineral Resources
Minerals are divided into energy (fuel) minerals and solid minerals.
*   **Petroleum and Natural Gas:** The backbone of Nigeria's economy. Located in the sedimentary rocks of the Niger Delta (Rivers, Bayelsa, Delta, Akwa Ibom) and offshore in the Atlantic.
*   **Coal:** Used historically for running trains and generating power. Found largely in **Enugu** (Onyeama and Iva Valley mines) and parts of Kogi.
*   **Limestone:** Essential for manufacturing cement. Found in **Ewekoro/Shagamu** (Ogun), **Obajana** (Kogi), **Gboko** (Benue), and **Sokoto**.
*   **Tin and Columbite:** Mined on the **Jos Plateau**.
*   **Gold:** Found in the basement complex rocks of **Ilesha** (Osun state) and **Zamfara** state.
*   **Lead and Zinc:** Found in **Abakaliki** (Ebonyi) and parts of Taraba.
*   **Iron Ore:** Vital for steel production. Found heavily in **Itakpe** (Kogi state).

### 2. Soil Resources
*   **Alluvial soils** along the Niger-Benue trough and coastal deltas support rice and sugarcane.
*   **Loamy/Forest soils** in the south support cocoa, rubber, and oil palm.
*   **Sandy/Savanna soils** in the north support groundnuts, cotton, and cereals.

### 3. Water Resources
*   **Surface Water:** The Atlantic Ocean, Rivers Niger and Benue, Lake Chad, and numerous smaller rivers provide water for drinking, irrigation, fishing, transportation, and HEP (Kainji, Shiroro).
*   **Groundwater:** Stored in aquifers, primarily in the sedimentary basins (like the Chad Basin artesian wells).

### 4. Vegetation Resources
*   **Forests:** Provide valuable timber (hardwoods like Iroko, Mahogany, Obeche) mostly in Edo, Ondo, and Cross River states. They also provide medicinal plants, wild fruits, and rubber.
*   **Savanna:** The vast grasslands of the north provide excellent pasture for millions of cattle, sheep, and goats.

## Uses and Conservation of Natural Resources

**Objective viii: indicate the uses and conservation of natural resources.**
**Content v (part): uses and conservation.**

### Uses of Natural Resources
*   **Revenue Generation:** Exporting crude oil and natural gas accounts for over 80% of Nigeria's foreign exchange earnings.
*   **Raw Materials for Industries:** Limestone for cement, iron ore for steel making, timber for furniture, and cotton for textiles.
*   **Food and Agriculture:** Soils provide the base for farming; rivers and oceans provide fish.
*   **Energy:** Rivers provide HEP; coal and gas run thermal power stations; wood provides fuel for rural cooking.
*   **Employment:** Mining, farming, fishing, and lumbering employ millions of Nigerians.

### Conservation of Natural Resources
Conservation means the careful, sustainable use and management of natural resources so they are not wasted, destroyed, or exhausted for future generations.

**1. Conservation of Minerals (Non-renewable):**
*   Since minerals cannot grow back once mined, they must be conserved by **recycling** scrap metals.
*   Preventing wastage during extraction (e.g., stopping gas flaring in the Niger Delta).
*   Developing alternative energy sources (solar, wind) to reduce over-reliance on petroleum and coal.

**2. Conservation of Vegetation (Forests):**
*   **Afforestation:** Planting trees in areas that have never had forests, especially in the north to stop desertification.
*   **Reforestation:** Planting two or more trees for every tree cut down in timber regions.
*   Creating **Forest Reserves** and National Parks (e.g., Cross River National Park, Yankari Games Reserve).
*   Enacting strict laws against indiscriminate logging, bush burning, and poaching.

**3. Conservation of Soil:**
*   Practicing good farming methods like **crop rotation**, **cover cropping**, and **mulching** to maintain soil nutrients.
*   Using **terracing** and contour ploughing on highlands (like the Jos Plateau) to prevent soil erosion.
*   Avoiding overgrazing by cattle.

**4. Conservation of Water:**
*   Building dams and reservoirs (like Kainji or Tiga Dam) to store water for the dry season.
*   Treating industrial waste before releasing it into rivers to prevent **water pollution**.
*   Protecting watersheds (the highland forests where rivers begin) from deforestation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Relief** | The physical shape, height, and slope of the land (highlands and lowlands). |
| **Geology** | The rock structure of an area; in Nigeria, mostly split between Basement Complex and Sedimentary rocks. |
| **Harmattan** | A dry, dusty North-East Trade Wind that blows from the Sahara Desert across Nigeria during the dry season. |
| **Confluence** | The place where two rivers meet (e.g., Lokoja, where the Niger and Benue meet). |
| **Brain Drain** | The emigration of highly skilled and educated people from a country, negatively affecting its economy. |
| **Remittances** | Money transferred by a foreign worker back to their home country. |
| **Conservation** | The careful and sustainable management of natural resources to prevent exhaustion. |
| **Afforestation** | Planting trees to create a forest in an area that previously lacked one. |
| **Reforestation** | Planting trees to replace those that have been cut down. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Mistaking the latitudes and longitudes:** A very common JAMB trap. Nigeria is **4°N to 14°N** and **3°E to 15°E**. Do not mix up the North and East, or the numbers!
*   **Confusing rock types and minerals:** Remember: **Solid minerals** (gold, tin, columbite) are found in **Basement Complex rocks**. **Fossil fuels** (crude oil, natural gas, coal) and **limestone** are found in **Sedimentary basins**.
*   **Highlands vs. Lowlands impact:** Highlands like the Jos Plateau are cooler, and because they are free of tsetse flies, they are great for cattle and temperate crops, but bad for road building. Lowlands are prone to flooding.
*   **Neighbours:** Memorize the map borders. Cameroon is East, Benin is West, Niger is North, Chad is North-East. 
*   **Rural-urban migration effects:** Distinguish between effects on the rural area (source) and the urban area (destination). Loss of able-bodied men is a rural problem; slum creation is an urban problem.

## Quick summary

1. Nigeria lies between latitudes 4°N to 14°N and longitudes 3°E to 15°E, with an area of about 923,768 km², divided into 36 states and the FCT.
2. It is bordered by Niger (North), Chad (North-East), Cameroon (East), Benin (West), and the Atlantic Ocean (South).
3. The population exceeds 200 million, comprising over 250 ethnic groups (major ones: Hausa/Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo).
4. Nigeria's geology is roughly 50% Basement Complex (gold, tin) and 50% Sedimentary rocks (petroleum, coal, limestone).
5. The climate is tropical, with a wet south (rainforest, root crops) and a dry north (savanna, grain crops, cattle).
6. Population density is highest in commercial, fertile, and historical centres (Lagos, Kano, Aba) and lowest in dry or rugged areas (Borno, Taraba).
7. Rural-urban migration is the dominant type of movement, causing urban congestion and rural agricultural decline.
8. Natural resources (minerals, water, soil, vegetation) must be conserved through recycling, afforestation, pollution control, and sustainable farming.

## Practice questions

**1. The latitudinal and longitudinal extent of Nigeria is approximately**
- A. 4°S to 14°S and 3°W to 15°W
- B. 4°N to 14°N and 3°E to 15°E
- C. 3°N to 15°N and 4°E to 14°E
- D. 14°N to 24°N and 3°E to 15°E

**Answer: B.** Nigeria is entirely north of the equator (4°N to 14°N) and entirely east of the Greenwich Meridian (3°E to 15°E).

**2. Which of the following countries shares Nigeria's eastern boundary?**
- A. Benin Republic
- B. Niger Republic
- C. Cameroon
- D. Ghana

**Answer: C.** Cameroon forms the long eastern boundary of Nigeria. Benin is to the west, and Niger is to the north.

**3. The ethnic group predominantly found in the North-East of Nigeria is the**
- A. Yoruba
- B. Kanuri
- C. Igbo
- D. Tiv

**Answer: B.** The Kanuri are the dominant ethnic group in the North-East, particularly in Borno and Yobe states.

**4. Which of the following minerals is associated with the sedimentary rocks of Nigeria?**
- A. Tin
- B. Columbite
- C. Gold
- D. Limestone

**Answer: D.** Limestone (along with coal and petroleum) is found in sedimentary basins. Tin, columbite, and gold are found in the Basement Complex rocks.

**5. The cool climate of the Jos Plateau, which supports dairy farming and temperate crops, is primarily a result of its**
- A. latitude
- B. vegetation
- C. relief
- D. proximity to the ocean

**Answer: C.** Relief (altitude) lowers temperature. The high elevation of the Jos Plateau makes it significantly cooler than the surrounding lowlands.

**6. A major negative effect of rural-urban migration on the rural source regions in Nigeria is**
- A. the creation of urban slums
- B. an increase in the crime rate
- C. traffic congestion
- D. a decline in agricultural production

**Answer: D.** As able-bodied youths move to the cities, the rural areas are left with an aging population, which leads to a severe shortage of farm labour and decreased food production. Options A, B, and C are problems for the urban destination, not the rural source.

**7. Which of the following pairs of cities represents highly densely populated areas in Nigeria?**
- A. Lagos and Kano
- B. Jalingo and Damaturu
- C. Minna and Lokoja
- D. Maiduguri and Makurdi

**Answer: A.** Lagos and Kano are major commercial and historical hubs, making them some of the most densely populated cities in Nigeria.

**8. The practice of planting trees in an area where there was previously no forest is known as**
- A. deforestation
- B. selective logging
- C. afforestation
- D. reforestation

**Answer: C.** Afforestation is the creation of a new forest. Reforestation (Option D) is replacing a forest that has been cut down.

**9. Which of the following rivers flows into Lake Chad?**
- A. River Niger
- B. River Benue
- C. River Yobe
- D. River Cross

**Answer: C.** River Yobe flows into the inland drainage basin of Lake Chad. The Niger, Benue, and Cross rivers flow southward toward the Atlantic Ocean.

**10. Coal in Nigeria is largely mined in**
- A. Jos
- B. Enugu
- C. Itakpe
- D. Ewekoro

**Answer: B.** Enugu is the historic centre of coal mining in Nigeria. Jos is known for tin, Itakpe for iron ore, and Ewekoro for limestone.
