## Agricultural Systems in Nigeria

Agriculture remains a major pillar of the Nigerian economy, providing food for the population and raw materials for industries. Farmers in Nigeria use different methods to cultivate crops and rear animals depending on the land available, the climate, and the level of technology.

### Comparing the farming systems practised in Nigeria

**Objective i: compare the farming systems practised in Nigeria.**

Farming systems are the various ways farmers organise their land, labour, and capital to produce crops and livestock. The most important systems practised in Nigeria include:

1. **Shifting Cultivation:** The farmer clears a piece of land, farms it for a few years until the soil loses its fertility, and then completely abandons it to clear a new plot. Crucially, the farmer **moves his settlement and family** to the new location. Due to population growth and land scarcity, this system is now very rare.
2. **Rotational Bush Fallowing:** This is an improvement over shifting cultivation. The farmer cultivates a plot of land for a period and then leaves it to rest (fallow) so it can regain its fertility naturally while he moves to another plot. Unlike shifting cultivation, **the farmer's settlement remains permanent**.
3. **Crop Rotation:** This involves growing different types of crops on the same piece of land in a definite sequence or cycle over a period of years (e.g., planting deep-rooted yams one year, followed by shallow-rooted maize, and then nitrogen-fixing cowpeas). It helps maintain soil fertility and breaks the life cycle of pests.
4. **Mixed Farming:** This is the cultivation of crops and the rearing of animals on the **same piece of land**. The system is highly efficient because the crops and animals support each other: animal droppings provide organic manure for the crops, while crop residues (like maize stalks) are used as feed for the animals.
5. **Plantation Agriculture:** This involves the large-scale, commercial cultivation of a single cash crop (mono-cropping), such as cocoa, rubber, or oil palm. It requires huge capital, extensive land, hired labour, and scientific management. 
6. **Pastoral Farming (Nomadic Herding):** This system involves the rearing of grazing animals (like cattle, sheep, and goats). In Nigeria, it is heavily practised by Fulani herdsmen who move from place to place in search of pasture and water—a seasonal movement known as **transhumance**.

| Feature | Bush Fallowing | Mixed Farming | Plantation Agriculture | Nomadic Herding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary focus** | Growing food crops | Crops and animals together | Single cash crop (mono-cropping) | Rearing livestock |
| **Scale** | Small scale (peasant) | Small to medium scale | Very large scale (estates) | Large herds over vast areas |
| **Settlement** | Permanent | Permanent | Permanent | Mobile (nomadic) |
| **Capital & Tools** | Low; hoes and cutlasses | Moderate; some machinery/ploughs | Very high; mechanised | Low; relies on natural pasture |
| **Purpose** | Mostly subsistence | Subsistence and commercial | Strictly commercial (export/industry) | Subsistence and commercial |

### Major crops produced and problems encountered

**Objective ii: identify the crops produced and the problems encountered.**

Nigeria's diverse climate allows for the cultivation of a wide variety of crops. Crops are broadly divided into **food crops** (grown mainly for local consumption) and **cash crops** (grown mainly for sale, export, or industrial use).

**Major Food Crops:**
- **Root crops:** Yam and cassava (widely grown in the South and Middle Belt).
- **Cereals:** Sorghum, millet, and maize (dominant in the North due to lower rainfall), and rice (grown in swampy or irrigated areas like Kebbi and Ebonyi).
- **Legumes:** Cowpeas (beans) and groundnuts, mostly grown in the North.

**Major Cash Crops and their Locations:**
- **Cocoa:** The major cash crop of the **South-West** (Ondo, Osun, Oyo, Ekiti, Ogun) and parts of the South-South (Cross River).
- **Oil Palm:** Thrives in the heavy rainfall of the **South-East** and **South-South** (Edo, Delta, Rivers, Imo, Abia, Akwa Ibom).
- **Rubber:** Grown mainly in the rain forests of **Edo and Delta States**.
- **Groundnut and Cotton:** These require less rainfall and are the dominant cash crops in the **North** (Kano, Katsina, Kaduna, Jigawa).

**Problems of Agricultural Development in Nigeria:**
Despite its potential, agriculture in Nigeria faces severe challenges:
1. **Land Tenure System:** Traditional inheritance laws divide land among many children, leading to **land fragmentation**. These small, scattered plots make large-scale mechanised farming impossible.
2. **Inadequate Finance and Credit:** Peasant farmers lack the capital to buy modern machinery, improved seeds, or fertilisers, and they often cannot access bank loans because they lack collateral.
3. **Poor Transport Infrastructure:** Bad rural roads make it difficult and expensive to transport harvested crops to urban markets, leading to spoilage.
4. **Lack of Storage and Processing Facilities:** A significant percentage of farm produce (especially perishable items like tomatoes and yams) rots away post-harvest because there are no silos or cold-storage facilities.
5. **Pests and Diseases:** Crops are attacked by pests (like locusts and stem borers) and diseases (like cassava mosaic and cocoa black pod), while livestock suffer from diseases like trypanosomiasis (carried by the tsetse fly).
6. **Unpredictable Climate:** Reliance on rain-fed agriculture makes farmers vulnerable to droughts and floods. There is inadequate irrigation infrastructure.
7. **Rural-Urban Migration:** Able-bodied youths leave the villages for the cities in search of white-collar jobs, leaving farming to the elderly.
8. **Use of Crude Implements:** The continued use of simple tools like the hoe and cutlass limits the amount of land a farmer can cultivate and keeps yields low.

## Manufacturing Industries

**Content ii: Manufacturing Industries.**

Manufacturing is the process of converting raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods. In Nigeria, the manufacturing sector is growing but still faces significant hurdles.

### Types and location of major manufacturing industries

**Objective iii: identify the types and location of the major manufacturing industries.**

Manufacturing industries are broadly grouped into **light industries** (producing consumer goods like food, clothes, and shoes on a relatively small scale) and **heavy industries** (producing capital goods like steel, cement, and refined petroleum, requiring massive machinery and capital).

**Major Industries and their Locations in Nigeria:**
1. **Cement Industry (Heavy):** Uses bulky limestone. Located in **Obajana** (Kogi), **Ewekoro** (Ogun), **Sokoto** (Sokoto), **Gboko** (Benue), and **Nkalagu** (Ebonyi).
2. **Textile Industry (Light):** Uses cotton. Historically concentrated in **Kano**, **Kaduna**, and **Lagos**.
3. **Petroleum Refining (Heavy):** Processes crude oil. Located in **Port Harcourt** (Rivers), **Warri** (Delta), **Kaduna** (Kaduna), and the newer Dangote Refinery in **Lagos**.
4. **Iron and Steel Industry (Heavy):** Essential for industrialisation. Located in **Ajaokuta** (Kogi), **Aladja** (Delta), and **Osogbo** (Osun).
5. **Food and Beverages (Light):** Processes agricultural products into beer, soft drinks, flour, and packaged foods. Found heavily in major cities like **Lagos**, **Ibadan**, **Kano**, and **Aba**.
6. **Vehicle Assembly (Heavy):** Assembles imported parts. Examples include Peugeot in **Kaduna**, Volkswagen in **Lagos**, and Innoson Motors in **Nnewi** (Anambra).

### Factors of industrial location and problems associated with industries

**Objective iv: determine the factors of industrial location and the problems associated with the industries.**

**Factors Influencing Industrial Location:**
Entrepreneurs do not place factories randomly. They consider several factors to minimise costs and maximise profits:
1. **Nearness to Raw Materials:** Industries that use bulky, weight-losing raw materials (like limestone for cement or sugarcane for sugar) locate close to the source to save on transport costs. Industries that process perishable goods (like fruit canning) also locate near farms.
2. **Nearness to Market:** Industries that produce fragile goods (glass), highly perishable goods (bread), or weight-gaining goods (soft drinks, where water is added to a concentrate) must locate close to the consumers.
3. **Power Supply:** Heavy industries, such as iron and steel or aluminium smelting, consume massive amounts of electricity and are often located near power stations.
4. **Transport and Communication:** Factories need good roads, railways, or ports to bring in raw materials and ship out finished goods. This is why many factories are in coastal cities like Lagos and Port Harcourt.
5. **Availability of Labour:** Industries require both skilled technical workers and unskilled labour, drawing factories to highly populated urban centres.
6. **Government Policy:** A government might force an industry to locate in a rural or underdeveloped area to create jobs and ensure balanced regional development, or establish industrial estates with tax incentives to attract investors.

**Problems Associated with Manufacturing:**
- **Erratic Power Supply:** The most severe problem in Nigeria. Factories rely on expensive diesel generators, which drastically increases the cost of production.
- **Shortage of Raw Materials:** Poor agricultural output and restrictions on imports often leave factories without enough inputs.
- **Inadequate Capital:** High interest rates from banks make it hard for industrialists to expand.
- **Poor Transport Network:** Bad roads delay the delivery of raw materials and the distribution of finished goods.

**Problems Associated with Marketing Manufactured Goods:**
- **Smuggling and Dumping:** Cheap, substandard foreign goods are smuggled into the country, heavily undercutting local manufacturers who have higher production costs.
- **Low Purchasing Power:** Widespread poverty means many citizens cannot afford manufactured goods, reducing the market size.
- **Poor Quality:** Some local goods are of lower quality compared to imported alternatives, making consumers reluctant to buy them.

## Transportation and Communication

**Content iii: Transportation and Communication.**

Transportation is the physical movement of people, goods, and services from one place to another. Communication is the transmission of information, ideas, or messages without the physical movement of the communicators. 

### Relationship between transport and communication

**Objective v: establish the relationship between transport and communication.**

Transport and communication are deeply interconnected and support each other in several ways:
1. **Substitution:** Effective communication can replace the need for physical transport. A phone call, an email, or a video conference eliminates the need to travel hundreds of kilometres to deliver a message or hold a meeting.
2. **Enhancement of Safety and Efficiency:** Transport systems cannot function safely without communication. Air traffic controllers use radios and radar to guide aeroplanes; ships use maritime communication to avoid collisions; railways use signalling systems to prevent crashes.
3. **Facilitation of Trade:** In business, communication is used to order goods and track shipments, while transport is used to physically deliver those goods to the buyer.
4. **Shared Networks:** Very often, communication infrastructure (like telephone lines or underground fibre-optic cables) is laid directly alongside transport routes (like roads or railway lines) because the land has already been cleared and secured.

### Modes of transportation and communication and their relative advantages and disadvantages

**Objective vi: relate the modes of transportation and communication to their relative advantages and disadvantages.**

**Modes of Transportation:**

| Mode | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Road** | Most flexible; provides **door-to-door** service; feeds other transport modes; relatively cheap over short distances. | High rate of accidents; causes traffic congestion; limited capacity for heavy goods; roads require constant, expensive maintenance. |
| **Rail** | Best for moving **very bulky goods** over long distances on land; cheaper than road for heavy cargo; less affected by weather; safer than road. | Inflexible (fixed to tracks); cannot provide door-to-door service; very high cost of construction; slow in many developing countries. |
| **Water** (Inland and Ocean) | The **cheapest mode** overall; best for moving exceptionally heavy and bulky goods internationally (crude oil, cars); the track (the ocean) requires no maintenance. | **Very slow**; affected by weather (storms) and seasons (rivers drying up); risk of piracy; building ports is extremely expensive. |
| **Air** | The **fastest mode** of transport; ideal for urgent, highly perishable goods, and long-distance passenger travel; not hindered by mountains or forests. | The **most expensive** mode; very low carrying capacity; highly sensitive to bad weather (fog, storms); severe consequences if an accident occurs. |
| **Pipeline** | Very cheap to operate once built; the safest mode for transporting liquids and gases (crude oil, natural gas, water); provides a continuous flow. | Completely inflexible (fixed route); can only carry specific products (liquids/gases); highly prone to **vandalism and sabotage** (a major issue in the Niger Delta). |

**Modes of Communication:**

1. **Telecommunications (Mobile Phones, Internet, Radio Communication):**
   - *Advantages:* Instantaneous communication over global distances; allows for immediate feedback; the internet allows sharing of huge data files.
   - *Disadvantages:* Requires reliable electricity; can be expensive; dependent on network coverage, which is often poor in rural areas.
2. **Mass Media (Radio, Television, Newspapers):**
   - *Advantages:* Reaches millions of people simultaneously; radio is very effective for reaching remote rural populations in local languages.
   - *Disadvantages:* Mostly a one-way communication channel (delayed or no immediate feedback); newspapers require the audience to be literate.
3. **Postal Services (e.g., NIPOST):**
   - *Advantages:* Relatively cheap; can transport physical documents and small parcels anywhere in the world.
   - *Disadvantages:* Very slow compared to electronic communication; risks of letters being lost or damaged in transit.

## Trade: Regional and International Trade

**Content iv: Trade.**

Trade is the buying and selling of goods and services. It is divided into Internal (Local) Trade and International (External) Trade. In Geography, "Regional Trade" can refer to trade between different regions within a country (Inter-regional trade) or trade among countries in a specific geographical bloc (like ECOWAS).

### Major commodities of regional and international trade

**Objective vii: classify the major commodities of regional and international trade.**

**1. Inter-Regional Trade (Internal Trade within Nigeria):**
Nigeria's vast size means the North and South have different climates, producing different goods. This encourages massive internal trade:
- **From the North to the South:** The North supplies livestock (cattle, sheep, goats), hides and skins, groundnuts, beans, onions, tomatoes, and cotton.
- **From the South to the North:** The South supplies kolanut, palm oil, garri, plantain, yam, and manufactured goods (from industrial hubs like Lagos and Aba).

**2. Regional Trade (within ECOWAS):**
Nigeria trades with its West African neighbours. 
- **Exports to ECOWAS:** Crude oil, refined petroleum, manufactured plastics, and agricultural produce.
- **Imports from ECOWAS:** Livestock from Niger and Mali (Chad, a neighbour outside ECOWAS, also supplies livestock), specific manufactured items.

**3. International (External) Trade:**
This is trade between Nigeria and the rest of the world (e.g., USA, China, Europe).
- **Major Exports:** Nigeria's export trade is dangerously dominated by one commodity: **crude oil**, which accounts for over 80% of export earnings. Other exports include natural gas, cocoa, rubber, palm kernels, and sesame seeds.
- **Major Imports:** Because of its weak manufacturing sector, Nigeria imports heavy machinery, motor vehicles, electronics, chemicals, refined petroleum, and pharmaceuticals.

### Advantages and disadvantages of trade

**Advantages of Trade:**
- **Foreign Exchange:** Exporting goods generates foreign exchange needed for development.
- **Variety of Goods:** It allows a country to consume goods it cannot produce (e.g., Nigeria importing advanced medical equipment).
- **Employment:** Millions of people work in the import/export sector, logistics, and retail.
- **Government Revenue:** The government earns massive revenue from tariffs, import duties, and export taxes.
- **International Relations:** Trade fosters peaceful relationships and cooperation between nations.

**Disadvantages of Trade:**
- **Dumping:** Substandard or highly subsidised foreign goods can flood the market, killing local infant industries.
- **Over-dependence:** Nigeria relies too heavily on crude oil exports and imported manufactured goods, making the economy vulnerable to global price shocks.
- **Balance of Trade Deficit:** Importing more than a country exports leads to debt and depletes foreign reserves.

## Tourism

**Content v: Tourism.**

### What is tourism?

**Tourism** is the movement of people from their normal places of residence to other places for leisure, pleasure, recreation, study, or cultural exchange. A person who engages in this activity is a tourist. 

### Importance of tourism and tourist centres

**Objective viii: identify the importance of tourism and tourist centres.**

**Major Tourist Centres in Nigeria:**
Nigeria has immense potential for tourism, with rich cultural heritage and natural landmarks. Important centres include:
1. **Yankari Game Reserve (Bauchi State):** Famous for wildlife (elephants, baboons, antelopes) and the Wikki Warm Springs.
2. **Obudu Mountain Resort (Cross River State):** Known for its temperate-like climate, scenic mountain views, and a spectacular cable car.
3. **Olumo Rock (Ogun State):** A massive outcrop of granite rocks in Abeokuta that served as a refuge during inter-tribal wars.
4. **Ikogosi Warm Springs (Ekiti State):** A unique geological site where a cold spring and a warm spring meet and flow side by side.
5. **Idanre Hills (Ondo State):** A breathtaking landscape featuring a high plain with spectacular valleys.
6. **Ogbunike Caves (Anambra State):** A collection of historical and cultural caves.
7. **Erin Ijesha Waterfalls (Osun State):** Also known as Olumirin waterfalls, featuring seven cascades.
8. **Kainji Lake National Park (Niger/Kwara States):** Combines wildlife viewing with the massive hydroelectric dam reservoir.

**Importance of Tourism:**
- **Revenue Generation:** It is a major source of foreign exchange (from international tourists) and local income.
- **Employment Creation:** It provides direct jobs for tour guides, hotel staff, drivers, and artisans selling souvenirs.
- **Infrastructural Development:** To attract tourists, governments are forced to build good roads, airports, and ensure steady water and electricity in tourist areas.
- **Preservation of Culture and Wildlife:** Tourism provides a financial incentive to protect endangered animals (in game reserves) and maintain historical cultural sites.
- **National Integration:** Domestic tourism encourages Nigerians to visit different parts of the country, fostering unity.

### Problems of tourism and their solutions

**Objective ix: account for the problems of tourism and their solutions.**

**Problems Facing Tourism in Nigeria:**
- **Poor Infrastructure:** Bad roads make it difficult to reach tourist sites, and erratic electricity makes hotel stays uncomfortable.
- **Insecurity:** Kidnapping, banditry, and insurgency severely discourage both domestic and international tourists.
- **Poor Publicity:** Many beautiful tourist sites are not aggressively advertised to the outside world or even to Nigerians.
- **Inadequate Funding:** Tourist boards often lack the money to properly maintain and upgrade facilities.
- **Low Income Levels:** Widespread poverty means that most citizens do not have the disposable income to travel for leisure.

**Solutions to Tourism Problems:**
- **Improved Security:** The government must guarantee the safety of lives and properties across the country and deploy special tourist police to hotspots.
- **Infrastructural Upgrade:** Massive investment is required to rehabilitate roads leading to tourist centres and ensure a steady power supply.
- **Publicity Campaigns:** The government and private sector should use digital media, international fairs, and documentaries to showcase Nigeria's attractions.
- **Private Sector Investment:** Encouraging private companies to build world-class hotels and resorts near tourist sites.
- **Proper Funding:** Government must allocate adequate funds to tourism boards for site maintenance and staff training.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Permanent vs Temporary Settlements:** A common JAMB trick is asking for the difference between shifting cultivation and bush fallowing. Remember: in shifting cultivation, the settlement moves; in bush fallowing, the settlement is permanent.
- **Mixed Farming vs Crop Rotation:** Mixed farming mixes crops and *animals*. Crop rotation mixes different *crops over time*.
- **Cash Crop Locations:** Always associate Cocoa with the South-West, Rubber with Edo/Delta, Oil Palm with the South-East/South-South, and Groundnut/Cotton with the North.
- **Industrial Location:** Cement industries are located near raw materials (limestone) because limestone is bulky and loses weight during production. Do not choose "nearness to market" for cement.
- **Transport vs Communication:** If a question asks how transport and communication relate, look for the option that says "communication can serve as a substitute for physical transportation".
- **Pipeline Disadvantages:** The most tested disadvantage of pipelines is that they are inflexible (fixed route) and strictly limited to liquids/gases.
- **Regional Trade:** If asked what the North supplies to the South, look for cattle, beans, or onions. If asked what the South supplies to the North, look for kolanut, palm oil, or manufactured goods.

## Quick summary

1. Agriculture in Nigeria involves systems like bush fallowing, mixed farming, and nomadic herding. Poor funding, bad roads, and land fragmentation are major challenges.
2. Major crops follow climate zones: groundnut and cotton in the drier North; cocoa, rubber, and oil palm in the wetter South.
3. Manufacturing is divided into light (textiles, food) and heavy (cement, petroleum, steel) industries, heavily influenced by raw materials, market, and power supply.
4. The manufacturing sector struggles severely with erratic power supply, lack of capital, and smuggling.
5. Transport modes (road, rail, water, air, pipeline) each have unique advantages. Water is cheapest for bulky international goods; air is fastest but expensive; rail is best for bulky inland goods.
6. Communication (telephones, internet, radio) enhances transport safety and can substitute for physical travel.
7. Internal trade thrives on ecological differences between the North (livestock, cereals) and the South (root crops, forest products, manufactured goods).
8. Tourism (e.g., Yankari Game Reserve, Obudu Mountain Resort) is vital for foreign exchange and job creation, but is hindered by insecurity and poor infrastructure.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following farming systems involves the movement of farmers and their families to a new settlement when soil fertility drops?**

- A. Crop rotation
- B. Mixed farming
- C. Shifting cultivation
- D. Rotational bush fallowing

**Answer: C.** Shifting cultivation involves abandoning both the farm and the settlement. In rotational bush fallowing, the farm is abandoned to rest, but the settlement remains permanent.

**2. A major cash crop cultivated predominantly in the South-Western part of Nigeria is**

- A. Groundnut
- B. Cotton
- C. Cocoa
- D. Rubber

**Answer: C.** Cocoa thrives in the tropical rainforest climate of the South-West. Groundnut and cotton are grown in the North, and rubber is dominant in Edo and Delta States.

**3. The most critical problem facing the manufacturing industry in Nigeria today is**

- A. Lack of raw materials
- B. Over-population
- C. Lack of market
- D. Erratic power supply

**Answer: D.** Erratic power supply forces industries to rely on expensive generators, driving up the cost of production and making goods uncompetitive.

**4. A major reason for locating a cement factory in Obajana or Ewekoro is the**

- A. Availability of cheap labour
- B. Nearness to raw materials
- C. Proximity to the sea port
- D. Nearness to the market

**Answer: B.** Cement production uses limestone, a very bulky and weight-losing raw material. To save on transport costs, cement factories are built right next to limestone deposits.

**5. The most suitable mode of transport for moving very bulky goods across continents is**

- A. Air transport
- B. Rail transport
- C. Water transport
- D. Pipeline transport

**Answer: C.** Ocean (water) transport is the cheapest and most effective mode for moving massive, heavy goods like vehicles and crude oil across international borders.

**6. Which of the following best describes the relationship between transport and communication?**

- A. Both require tarred roads to function
- B. Transport is generally faster than communication
- C. Communication can serve as a substitute for physical transportation
- D. Both consume equal amounts of electricity

**Answer: C.** A major relationship is substitution; making a phone call or sending an email can remove the need to travel physically to deliver a message.

**7. A major disadvantage of pipeline transportation is that it**

- A. Is heavily affected by bad weather
- B. Is restricted to transporting only liquids and gases
- C. Causes massive traffic congestion
- D. Requires daily maintenance of the pipes

**Answer: B.** Pipelines are completely inflexible regarding the type of goods they can carry; they cannot transport solid goods or people.

**8. In the inter-regional trade within Nigeria, which of the following commodities is largely moved from the North to the South?**

- A. Cattle
- B. Kolanut
- C. Palm oil
- D. Manufactured goods

**Answer: A.** The North, due to its savanna vegetation, is excellent for rearing livestock like cattle, which are then transported to the South. Kolanut, palm oil, and manufactured goods move from the South to the North.

**9. The farming system that involves the cultivation of crops and rearing of animals on the same piece of land is known as**

- A. Crop rotation
- B. Plantation agriculture
- C. Pastoral farming
- D. Mixed farming

**Answer: D.** Mixed farming integrates both crop cultivation and animal husbandry, allowing them to mutually benefit each other (e.g., manure for crops, crop residue for animal feed).

**10. Yankari Game Reserve, a major tourist centre in Nigeria, is located in**

- A. Cross River State
- B. Ogun State
- C. Bauchi State
- D. Ekiti State

**Answer: C.** Yankari Game Reserve is in Bauchi State. Obudu is in Cross River, Olumo Rock is in Ogun, and Ikogosi is in Ekiti.
