## Meaning of ECOWAS

**Objective i: State the meaning, purpose and objectives (Meaning).**
**Content i: Meaning and objectives (Meaning).**

**ECOWAS** stands for the **Economic Community of West African States**. It is a regional intergovernmental organization created to promote economic, political, and cultural cooperation and integration among countries in West Africa.

It was founded on **May 28, 1975**, when 15 West African countries signed the **Treaty of Lagos** in Lagos, Nigeria. The organization's headquarters was originally in Lagos but was later moved to **Abuja, Nigeria**, which remains its administrative capital today.

ECOWAS is one of the largest and most prominent regional economic communities in Africa, covering a land area of over 5.1 million square kilometres and serving a population of over 400 million people.

## Purpose and Mandate of ECOWAS

**Objective i: State the meaning, purpose and objectives (Purpose).**
**Objective iii: Understand the purpose/mandate of the organization.**
**Content iii: Purpose/mandate.**

The core **purpose and mandate** of ECOWAS is to promote economic integration across all fields of activity of the constituting countries. 

Specifically, the mandate is to create a single, large trading bloc by integrating the various economies of West Africa. This means turning 15 separate, smaller markets into one massive unified market where goods, people, and money can move freely without barriers.

Over the years, the mandate has expanded beyond just economics to include **peace, security, and political stability**. Because economic growth is impossible in a war zone, ECOWAS took on the mandate of resolving conflicts. It established the ECOWAS Monitoring Group (**ECOMOG**)—a regional peacekeeping force heavily backed by Nigeria—which played major roles in ending civil wars in Liberia and Sierra Leone in the 1990s.

Today, the overall vision of the mandate is to shift from an "ECOWAS of States" (a group of governments) to an "ECOWAS of People" (a community where ordinary citizens feel the direct benefits of integration in their daily lives).

## Objectives of ECOWAS

**Objective i: State the meaning, purpose and objectives (Objectives).**
**Content i: Meaning and objectives (Objectives).**

To achieve its broad mandate, the Treaty of Lagos set out specific **objectives** for the organization. These are the practical goals the member states agreed to pursue:

1. **Elimination of customs duties and tariffs:** To remove taxes on goods traded among member countries (free trade) so that products become cheaper for consumers.
2. **Abolition of quantitative trade restrictions:** To remove quotas (limits on the volume of goods that can be imported or exported) between member states.
3. **Free movement of people, services, and capital:** To allow citizens of member countries to travel, live, and work in any other member country without needing a visa. The introduction of the **ECOWAS passport** was a major step towards this.
4. **Harmonization of policies:** To create unified policies for agriculture, industry, transport, and communications across the region.
5. **Establishment of a common currency:** To replace the various national currencies (like the Naira, Cedi, and CFA franc) with a single regional currency (proposed to be called the **Eco**) to make trade easier.
6. **Creation of a common external tariff:** To charge the exact same import duties on goods coming from outside West Africa (like from Europe or Asia), no matter which ECOWAS country the goods enter first.
7. **Establishment of a Fund for Cooperation, Compensation, and Development:** To provide financial help to poorer member countries that might lose revenue as tariffs are removed, and to fund joint development projects.

## Member States of ECOWAS

**Objective ii: Identify and locate the member countries.**
**Content ii: Member states.**

ECOWAS officially consists of **15 member states**. When the organization was formed in 1975, there were 15 founding members. Cape Verde joined in 1977, making it 16. However, **Mauritania withdrew** from the organization in 2000 to focus on the Arab Maghreb Union, bringing the number back to 15.

To identify and remember the member countries, it is helpful to group them by their official languages, which reflect their colonial history:

| Language Group | Member Countries |
| --- | --- |
| **Anglophone** (English-speaking) | Nigeria, Ghana, Sierra Leone, Liberia, The Gambia |
| **Francophone** (French-speaking) | Benin, Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Guinea, Mali, Niger, Senegal, Togo |
| **Lusophone** (Portuguese-speaking) | Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau |

### Locating the Member States

When studying the map of West Africa, you should be able to locate these countries. 
- **Nigeria** is the largest by population and economy, located on the eastern edge of West Africa, bordering the Gulf of Guinea.
- **Senegal** is on the far western edge of the continent, surrounding **The Gambia**.
- Landlocked countries (those with no coastline) include **Mali, Burkina Faso,** and **Niger** in the Sahel region to the north.
- **Cape Verde** is an island nation located in the Atlantic Ocean, off the coast of Senegal.
- The southern coast is lined with countries like **Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo,** and **Benin**.

## Advantages and Disadvantages of ECOWAS

**Content iv: Advantages and disadvantages.**

While ECOWAS brings massive benefits to the West African region as a whole, membership also creates some challenges or disadvantages for individual countries. 

### Advantages (Benefits of Membership)

1. **Larger Market for Goods:** Instead of selling only to their own populations, industries have access to over 400 million consumers. For example, a factory in Nigeria can easily export goods to Ghana or Benin.
2. **Free Movement of Citizens:** The ECOWAS protocol on free movement allows citizens to travel across borders without a visa for up to 90 days. This encourages cross-border trade, tourism, and cultural exchange.
3. **Regional Security and Peacekeeping:** Through ECOMOG, ECOWAS has successfully restored peace in war-torn member states like Liberia and Sierra Leone, and intervened politically to stop unconstitutional power grabs.
4. **Infrastructural Development:** Member states collaborate on large-scale projects that a single country could not afford alone, such as the **West African Power Pool (WAPP)** and the Lagos-Dakar Trans-West African Coastal Highway.
5. **Cultural Integration:** Sports (like regional football tournaments), education, and trade foster a sense of unity and reduce historical divides between Anglophone and Francophone countries.
6. **Stronger Bargaining Power:** By negotiating as a single bloc, ECOWAS countries have a stronger voice in global trade talks with major powers like the European Union or the USA.

### Disadvantages (Negative Impacts of Membership)

1. **Loss of Tariff Revenue:** Many small countries rely heavily on import and export taxes. By eliminating tariffs on goods from other member states, these countries lose a major source of government income.
2. **Fear of Domination:** Because Nigeria accounts for about half of the region's population and GDP, smaller nations often fear that Nigerian businesses will dominate their local markets and stifle their home-grown industries.
3. **Loss of Sovereignty:** Member states must sometimes give up control over their own policies. For instance, by adopting a common external tariff, a country can no longer independently decide how to tax imports from outside Africa.
4. **Spread of Cross-Border Crime:** The free movement of people makes it easier for smugglers, human traffickers, and illegal arms dealers to move across borders without detection.
5. **Unequal Benefits:** The advantages of integration tend to favour the more industrialized and economically stronger countries (like Nigeria, Ghana, and Côte d'Ivoire), leaving poorer nations behind.

## Problems and Solutions of ECOWAS

**Objective iv: Evaluate the prospects and problems of the organization.**
**Content v: Problems and solutions.**

Despite its successes, ECOWAS faces severe internal problems that hinder its progress. Over the years, leaders have proposed several solutions to overcome these barriers.

| Problem facing ECOWAS | Suggested Solution |
| --- | --- |
| **Similar Agricultural and Primary Products:** Most ECOWAS countries produce and export the same raw materials (like cocoa, groundnuts, timber, and crude oil). Therefore, they have very little to sell to each other, leading to low intra-regional trade. | **Industrialization and Diversification:** Countries must process their raw materials into finished goods. If Côte d'Ivoire makes chocolate and Nigeria makes textiles, they can trade profitably. |
| **Poor Transportation and Communication:** The road, rail, and air links between West African countries are poorly developed. It is sometimes easier to fly to Europe than to fly from one West African capital to another. | **Investment in Infrastructure:** Hasten the completion of regional projects like the Trans-West African Highway network and improve cross-border railway and flight connectivity. |
| **Multiple Currencies:** Trading across borders is expensive and confusing because member states use different currencies (Naira, Cedi, CFA franc, Leone) with unstable exchange rates. | **Single Regional Currency (Eco):** Speed up the implementation of a single currency so that a trader from Ghana can buy goods in Togo without losing money on currency exchange. |
| **Language Barriers:** The division into English, French, and Portuguese-speaking countries makes communication in business and politics difficult. | **Educational Policies:** Promote the teaching of English in Francophone/Lusophone countries and French in Anglophone countries to bridge the communication gap. |
| **Political Instability:** Frequent military coups d'état (e.g., in Mali, Guinea, Burkina Faso, and Niger) and civil unrest disrupt economic planning and scare away foreign investors. | **Strengthen Democratic Institutions:** Enforce strict sanctions and zero tolerance for unconstitutional changes of government, and ensure good governance to prevent unrest. |
| **Failure to Pay Dues:** Many member states fail to pay their financial contributions to the ECOWAS secretariat, leaving the organization underfunded. | **Automatic Levies:** Implement and enforce a community levy (a small percentage tax on imports from outside ECOWAS) that goes directly to the organization's funds. |
| **Border Delays and Extortion:** Despite the free movement protocol, citizens face numerous illegal checkpoints, harassment, and bribery demands from border officials. | **Strict Enforcement:** Governments must discipline corrupt border officials and use modern technology (like joint border posts and electronic scanning) to speed up clearance. |

## Prospects of ECOWAS

**Objective iv: Evaluate the prospects and problems of the organization.**

The word **prospects** refers to the future chances of success. Despite the heavy problems listed above, the future outlook for ECOWAS has several bright spots:

1. **The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA):** ECOWAS is positioning itself to be a powerful bloc within this new Africa-wide free trade zone. This gives West African businesses access to the entire African continent.
2. **The Single Currency (Eco):** If successfully launched, the Eco will drastically reduce the cost of doing business, boost cross-border trade, and stabilize inflation across the region.
3. **Youth Population:** West Africa has a rapidly growing, young, and increasingly tech-savvy population. If properly educated and employed, this demographic can drive massive economic growth and innovation.
4. **Energy Independence:** Through the West African Power Pool (WAPP) and projects like the West African Gas Pipeline, the region has good prospects of solving its electricity challenges by sharing energy resources across borders.
5. **Stronger Security Cooperation:** With the rise of terrorism in the Sahel, ECOWAS member states are increasingly pooling their intelligence and military resources, creating a prospect for a more united and secure region in the future.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ECOWAS** | Economic Community of West African States, founded in 1975 to promote economic integration. |
| **Treaty of Lagos** | The foundational agreement signed on May 28, 1975, that created ECOWAS. |
| **ECOMOG** | ECOWAS Monitoring Group; a multilateral armed force established by ECOWAS for peacekeeping operations. |
| **Tariff** | A tax imposed by a government on imported or exported goods. |
| **Free Trade Area** | A region where member countries agree to reduce or eliminate trade barriers (like tariffs and quotas) among themselves. |
| **Common External Tariff** | A uniform tax rate applied by all member states on goods imported from outside the ECOWAS region. |
| **Anglophone** | English-speaking countries (e.g., Nigeria, Ghana). |
| **Francophone** | French-speaking countries (e.g., Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire). |
| **Lusophone** | Portuguese-speaking countries (e.g., Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau). |
| **Eco** | The proposed name for the common single currency to be used by ECOWAS member states. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Founding details:** Remember that ECOWAS was founded in **1975** in **Lagos**, but its headquarters is now in **Abuja**.
- **Member states count:** The standard number of member states is **15**. Remember that **Mauritania** withdrew in 2000. Cameroon is in Central Africa and is **not** an ECOWAS member—this is a very common trap in JAMB questions.
- **Primary vs Secondary Goods:** A major reason why ECOWAS countries do not trade much with each other is that they all produce similar **primary/agricultural goods**. They don't manufacture enough finished (secondary) products to sell to one another.
- **ECOMOG's role:** Do not confuse ECOWAS (the economic and political body) with ECOMOG (its armed peacekeeping wing). ECOMOG stands for ECOWAS Monitoring Group.
- **Language groups:** Be able to identify which countries speak which language. For example, The Gambia and Sierra Leone are Anglophone, while Senegal and Togo are Francophone.
- **OAU/AU vs ECOWAS:** ECOWAS is for West Africa only. Do not choose options relating to the entire African continent when asked about ECOWAS objectives, unless it relates to acting as a bloc.

## Quick summary

1. ECOWAS was established on May 28, 1975, by the Treaty of Lagos, with its current headquarters in Abuja, Nigeria.
2. Its primary mandate is to promote economic integration, free trade, and political cooperation among West African states.
3. The organization has 15 member states, divided mainly into Francophone, Anglophone, and Lusophone countries. Mauritania left in 2000.
4. Key objectives include eliminating tariffs, allowing the free movement of people and goods, and establishing a single regional currency (the Eco).
5. Advantages include a larger market size, infrastructural development, and regional security (ECOMOG), but disadvantages include loss of tariff revenue and smaller nations' fear of domination by Nigeria.
6. Major problems facing ECOWAS include similar agricultural products causing low intra-regional trade, poor transport networks, language barriers, and political instability.
7. Solutions include industrialization, improving infrastructure, enforcing democratic governance, and adopting a single currency to ease trade.

## Practice questions

**1. ECOWAS was established in the year**
- A. 1963
- B. 1975
- C. 1980
- D. 1995

**Answer: B.** ECOWAS was founded on May 28, 1975, when 15 West African nations signed the Treaty of Lagos.

**2. Which of the following countries is NOT a member of ECOWAS?**
- A. Cape Verde
- B. Cameroon
- C. The Gambia
- D. Guinea-Bissau

**Answer: B.** Cameroon is located in Central Africa and is a member of the Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS), not ECOWAS.

**3. The administrative headquarters of ECOWAS is located in**
- A. Accra, Ghana
- B. Dakar, Senegal
- C. Lagos, Nigeria
- D. Abuja, Nigeria

**Answer: D.** While ECOWAS was founded in Lagos, the headquarters was moved to and is currently located in Abuja, Nigeria.

**4. A major factor hindering the volume of trade among ECOWAS member states is the**
- A. production of similar primary products
- B. high population of the region
- C. use of a common currency
- D. complete absence of natural resources

**Answer: A.** Because most West African countries export similar agricultural and raw materials, they have little to trade with each other, leading to low intra-regional trade.

**5. The armed peacekeeping wing established by ECOWAS to resolve conflicts in member states is known as**
- A. AfCFTA
- B. ECOMOG
- C. Interpol
- D. UNAMID

**Answer: B.** ECOMOG (ECOWAS Monitoring Group) was established to maintain peace and security, notably intervening in Liberia and Sierra Leone.

**6. Which West African country withdrew its membership from ECOWAS in the year 2000?**
- A. Morocco
- B. Mauritania
- C. Togo
- D. Liberia

**Answer: B.** Mauritania withdrew from ECOWAS in 2000 to concentrate its efforts on the Arab Maghreb Union.

**7. The proposed single currency for member states of ECOWAS is called the**
- A. Cedi
- B. CFA franc
- C. Eco
- D. Afro

**Answer: C.** The Eco is the proposed name for the common currency that ECOWAS plans to introduce to replace individual national currencies and ease trade.

**8. Which of the following is a disadvantage of ECOWAS membership for smaller countries?**
- A. Access to a larger regional market
- B. Freedom of citizens to travel without visas
- C. Loss of import tariff revenues
- D. Improved regional infrastructure

**Answer: C.** By removing customs duties on goods imported from other member states to promote free trade, governments of smaller nations lose a significant source of their revenue.

**9. Grouping member states by their official languages, which of the following pairs are both Lusophone (Portuguese-speaking)?**
- A. Senegal and Togo
- B. Nigeria and Ghana
- C. Mali and Niger
- D. Cape Verde and Guinea-Bissau

**Answer: D.** Cape Verde and Guinea-Bissau are the two Portuguese-speaking (Lusophone) members of ECOWAS.

**10. The primary purpose of establishing ECOWAS was to**
- A. create a joint military force against European powers
- B. promote economic integration and cooperation in West Africa
- C. establish a single religion for all West African countries
- D. eradicate infectious diseases in the region

**Answer: B.** The core mandate and primary purpose of ECOWAS is to foster interstate economic and political cooperation and integration in West Africa.
