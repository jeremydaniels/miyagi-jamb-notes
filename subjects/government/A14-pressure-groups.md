# JAMB Government Notes: Pressure Groups

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 14 of the JAMB Government syllabus: Pressure Groups. It covers two main areas:

- **a. Definition, types, functions and modes of operation.**
- **b. Differences between Pressure Groups and Political Parties.**

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. evaluate the functions and the modus operandi of pressure groups;
2. distinguish between pressure groups and political parties.

## Definition of pressure groups

**Content a: Definition.**

A **pressure group** (also called an interest group) is an organised body of people who share common interests, goals or professions, and who come together to influence government policies and decisions in their favour. 

The most important thing to remember about pressure groups is that **they do not seek to take over the control of government**. Instead, they stay outside the government and apply "pressure" on the lawmakers and executives to make sure that the laws and policies made benefit their members or the cause they support.

Examples of pressure groups in Nigeria include the **Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC)**, the **Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU)**, and the **Nigerian Bar Association (NBA)**.

## Types of pressure groups

**Content a: types.**

Because people have different interests, there are many different types of pressure groups. Textbooks sometimes use slightly different names for these categories, but they generally include the following:

### 1. Economic / Occupational / Professional groups
These groups are formed by people in the same line of work or profession. Their main goal is to protect the economic well-being, jobs, salaries, and working conditions of their members. This is the most common type of pressure group.
- **Nigerian examples:** Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC), Trade Union Congress (TUC), Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU), Nigerian Medical Association (NMA), and the Nigerian Union of Teachers (NUT).

### 2. Promotional / Cause / Attitude groups
These groups promote a specific cause, idea, or social issue. Unlike occupational groups that fight only for their members, promotional groups fight for what they believe is good for the whole society or a specific vulnerable group (like the environment, human rights, or children).
- **Nigerian examples:** Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP), Civil Liberties Organisation (CLO), and Amnesty International.

### 3. Religious groups
These are groups formed by followers of a particular religion to protect and advance their religious interests, beliefs, and practices within the country.
- **Nigerian examples:** Christian Association of Nigeria (CAN) and the Nigerian Supreme Council for Islamic Affairs (NSCIA).

### 4. Socio-cultural / Ethnic groups
These groups are formed to promote and protect the identity, culture, and political interests of a specific ethnic group or region.
- **Nigerian examples:** Afenifere (representing Yoruba interests), Ohanaeze Ndigbo (representing Igbo interests), and the Arewa Consultative Forum (representing Northern Nigerian interests).

### 5. Institutional groups
These are groups that are already part of the state machinery or large organizations (like the military, the civil service, or government agencies). Although their main job is not to act as a pressure group, they can apply pressure on the government when their corporate interests are threatened. 
- **Example:** The military establishment negotiating for better defence budgets.

### 6. Anomic groups
Anomic pressure groups are spontaneous, unorganised, and temporary groups that form suddenly, usually in reaction to a specific government action or crisis. They do not have a formal structure or registered offices. Their actions often take the form of street riots, protests, or mob actions, and they usually disperse once the immediate issue is over.
- **Example:** Spontaneous street protests by market women or youths over a sudden hike in the price of petrol.

## Functions of pressure groups

**Content a and Objective i: evaluate the functions of pressure groups.**

Pressure groups play a vital role in any democratic society. Their major functions include:

1. **Protecting members' interests (Welfare):** Their primary function is to defend and improve the economic and social welfare of their members. For example, the NLC constantly fights for a fair minimum wage for Nigerian workers.
2. **Checking the excesses of government:** They act as a watchdog on the government, ensuring that the government does not abuse its powers or make dictatorial policies. They hold political leaders accountable.
3. **Political education and socialization:** Pressure groups educate their members and the general public about their rights, government policies, and the political system. They organize seminars, public debates, and media campaigns.
4. **Providing expert advice to the government:** Because many pressure groups are made up of professionals, they provide the government with valuable data and expert advice when laws are being made. For example, the Nigerian Medical Association (NMA) advises the government on health policies.
5. **Promoting political participation:** They encourage ordinary citizens to get involved in the political process, such as participating in public debates, signing petitions, and voting.
6. **Acting as a link:** They serve as a vital communication link between the government and the governed, taking the feelings of the masses to the government and explaining government policies to the masses.

## Modes of operation (Modus Operandi) of pressure groups

**Content a and Objective i: evaluate the modus operandi of pressure groups.**

The syllabus uses the term **modus operandi**, which is a Latin phrase meaning "mode of operation" or "method of working". Pressure groups use various methods to press home their demands and force the government to listen to them.

1. **Lobbying:** This is the most common and peaceful method. Lobbying means persuading lawmakers (legislators), ministers, and other government officials to make or change laws and policies in favour of the pressure group. Lobbyists may hold private meetings with politicians, buy them dinners, or present research data to convince them.
2. **Strikes:** Used mainly by occupational and labour unions. A strike is an organized refusal to work until specific demands (like salary increases) are met. Strikes paralyse government or private business operations, forcing the employer to negotiate. (e.g., ASUU strikes shutting down universities).
3. **Protests and Demonstrations:** Groups organize peaceful public marches, carrying placards and chanting slogans to draw the attention of the government and the public to their grievances.
4. **Ultimatums:** A pressure group may give the government a final warning with a specific deadline. They state that if their demands are not met by the deadline, they will take severe actions, such as going on a nationwide strike.
5. **Use of Mass Media:** Groups use newspapers, television, radio, and social media to win public sympathy. They write articles, hold press conferences, and run campaigns to put public pressure on the government.
6. **Boycotts:** This involves organizing members and the public to refuse to buy a certain product, use a particular service, or participate in an event to protest an unfair policy.
7. **Litigation (Court actions):** If a government policy violates the constitution or the rights of the group's members, the pressure group can take the government to court. Promotional groups like SERAP frequently use this method in Nigeria.

## Differences between Pressure Groups and Political Parties

**Content b and Objective ii: distinguish between pressure groups and political parties.**

Although both pressure groups and political parties are organizations that try to influence government, their goals, methods, and structures are very different. JAMB frequently tests this distinction.

| Feature | Pressure Groups | Political Parties |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Objective** | To **influence** government policies and decisions to favour their members or cause. | To **contest elections, win, and take over control** of the government. |
| **Fielding Candidates** | They **do not** nominate or present candidates for political elections. | They **present and sponsor candidates** for political elections. |
| **Scope of Interests** | They have a **narrow** focus, caring mainly about specific issues (e.g., doctors' welfare, education). | They have a **broad** focus, dealing with all national issues (economy, health, defence, foreign policy). |
| **Membership** | Membership is usually **exclusive** and restricted to people of the same profession, religion, or belief. | Membership is **inclusive** and open to all adult citizens regardless of profession or ethnicity. |
| **Accountability** | They are accountable mainly to **their registered members**. | They are accountable to the **general public** (the electorate). |
| **Ideology** | May not have a comprehensive political ideology; they just want their specific demands met. | Usually have a defined political ideology (e.g., capitalism, socialism) that guides all their policies. |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pressure Group** | An organized body of people seeking to influence government policies in their favour without seeking to control the government. |
| **Lobbying** | The act of privately persuading lawmakers and government officials to support a specific policy or cause. |
| **Modus Operandi** | The method or mode of operation used by a group to achieve its aims. |
| **Promotional Group** | A pressure group that fights for a specific cause (like human rights or the environment) that benefits society, rather than just its own members. |
| **Anomic Group** | A spontaneous, unorganized mob or crowd that forms suddenly to protest an immediate issue. |
| **Ultimatum** | A final warning with a time limit, after which a group promises to take action (like a strike) if their demands are not met. |
| **Litigation** | Taking legal action; suing the government in a court of law. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **The number one difference:** If a JAMB question asks for the main difference between a political party and a pressure group, look for the option about **taking over the government** or **contesting elections**. Political parties want power; pressure groups only want influence.
- **Know the types:** Be able to classify common Nigerian groups. ASUU and NMA are occupational/economic. CAN is religious. SERAP is promotional. Afenifere is socio-cultural.
- **Anomic vs. Institutional:** Remember that *anomic* groups are violent, spontaneous, and short-lived (like a riot). *Institutional* groups are formal parts of the state (like the army or police) that occasionally act like pressure groups.
- **Lobbying is peaceful:** Lobbying does not mean threatening the government; it means persuading them through meetings, data, and discussions.

## Quick summary

1. A pressure group is an organization that seeks to influence government policies in favour of its members without attempting to control the government.
2. Types of pressure groups include economic/occupational (e.g., NLC), promotional/cause (e.g., SERAP), religious (e.g., CAN), socio-cultural (e.g., Afenifere), institutional, and anomic groups.
3. Their main functions are protecting members' welfare, checking government excesses, educating the public politically, and providing expert advice to lawmakers.
4. The modus operandi (methods of operation) of pressure groups include lobbying, strikes, protests, boycotts, using the mass media, issuing ultimatums, and taking court actions (litigation).
5. The biggest difference between pressure groups and political parties is that political parties contest elections to control the government, while pressure groups do not.
6. Political parties have broad, national interests and open membership, whereas pressure groups focus on specific, narrow interests and often have restricted membership.

## Practice questions

**1. The primary aim of a pressure group is to**

- A. capture political power and run the government
- B. influence government policies in favour of its members
- C. conduct free and fair elections
- D. change the constitution of the country

**Answer: B.** A pressure group's main goal is to put pressure on the government to make policies that benefit its members, without trying to take over the government itself.

**2. Which of the following is the most significant difference between a political party and a pressure group?**

- A. Political parties are recognized by the constitution, while pressure groups are illegal
- B. Pressure groups contest elections, while political parties do not
- C. Political parties seek to win elections and control government, while pressure groups only seek to influence policies
- D. Pressure groups have broad national interests, while political parties focus on narrow issues

**Answer: C.** The defining difference is the quest for political power. Political parties want to run the government; pressure groups just want to influence the people running the government. Option D is exactly the wrong way around.

**3. The Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC) and the Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) are examples of**

- A. religious pressure groups
- B. promotional pressure groups
- C. occupational/economic pressure groups
- D. anomic pressure groups

**Answer: C.** NLC and ASUU represent workers in specific lines of work and fight primarily for their salaries, working conditions, and economic welfare.

**4. A pressure group that fights for the protection of human rights and the environment for the benefit of everyone is known as a/an**

- A. economic group
- B. anomic group
- C. promotional group
- D. institutional group

**Answer: C.** Promotional (or cause) groups promote a specific cause that benefits the wider society, not just their registered members.

**5. Which of the following describes the "lobbying" method used by pressure groups?**

- A. Forcing lawmakers to change laws by kidnapping them
- B. Persuading lawmakers and government officials to make policies in their favour
- C. Organizing a nationwide strike to paralyse the economy
- D. Taking the government to the Supreme Court

**Answer: B.** Lobbying is a peaceful method of persuasion involving discussions, meetings, and presenting data to legislators and executives.

**6. A spontaneous, unorganized group of people protesting violently against a sudden increase in fuel prices is best described as an**

- A. institutional pressure group
- B. socio-cultural group
- C. anomic pressure group
- D. occupational group

**Answer: C.** Anomic groups are characterized by being spontaneous, unorganized, and often resorting to mob action or riots in response to a sudden issue.

**7. Which of the following is NOT a function of pressure groups?**

- A. Providing expert advice to the government
- B. Nominating candidates for general elections
- C. Checking the excesses of the government
- D. Educating members of the public on their rights

**Answer: B.** Nominating and presenting candidates for elections is the exclusive function of a political party, not a pressure group.

**8. When a pressure group gives the government a final warning with a deadline to meet its demands or face a strike, it is issuing a/an**

- A. boycott
- B. ultimatum
- C. litigation
- D. memorandum

**Answer: B.** An ultimatum is a final demand or statement of terms, warning that serious actions (like a strike) will happen if the deadline is missed.

**9. Arewa Consultative Forum and Ohanaeze Ndigbo are examples of which type of pressure group?**

- A. Religious groups
- B. Promotional groups
- C. Socio-cultural groups
- D. Institutional groups

**Answer: C.** These are socio-cultural or ethnic pressure groups formed to protect and promote the interests, culture, and political voice of specific ethnic regions in Nigeria.

**10. Pressure groups can use litigation as a modus operandi. This means they**

- A. use the mass media to criticize the government
- B. advise lawmakers on how to write the budget
- C. tell their members to refuse to buy certain goods
- D. challenge unfavourable government policies in a court of law

**Answer: D.** Litigation means taking a case to court. Pressure groups use it to seek legal protection against unfair or unconstitutional government actions.
