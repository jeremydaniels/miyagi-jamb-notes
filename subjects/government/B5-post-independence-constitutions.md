# JAMB Government Notes: Post-Independence Constitutions

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 5 of the JAMB Government syllabus: Post-Independence Constitutions. 

The syllabus covers four key Nigerian constitutions: the 1963, 1979, 1989, and 1999 (As Amended) constitutions. 

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Assess the workings of the various Constitutions.
2. Analyse the merits and demerits of the Post-Independence Constitutions in Nigeria.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** While the syllabus groups the "characteristics and shortcomings" of all four constitutions together, questions in JAMB often require you to know the exact features of each one. Textbooks sometimes differ on whether to classify the 1989 Constitution as having "worked" since the Third Republic was aborted, but we will examine its intended features as required by the syllabus. 

## Objective i: Assess the workings of the various Constitutions

Nigeria gained independence on October 1, 1960, with the Independence Constitution. However, this constitution still retained the British Queen as Nigeria's ceremonial Head of State, meaning Nigeria was not fully sovereign. To fix this, Nigeria began creating its own **post-independence constitutions**. 

Let us look at the characteristics and workings of the four major constitutions that followed.

### The 1963 Constitution

The 1963 Constitution is famously known as the **First Republican Constitution**. It came into effect on October 1, 1963, exactly three years after independence, officially turning Nigeria into a Republic. 

**Characteristics and features:**
*   **Republican Status:** The British Queen was replaced by a Nigerian citizen as the ceremonial Head of State (President). Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe became the first ceremonial President.
*   **Parliamentary System Retained:** Like the 1960 Constitution, it kept the British parliamentary system. The Prime Minister (Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa) remained the Head of Government.
*   **Supreme Court became the Highest Court:** It abolished appeals to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London. The Supreme Court of Nigeria became the highest court of appeal.
*   **Fundamental Human Rights:** It firmly guaranteed the fundamental human rights of citizens.
*   **Federal Structure:** Nigeria remained a federation with four regions (the Mid-Western Region was created in 1963).

**How it worked in practice (The Workings):**
The 1963 Constitution operated during Nigeria's First Republic (1963–1966). In practice, its workings were troubled by intense regional rivalry and ethnic politics. 
Because power was shared between a ceremonial President and an executive Prime Minister, tensions arose, especially following the controversial 1964 federal elections. The constitution could not prevent the massive census crises (1962/1963) or the Western Region election crisis (1965). The violent political breakdown eventually led to the collapse of the First Republic when the military took over in a bloody coup on January 15, 1966.

### The 1979 Constitution

After 13 years of military rule, the regime of General Olusegun Obasanjo handed over power to civilians, ushering in the Second Republic under the 1979 Constitution.

**How it was made:**
The military government set up a **Constitution Drafting Committee (CDC)** of 49 "wise men" headed by Chief Rotimi Williams to draft the constitution. After the CDC finished, a **Constituent Assembly** (headed by Justice Udo Udoma) debated and ratified it. 

**Characteristics and features:**
*   **Presidential System of Government:** It discarded the British parliamentary system and introduced the American-style executive presidential system. The President (Alhaji Shehu Shagari) was now both Head of State and Head of Government.
*   **Clear Separation of Powers:** It separated the Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary, complete with checks and balances. 
*   **Federal Character Principle:** This was introduced for the first time. It mandated that government appointments must reflect the federal nature (diversity) of Nigeria to promote national unity and prevent one ethnic group from dominating.
*   **Fixed Tenure of Office:** The President and State Governors had a fixed term of 4 years, renewable for only one more term.
*   **Creation of Public Commissions:** It established bodies like the Federal Electoral Commission (FEDECO) and the Code of Conduct Bureau.

**How it worked in practice (The Workings):**
The 1979 Constitution operated during the Second Republic (1979–1983). While the presidential system provided a strong national leader, the system proved very expensive to run. The workings of the constitution were frequently stalled by clashes between the Executive and the Legislature, a famous example being the impeachment of the Governor of Kaduna State, Balarabe Musa, by a hostile state assembly. Widespread corruption, economic mismanagement, and the heavily rigged 1983 elections caused the military (led by Major General Muhammadu Buhari) to overthrow the government on December 31, 1983.

### The 1989 Constitution

The 1989 Constitution was drafted during the military regime of General Ibrahim Babangida, intended to usher in Nigeria's Third Republic. A Constituent Assembly headed by Justice Anthony Aniagolu debated the draft.

**Characteristics and features:**
*   **Two-Party System:** The most unique feature was that it strictly limited the country to only two political parties: the Social Democratic Party (SDP) and the National Republican Convention (NRC).
*   **Presidential System:** It retained the presidential system from 1979.
*   **Local Government Autonomy:** It recognized local governments as a distinct and independent third tier of government.
*   **Federal Capital Territory:** It formally recognized Abuja as the new Federal Capital Territory.

**How it worked in practice (The Workings):**
This constitution is often called a "stillborn" or "aborted" constitution because it never fully came into operation. While elections were held for local governments, state governors, and the National Assembly, the transition was abruptly halted when General Babangida annulled the June 12, 1993 presidential election (widely believed to have been won by Chief M.K.O. Abiola). The Third Republic collapsed before it truly began, and General Sani Abacha eventually seized power.

### The 1999 Constitution (As Amended)

Following the death of General Abacha in 1998, General Abdulsalami Abubakar promised a quick return to democracy. He set up the Justice Niki Tobi Constitutional Debate Co-ordinating Committee. They largely updated the 1979 Constitution to create the 1999 Constitution, which ushered in the Fourth Republic.

**Characteristics and features:**
*   **Presidential System Retained:** It keeps the executive presidential system with a 4-year renewable tenure.
*   **Bicameral National Assembly:** The legislature consists of the Senate (109 members) and the House of Representatives (360 members).
*   **774 Local Governments:** It specifically lists 774 Local Government Areas in its schedule.
*   **Immunity Clause:** Section 308 grants immunity from criminal prosecution to the President, Vice President, Governors, and Deputy Governors while they are in office.
*   **Exclusive Legislative List:** It places enormous power in the hands of the federal government (the exclusive list contains 68 items).

**How it worked in practice (The Workings):**
The 1999 Constitution has guided Nigeria's Fourth Republic from 1999 to the present day, making it the longest-running democratic constitution in Nigeria's history. 
However, it has faced massive criticism for being imposed by the military (it begins with "We the people...", which critics say is a lie). Over the years, it has undergone several amendments (hence "As Amended") to fix practical problems, such as updating electoral rules and reducing the age limits for running for office (the "Not Too Young To Run" amendment).

## Objective ii: Analyse the merits and demerits of the Post-Independence Constitutions in Nigeria

When examining how well these constitutions served the nation, we must look at their advantages (merits) and their weaknesses (demerits or shortcomings). 

### Merits and Demerits of the 1963 Constitution

| Merits | Demerits (Shortcomings) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fully Indigenous:** It was the first constitution made fully by Nigerians, without British interference. | **Fusion of Powers:** Because it was parliamentary, the executive and legislature were fused, limiting checks and balances. |
| **Judicial Independence:** It ended the authority of the British Privy Council, making the Nigerian Supreme Court the highest judicial body. | **Executive Friction:** The sharing of power between a Ceremonial President and an Executive Prime Minister caused confusion and political clashes. |
| **Fundamental Rights:** It boldly entrenched the rights of citizens into law. | **Regionalism:** The constitution left the regions too powerful, encouraging tribalism and weakening the central government. |

### Merits and Demerits of the 1979 Constitution

| Merits | Demerits (Shortcomings) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Clear Separation of Powers:** The Presidential system clearly separated the Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary, preventing the fusion seen in 1963. | **Expensive to Run:** The Presidential system, with its duplication of offices and large cabinets, drained the country’s resources. |
| **National Unity:** The introduction of the **Federal Character** principle ensured that all ethnic groups were represented in federal appointments. | **Concentration of Power:** The President had enormous executive powers, which could easily lead to dictatorship or abuse of office. |
| **Fixed Term:** Knowing the President had a fixed 4-year term brought stability, unlike the parliamentary system where a vote of no confidence could remove the leader anytime. | **Executive-Legislative Clashes:** The strict separation of powers sometimes led to the legislature and executive fighting and delaying government work. |

### Merits and Demerits of the 1989 Constitution

| Merits | Demerits (Shortcomings) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Two-Party System:** Limiting the country to two parties (SDP and NRC) successfully forced politicians across different ethnic groups to unite, briefly reducing ethnic voting. | **Limited Political Choice:** A mandatory two-party system denied citizens the freedom to form or choose other parties, violating freedom of association. |
| **Local Government Autonomy:** It recognized the local government as a strict third tier of government, bringing development closer to the grassroots. | **Military Imposition:** It was heavily manipulated by the Babangida military junta, leading to a lack of complete trust by the citizens. |
| **Strict Requirements for Parties:** Parties had to show presence in at least two-thirds of the states, ensuring they were truly national. | **Aborted Implementation:** Because the transition was truncated, the constitution's merits were never fully realized in practice. |

### Merits and Demerits of the 1999 Constitution (As Amended)

| Merits | Demerits (Shortcomings) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Longevity and Stability:** It has successfully sustained the Fourth Republic since 1999 without a military takeover. | **False Origin:** It starts with the phrase "We the people of the Federal Republic of Nigeria," but it was actually decreed into law by the military without a national referendum. |
| **Clear Democratic Structures:** It maintains a clear separation of powers and guarantees fundamental human rights. | **Over-centralization:** The Federal Government is given too much power on the Exclusive Legislative List, weakening the states (violating true federalism). |
| **Flexibility to Amend:** The National Assembly has successfully amended it several times to fix pressing issues (e.g., electoral reforms). | **Immunity Clause:** Section 308 protects governors and the president from prosecution while in office, which has sometimes encouraged massive corruption. |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Republic** | A state where the head of state is a citizen (usually an elected president), rather than a monarch like a king or queen. |
| **Ceremonial President** | A head of state who performs official and traditional duties but has no real political or executive power (e.g., Nnamdi Azikiwe in 1963). |
| **Executive President** | A head of state who is also the head of government, possessing real political and administrative power (introduced in 1979). |
| **Privy Council** | The highest court of appeal in Britain, which served as Nigeria's highest court until the 1963 Constitution abolished it. |
| **Federal Character** | A principle introduced in 1979 to ensure that public appointments reflect the diverse ethnic and regional makeup of Nigeria. |
| **CDC** | Constitution Drafting Committee; the body of 49 members that drafted the 1979 Constitution. |
| **Immunity Clause** | A section in the 1999 Constitution that prevents the President, Vice President, Governors, and Deputy Governors from facing criminal trial while in office. |
| **Exclusive Legislative List** | A list of items in the constitution that only the Federal Government has the power to make laws about. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **1960 vs 1963:** A very common JAMB trap is asking which constitution made Nigeria a republic. Remember, Nigeria became independent in **1960** (Independence Constitution) but became a Republic in **1963** (Republican Constitution).
*   **Highest Court of Appeal:** The **1963** constitution is the one that made the Supreme Court of Nigeria the highest court of appeal by abolishing the Privy Council.
*   **Systems of Government:** Remember the shift! The 1963 constitution used the **Parliamentary** system. The 1979 constitution introduced the **Presidential** system. 
*   **Federal Character:** Always associate the introduction of the Federal Character principle with the **1979** Constitution.
*   **Two-Party System:** The strict two-party system (SDP and NRC) is the trademark of the **1989** Constitution. 
*   **Immunity Clause and "We the people":** These are the two most frequently tested criticisms (demerits) of the **1999** Constitution.

## Quick summary

1. The 1963 Republican Constitution replaced the Queen with a ceremonial President (Nnamdi Azikiwe) and made the Supreme Court Nigeria's highest court, but retained the parliamentary system.
2. The 1979 Constitution abandoned the parliamentary system for an executive presidential system, and it introduced the Federal Character principle to promote unity.
3. The 1989 Constitution, drafted under General Babangida, uniquely introduced a strict two-party system but was aborted before it could fully take effect.
4. The 1999 Constitution ushered in the Fourth Republic and retained the presidential system, but is criticized for being imposed by the military and giving too much power to the center.
5. A major demerit of the 1963 Constitution was the friction between the ceremonial Head of State and the executive Prime Minister.
6. A major demerit of the 1979 and 1999 Constitutions is that the presidential system is very expensive to operate.
7. The immunity clause in the 1999 Constitution has been widely criticized for shielding corrupt executives from prosecution while in office.

## Practice questions

**1. The constitution that established the Supreme Court as the highest court of appeal in Nigeria was the**
- A. 1960 Independence Constitution
- B. 1963 Republican Constitution
- C. 1979 Presidential Constitution
- D. 1999 Constitution

**Answer: B.** The 1963 Constitution abolished appeals to the British Privy Council, making the Supreme Court of Nigeria the highest judicial authority.

**2. Which of the following constitutions introduced the presidential system of government in Nigeria?**
- A. 1963 Constitution
- B. 1979 Constitution
- C. 1989 Constitution
- D. 1999 Constitution

**Answer: B.** The 1979 Constitution scrapped the British parliamentary system used in the First Republic and introduced the American-style executive presidential system.

**3. A major characteristic of the 1989 Constitution of Nigeria was the introduction of a**
- A. Multi-party system
- B. Zero-party system
- C. Two-party system
- D. One-party system

**Answer: C.** The 1989 Constitution mandated a strict two-party system, leading to the creation of the SDP and NRC by the military government.

**4. The principle of Federal Character was first introduced in Nigeria by the**
- A. 1963 Constitution
- B. 1979 Constitution
- C. 1989 Constitution
- D. 1999 Constitution

**Answer: B.** The 1979 Constitution introduced the Federal Character principle to ensure fair representation of all ethnic and regional groups in government appointments.

**5. One of the major criticisms of the 1999 Constitution is that**
- A. It was not drafted by Nigerians
- B. It does not recognize local governments
- C. It was decreed into existence by the military
- D. It operates a parliamentary system

**Answer: C.** Critics argue that the 1999 Constitution begins with a lie ("We the people...") because it was imposed by the military regime of Abdulsalami Abubakar without a national referendum.

**6. In the 1963 Constitution, the executive power of the state was vested in the**
- A. Ceremonial President
- B. Queen of England
- C. Prime Minister
- D. Chief Justice

**Answer: C.** Under the 1963 parliamentary system, the Prime Minister was the Head of Government holding executive power, while the President was merely a ceremonial Head of State.

**7. Which of the following is a prominent demerit of the presidential system under the 1979 and 1999 Constitutions?**
- A. Fusion of powers
- B. Lack of fundamental human rights
- C. It is very expensive to operate
- D. The president cannot be impeached

**Answer: C.** The presidential system duplicates offices and requires a large cabinet and bureaucracy, making it highly expensive to run compared to the parliamentary system.

**8. The transition programme that was supposed to fully implement the 1989 Constitution was aborted by the**
- A. Assassination of Murtala Mohammed
- B. Annulment of the June 12, 1993 presidential election
- C. Nigerian Civil War
- D. Coup against Shehu Shagari

**Answer: B.** The Third Republic, which was meant to operate the 1989 Constitution, was truncated when General Ibrahim Babangida annulled the June 12, 1993 elections.

**9. Section 308 of the 1999 Constitution is controversial because it**
- A. Allows the president to rule for life
- B. Gives total independence to the judiciary
- C. Grants immunity from prosecution to specific executive officeholders
- D. Prevents the creation of new states

**Answer: C.** The immunity clause protects the President, Vice President, Governors, and Deputy Governors from criminal trial while in office, which critics say encourages corruption.

**10. The body responsible for drafting the 1979 Constitution was the**
- A. Constituent Assembly only
- B. Constitution Drafting Committee (CDC)
- C. Supreme Military Council
- D. National Assembly

**Answer: B.** The military set up a 49-member Constitution Drafting Committee (CDC) headed by Chief Rotimi Williams to draft the 1979 Constitution.
