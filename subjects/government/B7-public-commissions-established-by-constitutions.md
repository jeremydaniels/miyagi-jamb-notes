# JAMB Government Notes: Public Commissions Established by the 1979 and Subsequent Constitutions

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 7 of the JAMB Government syllabus: Institutions of Government in Post-Independence Nigeria – Public Commissions Established by the 1979 and Subsequent Constitutions. 

It covers the following public commissions:
- The Civil Service Commission
- The Public Complaints Commission
- Electoral Commissions
- National Boundary Commission
- Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC)
- Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC)
- Others (e.g., Code of Conduct Bureau, Federal Character Commission)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Evaluate the operations of Public Commissions.
2. Assess the problems of the Public Commissions and their constraints.

*Note on syllabus and textbook variations:* While the syllabus groups all these under "Commissions Established by the 1979 and Subsequent Constitutions", textbooks often point out that the ICPC (2000) and EFCC (2003) were established by Acts of the National Assembly rather than directly by the text of the Constitution. However, they are treated here as constitutional public commissions because they serve similar independent executive functions. 

## Introduction to Public Commissions

**Public Commissions** are independent or semi-independent bodies set up by the government to perform specific administrative, executive, or regulatory functions outside the normal day-to-day running of government ministries. 

To prevent these bodies from being easily manipulated by politicians, their existence, powers, and structures are usually written directly into the Constitution (such as the 1979 and 1999 Constitutions) or backed by strong Acts of parliament. This constitutional backing is intended to give them the freedom to do their work fairly and objectively.

## The Civil Service Commission

The **Civil Service Commission** is the body responsible for managing the personnel of the civil service. In Nigeria's federal system, there is a **Federal Civil Service Commission (FCSC)** for federal workers, and each state has its own **State Civil Service Commission** for state workers.

**Objectives:**
- To ensure an efficient, disciplined, and competent civil service.
- To protect civil servants from the political influence of whichever party is in power.
- To ensure that recruitment into the federal civil service reflects the **Federal Character Principle** (making sure all states and ethnic groups are fairly represented).

**Functions:**
1. **Recruitment:** It advertises vacancies, conducts interviews, and employs qualified personnel into the civil service.
2. **Promotion:** It evaluates the performance of civil servants and promotes them to higher grade levels.
3. **Discipline:** It investigates cases of misconduct and disciplines erring civil servants (e.g., through suspension, demotion, or dismissal).
4. **Transfer and Retirement:** It approves the transfer of civil servants between ministries and processes their retirement when they reach the statutory age or years of service.

## The Public Complaints Commission

The **Public Complaints Commission (PCC)** is Nigeria's version of the **Ombudsman**. It was first established by a military decree in 1975 and was later embedded in the 1979 and 1999 Constitutions.

**Objectives:**
- To protect ordinary citizens against administrative injustice, abuse of power, or unfair treatment by government officials or private companies.
- To provide a free, fast, and simple way for citizens to seek redress (justice) without going through the expensive and slow court system.

**Functions:**
1. **Receiving Complaints:** It receives petitions from citizens who feel they have been cheated, delayed, or treated unfairly by government agencies, public corporations, or even private employers.
2. **Investigation:** It investigates these complaints by demanding documents and summoning officials for questioning.
3. **Recommendation:** It recommends solutions, such as asking an agency to pay a worker's withheld salary, reverse an unfair dismissal, or improve a poor public service.
4. **Referral:** It can refer criminal matters discovered during its investigations to the police or anti-corruption agencies.

## Electoral Commissions

An **Electoral Commission** is the body legally empowered to organize and conduct elections. In Nigeria's history, different names have been used: FEDECO (1979 Constitution), NEC (1989 Constitution), NECON (1995 draft), and **INEC (Independent National Electoral Commission)** under the 1999 Constitution (as amended). At the state level, there are **State Independent Electoral Commissions (SIECs)**, which conduct local government elections.

**Objectives:**
- To organize free, fair, credible, and transparent elections.
- To ensure a smooth democratic transition of power from one administration to another.

**Functions:**
1. **Registration of Political Parties:** INEC registers new political parties and monitors their activities and finances.
2. **Voter Registration:** It registers eligible voters, maintains the voters' register, and issues Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs).
3. **Conduct of Elections:** It provides electoral materials, deploys electoral officers, conducts the voting process, and announces the results.
4. **Constituency Delimitation:** It divides the country or states into electoral districts (constituencies) for equal representation.
5. **Voter Education:** It educates the public on their voting rights, how to vote, and the dangers of electoral violence.

## National Boundary Commission

The **National Boundary Commission (NBC)** was established to deal with border issues, both within Nigeria (between states and local governments) and externally (between Nigeria and neighbouring countries like Cameroon, Niger, Chad, and Benin).

**Objectives:**
- To prevent and resolve boundary disputes peacefully.
- To clearly define and manage Nigeria's internal and international borders.

**Functions:**
1. **Boundary Delimitation:** It physically traces, maps, and demarcates boundaries between states, local governments, and communities.
2. **Dispute Resolution:** It intervenes in communal or state border clashes to negotiate peaceful settlements.
3. **Advisory Role:** It advises the Federal Government on issues relating to border security, trans-border cooperation, and international boundary laws (such as the Bakassi Peninsula dispute with Cameroon).

## Anti-Corruption Commissions: ICPC and EFCC

To fight the high rate of corruption that plagued Nigeria's early republics and military regimes, two major anti-graft agencies were created. Although their functions are similar, they have different focus areas.

### Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC)
Established in **2000**, the ICPC focuses primarily on **corruption in the public service** (bribery, abuse of office, inflation of contracts).

**Functions:**
1. **Investigation and Prosecution:** Receiving petitions, investigating, and prosecuting public officers involved in bribery and corrupt practices.
2. **System Review:** Examining the practices and procedures of government ministries to identify loopholes that encourage corruption, and advising them on how to fix these gaps.
3. **Public Education:** Educating the public on the negative effects of corruption and fostering public support in fighting it.

### Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC)
Established in **2003**, the EFCC was created largely to combat **economic and financial crimes** in both the public and private sectors. It focuses heavily on money laundering, bank fraud, cybercrime, and "Advance Fee Fraud" (commonly known as 419).

**Functions:**
1. **Investigation and Prosecution:** Investigating and prosecuting all economic and financial crimes.
2. **Asset Recovery:** Tracing, seizing, and confiscating properties and bank accounts linked to criminal activities.
3. **International Collaboration:** Working with international bodies like Interpol and foreign governments to track money launderers and cybercriminals across borders.

### Comparing ICPC and EFCC

| Feature | ICPC (Independent Corrupt Practices Commission) | EFCC (Economic and Financial Crimes Commission) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Year Established** | 2000 | 2003 |
| **Primary Focus** | Public sector corruption, bribery, abuse of office. | Financial crimes, money laundering, cybercrimes, bank fraud. |
| **Scope** | Mostly focuses on government officials and ministries. | Focuses on both government officials and private individuals (e.g., internet fraudsters, bankers). |
| **System Review** | Mandated to review and fix government procedures to prevent corruption. | Focuses more on intelligence, enforcement, and asset recovery. |

## Other Public Commissions

A few other important constitutional commissions include:
- **The Code of Conduct Bureau (CCB):** Established to ensure a high standard of public morality. All high-ranking public officers must declare their assets to the CCB before taking office and after leaving office. It prosecutes offenders at the **Code of Conduct Tribunal (CCT)**.
- **The Federal Character Commission (FCC):** Ensures that the principle of "Federal Character" is strictly observed in the distribution of public appointments and socio-economic amenities, ensuring no ethnic group or state dominates the federal government.

## Evaluating the operations of Public Commissions

**Objective i: evaluate the operations of Public Commissions.**

To properly evaluate these commissions, we must look at their operational achievements and how effectively they carry out their constitutional mandates. 

**Achievements and Effectiveness:**
1. **Specialization and Focus:** By delegating specific tasks to specialized bodies, the government handles complex issues more efficiently. For example, INEC focuses solely on the immense task of elections, while the NBC focuses solely on border administration, allowing government ministries to focus on general governance.
2. **Asset Recovery and Anti-Corruption:** The EFCC and ICPC have fundamentally changed the fight against corruption in Nigeria. They have successfully recovered billions of naira in stolen public funds and secured high-profile convictions of ex-governors and officials that the regular police force historically struggled to handle.
3. **Avenue for Redress:** The Public Complaints Commission (PCC) has proven successful as a voice for the common citizen. It has helped thousands of voiceless Nigerians retrieve unpaid benefits, fight administrative injustice, and resolve workplace disputes without paying legal fees.
4. **Democratic Continuity:** Despite widespread criticisms of elections, INEC has successfully overseen civilian-to-civilian transitions since 1999, which is the longest uninterrupted democratic period in Nigeria's history.

## Assessing the problems and constraints of Public Commissions

**Objective ii: assess the problems of the Public Commissions and their constraints.**

Despite their achievements, public commissions face severe constraints that hinder their optimal performance.

**General Problems and Constraints:**
1. **Lack of True Independence:** This is often called the "He who pays the piper dictates the tune" problem. The heads of these commissions are appointed by the President (or Governor, at the state level) and rely heavily on the executive arm for their funding. This makes it difficult for them to be completely neutral, especially when investigating or opposing the government in power.
2. **Inadequate Funding and Resources:** Almost all commissions complain of poor funding, which limits their reach. The PCC cannot afford to maintain operational offices in every local government, INEC struggles with the logistics of deploying materials nationwide, and the NBC lacks modern mapping and surveying equipment.
3. **Weak Legal Frameworks:** Some commissions lack the power to bite. The PCC, for instance, can only *recommend* actions; it cannot legally enforce them or penalize offenders directly. Furthermore, anti-corruption agencies rely on a very slow regular court system to secure convictions, where lawyers use endless adjournments to delay cases for years.
4. **Political Interference and Witch-hunting:** Politicians often try to interfere with the Civil Service Commission's recruitment to favor their supporters. Similarly, anti-graft agencies (EFCC and ICPC) are frequently accused by the public and opposition parties of being used by the ruling government to harass political opponents while ignoring corrupt members of the ruling party.
5. **Security Risks and Uncooperative Publics:** Field workers for the National Boundary Commission are sometimes attacked by angry locals while trying to demarcate disputed lands. Meanwhile, powerful government officials often refuse to honor summons from the PCC.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Public Commission** | An independent government body established to perform specific administrative, executive, or regulatory functions. |
| **Ombudsman** | An official appointed to investigate citizens' complaints against public authorities (known in Nigeria as the Public Complaints Commission). |
| **Federal Character Principle** | A constitutional policy ensuring equitable representation of different states and ethnic groups in federal government appointments. |
| **Constituency Delimitation** | The process of dividing a country into geographical areas for the purpose of equal representation in elections. |
| **Advance Fee Fraud (419)** | A type of scam where a victim is tricked into paying money upfront in the hope of receiving a larger sum later; a major focus of the EFCC. |
| **Red Tape / Bureaucracy** | Excessive adherence to rules and formalities that slows down decision-making, a common problem in civil service management. |
| **Money Laundering** | Concealing the origins of illegally obtained money, typically by passing it through complex banking transfers or businesses. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **PCC = Ombudsman:** In JAMB, if a question asks for the Nigerian equivalent of the Ombudsman, the answer is always the Public Complaints Commission (PCC).
- **ICPC vs EFCC:** Remember that **ICPC** is heavily tied to *bribery and public service corruption*, while **EFCC** is tied to *financial crimes, cybercrimes, and money laundering*.
- **Civil Service Commission limitation:** The Civil Service Commission controls the recruitment and discipline of workers in *ministries* (civil servants), **not** workers in public corporations/parastatals (public servants like university lecturers or NNPC staff).
- **The limits of the PCC:** A common trick question asks about the powers of the PCC. Remember that the PCC **cannot** enforce its decisions or jail people; it can only investigate and **recommend**.
- **Federal Character:** If a question asks which commission is most directly affected by the Federal Character Principle during recruitment, look for the Federal Civil Service Commission.

## Quick summary

1. Public Commissions are specialized bodies set up by the Constitution or Acts of parliament to handle specific government duties independently.
2. The Civil Service Commission handles the recruitment, promotion, and discipline of workers in government ministries, but suffers from political interference.
3. The Public Complaints Commission acts as the Ombudsman, helping citizens fight administrative injustice for free, though it lacks enforcement powers.
4. Electoral Commissions (INEC and SIECs) organize and conduct elections, register parties, and delimit constituencies, but struggle with electoral violence and logistics.
5. The National Boundary Commission resolves internal and external border disputes through mapping and dialogue.
6. The ICPC focuses on public sector corruption and system reviews, while the EFCC focuses on economic crimes, money laundering, and advance fee fraud.
7. The major constraints facing all public commissions in Nigeria include lack of true financial independence, poor funding, political interference, and slow judicial processes.

## Practice questions

**1. The Nigerian institution that performs the function of an Ombudsman is the**
- A. Code of Conduct Bureau
- B. Public Complaints Commission
- C. Independent Corrupt Practices Commission
- D. Federal Civil Service Commission

**Answer: B.** The Public Complaints Commission investigates citizens' complaints against administrative injustice, which is the exact definition of an Ombudsman. 

**2. Which of the following commissions is responsible for the recruitment, promotion, and discipline of workers in government ministries?**
- A. Public Service Commission
- B. Federal Character Commission
- C. Civil Service Commission
- D. Ministry of Labour

**Answer: C.** The Civil Service Commission handles the personnel management (recruitment, promotion, discipline) of civil servants working in government ministries.

**3. A major limitation of the Public Complaints Commission in Nigeria is that it**
- A. has no power to investigate government officials
- B. can only investigate cases in the federal capital
- C. lacks the executive power to enforce its recommendations
- D. charges citizens high fees to investigate cases

**Answer: C.** The PCC can investigate, but it can only recommend solutions. It does not have the power to force agencies to comply or to jail offenders. A common misunderstanding is that it acts like a court, but it does not.

**4. The body tasked with dividing the country into electoral districts is the**
- A. National Boundary Commission
- B. Independent National Electoral Commission
- C. National Population Commission
- D. Federal Character Commission

**Answer: B.** INEC is responsible for constituency delimitation (dividing the country into electoral districts). The National Boundary Commission (Option A) deals with physical state and national borders, not electoral constituencies.

**5. Which of the following agencies was specifically established to tackle Advance Fee Fraud and money laundering?**
- A. Code of Conduct Bureau
- B. Public Complaints Commission
- C. Economic and Financial Crimes Commission
- D. Independent Corrupt Practices Commission

**Answer: C.** The EFCC was created specifically to combat economic and financial crimes, including money laundering and Advance Fee Fraud (419). The ICPC (Option D) focuses more on bribery and public service corruption.

**6. The requirement for public officials to declare their assets before taking office is enforced by the**
- A. EFCC
- B. ICPC
- C. Code of Conduct Bureau
- D. Public Complaints Commission

**Answer: C.** The Code of Conduct Bureau (CCB) enforces the declaration of assets by public officers to ensure public morality and prevent illicit enrichment. 

**7. A common constraint faced by almost all public commissions in Nigeria is**
- A. overstaffing of their departments
- B. too much constitutional power
- C. financial dependence on the executive arm
- D. lack of public interest in their activities

**Answer: C.** Financial and operational dependence on the executive arm (which appoints their leaders and controls their funding) limits their true independence and effectiveness. 

**8. Which of the following commissions intervenes to resolve border clashes between two states in Nigeria?**
- A. Federal Character Commission
- B. National Boundary Commission
- C. Independent National Electoral Commission
- D. Public Complaints Commission

**Answer: B.** The National Boundary Commission is responsible for demarcating internal and international borders and resolving border disputes peacefully.

**9. Under the 1999 Constitution, the body responsible for conducting local government elections is the**
- A. Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC)
- B. State Independent Electoral Commission (SIEC)
- C. Local Government Electoral Tribunal
- D. Federal Electoral Commission (FEDECO)

**Answer: B.** While INEC conducts federal and state-level elections (President, Governors, National and State Assemblies), the State Independent Electoral Commissions (SIECs) conduct elections for local government chairmen and councillors.

**10. The Federal Character Principle most directly affects the operations of which of the following commissions?**
- A. Economic and Financial Crimes Commission
- B. National Population Commission
- C. Civil Service Commission
- D. Public Complaints Commission

**Answer: C.** The Civil Service Commission (at the federal level) must apply the Federal Character Principle in its recruitment to ensure that all states are fairly represented in the federal civil service.
