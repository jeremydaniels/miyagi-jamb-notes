# JAMB Government Notes: Nigeria’s Foreign Policy

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 2 of the JAMB Government syllabus: Nigeria's Foreign Policy. It covers how Nigeria interacts with other countries on the global stage.

## Major objectives of Nigeria’s foreign policy

**Objective i: identify the major objectives of Nigeria’s foreign policy.**

**Foreign policy** is the set of strategies and principles that guide how a country interacts with other nations. Since gaining independence in 1960, Nigeria's foreign policy has been guided by specific objectives. 

In the 1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (as amended), Chapter II (Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy) lists the foreign policy objectives in Section 19. They include:

1. **Promotion and protection of the national interest:** The most important goal of any country's foreign policy is to protect its own survival, security, and economic prosperity.
2. **Promotion of African integration and support for African unity:** Africa is famously described as the "centre-piece" of Nigeria's foreign policy. This means Nigeria prioritises the peace, development, and unity of the African continent.
3. **Promotion of international co-operation:** Nigeria aims to work with other nations to consolidate universal peace and mutual respect among all nations.
4. **Respect for international law and treaty obligations:** Nigeria commits to solving international disputes through negotiation, mediation, arbitration, or other peaceful means.
5. **Promotion of a just world economic order:** Nigeria advocates for fairness in global trade and economic relations, ensuring that developing nations are not exploited by richer, industrialised countries.

## Relations with major powers

**Content a: Relations with major powers.**

Nigeria interacts closely with the world's major economic and military powers to secure trade, technology, foreign investment, and security assistance. These powers include the United Kingdom, the United States, Russia (formerly the Soviet Union), China, and France.

### The United Kingdom (Britain)
As Nigeria's former colonial master, Britain has deep historical, cultural, and economic ties with Nigeria. 
*   **Cooperation:** Nigeria is a key member of the Commonwealth of Nations (an organisation mostly made up of former British colonies). Britain remains a major trading partner and a destination for many Nigerian students and professionals.
*   **Tensions:** Relations have occasionally been strained. For example, in 1984, the attempted kidnapping (in London) of a former Nigerian minister, Umaru Dikko, by Nigerian and Israeli agents severely damaged diplomatic relations for a few years.

### The United States of America (USA)
The USA is one of Nigeria's most important partners.
*   **Cooperation:** Historically, the USA was one of the largest buyers of Nigerian crude oil, though this reduced as the US developed its own oil resources. Nigeria modelled its presidential system of government (adopted in 1979) after the American system. The two countries cooperate heavily on security (fighting terrorism) and health (funding for HIV/AIDS and malaria).
*   **Tensions:** The US has occasionally criticised Nigeria's human rights record or democratic processes, but the relationship remains fundamentally strong.

### Russia (and the former Soviet Union)
During the early years of independence, Nigeria's Prime Minister, Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa, was suspicious of the communist Soviet Union and delayed establishing full relations.
*   **Cooperation:** Relations improved dramatically during the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970). When Nigeria's traditional Western allies (the US and UK) hesitated to sell offensive weapons to the Federal Government, the Soviet Union stepped in to supply MiG fighter jets and artillery. Later, the Soviet Union helped build the Ajaokuta Steel Company. Today, Russia and Nigeria cooperate in military training, energy, and agriculture.

### France
Nigeria is completely surrounded by Francophone (French-speaking) countries: Benin, Niger, Chad, and Cameroon.
*   **Tensions:** In 1961, Nigeria broke diplomatic ties with France because France tested a nuclear atomic bomb in the Sahara Desert, which Nigeria saw as a threat to African safety. Also, during the Nigerian Civil War, France supported the secessionist Republic of Biafra, causing friction.
*   **Cooperation:** Today, relations are much better. France is a major investor in Nigeria (especially in oil and construction), and they cooperate on regional security against insurgencies in West Africa.

### China
In recent decades, China has become a massive partner for Nigeria, focusing heavily on economic diplomacy.
*   **Cooperation:** China provides loans and technical expertise to build major infrastructure in Nigeria, such as railways, airport terminals, and power plants. Trade between the two countries is huge, making China one of Nigeria's most significant modern allies.

## Relations with developing countries

**Content b: Relations with developing countries, e.g. the Technical Aid Corps (TAC), Peace-Keeping Operations etc.**

Nigeria considers itself a leader in the "Global South" (developing countries in Africa, Asia, Latin America, and the Caribbean). To strengthen ties with these nations, Nigeria uses several foreign policy tools.

### The Technical Aid Corps (TAC)
Established in **1987** during the military regime of General Ibrahim Babangida, the Technical Aid Corps (TAC) scheme is a volunteer programme designed to promote South-South cooperation (cooperation among developing countries). 

**How it works:**
*   Nigeria recruits young, qualified professionals (such as medical doctors, nurses, engineers, teachers, and agricultural experts).
*   These volunteers are sent to African, Caribbean, and Pacific (ACP) countries that have requested their specific skills.
*   They serve for two years. The host country provides accommodation, while the Nigerian government pays their salaries and allowances.

**Benefits:**
*   It serves as an alternative to giving direct financial aid, which Nigeria could ill afford during the economic downturns of the 1980s.
*   It creates a positive image for Nigeria abroad.
*   It strengthens diplomatic and cultural ties between Nigeria and the receiving nations.

### Peace-Keeping Operations
Nigeria has a long and proud history of participating in international peace-keeping missions. This helps stabilise developing regions that have been torn apart by conflict.

*   **United Nations (UN) Missions:** Just days after independence in 1960, Nigeria sent troops to the UN peacekeeping mission in the Congo (ONUC). Since then, Nigerian troops and police have served in UN missions in Lebanon, Yugoslavia, Rwanda, Somalia, and Darfur (Sudan).
*   **ECOWAS / ECOMOG:** Nigeria was the driving force behind the Economic Community of West African States Monitoring Group (ECOMOG). In the 1990s, Nigeria spent billions of dollars and lost hundreds of soldiers leading ECOMOG to restore peace in civil-war-torn Liberia and Sierra Leone.
*   **African Union (AU) Missions:** Nigeria has also provided troops for AU peacekeeping efforts across the continent.

By contributing to peace-keeping, Nigeria proves its commitment to the objective of "promoting international co-operation and universal peace."

## Nigeria’s Non-Alignment Policy

**Content c: Nigeria’s Non-Alignment Policy.**
**Objective ii: analyse Nigeria’s non-aligned posture during the cold war.**

### What was the Cold War?
After the Second World War ended in 1945, the world became divided into two rival ideological blocs:
1. **The Western Capitalist Bloc:** Led by the United States of America.
2. **The Eastern Communist Bloc:** Led by the Soviet Union (USSR).

This period of intense political and military rivalry (without direct fighting between the two superpowers) was called the **Cold War**, lasting until the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991.

### The Policy of Non-Alignment
Upon gaining independence in 1960, Nigeria joined the **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)**. 
**Non-alignment** is a foreign policy posture where a country refuses to formally ally itself with either the Western bloc or the Eastern bloc. 

**Why did Nigeria adopt Non-Alignment?**
*   To protect its newly won independence and national sovereignty.
*   To avoid being dragged into superpower conflicts.
*   To retain the freedom to judge international issues based on national interest, rather than being told what to do by a superpower.
*   To attract economic aid and technical assistance from *both* the East and the West.

*Note: Non-alignment does not mean "neutrality". A neutral country stays completely out of world affairs (like Switzerland). A non-aligned country is actively involved in the UN and world politics but refuses to permanently join a superpower's team.*

### Analysing Nigeria’s Non-Aligned Posture
While Nigeria declared itself non-aligned on paper, its actual posture during the Cold War often fluctuated.

**1. The Pro-West Era (The First Republic: 1960–1966)**
Under Prime Minister Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa, Nigeria’s non-alignment was heavily tilted towards the West (Britain and the USA).
*   **The Anglo-Nigerian Defence Pact:** In 1960, Balewa’s government signed a secret defence pact with Britain, allowing British military forces to maintain a base in Kano. Nigerian students and opposition politicians strongly protested, arguing that this violated the principle of non-alignment. The public pressure forced the government to cancel the pact in 1962.
*   **Skepticism of the East:** The government placed restrictions on Nigerians travelling to communist countries and delayed opening an embassy in the Soviet Union.
*   **An independent voice:** However, the government did show flashes of true independence, such as breaking diplomatic ties with France in 1961 over atomic tests in the Sahara, which annoyed Western powers.

**2. The True Test: The Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970)**
The civil war forced Nigeria to balance its non-aligned posture.
*   When the Federal Military Government (under Gen. Yakubu Gowon) needed weapons to fight the Biafran secessionists, it first asked Britain and the USA. Both countries hesitated or refused to sell offensive weapons.
*   Nigeria then turned to the Eastern bloc (the Soviet Union), which eagerly supplied the necessary fighter jets and artillery. 
*   This period marked a shift towards true non-alignment, as Nigeria realised it could not rely solely on the West and needed a balanced relationship with both blocs.

**3. The Afrocentric Era (1970s onwards)**
After the Civil War, Nigeria became more confident and economically powerful due to the oil boom. Under leaders like Gen. Murtala Mohammed and Gen. Olusegun Obasanjo, Nigeria took strong, independent stances—especially against apartheid in South Africa and colonialism in Angola—often directly opposing the USA and Britain. 

In summary, Nigeria's non-alignment posture started out as heavily pro-Western, but matured into a more balanced, independent, and Africa-focused policy as the Cold War progressed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Foreign policy** | A government's strategy for dealing with other nations. |
| **National interest** | The goals and ambitions of a nation (economic, military, or cultural) that guide its foreign policy. |
| **Afrocentricity** | Making Africa the centre-piece of Nigeria's foreign policy. |
| **Technical Aid Corps (TAC)** | A volunteer programme established in 1987 that sends Nigerian professionals to assist other developing countries. |
| **ECOMOG** | Economic Community of West African States Monitoring Group; a West African peace-keeping force heavily backed by Nigeria. |
| **Cold War** | The period of political tension (1945–1991) between the capitalist West (led by USA) and communist East (led by USSR). |
| **Non-Alignment** | The policy of not forming a formal alliance with either superpower bloc during the Cold War. |
| **Neutrality** | Staying completely out of international conflicts (unlike non-alignment, which allows for active participation without taking a fixed side). |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Centre-piece of Foreign Policy:** If an exam asks what the centre-piece or core of Nigeria's foreign policy is, the answer is always **Africa**.
*   **Non-Alignment vs Neutrality:** A common mistake is confusing these two. Non-alignment means not joining a superpower bloc, but still being active in global politics. Neutrality means refusing to take a side in *any* conflict at all. Nigeria was non-aligned, but never neutral (e.g., Nigeria actively fought against apartheid).
*   **The Defence Pact:** Remember that the Anglo-Nigerian Defence Pact was cancelled in **1962** due to student protests. This is a classic exam question showing the power of public opinion on foreign policy.
*   **France and the Sahara:** Know the reason Nigeria broke ties with France in 1961: French nuclear testing in the Sahara Desert.
*   **Arms during the Civil War:** Remember that the **Soviet Union (USSR)** supplied arms to the Nigerian Federal Government during the civil war when Western allies hesitated.

## Quick summary

1. Nigeria's foreign policy objectives include promoting national interest, African unity, international cooperation, respect for international law, and a just economic order.
2. Nigeria maintains relations with major powers (USA, UK, China, Russia, France) for trade, technology, and security, though tensions have occurred historically.
3. The Technical Aid Corps (TAC), established in 1987, shares Nigerian professional expertise with other developing nations (ACP countries) to promote South-South cooperation.
4. Nigeria has a strong track record of peace-keeping, contributing heavily to UN, AU, and ECOWAS (ECOMOG) missions in places like Congo, Liberia, and Sierra Leone.
5. During the Cold War, Nigeria adopted a policy of Non-Alignment, refusing to formally join either the US-led capitalist bloc or the Soviet-led communist bloc.
6. The cancellation of the Anglo-Nigerian Defence Pact in 1962 and the purchase of arms from the USSR during the Civil War were key tests of Nigeria's non-aligned posture.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following is considered the centre-piece of Nigeria's foreign policy?**
- A. The Non-Aligned Movement
- B. The Commonwealth of Nations
- C. Africa
- D. The United Nations

**Answer: C.** Africa is widely described as the centre-piece of Nigeria's foreign policy, meaning the peace, unity, and development of the African continent are its top priorities.

**2. The Technical Aid Corps (TAC) was established primarily to**
- A. provide military assistance to African countries.
- B. promote economic and social development through the sharing of manpower.
- C. fight against apartheid in South Africa.
- D. secure loans from the World Bank.

**Answer: B.** TAC was created to provide skilled Nigerian professionals to other developing countries, promoting South-South cooperation and sharing manpower instead of giving financial aid.

**3. During the Cold War, Nigeria’s policy of non-alignment meant that it**
- A. refused to join any international organisation.
- B. remained neutral and silent on all international political issues.
- C. refused to align permanently with either the Eastern or Western blocs.
- D. maintained diplomatic relations only with African countries.

**Answer: C.** Non-alignment means refusing to formally join the US-led capitalist bloc or the Soviet-led communist bloc, allowing the country to make independent decisions. It does not mean staying silent or neutral on world issues (misunderstanding behind Option B).

**4. The Anglo-Nigerian Defence Pact was abrogated (cancelled) in 1962 largely due to**
- A. the outbreak of the Nigerian Civil War.
- B. pressure and protests from Nigerian students and the public.
- C. the United Nations demanding its cancellation.
- D. a military coup d'état.

**Answer: B.** University students and opposition politicians heavily protested the pact, arguing it compromised Nigeria's sovereignty and violated its non-aligned status, forcing the government to cancel it.

**5. Which major power supplied arms to the Federal Military Government during the Nigerian Civil War when traditional allies hesitated?**
- A. The United States of America
- B. France
- C. The Soviet Union
- D. China

**Answer: C.** When the US and Britain hesitated to sell offensive weapons, the Federal Government turned to the Soviet Union, which supplied fighter jets and artillery. France actually supported Biafra.

**6. Nigeria severed diplomatic relations with France in 1961 because France**
- A. supported Biafra during the civil war.
- B. tested an atomic bomb in the Sahara Desert.
- C. refused to buy Nigerian crude oil.
- D. colonised neighbouring West African countries.

**Answer: B.** In 1961, France conducted nuclear tests in the Sahara Desert. Nigeria saw this as a threat to the continent and broke off diplomatic ties in protest.

**7. Which of the following is a core objective of Nigeria's foreign policy as stated in the 1999 Constitution?**
- A. Promotion of a just world economic order
- B. Establishment of a one-party system across Africa
- C. Domination of West African trade
- D. Alignment with capitalist economies

**Answer: A.** The promotion of a just world economic order is one of the foreign policy objectives listed in Section 19 of the 1999 Constitution.

**8. ECOMOG was a peace-keeping initiative primarily championed by Nigeria to resolve conflicts in**
- A. Congo and Rwanda.
- B. Liberia and Sierra Leone.
- C. Sudan and Somalia.
- D. Angola and Mozambique.

**Answer: B.** ECOMOG (the ECOWAS Monitoring Group) was heavily funded and led by Nigeria to restore peace during the civil wars in Liberia and Sierra Leone in the 1990s.

**9. The Technical Aid Corps scheme was introduced during the administration of**
- A. Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa
- B. General Yakubu Gowon
- C. General Ibrahim Babangida
- D. Chief Olusegun Obasanjo

**Answer: C.** The TAC scheme was established in 1987 by the military government of General Ibrahim Babangida.

**10. In the early years of independence, Nigeria's non-aligned posture was often criticised for being**
- A. too Afrocentric.
- B. pro-Eastern bloc.
- C. pro-Western bloc.
- D. entirely neutral.

**Answer: C.** Under Prime Minister Balewa, Nigeria was non-aligned on paper but heavily favoured the West (Britain and the USA) in its economic and diplomatic actions, which drew criticism from the public.
