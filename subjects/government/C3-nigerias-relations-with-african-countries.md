## Africa as the “Centrepiece” of Nigeria’s Foreign Policy

**Content a: Africa as the “centre piece” of Nigeria’s foreign policy – guiding principles, implementation and implications.**

Since Nigeria gained independence in 1960, its foreign policy has been primarily focused on the African continent. This focus is what is meant by describing Africa as the **"centrepiece"** (the most important part) of Nigeria's foreign policy. 

The concept began under the government of the First Republic (Prime Minister Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa), who strongly believed that Nigeria had a historic mission to lead and protect Africa. However, the phrase "Africa as the centrepiece" was officially adopted and popularised during the military regime of **General Murtala Mohammed** and **General Olusegun Obasanjo** (1975–1979), following the recommendations of a committee on foreign policy headed by Professor Adebayo Adedeji.

Every Nigerian government since then—whether military or civilian—has maintained this Afrocentric (Africa-focused) approach.

### Guiding Principles

The "centrepiece" policy is built on several core principles that guide how Nigeria relates to other African nations:

1. **Total Eradication of Colonialism and White Minority Rule:** Nigeria was strictly opposed to the colonization of any African territory and fought against the racist Apartheid regime in South Africa and minority rule in Rhodesia (now Zimbabwe).
2. **Sovereign Equality of all African States:** Nigeria treats all African countries as equal partners in diplomacy, regardless of their size, population, or economic strength.
3. **Non-interference in the Internal Affairs of Other States:** Nigeria believes that every country has the right to govern itself without external meddling, a principle borrowed from the charters of the UN and the Organization of African Unity (OAU).
4. **Promotion of African Unity and Economic Integration:** Nigeria actively supports organizations that bring African countries together, such as the African Union (AU) and the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS).
5. **Peaceful Settlement of Disputes:** Nigeria prefers negotiation, mediation, and diplomacy over war when resolving conflicts between African nations.

### Implementation: Evaluating the Role of Nigeria in Continental Affairs

**Objective i: evaluate the role of Nigeria in continental affairs.**

Nigeria has backed up its "centrepiece" policy with massive financial, military, and diplomatic support across the continent. We can evaluate Nigeria's role in African affairs through several distinct areas:

#### 1. Decolonization and the Anti-Apartheid Struggle
Nigeria played a leading role in liberating African nations from colonial and racist rule.
*   **Angola (1975):** In a bold diplomatic move that defied the Western powers, the Murtala Mohammed regime officially recognized the Popular Movement for the Liberation of Angola (MPLA) as the legitimate government of Angola, tipping the scales in their favour.
*   **South Africa:** Nigeria was famously designated a "Frontline State" in the fight against Apartheid, even though it is geographically located in West Africa, far from South Africa. The Nigerian government set up the Southern Africa Relief Fund (SARF), to which Nigerian civil servants voluntarily contributed a portion of their salaries. Nigeria also provided scholarships for South African students and boycotted international sporting events (like the 1986 Commonwealth Games) to protest against countries supporting the Apartheid regime.
*   **Zimbabwe:** Nigeria nationalized the assets of British Petroleum (BP) in 1979 to pressure the British government into granting independence to Zimbabwe.

#### 2. Peacekeeping and Conflict Resolution
Nigeria is recognized as the "policeman" of West Africa and a major contributor to continental peace.
*   **ECOMOG:** In 1990, Nigeria spearheaded the creation of the ECOWAS Monitoring Group (ECOMOG). Under the military regime of General Ibrahim Babangida (and later Sani Abacha), Nigeria provided the bulk of the troops, military hardware, and funding to stop the brutal civil wars in **Liberia** and **Sierra Leone**. 
*   **Other Interventions:** Nigerian troops and police have participated in numerous United Nations (UN) and African Union (AU) peacekeeping missions in countries like Congo, Rwanda, Sudan (Darfur), and Somalia. Nigeria also restored the democratically elected government of São Tomé and Príncipe after a military coup in 2003.

#### 3. Economic Integration and Assistance
*   **ECOWAS:** Nigeria was a founding father of ECOWAS in 1975, largely through the efforts of General Yakubu Gowon and President Gnassingbé Eyadéma of Togo.
*   **Technical Aid Corps (TAC):** Established in 1987 by the Babangida administration, the TAC scheme sends young Nigerian professionals (doctors, engineers, teachers, lawyers) to other African, Caribbean, and Pacific nations to assist in their development. This serves as a powerful tool for Nigerian "soft power."
*   **Trust Funds:** Nigeria established a Nigeria Trust Fund (NTF) under the African Development Bank (AfDB) in 1976 to provide soft loans to less developed African countries.

#### 4. Political Leadership
*   Nigeria has consistently hosted major African summits and provided leadership in the transition from the old Organization of African Unity (OAU) to the modern African Union (AU) in 2002.
*   Nigeria frequently acts as a mediator in political crises within member states of ECOWAS (for example, intervening diplomatically during political unrest in Mali, Guinea, and The Gambia).

### Implications of the Afrocentric Policy

The "Africa as the centrepiece" policy has had major consequences (implications) for Nigeria, both positive and negative.

| Positive Implications (Merits) | Negative Implications (Criticisms) |
| --- | --- |
| **Respect and Prestige:** It earned Nigeria the title of "Giant of Africa" and immense respect in global politics. | **"Father Christmas" Diplomacy:** Critics argue Nigeria spends too much money developing other African nations while its own citizens suffer from poverty and poor infrastructure. |
| **Regional Stability:** By funding ECOMOG, Nigeria stopped rebel wars in neighboring countries from spreading to its own borders. | **Loss of Lives:** Thousands of Nigerian soldiers died in foreign civil wars (e.g., Liberia and Sierra Leone) that did not directly threaten Nigeria. |
| **Liberation of the Continent:** Nigeria's aggressive push helped speed up the end of colonialism and Apartheid. | **Ingratitude and Xenophobia:** Despite Nigeria's massive sacrifices for Southern Africa, Nigerians have sometimes been victims of xenophobic (anti-foreigner) attacks in countries like South Africa. |
| **Economic Opportunities:** Championing ECOWAS has opened up markets for Nigerian banks, businesses, and traders across West Africa. | **Neglect of Domestic Interests:** Some scholars argue that a country's foreign policy should put its own national economic interests first, rather than focusing solely on continental brotherhood. |

## NEPAD: New Partnership for Africa’s Development

**Content b: NEPAD – origin, objectives and implications.**

As the 21st century approached, African leaders realized that despite political independence, the continent was still suffering from massive poverty, heavy foreign debt, poor infrastructure, and marginalization in the global economy. To fix this, they created a new economic blueprint called **NEPAD** (New Partnership for Africa's Development).

### Origin of NEPAD

NEPAD was officially adopted by African Heads of State at the 37th summit of the Organization of African Unity (OAU) in **Lusaka, Zambia, in July 2001**. 

It was born out of the merger of several different economic plans proposed by prominent African leaders:
1. **The Millennium Partnership for the African Recovery Programme (MAP):** Promoted by President Thabo Mbeki of South Africa, President Olusegun Obasanjo of Nigeria, and President Abdelaziz Bouteflika of Algeria.
2. **The OMEGA Plan:** Proposed by President Abdoulaye Wade of Senegal, focusing on infrastructure and education.
3. **The Compact for African Recovery:** Initiated by the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa (UNECA).

These plans were combined to form the New African Initiative (NAI), which was later renamed **NEPAD** in October 2001. NEPAD was later integrated fully into the structure of the African Union (AU) as its primary economic development programme (now known as the African Union Development Agency, AUDA-NEPAD).

**The Founding Fathers of NEPAD** are generally recognized as the leaders of Nigeria (Obasanjo), South Africa (Mbeki), Algeria (Bouteflika), Senegal (Wade), and Egypt (Hosni Mubarak).

### Objectives of NEPAD

NEPAD is essentially a pledge by African leaders, based on a common vision, that they have a duty to end poverty and put their countries on a path of sustainable growth. Its main objectives are:

1. **To eradicate poverty** across the African continent.
2. **To promote sustainable growth and development**, ensuring that future generations can thrive.
3. **To integrate Africa into the global economy**, stopping the marginalization of Africa in international trade.
4. **To empower women** and promote their role in all areas of development.
5. **To promote good governance**, which includes democratic principles, human rights, and stopping corruption. (African leaders realized that without peace, democracy, and accountable governments, foreign investors would not bring money to Africa).

### The African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM)

A unique and very important feature of NEPAD is the **African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM)**, introduced in 2003. 
*   **What it is:** The APRM is a voluntary tool where African countries agree to assess and monitor each other's political and economic governance. 
*   **How it works:** An independent panel of experts visits a member country, evaluates its democracy, human rights, and economic management, and writes a report. This report is presented to other African leaders ("peers"). 
*   **Why it matters:** It marks a shift from the old OAU days where leaders ignored the bad behaviour of dictators. With the APRM, African leaders encourage each other to govern well and hold each other accountable.

### Assessing the Role of NEPAD in Developing Africa

**Objective ii: assess the role of NEPAD in developing Africa.**

Has NEPAD been successful? We can evaluate its role and implications by looking at its achievements and its challenges.

#### Positive Roles and Achievements (Successes)
1. **African Ownership:** Before NEPAD, economic plans for Africa were usually forced on the continent by the IMF or World Bank. NEPAD is uniquely "homegrown"—created by Africans, for Africans.
2. **Focus on Good Governance:** Through the APRM, NEPAD has pressured several African countries to hold freer elections, improve human rights, and reduce corruption. Nigeria, for example, has undergone the peer review process.
3. **Infrastructure Development:** NEPAD has helped mobilize funds and design plans for cross-border projects, such as regional highways, energy pools (sharing electricity across borders), and broadband internet networks.
4. **Agriculture (CAADP):** NEPAD launched the Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Programme (CAADP), which pushed African governments to commit at least 10% of their national budgets to agriculture to ensure food security.
5. **Changing Africa's Image:** By demonstrating a commitment to democracy and accountability, NEPAD helped convince Western countries and the G8 (now G7) to forgive massive amounts of debt owed by African countries (including Nigeria's Paris Club debt relief under President Obasanjo).

#### Challenges and Limitations (Failures)
1. **Over-reliance on Foreign Funding:** While the *idea* of NEPAD is African, the *money* to run its projects relies heavily on Western donors (the UN, the G8, the EU). Critics argue this makes it a "begging bowl" rather than a true partnership.
2. **Lack of Grassroots Involvement:** NEPAD was designed by presidents and elite politicians. Most ordinary African citizens, civil society groups, and grassroots organizations do not know what NEPAD is or feel involved in it.
3. **Implementation Problems:** African leaders are good at signing agreements at summits but poor at putting them into action. Many infrastructure projects remain on paper because countries fail to pay their required dues.
4. **Political Instability:** Wars, terrorism (like Boko Haram and Al-Shabaab), and a recent wave of military coups in West Africa have severely disrupted NEPAD's goals for economic integration and peace.
5. **The APRM is Toothless:** The African Peer Review Mechanism is voluntary, and it has no power to punish a country or leader that refuses to follow good governance practices.

## Key terms

| **Centrepiece** | The most prominent or important feature. In this context, it means Africa is the main focus of Nigeria's diplomacy. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Who coined "Centrepiece"?** A very common JAMB question asks under which regime the "Africa as the centrepiece" policy was formalized. The correct answer is the **Murtala/Obasanjo military regime** (1975–1979), following the Adedeji panel report.
*   **Don't confuse NEPAD with ECOWAS or AU.** NEPAD is an *economic programme/blueprint*, not a separate international organization. It is now a development agency under the African Union.
*   **Nigeria in Angola:** Remember that Nigeria's boldest foreign policy move in the 1970s was the recognition of the **MPLA** in Angola, opposing the stance of the USA.
*   **Founding Fathers of NEPAD:** Memorize the key leaders: Obasanjo (Nigeria), Mbeki (South Africa), Bouteflika (Algeria), and Wade (Senegal).
*   **"Father Christmas" criticism:** If a question asks about the major criticism of Nigeria's Afrocentric policy, look for options relating to neglecting domestic problems or wasting resources abroad.
*   **The function of APRM:** The African Peer Review Mechanism is about **good governance and accountability**, not a military alliance or a trade tariff.

## Quick summary

1. Africa has been the "centrepiece" (main focus) of Nigeria's foreign policy since independence, but the policy was officially formalized under the Murtala/Obasanjo military regime (1975–1979).
2. The guiding principles include the eradication of colonialism/Apartheid, sovereign equality, non-interference, and the promotion of economic integration.
3. Nigeria implemented this by supporting liberation movements in Southern Africa (Angola, South Africa, Zimbabwe), funding peacekeeping missions (ECOMOG), and spearheading ECOWAS.
4. While the policy earned Nigeria prestige as the "Giant of Africa," critics argue it drained national wealth at the expense of domestic development (the "Father Christmas" syndrome).
5. NEPAD (New Partnership for Africa's Development) was adopted in 2001 to eradicate poverty, promote sustainable development, and integrate Africa into the global economy.
6. The major founders of NEPAD were the presidents of Nigeria, South Africa, Algeria, Senegal, and Egypt.
7. NEPAD's most unique feature is the African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM), which encourages good governance through mutual accountability among African states.
8. Although NEPAD has promoted African ownership of development and improved some infrastructure and governance, it is heavily criticized for relying too much on Western funding and lacking grassroots participation.

## Practice questions

**1. The foreign policy principle of "Africa as the centrepiece" was officially articulated and adopted under the regime of**

- A. Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa
- B. General Yakubu Gowon
- C. General Murtala Mohammed / Olusegun Obasanjo
- D. General Ibrahim Babangida

**Answer: C.** While Balewa laid the foundation, the Adedeji committee under the Murtala/Obasanjo regime officially articulated and popularized the "centrepiece" concept.

**2. Which of the following is a major criticism of Nigeria’s Afrocentric foreign policy?**

- A. It made Nigeria too dependent on the United Nations.
- B. It focuses too much on domestic economic growth at the expense of diplomacy.
- C. It neglected the socio-economic development of Nigerian citizens in favour of other African nations.
- D. It caused the total collapse of ECOWAS.

**Answer: C.** Critics refer to this as the "Father Christmas" diplomacy, arguing that Nigeria spent huge resources liberating and securing other African nations while its own infrastructure and citizens suffered.

**3. In 1975, Nigeria took a bold anti-imperialist step that shocked Western powers by recognizing the MPLA as the legitimate government of**

- A. South Africa
- B. Angola
- C. Zimbabwe
- D. Mozambique

**Answer: B.** Under Murtala Mohammed, Nigeria recognized the Popular Movement for the Liberation of Angola (MPLA), defying the United States and apartheid South Africa.

**4. The African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM) is a core component of**

- A. ECOWAS
- B. The Non-Aligned Movement
- C. NEPAD
- D. The Technical Aid Corps

**Answer: C.** The APRM is a unique feature of NEPAD designed to ensure that participating African countries adhere to agreed values of good political and economic governance.

**5. Which of the following leaders was one of the principal architects of NEPAD?**

- A. Nelson Mandela
- B. Thabo Mbeki
- C. Kwame Nkrumah
- D. Julius Nyerere

**Answer: B.** Thabo Mbeki (South Africa), along with Olusegun Obasanjo (Nigeria) and others, merged their economic recovery plans to form NEPAD in 2001.

**6. NEPAD was established primarily to**

- A. create a joint African military command to fight terrorism.
- B. eradicate poverty and promote sustainable development in Africa.
- C. remove all borders and create a single African country.
- D. challenge the authority of the United Nations in Africa.

**Answer: B.** NEPAD is an economic blueprint aimed at poverty eradication, sustainable growth, and the integration of Africa into the global economy.

**7. A notable achievement of Nigeria's role as the "policeman" of West Africa was its leadership and funding of**

- A. NEPAD
- B. ECOMOG
- C. SARF
- D. The OAU Liberation Committee

**Answer: B.** Nigeria spearheaded and provided the bulk of the troops and funding for ECOMOG, which restored peace in Liberia and Sierra Leone.

**8. One of the main reasons NEPAD is often criticized by scholars is its**

- A. over-reliance on financial support from Western donors and institutions.
- B. refusal to interact with the global economy.
- C. aggressive military stance against former colonial masters.
- D. strict penalties for countries that fail the peer review process.

**Answer: A.** Despite being an African initiative, NEPAD relies heavily on external funding (from the G7, World Bank, etc.) to execute its projects, leading to questions about true African ownership.

**9. The Technical Aid Corps (TAC) was introduced by Nigeria as a foreign policy tool to**

- A. spy on rival African nations.
- B. provide military hardware to liberation movements.
- C. send young Nigerian professionals to assist other developing nations.
- D. collect taxes from Nigerians living in the diaspora.

**Answer: C.** Established in 1987, the TAC sends Nigerian professionals like doctors and teachers to African, Caribbean, and Pacific countries to aid their development and boost Nigeria's soft power.

**10. By declaring itself a "Frontline State", Nigeria was primarily committing itself to**

- A. fighting the French influence in West Africa.
- B. the total eradication of the Apartheid regime in South Africa.
- C. securing its borders against the Republic of Cameroon.
- D. becoming the permanent headquarters of the African Union.

**Answer: B.** Even though Nigeria is not geographically located in Southern Africa, it was deeply involved financially and diplomatically in the fight to end Apartheid and white minority rule, earning it the title of an honorary "Frontline State."
