GOVERNMENT

## GENERAL OBJECTIVES

The aim of the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME) syllabus in Government is to prepare the candidates for the Board’s examination. It is designed to test their knowledge in Government, with a view to determining their suitability for placement in institutions of higher learning in Nigeria.

These objectives are to:

i. appreciate the meaning of Government;
ii. analyse the framework and specify the institutions of Government;
iii. appreciate the basic principles of democratic governance and their application in Nigeria;
iv. explain the concept of citizenship and define the duties and obligations of a citizen;
v. appreciate the process of political development in Nigeria;
vi. evaluate the political development and problems of governance in Nigeria;
vii. understand the determinants and dynamics of foreign policy as they relate to Nigeria;
viii. assess the role of Nigeria as a member of the international community and the workings of international organizations.

**Additional checks by Claude (against the PDF)**

*   **Checked against the PDF:** the transcription above matches the PDF text word for word, including the printed errors.
*   **Unofficial or out of date?** The PDF is pages 94 to 101 of a larger JAMB booklet, so it has no title page, edition year or "valid for" years. It looks like the official UTME format (same wording and layout as the other JAMB syllabuses in this repo), but this cannot be confirmed from the document. Its recommended texts include 2020, 2022 and 2023 editions, so it is recent. Nothing in it looks like a WAEC/NECO topic.
*   **Part III Topic 4 and Part IV Topic 1 overlap.** Both name the UN, the Commonwealth, the OAU/AU, ECOWAS and OPEC. Part III looks at Nigeria's involvement and what Nigeria gains. Part IV looks at the organisations themselves (origin, structure, achievements, problems). Part IV also adds the "African Petroleum Producers Association" (APPA), which is not listed in Part III; the notes should say what is reliably known and mark anything unsure.
*   **Dated or changed items (hedge in the notes):** the OAU was replaced by the African Union in 2002; the "Third Republic" (early 1990s transition) was never completed; the 1989 Constitution never fully came into force; "1999 (As Amended)" has been amended more than once and may be amended again; the military-era programmes named under Part II Topic 12 (SAP, DFRRI, People's Bank, MAMSER) are historical; the names, powers and funding of commissions (Part II Topic 7), NEPAD (Part III Topic 3) and Nigeria's foreign-policy emphases change over time.
*   **Spelling as printed:** "Oliver Lyttleton" (Part II Topic 4d) is usually spelled "Lyttelton" in textbooks; "Imperialists Penetration" (Part II Topic 2) is the syllabus's own wording.
*   **Possible overlaps inside Part 1:** Topic 3 ("Monarchy") and Topic 6 ("Monarchical" system) both involve monarchy, and Topic 4 (arms of government) overlaps with Topic 9 (separation of powers). Textbooks classify forms, structures and systems of government differently; say so.
