# JAMB History Notes: Kanem-Borno in the 19th Century

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section B, Topic 2 of the JAMB History syllabus: The Nigeria Area 1800 – 1900 – Kanem-Borno. It covers the following content items:

- a. The long survival and collapse of the Saifawa dynasty
- b. Borno under the Shehus
- c. Borno under Rabeh
- d. Borno and the Sokoto caliphate.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. highlight the factors for the long survival of the Saifawa dynasty;
2. determine the factors that led to the collapse of the Saifawa dynasty;
3. examine Borno under the administration of the Shehus;
4. assess the role of Rabeh in Borno’s history.

## Factors for the long survival of the Saifawa dynasty

**Objective i: highlight the factors for the long survival of the Saifawa dynasty.**
**Content a: The long survival and collapse of the Saifawa dynasty (part 1).**

The **Saifawa (or Sefuwa) dynasty** was the ruling family of the Kanem-Borno Empire. It is one of the longest-lasting royal dynasties in human history, ruling for over a thousand years (from roughly the 9th century until 1846). 

The kings of the Saifawa dynasty used the title **Mai**. Several key factors helped them survive for so long:

1. **Strategic Location and Wealth from Trade:** Kanem-Borno was located near Lake Chad, at a crossroads of the Trans-Saharan trade routes. The empire controlled the valuable salt routes to Bilma and trade routes leading north to Tripoli and Fezzan. Wealth from trading slaves, natron (a salt-like mineral), and ivory funded the state and its army.
2. **Islam as a Unifying Force:** The Saifawa rulers adopted Islam as early as the 11th century. Islam provided a common religious identity that united different ethnic groups. It also opened up diplomatic and trade relations with North Africa and the Middle East, bringing in Muslim scholars, wealth, and new ideas.
3. **Strong Military Force:** The Saifawa dynasty maintained a powerful army. They imported horses from North Africa to build a formidable cavalry. By the time of the great Mai Idris Alooma (who ruled in the late 16th century), Borno had imported firearms (muskets) from the Ottoman Empire, giving them a massive advantage over their neighbours.
4. **Effective Central Administration:** The Mai ruled through a well-organized system of government. He was assisted by a council of state called the **Majlis**, made up of high-ranking nobles, military commanders, and royal family members. The empire was divided into fiefs (territories) controlled by trusted officials who collected taxes and raised armies for the Mai.
5. **Adaptability and Flexibility:** When faced with a major crisis, the dynasty knew how to adapt. For example, in the late 14th century, when the rival Bulala people attacked Kanem, the Saifawa dynasty did not collapse. Instead, they retreated to the west of Lake Chad and established a new capital at **Ngazargamu** in Borno, where they rebuilt their power.

## Borno and the Sokoto Caliphate

**Content d: Borno and the Sokoto caliphate.**

In the early 19th century, Borno faced its greatest threat yet: the Fulani Jihad led by **Usman dan Fodio** (also spelt Uthman dan Fodio), which had already established the Sokoto Caliphate in neighbouring Hausaland (see `B1-the-sokoto-caliphate.md`).

### The Fulani Attacks
There were many Fulani pastoralists living within Borno. Inspired by Usman dan Fodio's jihad in Hausaland, Fulani leaders in Borno (such as Gwoni Mukhtar and Malam Zaki) launched their own uprisings against the Mai of Borno around 1804. 

The Fulani jihadists argued that the rulers of Borno were corrupt and mixed Islamic practices with traditional, un-Islamic customs. By 1808, the Fulani forces successfully attacked and captured the Saifawa capital, **Ngazargamu**, forcing the reigning king, **Mai Dunama Lefiami**, to flee.

### The Intellectual Debate
The conflict between Borno and the Sokoto Caliphate was not just fought with swords; it was also fought with pens. Mai Dunama asked for the help of a brilliant Islamic scholar and military leader, **Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi**, who was living in Borno.

Al-Kanemi mobilized armed support to fight off the Fulani, but he also wrote a famous series of letters to the leaders of the Sokoto Caliphate, specifically to Usman dan Fodio's son, **Muhammad Bello**. 
*   **Al-Kanemi's argument:** He argued that Borno had been a Muslim state for centuries. He stated that while the people of Borno might commit sins, committing sins did not make them unbelievers (pagans). Therefore, a jihad (holy war) against them was illegal under Islamic law.
*   **Sokoto's argument:** Muhammad Bello replied that the rulers of Borno were secretly supporting the Hausa kings (who were enemies of the jihad) and were allowing pagan practices, which justified the attacks.

Ultimately, al-Kanemi's military leadership successfully drove the Fulani out of Ngazargamu and secured Borno's independence. Borno never became part of the Sokoto Caliphate, though the western parts of its territory were lost to Sokoto.

## The collapse of the Saifawa dynasty

**Objective ii: determine the factors that led to the collapse of the Saifawa dynasty.**
**Content a: The long survival and collapse of the Saifawa dynasty (part 2).**

Although al-Kanemi saved Borno from the Sokoto Caliphate, his intervention was the beginning of the end for the Saifawa dynasty. The collapse of the 1,000-year-old dynasty was caused by a combination of internal weakness and external pressure.

### Factors that led to the collapse

1. **Military and Administrative Weakness:** By the late 18th century, the Saifawa Mais had lost their military strength. They had become weak, figurehead rulers who rarely left their palaces, leaving the defence of the empire to ineffective nobles. 
2. **The Impact of the Fulani Jihad:** The Fulani attacks between 1804 and 1808 exposed the military weakness of the Saifawa rulers. Because the Mai’s army could not defeat the Fulani, the Mai lost the respect and loyalty of his subjects.
3. **The Rise of al-Kanemi:** Because Mai Dunama could not defend his own capital, he relied entirely on **Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi**. After driving out the Fulani, al-Kanemi became the true hero of Borno. He refused to hand back military power to the Mai. Instead, al-Kanemi became the *de facto* (actual) ruler of Borno, while the Mai was reduced to a powerless puppet.
4. **Economic Decline:** The loss of western territories to the Sokoto Caliphate and constant warfare disrupted trade routes, which reduced the wealth that the Mai relied on to reward his supporters.
5. **The Final Rebellion (1846):** Al-Kanemi died in 1837, and his son, **Umar**, took over as the real power in Borno. The reigning Mai, **Mai Ibrahim**, decided he had had enough of being a puppet. In 1846, Mai Ibrahim secretly plotted with the neighbouring Wadai Empire to attack Umar and restore Saifawa power. 

The plot failed. When Umar discovered the betrayal, he had Mai Ibrahim executed. Shortly after, Ibrahim's young son, Ali, was killed in battle. With no powerful royal family members left, Shehu Umar officially ended the Saifawa dynasty in **1846** and took full control of Borno.

| Factor | How it contributed to the collapse |
| --- | --- |
| **Fulani Jihad** | Exposed the Mai's weakness and forced the Mai to rely on outside help (al-Kanemi). |
| **Rise of al-Kanemi** | Transferred real military and political power away from the Mai to al-Kanemi's family. |
| **Failed 1846 Rebellion** | Gave Shehu Umar the justification to execute the last Mai and abolish the dynasty entirely. |

## Borno under the administration of the Shehus

**Objective iii: examine Borno under the administration of the Shehus.**
**Content b: Borno under the Shehus.**

After the collapse of the Saifawa dynasty, the rulers of Borno took the title of **Shehu** (derived from the Arabic *Sheikh*, meaning scholar or leader). The period from the rise of al-Kanemi until the late 19th century is known as the era of the **al-Kanemi dynasty**.

### Administration under Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi
Although al-Kanemi never took the title of Mai, he was the true ruler of Borno until his death in 1837. 
*   **New Capital:** He built a new capital at **Kukawa** in 1814, shifting the centre of power away from the old Saifawa capital, Ngazargamu.
*   **Islamic Reforms:** As an Islamic scholar, al-Kanemi reformed Borno’s legal system. He enforced Sharia (Islamic law) strictly, discouraging traditional practices that he viewed as un-Islamic.
*   **New Advisers:** He ignored the old Saifawa nobles (*Kokenawa*) and instead ruled with an inner council made up of his own trusted friends, scholars, and Arab supporters who had fought alongside him.

### Administration under Shehu Umar (1837–1881)
Al-Kanemi's son, Umar, officially became the first **Shehu** after ending the Saifawa dynasty in 1846. His long reign shaped the administrative structure of the new Borno.
*   **Centralization of Power:** Shehu Umar further centralized power. To ensure loyalty, he stopped giving fiefs (lands) to hereditary nobles. Instead, he gave important administrative and military positions to trusted personal servants, commoners, and royal slaves (*Kachelas*). 
*   **Economic Revival:** Under Shehu Umar, Borno experienced an economic boom. Kukawa became a major centre for the Trans-Saharan trade, exporting slaves, ivory, and ostrich feathers in exchange for firearms and European manufactured goods.
*   **Foreign Relations:** Borno opened up to European explorers during this time. The famous German explorer **Heinrich Barth** stayed in Kukawa in 1851 and wrote detailed accounts of the Shehu's administration and the city's wealth.

### The Decline of the Shehus
After the death of Shehu Umar in 1881, Borno began to decline. His successors faced internal power struggles, civil wars, and shrinking revenues because the Trans-Saharan trade was drying up as Europeans began trading directly at the West African coast. By the early 1890s, the Shehu's army was weak, making Borno vulnerable to invasion.

## Borno under Rabeh

**Objective iv: assess the role of Rabeh in Borno’s history.**
**Content c: Borno under Rabeh.**

The weakness of the later Shehus attracted a powerful foreign invader: **Rabeh Fadlallah** (often written as Rabih az-Zubayr). 

### Who was Rabeh?
Rabeh was a Sudanese warlord. He was originally a military commander in the Sudan but moved westward into Central Africa with a private, highly disciplined army. His army was heavily armed with modern European rifles and artillery, which he acquired through trade.

### Rabeh's Conquest of Borno (1893)
In **1893**, Rabeh invaded Borno. The weak army of the Shehus was no match for Rabeh’s well-trained soldiers and modern weapons. Rabeh easily defeated the Borno forces, captured and destroyed the capital city of Kukawa, and declared himself the ruler of Borno. The surviving members of the al-Kanemi dynasty fled or were killed.

### Rabeh’s Administration (1893–1900)
Rabeh ruled Borno as a military dictator for seven years. 
*   **New Capital:** He abandoned the ruined city of Kukawa and built a new, heavily fortified capital at **Dikwa**.
*   **Military Rule:** Rabeh divided Borno into military districts, placing his loyal generals and commanders in charge of them. The traditional Kanuri leaders were completely sidelined or killed.
*   **Economic Exploitation:** His rule was very harsh. To maintain his large army and buy ammunition, Rabeh levied heavy taxes on the people. He also engaged in massive slave raiding, devastating the local population and ruining the agricultural economy.
*   **Mahdism:** Rabeh promoted the ideology of Mahdism (a revolutionary Islamic movement originating in Sudan), forcing his subjects to wear the patched garments of the Mahdists.

### The Role and Significance of Rabeh in Borno’s History
Historians view Rabeh's role in Borno's history in two ways:
1. **As a Destroyer:** Rabeh brought extreme suffering to Borno. He destroyed Kukawa, caused a massive loss of life through war and slave raiding, ruined the economy, and temporarily destroyed the al-Kanemi dynasty. 
2. **As a State Builder and Anti-Colonial Figure:** Conversely, Rabeh built a highly centralized, powerful military state in a short time. His strong, independent African state stood directly in the path of the advancing European colonial powers (especially the French).

**The End of Rabeh:** 
Rabeh's aggressive expansion eventually led to a clash with the French, who were trying to conquer the Lake Chad region. In **1900**, Rabeh was defeated and killed by French forces at the **Battle of Kousseri**. 

Following Rabeh's death, the European powers (Britain, France, and Germany) partitioned Borno among themselves. The British eventually restored a member of the al-Kanemi dynasty as the Shehu of British Borno, but only as a traditional ruler under British colonial control.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Mixing up the dynasties:** Remember that the **Saifawa dynasty** is the ancient one that fell, and the **al-Kanemi dynasty** (the Shehus) is the one that replaced it.
- **Why the Saifawa fell:** A common mistake is saying the Fulani jihad *ended* the Saifawa dynasty. The Fulani jihad *weakened* it, but the dynasty officially ended in **1846** when Shehu Umar executed Mai Ibrahim after a failed rebellion.
- **The intellectual debate:** JAMB often asks how Borno defended itself against the Sokoto Caliphate. Remember it was both through military force and an **intellectual debate** led by al-Kanemi against Muhammad Bello, arguing that Borno was already a Muslim state.
- **Rabeh's identity:** Rabeh was not a European colonialist; he was a Sudanese warlord. He was killed by the French, which paved the way for European colonization of the area.
- **Capitals matter:** Know the changing capitals. Ngazargamu (Saifawa) → Kukawa (Shehus/al-Kanemi) → Dikwa (Rabeh). 

## Quick summary

1. The Saifawa dynasty ruled Kanem-Borno for over 1,000 years, surviving due to Islam, wealth from Trans-Saharan trade, a strong military, and a flexible administration.
2. In the early 19th century, Borno was attacked by Fulani jihadists from the Sokoto Caliphate, who captured the capital, Ngazargamu.
3. Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi helped the Mai drive out the Fulani using military force and intellectual arguments, but in return, he kept true political power for himself.
4. The Saifawa dynasty collapsed officially in 1846 when Mai Ibrahim plotted against al-Kanemi’s son, Shehu Umar, leading to the execution of the royal family.
5. Borno under the Shehus (al-Kanemi dynasty) saw a centralized administration, an economic boom through trade, and a new capital at Kukawa, though the state weakened by the late 1800s.
6. In 1893, the Sudanese warlord Rabeh invaded Borno, destroyed Kukawa, and ruled as a harsh military dictator from his new capital, Dikwa.
7. Rabeh’s death at the hands of the French in 1900 marked the end of Borno's independence and the beginning of European colonial rule.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following was a major factor for the long survival of the Saifawa dynasty in Kanem-Borno?**
- A. The introduction of Christianity to the region
- B. Strategic location and wealth from the Trans-Saharan trade
- C. Protection provided by the British colonial government
- D. An alliance with the Sokoto Caliphate

**Answer: B.** Control of the Trans-Saharan trade routes provided the wealth needed to maintain a strong military and administration, which helped the dynasty survive for over 1,000 years.

**2. The scholar who defended Borno intellectually and militarily against the Fulani jihad was**
- A. Usman dan Fodio
- B. Rabeh Fadlallah
- C. Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi
- D. Mai Idris Alooma

**Answer: C.** Al-Kanemi mobilized forces to push back the Fulani and wrote letters to Sokoto leaders arguing that a jihad against Borno was unjustified. 

**3. In his intellectual debate with the Sokoto Caliphate, al-Kanemi argued that**
- A. Borno had a superior military and would crush Sokoto
- B. the people of Borno were traditionalists and wanted to remain so
- C. committing sins did not make the people of Borno unbelievers, so a jihad was illegal
- D. Usman dan Fodio was not a true Fulani leader

**Answer: C.** Al-Kanemi argued that since Borno was already an Islamic state, the sins of its people did not justify a holy war against them under Islamic law.

**4. The Saifawa dynasty officially collapsed and came to an end in**
- A. 1804
- B. 1846
- C. 1893
- D. 1914

**Answer: B.** The dynasty ended in 1846 when Shehu Umar executed Mai Ibrahim and his heir after Ibrahim attempted a rebellion with the help of the Wadai Empire. 

**5. Which title was officially adopted by the rulers of the al-Kanemi dynasty after the fall of the Saifawa?**
- A. Mai
- B. Emir
- C. Sultan
- D. Shehu

**Answer: D.** After ending the Saifawa dynasty, Umar officially adopted the title of Shehu, a term derived from the Arabic word Sheikh.

**6. Under the administration of Shehu Umar, political and military power in Borno was increasingly given to**
- A. the traditional hereditary nobles of the Saifawa era
- B. trusted slaves, commoners, and personal followers
- C. French and British colonial advisers
- D. Fulani leaders from the Sokoto Caliphate

**Answer: B.** To centralize power and ensure loyalty, Shehu Umar sidelined the old hereditary nobles and appointed trusted personal servants and slaves (Kachelas) to important positions.

**7. Borno was invaded and conquered in 1893 by a Sudanese warlord named**
- A. Gwoni Mukhtar
- B. Heinrich Barth
- C. Rabeh Fadlallah
- D. Mai Dunama

**Answer: C.** Rabeh Fadlallah (or Rabih az-Zubayr) invaded Borno with a well-armed private army in 1893, defeating the Shehu's forces.

**8. What was the capital city established by Rabeh during his rule over Borno?**
- A. Ngazargamu
- B. Kukawa
- C. Dikwa
- D. Lokoja

**Answer: C.** After destroying Kukawa, Rabeh built a new, heavily fortified capital at Dikwa, from which he ruled his military state. (Ngazargamu was the Saifawa capital; Kukawa was the Shehus' capital).

**9. How did Rabeh’s rule in Borno come to an end?**
- A. He was overthrown by a rebellion led by Shehu Umar.
- B. He died of natural causes and his son surrendered to the British.
- C. He was defeated and killed by the French at the Battle of Kousseri in 1900.
- D. He signed a treaty handing over power to the Sokoto Caliphate.

**Answer: C.** Rabeh’s expansionist policies led to a clash with the advancing French colonial forces, who defeated and killed him in 1900. 

**10. A major consequence of Rabeh's invasion of Borno was that it**
- A. led to a long period of peace and economic prosperity
- B. permanently restored the Saifawa dynasty to the throne
- C. united Borno and the Sokoto Caliphate into a single empire
- D. devastated the local population and temporarily destroyed the power of the Shehus

**Answer: D.** Rabeh's invasion and subsequent military rule caused immense suffering, ruined the economy through heavy taxation and slave raiding, and removed the al-Kanemi dynasty from power until the Europeans later restored them as figureheads.
