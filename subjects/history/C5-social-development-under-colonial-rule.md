# JAMB History Notes: Social Development under Colonial Rule

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 5 of the JAMB History syllabus: Social Development under Colonial Rule. It covers the following content items:

* a. Western education
* b. Urbanization/social integration
* c. Improvement unions
* d. Health institutions

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. identify the scope of social development under colonial rule;
2. examine the impact of urbanization on the people;
3. examine the level of social integration among the people.

## The scope of social development under colonial rule

**Objective i: identify the scope of social development under colonial rule.**

Before the British conquest, the peoples of the Nigeria area had their own indigenous social systems. They had traditional methods of educating their children, treating diseases, and building communities. 

When the British established colonial rule between 1900 and 1960, they introduced major changes to the way people lived, worked, and interacted. **Social development** under colonial rule refers to these sweeping changes in society. 

The scope of this social development includes four main areas:
1. **Western education:** The shift from traditional and Islamic education to classroom-based reading and writing in English.
2. **Modern health institutions:** The introduction of Western (orthodox) medicine, hospitals, and dispensaries to replace or exist alongside traditional medicine.
3. **Urbanization and social integration:** The rapid growth of new cities, the migration of people from villages to towns, and how different ethnic groups mixed in these new environments.
4. **Ethnic improvement unions:** The formation of self-help groups by city dwellers to support each other and develop their hometowns.

While these developments brought modern amenities and created a new educated elite, they also caused problems like crowded slums, the breakdown of traditional family values, and uneven development between different regions of Nigeria.

## Western education under colonial rule

**Content a: Western education.**

Western education was the most powerful tool of social change during the colonial period. It completely changed the social ladder, creating a new class of people who would eventually lead the fight for independence.

### The role of Christian missionaries
Western education was primarily introduced by **Christian missionaries**, not the colonial government. In the mid-19th century, groups like the Church Missionary Society (CMS), the Wesleyan Methodists, and the Roman Catholics began setting up primary schools in the southern coastal areas (such as Badagry, Abeokuta, and Calabar).

**Why did missionaries build schools?**
Their main goal was **evangelism**. They wanted Africans to be able to read the Bible, understand Christian teachings, and become local catechists or priests. 

### The role of the colonial government
At first, the British colonial government showed very little interest in education. It was only later, when the government and European trading companies needed **clerks, interpreters, and lower-level civil servants**, that the government began to give grants to mission schools and establish a few government schools. King's College in Lagos was established in 1909 to train a small elite group of Nigerians. Yaba Higher College was later established in 1932 to provide intermediate medical, engineering, and teaching training.

### The educational imbalance between North and South
One of the most significant effects of colonial education policy was the massive gap between the North and the South.
* **In the South:** Missionaries were welcomed (or at least tolerated) by many communities. They built hundreds of schools. As a result, the South had a large number of Western-educated people.
* **In the North:** Lord Lugard, the British High Commissioner, promised the Northern Emirs that Christian missionaries would not be allowed to interfere with their religion. Because schools were run by missionaries, Western education was kept out of most parts of the Muslim North. The colonial government built only a few schools (like Katsina College, established in 1921) to train the sons of Emirs and chiefs. 

This educational gap later caused intense political rivalry between the North and the South during the movement for independence.

### Comparing Traditional and Western Education

| Feature | Traditional Education | Western Education |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Setting** | Informal; took place at home, on the farm, or in the community. | Formal; took place in dedicated classrooms. |
| **Curriculum** | Practical skills (farming, weaving), moral values, history, and respect for elders. | Reading, writing, arithmetic, and Christian religious knowledge. |
| **Language** | Mother tongue (indigenous languages). | Mostly English language. |
| **Goal** | To integrate the child into the community and make them useful members of society. | Initially to convert people to Christianity; later to produce clerks and administrators. |

## Modern health institutions

**Content d: Health institutions.**

Before colonial rule, Nigerians relied on traditional healers, herbalists, and local midwives for healthcare. Diseases like malaria, smallpox, sleeping sickness, and leprosy were common and often fatal.

### The introduction of orthodox medicine
Just like education, modern (orthodox) medicine was pioneered by Christian missionaries. They set up small dispensaries, clinics, and leper colonies to treat the sick as a way of showing Christian charity and winning converts. Notable early missionary hospitals include the Sacred Heart Hospital in Abeokuta (established by Catholics).

### Government intervention
The colonial government eventually stepped in, but its initial aim was to protect **European administrators and merchants** from tropical diseases. The "white man's grave" reputation of West Africa forced the government to build hospitals, but these were strictly segregated. 
* There were **European Hospitals** (with the best equipment) for white officials.
* There were **African Hospitals** (often underfunded) for local people.

Over time, especially after the 1920s, the government began to build more hospitals, maternity centres, and dispensaries for the general public. They also launched vaccination campaigns against smallpox and fought epidemics of sleeping sickness in the North. 

### Impact of modern health institutions
1. **Population growth:** Better healthcare and vaccinations reduced infant mortality and increased life expectancy, leading to rapid population growth.
2. **Decline of traditional medicine:** Although traditional medicine continued to exist, Western medicine became a status symbol, and traditional healers lost some of their authority.
3. **Improved workforce:** A healthier population meant a stronger workforce for the colonial economy.

## Urbanization and social integration

**Content b: Urbanization/social integration.**

**Urbanization** is the movement of people from rural villages to live in towns and cities, causing those cities to grow rapidly. Under colonial rule, urbanization completely changed the map of Nigeria.

### The growth of new and old cities
While some traditional cities (like Kano and Ibadan) were already large before colonial rule, the British created entirely new urban centres and expanded others. 
* **Administrative centres:** Cities like Lagos, Kaduna, and Enugu grew rapidly because they were chosen as headquarters for colonial administration.
* **Mining towns:** Jos grew because of tin mining; Enugu grew because of coal mining.
* **Transport hubs:** The colonial railway lines and ports caused cities like Port Harcourt, Minna, and Kafanchan to spring up from nothing because they were crucial for exporting cash crops and minerals.

### The impact of urbanization on the people

**Objective ii: examine the impact of urbanization on the people.**

The rush of young men and women from villages to cities had deep effects on Nigerian society.

**Positive impacts:**
1. **Job opportunities:** People found wage employment in the civil service, the railways, the mines, and European trading companies (like UAC).
2. **Access to modern amenities:** Cities were the first places to get electricity, pipe-borne water, tarred roads, and hospitals.
3. **Rise of nationalism:** Cities brought different educated Nigerians together. Living close to their colonial masters, they saw the inequalities clearly. Cities like Lagos became the birthplaces of newspapers, trade unions, and nationalist political parties.
4. **New social classes:** A new class system based on education and wealth replaced the old system based on royalty and age. 

**Negative impacts:**
1. **Overcrowding and slums:** The cities grew too fast for the colonial government to plan for. This led to severe housing shortages, overcrowding, and the creation of slums.
2. **Breakdown of traditional values:** Away from the watchful eyes of village elders, traditional morality declined. Crime, prostitution, and juvenile delinquency became serious problems in the cities.
3. **Unemployment:** More people moved to the cities than there were jobs available, leading to urban poverty.

### The level of social integration

**Objective iii: examine the level of social integration among the people.**

**Social integration** refers to how well different ethnic and religious groups mixed, interacted, and formed a united society in the new urban centres. 

In colonial Nigerian cities, the level of social integration was **mixed**. There were forces pushing people together, but colonial policies and ethnic fears kept them apart.

**Factors promoting social integration:**
* **Shared workplaces:** Igbo, Yoruba, Hausa, and other groups worked side-by-side in the civil service, railways, and mines.
* **Common language:** English and Pidgin English became the languages of communication between people of different tongues.
* **Nationalist movements:** Educated elites from different regions joined the same trade unions and early political parties (like the NCNC) to fight British rule.
* **Inter-marriages:** Living in the same towns led to a gradual, though small, increase in marriages across ethnic lines.

**Factors preventing social integration (Segregation):**
* **Colonial Segregation Policies:** The British practiced residential segregation. They created the **GRA (Government Reserved Area)** for white officials, keeping them completely separate from Africans.
* **The Sabon Gari System:** In Northern cities (like Kano and Zaria), the colonial government created the **Sabon Gari** (meaning "new town" in Hausa; it served as the quarter for strangers, mainly southern migrants). Southern immigrants, who were mostly Christians, were forced to live in these segregated areas, away from the indigenous Muslim population. This prevented deep social mixing and bred suspicion.
* **Ethnic clustering:** Even in southern cities, people from the same ethnic group tended to live in the same neighbourhoods to feel safe, which limited cross-cultural interaction.

## Ethnic improvement unions

**Content c: Improvement unions.**

When people migrated from their rural villages to the big, strange, and often harsh cities, they felt lonely and insecure. To survive, they looked for people who spoke their language and shared their culture. This led to the formation of **Ethnic Improvement Unions** (also known as Town Unions or Progressive Unions).

### What were Improvement Unions?
These were socio-cultural associations formed by migrants living in urban centres to protect their interests and promote the development of their hometowns. They started as small village or clan meetings and eventually grew into massive ethnic associations.

**Examples of major Improvement Unions:**
* **The Ibibio State Union (formed in 1928):** One of the earliest and most successful. It famously raised funds to send six Ibibio students to universities in the UK and USA in 1938.
* **The Igbo State Union (formed in the 1930s/40s):** Grew out of various Igbo town unions to unify the Igbo people and promote education.
* **Egbe Omo Oduduwa (formed in 1945 in London, launched in Nigeria in 1948):** A pan-Yoruba cultural association founded by Obafemi Awolowo and others to unite the Yoruba people.
* **Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa (formed in 1949):** A Northern Nigerian cultural organization formed to promote the social and political interests of the North.

### The role and impact of Improvement Unions
1. **Welfare and mutual support:** They helped new arrivals find jobs and housing. They also paid for the hospital bills or funeral expenses of their members.
2. **Educational development:** They acted as self-help groups. Because the colonial government was slow to build schools, these unions taxed their members in the cities and used the money to build primary and secondary schools in their home villages. They also awarded overseas scholarships.
3. **Political mobilization:** Although they started as cultural and welfare groups, they eventually became heavily involved in politics. *Egbe Omo Oduduwa* gave birth to the Action Group (AG) political party, and *Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa* transformed into the Northern People's Congress (NPC). 
4. **Rise of ethnic nationalism:** While they brought development to their local areas, they also made people more loyal to their ethnic groups than to Nigeria as a whole. This contributed to the ethnic rivalry and tribal politics that troubled Nigeria just before and after independence.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Social development** | Changes in the way people live, learn, interact, and access healthcare within a society. |
| **Western education** | Formal, classroom-based learning introduced by European missionaries and the colonial government, focusing on reading, writing, and arithmetic. |
| **Orthodox medicine** | Modern, science-based healthcare system involving hospitals, doctors, and manufactured drugs. |
| **Urbanization** | The rapid migration of people from rural areas to towns, and the physical expansion of those towns into cities. |
| **Social integration** | The process by which different ethnic, religious, and social groups mix and interact to form a unified society. |
| **GRA** | Government Reserved Area; high-quality residential areas built by the colonial government exclusively for European officials. |
| **Sabon Gari** | Hausa for "new town"; the strangers' quarter in Northern Nigerian cities, designated by the colonial government for immigrants from the South. |
| **Improvement Unions** | Associations formed by city dwellers to support their members in the city and promote the development (especially schools) of their hometowns. |
| **Evangelism** | The spreading of the Christian gospel; the primary reason missionaries built schools. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Missionary vs. Government aims:** A very common JAMB question asks why schools were built. Remember: Missionaries built schools mainly for **evangelism** (to read the Bible). The colonial government supported schools mainly to produce **clerks and interpreters** for administration.
* **The North/South educational gap:** Understand *why* it happened. It was not because the North was entirely against learning (they had Islamic education), but because Lugard restricted Christian missionaries from operating in the Emirates, and missionaries were the main providers of Western education.
* **Purpose of colonial railways:** They were NOT built for passenger travel or social integration. They were built strictly to evacuate cash crops (like groundnuts and cocoa) and minerals (like coal and tin) to the seaports.
* **Sabon Gari's role:** Do not confuse Sabon Gari with social integration. It was a tool of **segregation**, keeping Southerners and Northerners apart in the same city.
* **Improvement Unions:** Remember that they started as socio-cultural welfare groups, not political parties. However, they laid the foundation for the ethnic-based political parties of the 1950s.

## Quick summary

1. Social development under colonial rule involved the introduction of Western education, modern healthcare, rapid urbanization, and the rise of ethnic improvement unions.
2. Christian missionaries were the pioneers of Western education and modern healthcare, aiming mostly to spread Christianity. 
3. The colonial government's policy of keeping missionaries out of the Muslim North created a massive educational gap between the North and the South.
4. Urbanization was driven by the colonial economy (railways, ports, mining, and administration), creating new cities like Port Harcourt, Enugu, and Jos.
5. While cities provided jobs and amenities, they also led to overcrowding, slums, and the breakdown of traditional family values.
6. Social integration in cities was mixed; while workplaces and Pidgin English brought people together, colonial policies like the Sabon Gari and GRA enforced segregation.
7. Migrants in cities formed Ethnic Improvement Unions (e.g., Ibibio State Union, Egbe Omo Oduduwa) to support each other and build schools in their hometowns, though these later fueled ethnic politics.

## Practice questions

**1. The primary aim of Christian missionaries in establishing schools in colonial Nigeria was to**
- A. produce clerks for the colonial administration
- B. train medical doctors and engineers
- C. facilitate the spread of the Christian religion
- D. prepare Nigerians for political independence

**Answer: C.** The missionaries' main goal was evangelism. They wanted Africans to learn how to read so they could study the Bible and become church workers.

**2. The massive gap in Western education between Northern and Southern Nigeria was primarily caused by**
- A. the lack of rivers in the North for missionaries to travel on
- B. Lord Lugard's policy of restricting Christian missionaries from the Muslim North
- C. the North's refusal to pay taxes for education
- D. the colonial government's desire to punish the Emirs

**Answer: B.** Lugard promised the Emirs that the colonial government would not interfere with Islam, which meant keeping out the Christian missionaries who were the main providers of Western education.

**3. Which of the following was a negative impact of rapid urbanization under colonial rule?**
- A. The rise of nationalist movements
- B. The introduction of pipe-borne water
- C. The creation of overcrowding and slums
- D. The formation of a new educated elite

**Answer: C.** Because cities grew faster than the colonial government planned for, it led to severe housing shortages, slums, and poor sanitary conditions.

**4. The colonial policy that hindered social integration by forcing southern immigrants to live in separate quarters in Northern cities was the**
- A. Indirect Rule system
- B. Sabon Gari system
- C. Government Reserved Area (GRA) policy
- D. Assimilation policy

**Answer: B.** The Sabon Gari ("new town"; the strangers' quarter) system segregated southern, largely Christian immigrants from the indigenous Muslim population, preventing deep social integration.

**5. What was the main reason the colonial government constructed railway lines in Nigeria?**
- A. To promote social integration among different ethnic groups
- B. To evacuate cash crops and minerals to the coast for export
- C. To transport students to Western schools
- D. To make travel comfortable for African traditional rulers

**Answer: B.** The colonial economy was extractive. Railways were built from the hinterland to the ports (Lagos and Port Harcourt) strictly to move raw materials to Europe.

**6. Ethnic Improvement Unions were formed in colonial cities primarily to**
- A. fight against the traditional rulers
- B. enforce traditional religion in the cities
- C. provide welfare for urban migrants and develop their hometowns
- D. assist the British in collecting taxes

**Answer: C.** They acted as mutual aid societies to help migrants survive city life and raised funds to build schools and amenities back in their home villages.

**7. Which of the following associations famously sponsored its brilliant sons to study abroad in 1938?**
- A. Egbe Omo Oduduwa
- B. Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa
- C. The Igbo State Union
- D. The Ibibio State Union

**Answer: D.** The Ibibio State Union is highly regarded in Nigerian history for its self-help efforts, famously raising money to send six students to the UK and USA in 1938.

**8. The early hospitals built by the colonial government were primarily intended to**
- A. cure traditional diseases among the rural population
- B. serve the health needs of European officials and merchants
- C. train African medical doctors
- D. replace Christian missionary dispensaries

**Answer: B.** Early colonial health policy focused heavily on protecting Europeans from tropical diseases, leading to the creation of segregated "European Hospitals".

**9. How did urbanization contribute to the rise of nationalism in Nigeria?**
- A. It separated people from different ethnic groups so they could plot independently.
- B. It brought educated elites together in one place, allowing them to form trade unions and political parties.
- C. It made the colonial masters give up their power willingly.
- D. It caused traditional rulers to declare war on the British.

**Answer: B.** Cities served as melting pots where educated Nigerians faced colonial discrimination together, shared ideas, published newspapers, and organized nationalist movements.

**10. *Egbe Omo Oduduwa* and *Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa* are examples of**
- A. European trading companies
- B. early colonial hospitals
- C. socio-cultural improvement unions that later influenced politics
- D. religious organizations that fought against Western education

**Answer: C.** Both were formed as cultural and mutual improvement associations for their respective ethnic groups (Yoruba and Northern peoples) but later morphed into or heavily backed political parties (AG and NPC).
