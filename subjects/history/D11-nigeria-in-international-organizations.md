## Introduction: Afrocentrism in Nigeria's Foreign Policy

Since independence in 1960, Nigeria's foreign policy has been consistently **Afrocentric** (Africa-centered). This means that the political and economic liberation, peace, and integration of the African continent have been the primary goals of its international relations. Nigeria has used its large population, military strength, and oil wealth to support and lead international organizations, especially in Africa.

## Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)

**Objective i & Content a: examine the role of Nigeria in ECOWAS**

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) is a regional political and economic union of West African countries.

### Formation and Nigeria's Leadership
Nigeria was the primary driving force behind the creation of ECOWAS. In the early 1970s, the Nigerian Head of State, **General Yakubu Gowon**, worked closely with President **Gnassingbé Eyadéma** of Togo to tour West Africa and convince other leaders of the need for regional integration. Their efforts led to the signing of the **Treaty of Lagos** on **May 28, 1975**, which formally established ECOWAS.

### Economic and Administrative Roles
*   **Headquarters:** In recognition of Nigeria's central role, the administrative headquarters (Secretariat) of ECOWAS is located in **Abuja**, Nigeria (it was originally in Lagos).
*   **Funding:** Nigeria is the largest financial contributor to ECOWAS, often providing the bulk of the organization’s budget.
*   **Economic Integration:** Nigeria has championed policies such as the free movement of people and goods across West Africa, the creation of an ECOWAS passport, and the push towards a single regional currency (the Eco), though the currency has faced delays.

### Security and Peacekeeping (ECOMOG)
Nigeria's most famous role in ECOWAS has been in security. In 1990, under the Babangida regime, ECOWAS formed a military arm called the **ECOWAS Monitoring Group (ECOMOG)**. Nigeria provided the vast majority of ECOMOG's troops, funding, and military commanders to intervene in civil wars in the region.

## The African Union (AU)

**Objective ii & Content b: assess the role of Nigeria in the AU**

The **Organization of African Unity (OAU)** was formed on **May 25, 1963**, in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. In **2002**, it was officially replaced by the **African Union (AU)**.

### Nigeria in the OAU (1963–2002)
*   **Founding the OAU:** Before 1963, independent African nations were divided into the radical "Casablanca Group" and the moderate "Monrovia Group." Nigeria's Prime Minister, **Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa**, played a key diplomatic role in bridging the gap between these groups, leading to the unified creation of the OAU.
*   **Anti-Apartheid and Decolonization:** Nigeria was one of the loudest voices in the OAU for the liberation of African countries still under colonial or white-minority rule (such as South Africa, Zimbabwe, and Angola). Although geographically far from Southern Africa, Nigeria contributed so much money, military training, and diplomatic support to liberation movements that the OAU recognized it as an honorary **"Frontline State."**
*   **Funding and Leadership:** Nigeria frequently hosted OAU summits (e.g., in Lagos) and was one of the organization's largest financial backers.

### Nigeria in the AU (2002 to present)
*   **Formation of the AU:** During the transition from the OAU to the AU, President Olusegun Obasanjo was heavily involved in drafting the new organization's structure.
*   **NEPAD:** Nigeria was a principal architect of the **New Partnership for Africa’s Development (NEPAD)**, an economic development program adopted by the AU to eradicate poverty and promote sustainable growth.
*   **Peace and Security Council:** Nigeria has consistently served on the AU Peace and Security Council and has contributed troops to AU peacekeeping missions across the continent.

## The Commonwealth of Nations

**Objective iii & Content c: evaluate the role of Nigeria in the Commonwealth of Nations**

The Commonwealth of Nations is a political association of mostly former territories of the British Empire. Nigeria joined the Commonwealth immediately upon gaining independence on **October 1, 1960**.

### Diplomatic Influence and Anti-Apartheid Stance
Nigeria has used its Commonwealth membership to campaign against colonialism and racism:
*   In **1961**, Nigeria (along with other nations) successfully pressured the Commonwealth to force apartheid South Africa to withdraw its membership.
*   In **1986**, Nigeria led a boycott of the Commonwealth Games in Edinburgh (Scotland) to protest the British government's refusal to impose strict economic sanctions on apartheid South Africa.
*   In 1979, the Nigerian government nationalized British Petroleum (BP) assets in Nigeria to pressure Britain over the independence of Zimbabwe (then Rhodesia).

### Suspension and Readmission
Nigeria’s relationship with the Commonwealth faced a major crisis in the 1990s.
*   **Suspension (1995):** During the military regime of General Sani Abacha, Nigeria was suspended from the Commonwealth in November 1995. This occurred after the military government executed environmental activist **Ken Saro-Wiwa and eight other Ogoni leaders** (the "Ogoni 9"), which the Commonwealth viewed as a severe human rights violation.
*   **Readmission (1999):** Nigeria was readmitted to the Commonwealth in May 1999 following the successful transition to democratic, civilian rule (the Fourth Republic).

### Hosting the Commonwealth
To signal its full return to the international community, Nigeria hosted the **Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM)** in Abuja in **2003**.

## Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC)

**Objective iv & Content d: assess the role of Nigeria in the OPEC**

OPEC is an intergovernmental organization of major oil-exporting nations that coordinates petroleum policies and stabilizes oil markets. Nigeria, a major oil producer, joined OPEC in **July 1971**, shortly after the Nigerian Civil War.

### Economic Importance
Because Nigeria's economy relies heavily on crude oil exports for government revenue and foreign exchange, OPEC membership is highly strategic. By acting together with other major producers (like Saudi Arabia and Venezuela), Nigeria gains a stronger voice in determining global oil prices than it would have alone.

### Roles and Contributions
*   **Quota Adherence:** Nigeria regularly participates in OPEC agreements to cut or increase oil production (quotas) to balance global supply and stabilize prices.
*   **Leadership:** Nigerians have held high leadership positions in OPEC. Most notably, **Dr. Rilwanu Lukman** served as OPEC Secretary-General and President for many years, helping to steer the organization through global oil crises.
*   **Hosting:** Nigeria has hosted OPEC conferences and summits in Abuja to discuss global energy policies.

## The United Nations Organization (UN)

**Objective v & Content e: examine the role of Nigeria in the UN**

Nigeria was admitted as the **99th member** of the United Nations on **October 7, 1960**, just days after independence. **Jaja Wachuku** was Nigeria's first Permanent Representative to the UN.

### Key Roles and Contributions
*   **Anti-Apartheid Leadership:** Nigeria chaired the **UN Special Committee against Apartheid** for over two decades. Prominent Nigerian diplomats, such as Alhaji Maitama Sule and Professor Ibrahim Gambari, used this platform to mobilize global action against the South African apartheid regime.
*   **UN Security Council:** Although not a permanent member, Nigeria has been elected to a non-permanent seat on the UN Security Council several times (e.g., 1978–1979, 1994–1995, 2010–2011, 2014–2015), representing the African bloc.
*   **Specialized Agencies:** Nigeria actively participates in UN agencies like the WHO, UNESCO, and UNICEF, and Nigerians have held leadership roles (for example, Dr. Babatunde Osotimehin led the UN Population Fund).
*   **Peacekeeping:** Nigeria is one of the world’s largest contributors of military and police personnel to UN peacekeeping missions worldwide.

## Conflict Resolution and Peacekeeping

**Objective vi: examine the role of Nigeria in conflict resolutions in the Congo, Chad, Liberia, Sierra Leone, Guinea and the Sudan**

Nigeria’s military has been highly active in resolving conflicts and restoring peace across Africa. 

### 1. The Congo (ONUC)
Shortly after independence in 1960, the Democratic Republic of the Congo fell into a severe political crisis and civil war (the Congo Crisis). The UN formed a peacekeeping force (ONUC). 
*   **Nigeria's Role:** Nigeria sent army battalions and police units. This was Nigeria's **very first international peacekeeping operation**. 
*   **Leadership:** The Nigerian commander, **Major General Johnson Thomas Umunnakwe (J.T.U.) Aguiyi-Ironsi**, performed so well that he was appointed the overall Force Commander of the entire UN mission in the Congo in 1964.

### 2. Chad
In the late 1970s and early 1980s, Chad (Nigeria's northeastern neighbour) experienced a violent civil war.
*   **Diplomacy:** Nigeria hosted several peace conferences in **Kano** and **Lagos** in 1979 to reconcile the warring Chadian factions.
*   **Peacekeeping:** Nigeria contributed troops to the OAU Peacekeeping Force deployed to Chad in the early 1980s, aiming to stabilize the country and prevent foreign interference.

### 3. Liberia
In 1989, a brutal civil war broke out in Liberia when Charles Taylor’s rebel forces attempted to overthrow President Samuel Doe.
*   **Nigeria's Role:** In 1990, the government of General Ibrahim Babangida pushed ECOWAS to create **ECOMOG**. Nigeria provided the bulk of the troops (over 70% at times), funding, and the Force Commanders (such as Joshua Dogonyaro and John Shagaya).
*   **Outcome:** Despite heavy casualties and high financial costs, Nigerian-led ECOMOG forces eventually secured the capital, Monrovia, and forced the factions into peace agreements, leading to elections in 1997.

### 4. Sierra Leone
Following the Liberian conflict, a civil war escalated in Sierra Leone. In 1997, the democratically elected president, Ahmad Tejan Kabbah, was overthrown by a military coup backed by the RUF rebels.
*   **Nigeria's Role:** Nigeria used ECOMOG forces to intervene in Sierra Leone. Nigerian troops launched a massive offensive, secured the capital city of Freetown, and successfully restored President Kabbah to power in 1998.
*   **Transition to the UN:** The mission was later absorbed into a larger UN mission (UNAMSIL), to which Nigeria remained a major troop contributor.

### 5. Guinea (Guinea-Bissau)
*Note: The JAMB syllabus refers to "Guinea," which generally points to the ECOMOG intervention in **Guinea-Bissau**.*
*   In 1998, a military rebellion broke out against the government of Guinea-Bissau. 
*   **Nigeria's Role:** As part of ECOMOG, Nigeria provided troops alongside other West African states to enforce a peace agreement, protect the government, and stabilize the country until elections could be held. 

### 6. The Sudan (Darfur)
In the early 2000s, a major conflict erupted in the Darfur region of Sudan, leading to a severe humanitarian crisis.
*   **Diplomacy:** In 2006, Nigeria hosted peace talks in its capital, resulting in the signing of the **Abuja Peace Agreement** between the Sudanese government and some rebel groups.
*   **Peacekeeping:** Nigeria was a leading contributor of troops and police to the AU Mission in Sudan (AMIS) and later the joint UN-AU Hybrid Operation in Darfur (UNAMID). Nigerian commanders also served in high leadership positions within the force.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Afrocentric | Focusing on Africa; the core principle of Nigeria’s foreign policy. |
| ECOWAS | Economic Community of West African States, formed in 1975 to promote regional economic integration. |
| ECOMOG | ECOWAS Monitoring Group; the West African peacekeeping force dominated by Nigeria. |
| OAU | Organization of African Unity, founded in 1963 and replaced by the AU in 2002. |
| AU | African Union, the continental body replacing the OAU. |
| Commonwealth | An association of nations consisting mainly of the UK and its former colonies. |
| OPEC | Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries, which coordinates global oil policies. |
| NEPAD | New Partnership for Africa’s Development, an AU economic framework heavily championed by Nigeria. |
| Frontline State | A title given to Nigeria by the OAU for its massive support to Southern African liberation movements, despite not sharing a border with the region. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Formation Dates are crucial:** Memorize the dates: UN (1960), Commonwealth (1960), OAU (1963), OPEC (1971), ECOWAS (1975).
*   **First Peacekeeping Mission:** Congo (1960) was Nigeria's first international peacekeeping mission, not Liberia.
*   **ECOMOG vs. ECOWAS:** Remember that ECOWAS is the political/economic body (formed in 1975), while ECOMOG is its military/peacekeeping arm (formed in 1990 for Liberia).
*   **The Suspension:** Nigeria was suspended from the Commonwealth in **1995** under **General Sani Abacha** due to human rights abuses (the Ogoni 9 execution), not during the Civil War.
*   **Frontline State Confusion:** JAMB often asks why Nigeria was called a "Frontline State." It was because of its immense financial and diplomatic support for the anti-apartheid struggle, *not* because of its geographical location.

## Quick summary

1.  Nigeria's foreign policy has always been Afrocentric, focusing on the peace, liberation, and integration of the African continent.
2.  Nigeria and Togo spearheaded the formation of ECOWAS in 1975, and Nigeria has provided its headquarters, major funding, and military backbone (ECOMOG).
3.  In the OAU/AU, Nigeria played a vital role in decolonization, funding anti-apartheid movements, and designing economic frameworks like NEPAD.
4.  Nigeria used the Commonwealth to fight apartheid but was temporarily suspended between 1995 and 1999 during the Abacha military regime.
5.  By joining OPEC in 1971, Nigeria gained leverage over global oil prices, with Nigerians like Rilwanu Lukman serving in top leadership roles.
6.  At the UN, Nigeria notably chaired the Special Committee against Apartheid for decades and has served on the Security Council as a non-permanent member.
7.  Nigeria has been a regional policeman, intervening to resolve conflicts in the Congo (1960), Chad, Liberia (ECOMOG, 1990), Sierra Leone, Guinea-Bissau, and Sudan (Darfur).

## Practice questions

**1. Which two leaders were the major driving forces behind the formation of ECOWAS?**
- A. Nnamdi Azikiwe and Kwame Nkrumah
- B. Yakubu Gowon and Gnassingbé Eyadéma
- C. Ibrahim Babangida and Charles Taylor
- D. Olusegun Obasanjo and Nelson Mandela

**Answer: B.** General Yakubu Gowon of Nigeria and President Gnassingbé Eyadéma of Togo led the diplomatic efforts that resulted in the 1975 Treaty of Lagos. The confusion in option A comes from early Pan-African leaders who advocated for unity, but they did not form ECOWAS.

**2. Nigeria's first involvement in United Nations peacekeeping operations was in**
- A. Liberia
- B. Sudan
- C. The Congo
- D. Sierra Leone

**Answer: C.** Nigeria sent troops to the Congo (ONUC) in 1960 immediately after independence. The confusion in option A (Liberia) is because it is Nigeria's most famous regional (ECOMOG) mission, but it happened much later (1990).

**3. Nigeria was suspended from the Commonwealth of Nations in 1995 due to**
- A. the annulment of the June 12 elections
- B. the outbreak of the Nigerian Civil War
- C. its withdrawal from the UN Security Council
- D. the execution of Ken Saro-Wiwa and eight other Ogoni leaders

**Answer: D.** The execution of the Ogoni 9 by the Abacha regime in November 1995 was viewed as a major human rights violation, prompting immediate suspension. The confusion in option A (June 12 annulment) is because it also caused international outrage, but it happened in 1993 under Babangida.

**4. Nigeria was recognized as a "Frontline State" by the OAU primarily because of its**
- A. shared geographical borders with South Africa
- B. massive financial and diplomatic support for liberation movements in Southern Africa
- C. large population and military size in West Africa
- D. role as the headquarters of the organization

**Answer: B.** Despite being geographically distant from Southern Africa, Nigeria contributed so heavily to anti-apartheid and liberation movements that it was granted this honorary status. The confusion in option A is because "frontline" normally implies physical borders, which was true for countries like Zambia, but not Nigeria.

**5. The military arm created by ECOWAS in 1990 to resolve the civil war in Liberia was**
- A. ECOMOG
- B. UNAMSIL
- C. AMIS
- D. ONUC

**Answer: A.** ECOMOG (ECOWAS Monitoring Group) was formed by ECOWAS in 1990. The confusion in option B is because UNAMSIL was a UN peacekeeping force that later operated in Sierra Leone.

**6. Nigeria joined the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) in**
- A. 1960
- B. 1971
- C. 1975
- D. 1990

**Answer: B.** Nigeria joined OPEC in 1971. The confusion in option A (1960) comes from mixing it up with the dates Nigeria joined the UN and the Commonwealth.

**7. In the United Nations, Nigeria achieved a major diplomatic milestone against racism by chairing the**
- A. World Health Organization
- B. Security Council permanently
- C. Special Committee against Apartheid
- D. Trusteeship Council

**Answer: C.** Nigeria chaired the UN Special Committee against Apartheid for decades, leading the global diplomatic fight against white minority rule in South Africa. The confusion in option B is because Nigeria has sought a permanent Security Council seat, but it has only ever held non-permanent seats.

**8. In which of the following countries did Nigerian troops successfully intervene to restore democratically elected President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah in 1998?**
- A. Liberia
- B. Guinea
- C. Chad
- D. Sierra Leone

**Answer: D.** ECOMOG forces led by Nigeria reversed the military coup in Sierra Leone and restored President Kabbah to power. The confusion in option A is because Liberia was ECOMOG's first and most famous theater of war.

**9. A prominent Nigerian who served as Force Commander of the UN peacekeeping mission in the Congo in 1964 was**
- A. Yakubu Gowon
- B. J.T.U. Aguiyi-Ironsi
- C. Olusegun Obasanjo
- D. Ibrahim Babangida

**Answer: B.** Major General J.T.U. Aguiyi-Ironsi commanded the Nigerian contingent and later the entire UN ONUC force in the Congo. The confusion in options A, C, and D is because they are famous military heads of state, but Ironsi uniquely led this UN mission.

**10. In 2006, Nigeria hosted peace talks and brokered an agreement (the Abuja Peace Agreement) to resolve the conflict in**
- A. Darfur, Sudan
- B. Monrovia, Liberia
- C. Kinshasa, Congo
- D. Ndjamena, Chad

**Answer: A.** The Abuja Peace Agreement of 2006 was aimed at resolving the conflict in Darfur, Sudan. The confusion in option D (Chad) is because Nigeria also hosted the Kano and Lagos accords, but those were for Chad in 1979.
