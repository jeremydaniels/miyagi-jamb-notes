## Background to the Interim National Government

The Interim National Government (ING) was a very brief unelected government that ruled Nigeria from **26 August 1993 to 17 November 1993** (a period of 82 days). To understand why it was formed, you must look at the political crisis of 1993.

General Ibrahim Babangida had led a long transition programme to return Nigeria to civilian rule. On **12 June 1993**, a presidential election was held. Most domestic and international observers described it as the freest and fairest in Nigeria's history. The two candidates were Chief M.K.O. Abiola of the Social Democratic Party (SDP) and Alhaji Bashir Tofa of the National Republican Convention (NRC).

Early results showed Chief M.K.O. Abiola winning across the country. However, on **23 June 1993**, before the final results were fully announced, the military government annulled (cancelled) the election. 

This annulment caused a massive political crisis. There were violent protests, riots, and strikes across the country, particularly in the South-West. Pro-democracy groups, such as the Campaign for Democracy (CD), demanded that the military hand over power to the winner of the June 12 election. Under immense pressure from the public, civil society, and parts of the military, General Babangida announced that he would "step aside". On **26 August 1993**, he handed power to an unelected, transitional body called the Interim National Government.

## The Role and Challenges of the Interim National Government

**Objective i: examine the role and challenges of the Interim National Government.**

### Formation and Role of the ING

The ING was established by the **Interim Government (Basic Constitutional Provisions) Decree No. 61 of 1993**. General Babangida appointed **Chief Ernest Shonekan**, a civilian businessman and former chief executive of the United Africa Company (UAC), as the Head of State and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces.

The ING was a mixed government consisting of civilian ministers (called secretaries) and military officers. Most notably, General Babangida left behind **General Sani Abacha** as the Secretary of Defence and Vice-Chairman of the ING.

The military gave the ING a specific **mandate (role)**:
1. **Conclude the transition programme:** Its primary task was to organize fresh local government and presidential elections by February 1994 and hand over to an elected civilian president in March 1994.
2. **Pacify the nation:** It was expected to calm the political tension and end the riots caused by the June 12 annulment.
3. **Manage the economy:** It was tasked with stopping the rapid decline of the Nigerian economy and managing the country's debts.

### The Challenges of the ING

From the very first day, the Interim National Government faced massive problems that made it nearly impossible for it to govern effectively.

#### 1. The Crisis of Legitimacy
The biggest challenge of the ING was that many Nigerians did not accept it as a legitimate government. 
* It was unelected and appointed by a military dictator.
* Pro-democracy groups, civil rights organizations (like the Campaign for Democracy), and students saw the ING as an illegal extension of military rule and a tool used to deny Chief M.K.O. Abiola his June 12 victory.
* Many state governors and politicians, particularly from the South-West, boycotted (refused to work with) the ING.
* Supporters of the June 12 election insisted that the only acceptable way forward was to announce the 1993 election results, not to hold fresh elections in 1994.

#### 2. Legal Challenges and the Court Declaration
The legal foundation of the ING was highly questioned. Pro-democracy activists and supporters of M.K.O. Abiola took the government to court.
* On **10 November 1993**, a Lagos High Court presided over by **Justice Dolapo Akinsanya** delivered a historic ruling. 
* The court ruled that Decree No. 61, which established the ING, was null and void (illegal) because General Babangida had signed it into law *after* he had legally lost his executive powers. 
* This ruling meant that, in the eyes of the law, the Interim National Government did not legally exist. This deeply weakened Chief Shonekan's authority.

#### 3. Economic Crisis and Labour Strikes
The ING inherited a struggling economy with high inflation and foreign debt. In an attempt to raise government revenue, the ING introduced a highly unpopular economic policy.
* In early November 1993, the ING removed the government subsidy on petroleum products. This caused the price of petrol to jump significantly (from 70 kobo per litre to 3.25 naira per litre, an increase of over 400%).
* This price hike caused transport fares and food prices to shoot up.
* In response, the **Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC)** and oil workers' unions (such as PENGASSAN and NUPENG) declared a nationwide strike. 
* The strikes crippled the economy, shut down banks and businesses, and paralyzed the country, further weakening the ING.

#### 4. Military Interference and Lack of Control
Although Chief Ernest Shonekan was the Commander-in-Chief on paper, he had no real control over the armed forces.
* **General Sani Abacha**, the Secretary of Defence, held the actual military power. 
* Because Shonekan had no military background and faced massive civilian opposition, he relied entirely on the military to maintain order.
* As protests and strikes intensified, it became clear that the civilian wing of the ING could not enforce its decisions without the military's approval, leaving the government vulnerable to a coup.

| :--- | :--- |
| **Political / Legitimacy** | Boycotts, protests, and rejection by citizens who wanted the June 12 results declared. |
| **Legal** | A High Court declared the government illegal, removing its constitutional authority. |
| **Economic** | Fuel price increases led to nationwide labour strikes, paralyzing the economy. |
| **Military** | The civilian Head of State had no real control over the army; General Abacha held the real power. |

### Achievements of the ING

Despite its short lifespan (82 days) and massive challenges, the Shonekan-led Interim National Government made a few notable moves:
1. **Release of political detainees:** The ING ordered the release of several pro-democracy activists, human rights campaigners, and journalists who had been jailed by the Babangida administration during the June 12 crisis.
2. **Unbanning of newspapers:** The government repealed military decrees that had proscribed (banned) several media houses, such as *The Punch*, *National Concord*, and *The Observer*, allowing them to publish again.
3. **Attempting a democratic framework:** It successfully inaugurated the National Assembly and made initial plans to conduct the new elections scheduled for 1994, though the government collapsed before these could be carried out.

### The Overthrow of the Interim National Government

The combination of the High Court ruling, the nationwide strikes over fuel prices, and the ongoing political crisis created a state of near-anarchy (lawlessness) in Nigeria by mid-November 1993. 

On **17 November 1993**, General Sani Abacha, along with other senior military officers, visited Chief Ernest Shonekan. Following this meeting, Shonekan announced his "resignation" as Head of State. Most historians and political analysts describe this event as a **bloodless palace coup**. 

General Sani Abacha immediately took over as the new military Head of State. He dismantled all remaining democratic institutions from the Babangida transition programme, including the National Assembly, the state civilian governors, and the two political parties (SDP and NRC), returning Nigeria to full military rule.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Interim National Government (ING)** | A temporary, unelected government set up to run Nigeria from August to November 1993. |
| **Annulment** | The official cancellation or invalidation of an event, such as the June 12, 1993 presidential election. |
| **Mandate** | The authority or order given to a government to carry out specific tasks (e.g., the ING's mandate to organize fresh elections). |
| **Legitimacy** | The widespread acceptance by the people that a government has the rightful authority to rule. |
| **Palace coup** | The overthrow of a government by individuals already within the ruling group or government, often without open violence. |
| **Proscribe** | To officially ban or prohibit something, such as when the military banned certain newspapers. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Common mistake:** Confusing the Interim National Government with a military regime. The ING was a *hybrid* (mixed) government headed by a *civilian* (Chief Ernest Shonekan), even though it was created by the military and included military officers.
* **Timeline check:** The ING was incredibly short. It lasted for only about three months (August to November 1993). Do not credit it with long-term policies. 
* **The Legal challenge:** Remember the specific reason the court declared the ING illegal: the decree that created it (Decree 61) was signed by Babangida after he had legally stepped down.
* **The trigger for the strike:** Know that the immediate cause of the massive NLC strike in November 1993 was the removal of the petrol subsidy, not just the June 12 annulment (though June 12 was the background tension).
* **Who took over:** Make sure you know that Shonekan handed over to General Sani Abacha, who was the Secretary of Defence in the ING.

## Quick summary

1. The Interim National Government (ING) was established on 26 August 1993 by General Ibrahim Babangida after he stepped aside due to the crisis caused by the annulment of the June 12 presidential election.
2. Chief Ernest Shonekan, a civilian, was appointed Head of State, while General Sani Abacha served as Secretary of Defence.
3. The ING's main role was to calm the country, manage the economy, and organize fresh presidential elections by February 1994.
4. It faced severe legitimacy challenges because pro-democracy groups and supporters of Chief M.K.O. Abiola rejected it, demanding the June 12 election results instead.
5. In November 1993, a Lagos High Court ruled that the military decree creating the ING was illegal, stripping the government of its constitutional authority.
6. An attempt to remove fuel subsidies caused petrol prices to jump, leading to paralyzing nationwide strikes by labour unions.
7. Despite achievements like unbanning newspapers and freeing some political prisoners, the ING collapsed on 17 November 1993 when General Abacha forced Shonekan to resign and took full military control.

## Practice questions

**1. The Interim National Government (ING) of 1993 was headed by**
- A. General Ibrahim Babangida
- B. Chief M.K.O. Abiola
- C. Chief Ernest Shonekan
- D. General Sani Abacha

**Answer: C.** Chief Ernest Shonekan, a civilian businessman, was appointed as the Head of the Interim National Government by General Babangida. Babangida created the ING, Abiola was the presumed winner of the annulled election, and Abacha overthrew the ING.

**2. The primary reason for the establishment of the Interim National Government was to**
- A. investigate corruption in the military
- B. conclude the transition programme following the June 12 election crisis
- C. rewrite the Nigerian constitution
- D. prepare the country for a civil war

**Answer: B.** The ING was given the mandate to calm the political tension caused by the annulment of the June 12, 1993 election and to organize fresh elections to complete the transition to civilian rule.

**3. Which military officer was left behind as the Secretary of Defence in the Interim National Government?**
- A. General Abdulsalami Abubakar
- B. General Olusegun Obasanjo
- C. General Ibrahim Babangida
- D. General Sani Abacha

**Answer: D.** General Sani Abacha was left in the ING as the Secretary of Defence and Vice-Chairman, which gave him the power to eventually take over the government.

**4. A major legal challenge that weakened the Interim National Government was**
- A. the International Court of Justice ordering it to step down
- B. the National Assembly impeaching the Head of State
- C. a Lagos High Court declaring its creation illegal and void
- D. the Supreme Court forcing it to declare the June 12 results

**Answer: C.** In November 1993, Justice Dolapo Akinsanya of a Lagos High Court ruled that Decree 61, which established the ING, was illegal, thereby stripping the government of its legal authority.

**5. What specific economic policy by the Interim National Government led to massive nationwide strikes by the Nigeria Labour Congress in November 1993?**
- A. The introduction of the Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP)
- B. The removal of the fuel subsidy and increase in petrol prices
- C. The banning of all trade unions
- D. The change of the national currency

**Answer: B.** The ING removed the subsidy on petroleum products, raising the price from 70 kobo to 3.25 naira. This triggered massive strikes that paralyzed the economy.

**6. Why did pro-democracy groups and civil rights organizations heavily oppose the Interim National Government?**
- A. They believed Chief Shonekan was not a Nigerian citizen
- B. They saw it as an illegitimate body designed to deny the June 12 election mandate
- C. They wanted the military to remain in power indefinitely
- D. They opposed the government's decision to ban all newspapers

**Answer: B.** Activists and supporters of the June 12 mandate refused to recognize the ING because it was unelected and was seen as a way to avoid declaring M.K.O. Abiola as the rightful winner.

**7. Despite its brief existence, one achievement of the Interim National Government was the**
- A. successful conduct of local government elections
- B. arrest of the leaders of the June 12 protests
- C. unbanning of several newspapers and release of political detainees
- D. completion of the federal capital territory in Abuja

**Answer: C.** To reduce political tension, Chief Shonekan released several activists who had been detained by the previous military regime and unbanned media houses like *The Punch* and *National Concord*.

**8. The Interim National Government came to an end in November 1993 when**
- A. Chief M.K.O. Abiola was sworn in as president
- B. Chief Ernest Shonekan resigned and General Sani Abacha took over
- C. fresh presidential elections were successfully held
- D. a popular civilian uprising overthrew the government

**Answer: B.** On 17 November 1993, following a meeting with top military officers, Chief Shonekan announced his resignation, and General Sani Abacha took control in what is widely described as a palace coup.

**9. What was the legal instrument used by General Babangida to set up the Interim National Government?**
- A. The 1979 Constitution
- B. The 1999 Constitution
- C. Decree No. 61 of 1993
- D. A resolution by the United Nations

**Answer: C.** The ING was established by the Interim Government (Basic Constitutional Provisions) Decree No. 61 of 1993, which was later declared illegal by a High Court.

**10. Approximately how long did the Interim National Government remain in power?**
- A. Three years
- B. One year
- C. Six months
- D. Less than three months

**Answer: D.** The ING was in power for just 82 days (from 26 August to 17 November 1993), making it a period of less than three months.
