# JAMB History Notes: The Abacha Regime

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section D, Topic 9 of the JAMB History syllabus: The Abacha Regime.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Assess the challenges and achievements of the Abacha Regime.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** The JAMB syllabus does not list specific content items for this topic, only the objective to assess its challenges and achievements. Therefore, these notes cover the key events, policies, political transitions, and economic measures of General Sani Abacha's government from 1993 to 1998, as agreed upon by standard Nigerian history textbooks. Following standard historical practice, "achievements" and "challenges" are treated as neutral lists of documented actions, policies, and outcomes. 

## Background: The Takeover of Power

General Sani Abacha came to power on **November 17, 1993**. Prior to this, Nigeria was governed by the **Interim National Government (ING)**, headed by Chief Ernest Shonekan. The ING had been put in place by General Ibrahim Babangida following the annulment of the June 12, 1993 presidential election.

The ING faced severe political challenges, widespread strikes, and a court ruling that declared it illegal. Citing the need to restore order and save the country from collapse, General Abacha, who was the Secretary of Defence under the ING, forced Chief Shonekan to resign and took over as the Head of State.

Upon taking power, General Abacha dissolved all democratic institutions that had been established during the Babangida transition programme. This included the National Assembly, the state assemblies, the state executive councils, and the two legal political parties (the SDP and the NRC). He replaced the state civilian governors with military administrators.

## Assessing the Regime: Documented Achievements

When history textbooks assess the "achievements" of the Abacha regime, they focus on specific administrative changes, economic interventions, and foreign policy actions that the government carried out.

### 1. Administrative Reorganisation and State Creation
To address demands for more equitable representation, the Abacha administration took two major steps:
*   **The National Constitutional Conference (1994–1995):** The government set up this conference to debate a new constitution. One of its most lasting recommendations was the division of Nigeria into **six geopolitical zones** (North-West, North-East, North-Central, South-West, South-East, and South-South) to help in the fair distribution of resources and political offices.
*   **Creation of Six New States (1996):** On October 1, 1996, General Abacha created six new states, bringing the total number of states in Nigeria to **36**. The new states were:
    1.  **Bayelsa** (carved out of Rivers State)
    2.  **Ebonyi** (carved out of Abia and Enugu States)
    3.  **Ekiti** (carved out of Ondo State)
    4.  **Gombe** (carved out of Bauchi State)
    5.  **Nasarawa** (carved out of Plateau State)
    6.  **Zamfara** (carved out of Sokoto State)
*   **Local Governments:** The regime also increased the number of local government areas to **774**, which remains the number recognized in the current Nigerian constitution.

### 2. Economic Policies and Institutions
The Abacha government introduced several measures to stabilize the economy, which was experiencing high inflation and banking failures when he took over.
*   **The Petroleum Trust Fund (PTF):** In 1994, the government increased the pump price of petroleum products. The extra revenue generated from this increase was channeled into a special fund called the PTF. The government appointed a former Head of State, Major-General Muhammadu Buhari, to chair it. The PTF was widely credited with funding the rehabilitation of roads, hospitals, schools, and water supply projects across the country.
*   **Failed Banks Decree:** To sanitize a corrupt banking sector, the government issued the Failed Banks (Recovery of Debts) and Financial Malpractices in Banks Decree of 1994. This led to the arrest and prosecution of several bank executives who had mismanaged depositors' funds, helping to restore some confidence in the banking system.
*   **Vision 2010:** In 1996, the government set up the Vision 2010 Committee, chaired by Chief Ernest Shonekan. Its goal was to create a long-term economic blueprint for Nigeria to become a developed nation by the year 2010. 
*   **Macroeconomic Stability:** According to economic records, the regime managed to reduce inflation significantly, stabilized the exchange rate of the Naira, and built up Nigeria's foreign reserves, partly aided by rising global oil prices and strict control over government spending.

### 3. Foreign Policy and Regional Security
General Abacha continued Nigeria's active role in West African security through **ECOMOG** (the ECOWAS Monitoring Group).
*   **Sierra Leone:** When the democratically elected government of President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah of Sierra Leone was overthrown by a military coup in 1997, Nigerian-led ECOMOG troops intervened. In February 1998 they ousted the military junta, and President Kabbah returned to Freetown on 10 March 1998.
*   **Liberia:** The regime continued to support ECOMOG operations in Liberia, which eventually helped bring the First Liberian Civil War to an end, leading to elections in 1997.

## Assessing the Regime: Documented Challenges

The "challenges" of the regime primarily revolved around domestic political opposition, human rights controversies, and the resulting international isolation.

### 1. Political Opposition and the June 12 Crisis
General Abacha's government faced strong opposition from pro-democracy groups demanding the validation of the annulled June 12, 1993 election, which Chief M.K.O. Abiola was widely believed to have won.
*   **NADECO:** In May 1994, the **National Democratic Coalition (NADECO)** was formed by politicians, activists, and retired military officers. NADECO openly challenged the military government, calling for Abacha to resign and hand over power to Abiola.
*   **Arrest of M.K.O. Abiola:** In June 1994, Chief Abiola publicly declared himself the lawful president of Nigeria. In response, the government arrested him and charged him with treason. He remained in detention throughout the Abacha regime.
*   **Strikes:** The regime faced severe strikes by trade unions, particularly the national oil workers' unions (NUPENG and PENGASSAN), which paralyzed the economy for several weeks in 1994 before their leaders were arrested and the unions dissolved.

### 2. Human Rights Issues and Coup Plots
The government dealt strictly with political opposition and dissent.
*   **Alleged Coup Plots:** The regime announced the discovery of coup plots in 1995 and 1997. Following the 1995 plot, several prominent figures, including former Head of State General Olusegun Obasanjo and his former deputy, Major-General Shehu Musa Yar'Adua, were arrested and imprisoned. (Yar'Adua later died in prison in 1997). In 1997, Abacha's own deputy, Lieutenant-General Oladipo Diya, and several other officers were arrested and sentenced to death for another alleged plot.
*   **Assassinations:** The period saw a number of unresolved assassinations of prominent political figures, including Alhaja Kudirat Abiola (Chief Abiola's wife) in 1996, and Pa Alfred Rewane, a major financial backer of NADECO.

### 3. The Ogoni Crisis and International Sanctions
One of the most severe challenges for the regime's international image occurred in November 1995. 
*   **Execution of the Ogoni Nine:** Ken Saro-Wiwa, a writer and environmental rights activist, alongside eight other leaders from Ogoniland, had been campaigning against environmental degradation caused by oil extraction. They were tried by a special military tribunal for the murder of four conservative Ogoni chiefs and sentenced to death. Despite global appeals for clemency, the government executed them in November 1995.
*   **Commonwealth Suspension:** The executions took place while the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM) was holding in New Zealand. In immediate response, Nigeria was **suspended from the Commonwealth of Nations**. 
*   **Sanctions:** The United States, the European Union, and other Western nations imposed limited economic and diplomatic sanctions on Nigeria, leading to a period of international isolation for the country.

### 4. The Transition Programme and Self-Succession
General Abacha announced a transition programme designed to return the country to civilian rule by October 1998.
*   **The Five Political Parties:** The electoral commission registered exactly five political parties: UNCP, CNC, NCPN, DPN, and GDM.
*   **Self-Succession Plan:** By early 1998, in a series of coordinated conventions, all five registered political parties adopted General Sani Abacha as their sole presidential candidate. Pro-democracy activists and critics described this as a "self-succession" plan, meaning the Head of State intended to transform himself into a civilian president without facing an electoral opponent.
*   **The G-34:** A group of 34 prominent Nigerian politicians and leaders (the Group of 34, or G-34) led by Dr. Alex Ekwueme publicly wrote to General Abacha, advising him against the self-succession plan.

## The End of the Regime

The political tension over the transition programme ended abruptly when General Sani Abacha died suddenly on **June 8, 1998**, at the presidential villa in Abuja. The official cause of death was announced as a heart attack.

Following his death, the Provisional Ruling Council (PRC) appointed the Chief of Defence Staff, **Major-General Abdulsalami Abubakar**, as the new Head of State. Abubakar immediately launched a new, shortened transition programme that successfully handed power over to a democratically elected civilian government in May 1999 (see `D10-transition-to-fourth-republic.md`).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ING** | Interim National Government, the short-lived civilian administration headed by Ernest Shonekan. |
| **NADECO** | National Democratic Coalition, a pro-democracy group formed in 1994 to push for the validation of the June 12 election. |
| **PTF** | Petroleum Trust Fund, an agency set up to manage extra fuel revenue for national infrastructure projects. |
| **Vision 2010** | A committee set up to create a long-term economic development blueprint for Nigeria. |
| **Geopolitical zones** | The six regional divisions (North-West, North-East, North-Central, South-West, South-East, South-South) proposed during the 1994/1995 Constitutional Conference. |
| **Commonwealth of Nations** | An international association consisting mostly of former territories of the British Empire, from which Nigeria was suspended in 1995. |
| **Self-succession** | A process where a military ruler attempts to become a civilian president by controlling the democratic transition process. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Remember the state creation numbers:** General Abacha created **six** new states in October 1996, bringing Nigeria's total to **36** states. (Do not confuse this with Babangida creating two in 1987 and nine in 1991).
*   **Know the cause of the Commonwealth suspension:** Nigeria was suspended from the Commonwealth in 1995 specifically because of the execution of Ken Saro-Wiwa and the Ogoni Nine.
*   **Distinguish the PTF:** The Petroleum Trust Fund (PTF) is closely associated with the Abacha regime's economic and infrastructure policies.
*   **Recognize NADECO:** NADECO was the primary domestic opposition group during the Abacha years, fighting for the June 12 mandate.
*   **ECOMOG achievements:** Abacha's major foreign policy action was using ECOMOG to restore democracy in Sierra Leone.
*   **Avoid mixing up transition programmes:** The Abacha transition featured five political parties that all adopted him as their candidate. The Babangida transition featured two parties (SDP and NRC). 

## Quick summary

1.  General Sani Abacha took power in November 1993 by removing the Interim National Government led by Ernest Shonekan.
2.  In 1996, the regime created six new states (Ebonyi, Bayelsa, Nasarawa, Zamfara, Gombe, Ekiti), bringing the total to 36, and increased local governments to 774.
3.  The 1994–1995 Constitutional Conference introduced the concept of Nigeria's six geopolitical zones.
4.  Economic interventions included the Petroleum Trust Fund (PTF) for infrastructure, the Failed Banks Decree to sanitize the banking sector, and the Vision 2010 committee.
5.  In foreign policy, Nigeria led ECOMOG forces to reinstate the democratic government of President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah in Sierra Leone.
6.  The regime faced severe opposition from NADECO and pro-democracy groups over the continued detention of Chief M.K.O. Abiola.
7.  In 1995, the execution of Ken Saro-Wiwa led to Nigeria's suspension from the Commonwealth of Nations and international sanctions.
8.  The transition programme featured five political parties that all adopted Abacha as their sole presidential candidate, a plan halted by his sudden death in June 1998.

## Practice questions

**1. General Sani Abacha came to power in 1993 after the resignation of the head of the Interim National Government,**
- A. Chief M.K.O. Abiola
- B. General Ibrahim Babangida
- C. Chief Ernest Shonekan
- D. General Abdulsalami Abubakar

**Answer: C.** Chief Ernest Shonekan was the head of the Interim National Government (ING) before Abacha forced his resignation in November 1993.

**2. How many new states were created by the Abacha administration in 1996?**
- A. Two
- B. Six
- C. Nine
- D. Twelve

**Answer: B.** The regime created six states (Bayelsa, Ebonyi, Ekiti, Gombe, Nasarawa, and Zamfara) on October 1, 1996, bringing the total to 36. Two were created by Babangida in 1987, and nine in 1991.

**3. Which of the following agencies was established by the Abacha regime to use extra oil revenue for infrastructure development?**
- A. Directorate of Food, Roads and Rural Infrastructure (DFRRI)
- B. National Economic Empowerment and Development Strategy (NEEDS)
- C. Petroleum Trust Fund (PTF)
- D. Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP)

**Answer: C.** The Petroleum Trust Fund (PTF) was established in 1994 to manage funds from increased fuel prices. DFRRI and SAP were Babangida's policies, while NEEDS was introduced later in the Fourth Republic.

**4. Nigeria was suspended from the Commonwealth of Nations in 1995 primarily due to the**
- A. annulment of the June 12 election
- B. execution of Ken Saro-Wiwa and eight other Ogoni leaders
- C. arrest of Chief M.K.O. Abiola
- D. self-succession plan of General Abacha

**Answer: B.** The execution of Ken Saro-Wiwa and the Ogoni Nine during the Commonwealth meeting in November 1995 resulted in immediate suspension. The June 12 annulment occurred under Babangida.

**5. The main pro-democracy group that actively opposed the Abacha regime and demanded the revalidation of the June 12 election was**
- A. NADECO
- B. Afenifere
- C. NUPENG
- D. The G-34

**Answer: A.** The National Democratic Coalition (NADECO) was formed in 1994 as the main opposition group demanding that the military step down for M.K.O. Abiola.

**6. A significant achievement of the 1994-1995 National Constitutional Conference under Abacha was the recommendation of**
- A. a two-party system for Nigeria
- B. the division of Nigeria into six geopolitical zones
- C. the adoption of a parliamentary system of government
- D. the abolition of local government areas

**Answer: B.** The Constitutional Conference recommended grouping the country into six geopolitical zones for fairer power-sharing and resource distribution. The two-party system was Babangida's policy.

**7. Nigerian troops under ECOMOG intervened in which West African country to restore its democratically elected president, who was overthrown in a 1997 coup?**
- A. Liberia
- B. The Gambia
- C. Sierra Leone
- D. Mali

**Answer: C.** Under Abacha, Nigeria led ECOMOG forces into Sierra Leone to oust a military junta and restore President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah.

**8. The Failed Banks Decree of 1994 was introduced by the Abacha administration to**
- A. prevent foreign banks from operating in Nigeria
- B. close down the Central Bank of Nigeria
- C. sanitize the banking sector and prosecute corrupt bank executives
- D. force commercial banks to merge

**Answer: C.** The decree was aimed at prosecuting executives who had mismanaged depositors' funds, thereby stabilizing the financial sector. 

**9. The political transition programme of General Sani Abacha was highly criticized by pro-democracy activists mainly because**
- A. it banned all politicians from the First Republic
- B. it involved only a two-party system
- C. all five registered political parties adopted Abacha as their sole presidential candidate
- D. it refused to register any political parties

**Answer: C.** Critics viewed the transition as a self-succession plan, as the five legal parties all chose Abacha to be their presidential candidate for the upcoming elections.

**10. General Sani Abacha's regime came to an end in June 1998 following his sudden death, and he was succeeded by**
- A. General Olusegun Obasanjo
- B. Chief Ernest Shonekan
- C. Major-General Abdulsalami Abubakar
- D. Lieutenant-General Oladipo Diya

**Answer: C.** Major-General Abdulsalami Abubakar succeeded Abacha as Head of State and organized a swift transition to civilian rule.
