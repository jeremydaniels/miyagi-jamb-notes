# JAMB History Notes: Sierra Leone, Liberia and Christian Missionary Activities in West Africa

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section E, Topic 2 of the JAMB History syllabus: Sierra Leone, Liberia and Christian Missionary Activities in West Africa. It covers two main content items:

* **a.** The foundation of Sierra Leone and Liberia and the spread of Christianity.
* **b.** The activities and impact of Christian missionaries.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Determine the factors that led to the founding of Sierra Leone and Liberia (Objective i).
2. Examine the importance of Sierra Leone and Liberia in the spread and impact of Christianity in West Africa (Objective ii).
3. Assess the impact of Christian missionary activities in West Africa (Objective iii).

## Factors that led to the founding of Sierra Leone and Liberia

**Objective i: determine the factors that led to the founding of Sierra Leone and Liberia; Content a: The foundation of Sierra Leone and Liberia and the spread of Christianity.**

The late 18th and early 19th centuries brought major changes to West Africa. In Europe and America, a strong anti-slavery movement began to grow. By 1807, Britain abolished the slave trade, and other nations gradually followed. This raised a major question: what should be done with freed slaves in Europe and America, and those rescued from illegal slave ships? 

The answer was the creation of two new settlements on the West African coast: **Sierra Leone** and **Liberia**.

### The Founding of Sierra Leone

Sierra Leone was founded mainly through the efforts of British **philanthropists** (people who donate time and money to help others), particularly a group of evangelical Christians known as the **Clapham Sect**, which included Granville Sharp, Thomas Clarkson, and William Wilberforce.

**Key factors for founding Sierra Leone:**
1. **The "Black Poor" in England:** After a 1772 court ruling (the Somerset Case) declared that slavery was not supported by English law, many slaves in England were freed. Others arrived after fighting for the British in the American War of Independence. They were mostly poor and unemployed in London. Granville Sharp and others wanted to find them a permanent home.
2. **Humanitarian and religious motives:** The philanthropists believed that returning freed Africans to their continent would allow them to build a free, Christian society. They hoped this settlement would spread the gospel and Western civilization to the rest of West Africa.
3. **Promotion of "Legitimate Trade":** To stop the slave trade permanently, abolitionists argued that it had to be replaced with trade in raw materials like palm oil, cotton, and timber. Sierra Leone was intended to be a model agricultural and trading colony.
4. **Resettlement of diverse freed groups:** Over time, different groups were resettled there:
   * **1787:** The first group of about 400 "Black Poor" arrived and founded a settlement called "Province of Freedom" (later Freetown).
   * **1792:** The "Nova Scotians" arrived. These were ex-slaves who had fought for Britain in America, were resettled in cold Nova Scotia (Canada), and asked to be moved to Africa.
   * **1800:** The "Maroons" arrived. These were rebellious ex-slaves from Jamaica.
   * **After 1807:** The **Recaptives** or Liberated Africans arrived. When Britain made the slave trade illegal, the British Royal Navy captured illegal slave ships on the Atlantic Ocean and released the slaves in Freetown. They eventually formed the largest part of the population and mixed with the other groups to form a new ethnic group known as the **Creoles (or Krios)**.

In 1808, the private company running the settlement could no longer afford it, and Sierra Leone became a **British Crown Colony**.

### The Founding of Liberia

Liberia was founded south of Sierra Leone, but its origins were rooted in the United States. It was driven by the **American Colonization Society (ACS)**, which was formed in 1816.

**Key factors for founding Liberia:**
1. **Fear of free African Americans:** In the US, the population of free black people was growing. Many white Americans, particularly slave owners in the South, feared that free blacks would encourage enslaved people to rebel. They wanted to remove free blacks from the United States.
2. **Humanitarian motives:** Some members of the ACS genuinely believed that black people would never achieve true equality or avoid racism in the United States. They felt that returning them to Africa was the only way they could enjoy true freedom and self-government.
3. **Missionary zeal:** Like the founders of Sierra Leone, supporters of Liberia wanted the settlers to bring Christianity and Western civilization to the indigenous peoples of Africa.
4. **Creation of a settlement:** In 1821, the ACS purchased land at Cape Mesurado (the site of present-day Monrovia, named after US President James Monroe) from local chiefs. The first settlers arrived in 1822. These settlers, along with later arrivals from America, became known as the **Americo-Liberians**.

Because the ACS was a private society and not a government, it could not legally protect the colony from British and French traders who refused to pay customs duties. To solve this, Liberia declared itself an **independent republic in 1847**, becoming the first modern independent republic in Africa.

## The importance of Sierra Leone and Liberia in the spread of Christianity

**Objective ii: examine the importance of Sierra Leone and Liberia in the spread and impact of Christianity in West Africa; Content a: The foundation of Sierra Leone and Liberia and the spread of Christianity.**

European missionaries often died quickly in West Africa due to diseases like malaria (the region was famously called the "White Man's Grave"). Because of this, Sierra Leone and Liberia became the most important launching pads for spreading Christianity across the region. They supplied the educated, acclimatized African missionaries who did most of the actual preaching and converting.

### 1. Base of Operations for Mission Societies
Freetown and Monrovia served as the headquarters for major European and American missionary societies, such as the Church Missionary Society (CMS) and the Wesleyan Methodist Missionary Society. From these safe, established Christian settlements, they planned their expansion into the interior of West Africa, such as Yorubaland and the Niger Delta.

### 2. Education and the Training of Indigenous Missionaries
Sierra Leone became the center of Western education in West Africa. In 1827, the CMS founded **Fourah Bay College** in Freetown to train African schoolmasters, catechists, and clergymen. It was the first Western-style higher institution in West Africa. Graduates from Sierra Leone traveled across West Africa to spread the gospel. Liberia also established schools and eventually Liberia College (1862).

### 3. The Role of the "Recaptives" (The Saro)
The most important factor in spreading Christianity was the return of the **Recaptives** to their original homelands. Many of the slaves freed in Freetown originally came from Yorubaland (due to the Yoruba civil wars). In Sierra Leone, they were converted to Christianity and educated. 
In the late 1830s and 1840s, many of these liberated Africans (known as the **Saro**) began returning to places like Badagry, Abeokuta, and Lagos. They invited European missionaries to join them. Because they knew the local languages and cultures, they were highly successful at converting their own people.

**Example:** **Samuel Ajayi Crowther**
The most famous example is Samuel Ajayi Crowther. Captured as a boy in Osogun (Yorubaland), he was freed by the British Navy and taken to Sierra Leone. He was educated at Fourah Bay College, ordained as a minister, and returned to Nigeria. He joined the 1841 Niger Expedition, played a major role in the CMS Yoruba mission, translated the Bible into Yoruba, and in 1864 was consecrated as the first African Anglican Bishop, leading the Niger Mission.

### 4. Language Translation
Because Sierra Leone was home to Africans from all over West Africa, missionaries could study local languages without leaving Freetown. African linguists and missionaries like Crowther translated the Bible, hymns, and prayer books into indigenous languages, which made Christianity much more appealing and accessible to the local populations.

### 5. Model Christian Communities
Sierra Leone and Liberia served as examples of what the missionaries wanted to achieve: communities where people wore Western clothes, practiced monogamous marriage, engaged in legitimate commerce, and attended church. They were the original hubs of the new Western-educated African elite.

## The activities and impact of Christian missionaries

**Objective iii: assess the impact of Christian missionary activities in West Africa; Content b: The activities and impact of Christian missionaries.**

Christian missionaries (from the CMS, Methodists, Baptists, Presbyterians, and Catholic societies) arrived in West Africa in large numbers during the 19th century. Their goal was to introduce the "Three Cs": **Christianity, Commerce, and Civilization**. Their activities profoundly changed the history, culture, and politics of West Africa.

### 1. Religious Impact
* **Spread of Christianity:** The most obvious impact was the introduction and widespread growth of Christianity in West Africa. Many communities, especially along the coast and in the non-Islamic hinterland, abandoned traditional religious practices and became Christians.
* **Decline of Traditional Religions:** Missionaries strongly condemned African traditional religions. They destroyed shrines, discouraged the worship of local deities, and banned traditional secret societies. 
* **Rise of African Independent Churches:** Because European missionaries often discriminated against African clergy and insisted on strict European cultural rules in worship, many Africans later broke away to form independent, indigenous African churches (like the Aladura churches in Nigeria), which blended Christian teachings with African cultural practices.

### 2. Educational Impact
* **Introduction of Western Education:** Missionaries built the first formal primary and secondary schools in West Africa. Notable early schools include the CMS Grammar School in Lagos (1859) and Hope Waddell Training Institute in Calabar (1895). 
* **Creation of a New Elite:** They taught reading, writing, arithmetic, and religious studies. This education created a new class of Western-educated African elites—teachers, clerks, interpreters, lawyers, and doctors—who became vital to both the colonial administration and later nationalist movements.
* **Development of Written Languages:** To make Africans read the Bible, missionaries reduced many unwritten West African languages into writing (creating alphabets, dictionaries, and grammar books for languages like Yoruba, Igbo, Twi, and Efik).
* **The printing press:** Missionaries introduced printing technology. For example, Rev. Henry Townsend published the first newspaper in Nigeria, *Iwe Irohin*, in Abeokuta in 1859.

### 3. Social and Medical Impact
* **Eradication of cruel practices:** Missionaries campaigned heavily against traditional practices they considered brutal. A famous example is the Scottish missionary **Mary Slessor**, who successfully fought to end the practice of killing twins in Calabar. They also campaigned against human sacrifice and the burial of slaves with deceased kings.
* **Introduction of Western Medicine:** Missionaries built clinics, dispensaries, and hospitals to treat tropical diseases, introducing modern healthcare, hygiene, and sanitation to local communities.
* **Cultural Alienation:** On the negative side, missionaries often confused European culture with Christianity. They taught Africans to despise their own culture, promoting European names (baptismal names), European clothing (even in hot weather), and European forms of marriage, leading to a loss of African cultural identity.

### 4. Economic Impact
* **Promotion of Legitimate Trade:** Missionaries actively encouraged Africans to farm cash crops like cocoa, palm produce, cotton, and groundnuts instead of trading in slaves. They believed legitimate trade would raise the standard of living and make the slave trade unnecessary.
* **Vocational Training:** Many missions established industrial institutions where Africans were taught trades like carpentry, printing, bricklaying, and modern agriculture.

### 5. Political Impact
* **Paving the way for Colonial Rule:** Missionaries often softened the ground for European political takeover. Whenever missionaries faced trouble from local rulers or internal wars (such as the Yoruba civil wars), they frequently called on their home governments for military protection. For example, missionary appeals were a major factor in the British bombardment of Lagos in 1851 and the eventual colonization of Nigeria.
* **Sowing seeds of Nationalism:** Ironically, by providing Western education and teaching ideas of human equality found in the Bible, missionaries equipped the new African elite with the intellectual tools to eventually challenge colonial rule and fight for independence.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Mixing up the founders:** Always remember: Sierra Leone was founded by **British philanthropists** (1787) for freed slaves from Britain, Canada, and the seas. Liberia was founded by the **American Colonization Society** (1822) for free African Americans.
* **Liberia's independence:** Unlike Sierra Leone which became a British colony, Liberia declared itself an independent republic in **1847** to protect its trade.
* **Samuel Ajayi Crowther:** He is a highly tested figure. Remember his background: a Recaptive, educated in Sierra Leone, first African Bishop, translated the Bible to Yoruba. 
* **Impact of Missionaries:** Do not focus only on religion. JAMB frequently asks about their educational impact (schools, written languages, *Iwe Irohin* newspaper) and social impact (Mary Slessor stopping the killing of twins). 
* **The "Trojan Horse":** Textbooks often describe missionaries as paving the way for colonial rule. If a question asks for a *political* consequence or a *negative* effect of missionary activities, look for options relating to encouraging colonial conquest or causing cultural alienation.

## Quick summary

1. Sierra Leone was founded in 1787 by British philanthropists to resettle freed slaves (the Black Poor, Nova Scotians, Maroons, and later, naval Recaptives).
2. Liberia was founded in 1822 by the American Colonization Society to relocate free African Americans due to racial tensions in the United States, becoming an independent republic in 1847.
3. Both settlements were vital in spreading Christianity because they produced Western-educated Africans, trained at institutions like Fourah Bay College, who became indigenous missionaries.
4. Returning Recaptives (the Saro), like Bishop Samuel Ajayi Crowther, played major roles in translating the Bible into local languages and converting their people.
5. Missionaries significantly changed West Africa by spreading Christianity, introducing Western education (reading, writing, and the printing press), and building hospitals.
6. They also fought against harmful traditional practices, such as human sacrifice and the killing of twins.
7. Economically, they promoted legitimate trade to replace the slave trade and provided vocational training.
8. Politically, missionary activities often paved the way for European colonial conquest, while simultaneously educating the elite who would eventually lead nationalist movements.

## Practice questions

**1. The primary motive for the founding of Sierra Leone was to:**
- A. establish a British military base in West Africa
- B. resettle freed and destitute slaves from England and America
- C. conquer the interior of West Africa for the British Empire
- D. exploit gold and diamond resources

**Answer: B.** Sierra Leone was founded by philanthropists to provide a home for the "Black Poor" from England, Nova Scotians, and later Recaptives.

**2. Which organization was responsible for the foundation of Liberia?**
- A. The Clapham Sect
- B. The Church Missionary Society
- C. The Royal Niger Company
- D. The American Colonization Society

**Answer: D.** The American Colonization Society (ACS) purchased land in 1821 to relocate free African Americans, resulting in the founding of Liberia. 

**3. The first Western-style higher educational institution established in West Africa to train teachers and missionaries was:**
- A. Hope Waddell Training Institute
- B. Yaba Higher College
- C. Fourah Bay College
- D. Liberia College

**Answer: C.** Fourah Bay College was founded in Sierra Leone in 1827 by the Church Missionary Society. 

**4. A major reason why Liberia declared itself an independent republic in 1847 was to:**
- A. break away from the harsh rule of the American government
- B. legally enforce customs duties against foreign European traders
- C. join the League of Nations
- D. declare war on neighboring African kingdoms

**Answer: B.** The ACS was a private society, so Liberia lacked the legal sovereignty to force French and British merchants to pay taxes or respect its borders. Declaring independence solved this. 

**5. Which prominent historical figure, educated in Sierra Leone, became the first African Bishop of the Anglican Church?**
- A. Nnamdi Azikiwe
- B. Herbert Macaulay
- C. Samuel Ajayi Crowther
- D. J.E. Casely Hayford

**Answer: C.** Samuel Ajayi Crowther, a Yoruba Recaptive, was educated at Fourah Bay College, translated the Bible into Yoruba, and was ordained as Bishop in 1864. 

**6. A significant negative impact of Christian missionary activities in West Africa was the:**
- A. introduction of modern healthcare
- B. promotion of legitimate trade
- C. alienation of Africans from their traditional culture
- D. establishment of printing presses

**Answer: C.** Missionaries often equated Christianity with European culture, leading them to suppress African traditions, names, and clothing. The other options were positive impacts. 

**7. In what way did Sierra Leone and Liberia specifically contribute to the spread of Christianity in West Africa?**
- A. By providing a base where European missionaries could safely live and train African evangelists
- B. By sending their armies to conquer non-Christian kingdoms
- C. By banning the practice of Islam across the region
- D. By exporting Bibles manufactured in their local factories

**Answer: A.** They served as safe headquarters for missionary societies and training grounds (like Fourah Bay College) for the indigenous African missionaries who did most of the evangelizing.

**8. The Scottish missionary famous for successfully campaigning against the killing of twins in Calabar was:**
- A. David Livingstone
- B. Mungo Park
- C. Henry Townsend
- D. Mary Slessor

**Answer: D.** Mary Slessor is widely noted for her social work in Calabar, particularly stopping the traditional practice of killing twins. 

**9. Missionaries in West Africa strongly encouraged the local population to switch from the slave trade to the production of palm oil, cotton, and cocoa. This economic policy was known as:**
- A. Imperialism
- B. Assimilation
- C. Legitimate trade
- D. Mercantilism

**Answer: C.** "Legitimate trade" or "legitimate commerce" was the term used by abolitionists and missionaries for trade in agricultural products, which they hoped would replace the trans-Atlantic slave trade.

**10. How did Christian missionaries inadvertently contribute to the later struggle for African independence?**
- A. They provided weapons to local chiefs to fight the colonial governments.
- B. They created a Western-educated elite that later used democratic ideas to demand self-rule.
- C. They signed treaties handing over African lands to the United Nations.
- D. They built independent African armies capable of defeating European forces.

**Answer: B.** By providing Western education and teaching biblical concepts of equality, missionaries created an educated class (lawyers, journalists, teachers) who later led nationalist movements against colonial rule.
