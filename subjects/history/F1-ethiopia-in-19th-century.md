# JAMB History Notes: Ethiopia in the 19th Century

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section F, Topic 1 of the JAMB History syllabus: Horn of Africa and Southern Africa – Ethiopia in the 19th Century. It has two content items:

* **a. The rise of Theodore II and his attempt at the unification of Ethiopia**
* **b. Menelik II and Ethiopian independence.**

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. examine the factors that led to the rise of Theodore II as the Emperor of Ethiopia;
2. analyse the strategies that were adopted to achieve Ethiopian unification;
3. assess the role of Menelik II in the maintenance of Ethiopian independence.

> **Note on the syllabus wording:** The syllabus uses the name **Theodore II**. In many modern history textbooks and encyclopaedias, he is referred to by his Ethiopian name, **Tewodros II**. They are the same person. Similarly, you may see different spellings for places and treaties depending on the textbook, such as Wuchale (Uccialli), Shewa (Shoa), and Magdala (Maqdala). The notes below provide the common alternatives.

## Background: The Zemene Mesafint (Era of the Princes)

To understand the achievements of the 19th-century emperors, we must first look at the condition of Ethiopia before 1855. From the mid-18th century to the mid-19th century, Ethiopia went through a period known as the **Zemene Mesafint** (the Era of the Princes or Era of the Judges). 

During this time:
* **The central government collapsed:** The Emperors, who lived in the ancient capital of Gondar, were mere figureheads with no real wealth or military power.
* **Regional division:** Powerful provincial lords (called *Ras*) controlled different regions, such as Tigray, Amhara, Gojjam, and Shewa (Shoa). They kept their own private armies and fought endless civil wars for dominance.
* **Religious division:** The Ethiopian Orthodox Church, a major unifying institution in the country, was weakened by internal doctrinal disputes.

Ethiopia was fragmented and vulnerable to foreign invasion, especially from expanding neighbouring powers like Egypt. This was the chaotic world into which Theodore II emerged.

## Content a: The rise of Theodore II and his attempt at the unification of Ethiopia

### Objective i: examine the factors that led to the rise of Theodore II as the Emperor of Ethiopia

Theodore II was born as **Kassa Hailu** around 1818 in Qwara, a frontier district near the Sudanese border. He began his public life as a *shifta* (a traditional Ethiopian term for a rebel, outlaw, or bandit who rejects the authority of a corrupt lord). Through intelligence and military brilliance, he built a large, loyal following and eventually conquered the divided Ethiopian state, crowning himself Emperor Theodore II (Tewodros II) in **1855**.

Several key factors made his rise possible:

1. **Outstanding military skill and charisma:** Kassa was a natural leader and a brilliant tactician. He trained his followers to be highly disciplined, initially using hit-and-run guerrilla tactics before building a force capable of fighting large conventional battles. His soldiers were fiercely loyal to him.
2. **Use of modern weapons and tactics:** Because he operated near the border with Sudan (which was controlled by Egypt), Kassa saw how effective modern firearms and disciplined infantry were. He actively acquired modern rifles from trade and captured weapons from his enemies, giving his forces a major advantage over traditional Ethiopian armies.
3. **Defeating the divided regional lords:** Between 1852 and 1855, Kassa systematically took advantage of the divisions of the Zemene Mesafint. In a series of brilliant military campaigns, he defeated the most powerful regional lords one by one, including Ras Ali of Yejju and Dejazmach Wube of Tigray. 
4. **Gaining the support of the Church:** Early in his rise, Kassa promised to end the doctrinal disputes dividing the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, restore its unity, and expel foreign religious influences. In return, the Abuna (the head of the Church) gave Kassa his crucial backing and agreed to crown him Emperor.
5. **The power of prophecy:** There was a popular Ethiopian prophecy that a righteous and just ruler named Theodore would rise to rebuild the empire to its former glory. By adopting the throne name Theodore II, Kassa used this prophecy to gain legitimacy and massive popular support.

### Objective ii: analyse the strategies that were adopted to achieve Ethiopian unification

Once crowned in 1855, Theodore II's primary vision was to turn Ethiopia into a centralized, modern, and unified state. To achieve this, he adopted several radical strategies:

1. **Centralization of political administration:** Theodore abolished the traditional system where powerful regional lords ruled their provinces independently by hereditary right. Instead, he divided the empire into smaller provinces and appointed his own loyal officials and relatives to govern them, making all governors directly answerable to him.
2. **Creation of a national standing army:** Before Theodore, soldiers were raised by local lords for specific campaigns and often survived by looting peasant farmers. Theodore created a disciplined, permanent national standing army. He introduced regular salaries, uniforms, and rations for his soldiers, strictly forbidding them from looting Ethiopian peasants. 
3. **Military modernization and weapon manufacturing:** Theodore knew that European powers were expanding globally. To defend his unified state, he needed modern weapons. He invited European missionaries and artisans to Ethiopia, but instead of allowing them to preach, he ordered them to manufacture firearms and artillery for his army. Most famously, he forced European craftsmen to build a massive mortar cannon, which he named "Sebastopol".
4. **Moving the capital to Magdala:** To break away from the old, corrupt politics of Gondar, Theodore established a new power base at a steep mountain fortress called **Magdala** (Maqdala). It served as his new capital, his treasury, and a secure prison for his political rivals.
5. **Land and financial reforms (Church confiscation):** Maintaining a large national standing army was very expensive. To raise funds, Theodore confiscated vast amounts of land owned by the wealthy Ethiopian Orthodox Church. He distributed this land to tax-paying peasants and used the revenue to pay his soldiers. He also reduced the number of priests in each church.

**The downfall of Theodore II:**
While his strategies laid the foundation for modern Ethiopia, they were too radical and implemented too harshly, leading to his downfall:
* **Internal rebellions:** His heavy taxation and harsh punishments caused widespread revolts. Crucially, confiscating Church land turned the powerful clergy against him; the Church excommunicated him and encouraged the peasants to rebel. 
* **Foreign conflict and the Napier Expedition (1868):** Seeking an alliance and skilled workers, Theodore wrote a letter to Britain's Queen Victoria. When the British government ignored the letter, an insulted Theodore imprisoned the British consul and several European missionaries at Magdala. In response, Britain sent a massive military rescue mission in 1867–1868, led by Sir Robert Napier.
* Because Theodore had alienated his own people, local Ethiopian lords helped the British army march safely through the country. In 1868, at the Battle of Magdala, Theodore's army was defeated by superior British firepower. To avoid the humiliation of capture, Emperor Theodore II committed suicide.

## Content b: Menelik II and Ethiopian independence

After Theodore's death, Emperor Yohannes IV ruled and spent much of his reign defending Ethiopia from Egyptian and Sudanese (Mahdist) invasions. When Yohannes died in battle in 1889, the powerful King of the Shewa region was crowned as **Emperor Menelik II**. 

Menelik II came to power just as the European "Scramble for Africa" was reaching its peak. European nations—especially Italy, Britain, and France—were rapidly taking over African territories. Despite this immense pressure, Ethiopia remained independent.

### Objective iii: assess the role of Menelik II in the maintenance of Ethiopian independence

Menelik II was a brilliant diplomat, a modernizer, and a pragmatic military leader. His role in preserving Ethiopia's sovereignty—making it one of only two African nations (alongside Liberia) to survive the Scramble for Africa uncolonized—was based on the following actions:

#### 1. Territorial expansion and economic consolidation
Even before becoming Emperor, and continuing throughout his reign, Menelik expanded his borders aggressively to the south, east, and west, bringing regions like the Oromo lands, Sidama, and the Ogaden under central control.
* **Wealth for arms:** This expansion gave him control over incredibly wealthy trade routes dealing in ivory, gold, coffee, and slaves. He used the massive tax revenues from this trade to buy modern European weapons.
* **Creating a buffer:** Expanding the empire's borders created a large territorial buffer that kept European colonial powers (who were settling on the coasts) far away from the Ethiopian centre.

#### 2. Shrewd diplomacy and the Treaty of Wuchale (1889)
In 1889, shortly after becoming Emperor, Menelik signed the **Treaty of Wuchale** (also spelled Uccialli) with Italy. The treaty recognized Menelik as Emperor, and in return, Menelik recognized Italian control over the coastal region bordering the Red Sea (which the Italians named Eritrea). 
However, a major crisis arose over **Article XVII (17)** of the treaty, because the two language versions of the document were deliberately written differently by the Italians:
* The **Amharic version** (Ethiopia's language) said that Menelik *could* use the services of the Italian government to communicate with other European powers, if he chose to do so.
* The **Italian version** stated that Menelik *must* conduct all his foreign affairs through Italy, which effectively meant Ethiopia was an Italian **protectorate** (a colony in all but name).

When Menelik discovered this deception, he firmly rejected the Italian interpretation. In 1893, feeling confident in his military strength, he formally denounced (tore up) the treaty, paid back a loan he had received from Italy, and declared Ethiopia completely independent.

#### 3. Military preparation and modernization
Menelik knew that tearing up the treaty would lead to an Italian invasion. He spent the years before the war preparing his nation:
* **Importing modern weapons:** He expertly played the rival European powers against each other. Because France and Russia were rivals of Italy and Britain, they were happy to sell Menelik vast quantities of modern rifles, machine guns, and artillery. He even bought weapons from Italy itself before the war began. 
* **Fostering national unity:** Unlike Theodore II, who alienated regional lords, Menelik used his diplomatic skills to unite the various *Ras* (princes). When the Italians invaded, leaders from all over Ethiopia, even Menelik's political rivals, marched with him to defend the nation.
* **Modernizing the state:** Menelik established a new, permanent capital at **Addis Ababa**. He introduced the telegraph, a postal system, modern banking, and began planning the Franco-Ethiopian railway to the coast, greatly improving the country's administration and logistics.

#### 4. The Battle of Adwa (1896)
In 1895, the Italian army advanced from Eritrea into northern Ethiopia. Menelik marched north to meet them with a massive, highly united, and well-equipped army estimated at over 100,000 men. 

* On **1 March 1896**, the two armies clashed at the **Battle of Adwa** in the northern mountains.
* The Italian commander, General Oreste Baratieri, made severe tactical errors in the difficult terrain, causing his smaller army to become divided. 
* The Ethiopian forces, heavily armed with modern artillery and rifles, overwhelmed the Italian brigades. Thousands of Italian soldiers were killed or captured in a total and crushing defeat.

**The Significance of Adwa:**
Menelik's victory at Adwa was one of the most important events in modern African history. 
* It forced Italy to sign the **Treaty of Addis Ababa** (1896), in which Italy formally cancelled the Treaty of Wuchale and recognized the absolute independence of Ethiopia.
* Following the battle, other European powers (Britain, France, and Russia) rushed to send diplomats to Addis Ababa to sign treaties acknowledging Ethiopian sovereignty.
* Ethiopia became a beacon of independence, black pride, and resistance against European imperialism for Africans and people of African descent worldwide.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Zemene Mesafint** | "Era of the Princes" (c. 1769–1855); a period of chaos when Ethiopia was divided and ruled by rival regional warlords. |
| **Shifta** | A traditional Ethiopian term for an outlaw, bandit, or rebel opposing a corrupt leader. Kassa Hailu began as a shifta. |
| **Abuna** | The head bishop of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church. |
| **Ras** | A traditional Ethiopian royal or noble title, equivalent to a Duke or powerful provincial lord. |
| **Magdala (Maqdala)** | The mountain fortress chosen by Theodore II as his capital; the site of his final defeat and suicide in 1868. |
| **Treaty of Wuchale (Uccialli)** | The 1889 treaty between Ethiopia and Italy that led to a dispute over Ethiopia's sovereignty due to mistranslated wording. |
| **Protectorate** | A country that is controlled and protected by a stronger external power, usually losing control over its own foreign policy. |
| **Battle of Adwa** | The decisive 1896 battle where Menelik II's forces defeated the Italian army, securing Ethiopian independence. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Common mistake:** Confusing Theodore II (Tewodros II) and Menelik II. Remember their different roles: **Theodore II** *began* the unification of Ethiopia but died in defeat at Magdala (1868). **Menelik II** later *defended* the unified nation's independence by defeating Italy at Adwa (1896).
* **Common mistake:** Misunderstanding the cause of the war with Italy. The dispute was specifically over **Article XVII (17)** of the Treaty of Wuchale. The Italian version claimed Ethiopia was an Italian protectorate, while the Amharic version did not.
* **Exam tip:** JAMB questions often use "Theodore II", but be prepared in case options use "Tewodros II" or "Kassa Hailu". They are all the same person.
* **Exam tip:** Theodore II angered the Church mainly because he **confiscated Church land** to pay his national army, not because he tried to change the religion. 
* **Exam tip:** The period of division and civil war *before* Theodore II's rise is called the **Zemene Mesafint** (Era of the Princes).

## Quick summary

1. Before 1855, Ethiopia suffered from deep divisions, weak central government, and civil wars during a period known as the *Zemene Mesafint* (Era of the Princes).
2. Kassa Hailu used military brilliance and modern firearms to defeat the rival regional lords, uniting the country and crowning himself Emperor Theodore II in 1855.
3. Theodore II attempted to modernize and centralize Ethiopia by replacing hereditary lords with appointed governors, building a paid national standing army, and manufacturing modern weapons.
4. Theodore's harsh rule and confiscation of Church land sparked rebellions. After he imprisoned British officials, a British expedition defeated his forces at Magdala in 1868, and he committed suicide.
5. Menelik II became Emperor in 1889 and greatly increased Ethiopia's wealth and size by expanding the empire's borders to the south and east.
6. The 1889 Treaty of Wuchale caused a major dispute with Italy because the Italian translation of Article XVII falsely claimed Ethiopia was an Italian protectorate.
7. Menelik II successfully prepared for war by uniting Ethiopia's regional leaders and buying massive quantities of modern firearms from European rivals.
8. In 1896, Menelik II's united and well-armed forces decisively defeated the Italian army at the Battle of Adwa, successfully preserving Ethiopian independence during the Scramble for Africa.

## Practice questions

**1. Before the rise of Theodore II, the period of Ethiopian history characterized by divided power and constant wars between regional lords was known as the:**
- A. Solomonic Era
- B. Scramble for Africa
- C. Zemene Mesafint
- D. Mfecane

**Answer: C.** The Zemene Mesafint (Era of the Princes) was a chaotic period before 1855 when the emperors in Gondar had no power, and regional warlords ruled independently.

**2. A major factor that directly contributed to the rise of Theodore II to the Ethiopian throne was his:**
- A. alliance with the Italian government
- B. military skill and strategic use of modern weapons
- C. claim to be a direct descendant of Queen Victoria
- D. support from Egyptian invading forces

**Answer: B.** Born as Kassa Hailu, he used excellent military tactics and modern firearms to defeat the divided regional princes and take the throne.

**3. To achieve the unification of Ethiopia, Theodore II implemented which of the following strategies?**
- A. Allowing powerful regional lords to rule independently without taxes
- B. Creating a paid, disciplined national standing army
- C. Moving the capital back to the ancient city of Gondar
- D. Handing over all national tax collection to the Orthodox Church

**Answer: B.** Theodore II abolished the private armies of local lords and created a centralized, salaried national army to unify the state. Option A is the opposite of his centralization policy.

**4. Why did Emperor Theodore II eventually face strong opposition from the Ethiopian Orthodox Church?**
- A. He tried to convert the country to Islam to form an alliance with Egypt
- B. He confiscated large areas of Church land to pay his soldiers
- C. He replaced the head of the Church with a British missionary
- D. He forced all priests to fight in his national army

**Answer: B.** To fund his expensive national standing army, Theodore confiscated vast estates owned by the Church, causing the clergy to turn against him and support rebellions.

**5. Emperor Theodore II's rule came to an end in 1868 following a military defeat at Magdala by forces from:**
- A. Italy
- B. France
- C. Britain
- D. Russia

**Answer: C.** After Theodore imprisoned British officials, Britain sent the Napier Expedition, which defeated his army at Magdala, leading to his suicide. (Italy invaded later, during Menelik II's reign).

**6. Which treaty caused a major dispute between Ethiopia and Italy due to differences in its translation?**
- A. Treaty of Addis Ababa
- B. Treaty of Versailles
- C. Treaty of Berlin
- D. Treaty of Wuchale

**Answer: D.** The 1889 Treaty of Wuchale (Uccialli) sparked conflict because the Amharic and Italian versions translated differently.

**7. Article XVII (17) of the Treaty of Wuchale was controversial because the Italian version implied that Ethiopia:**
- A. had surrendered its capital city to British forces
- B. had become an Italian protectorate
- C. was required to abandon the Orthodox Christian faith
- D. had conceded its entire southern region to France

**Answer: B.** The Italian version stated that Menelik must conduct his foreign affairs through Italy, effectively making Ethiopia an Italian protectorate without Menelik's consent.

**8. Emperor Menelik II was able to maintain Ethiopian independence primarily by:**
- A. relying entirely on British military protection
- B. surrendering fertile coastal lands to Egypt in exchange for peace
- C. equipping a united national army with modern European weapons
- D. agreeing to the Italian translation of the Treaty of Wuchale

**Answer: C.** Menelik II maintained independence by diplomatically uniting the Ethiopian princes and using trade wealth to buy modern firearms, which he used to defeat the invaders.

**9. The decisive battle in 1896 that stopped European colonization and preserved Ethiopian independence was the Battle of:**
- A. Magdala
- B. Isandlwana
- C. Adwa
- D. Omdurman

**Answer: C.** At the Battle of Adwa on 1 March 1896, Menelik II's forces crushed the Italian army, securing Ethiopia's sovereignty. (Isandlwana was a Zulu victory; Omdurman was in Sudan).

**10. One of the economic strategies Menelik II used to strengthen Ethiopia against foreign invasion was:**
- A. expanding the empire's borders south and east to control wealthy trade routes
- B. selling the capital city to foreign investors
- C. banning all trade with European nations
- D. abolishing the national currency

**Answer: A.** Menelik expanded his territory into regions like the Ogaden and Oromo lands, taking control of the lucrative trade in ivory, gold, and coffee to fund his purchase of modern weapons.
