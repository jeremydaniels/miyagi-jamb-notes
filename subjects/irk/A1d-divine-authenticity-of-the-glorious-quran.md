# JAMB Irk Notes: Divine Authenticity of the Glorious Qur’ān

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 1d of the JAMB Islamic Religious Studies syllabus: Divine Authenticity of the Glorious Qur’ān. It covers the scriptural and rational proofs of the Qur'an's divine origin, its inimitability (uniqueness), and the methods by which it was preserved for all generations. It has three content items:

- (i) Proof of the Divine authenticity of the Glorious Qur’ān (Q.4:82) (Q.41:42)
- (ii) Uniqueness of the Glorious Qur’ān (Q.39:27) (Q.17:88) (Q.75:16-19)
- (iii) Divine preservation of the Glorious Qur’ān (Q.15:9)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. evaluate the proof of the divine authenticity of the Glorious Qur’ān;
2. evaluate the uniqueness of the Glorious Qur’ān;
3. examine the ways by which the Glorious Qur’ān was preserved.

*(Note: The detailed historical phases of the Qur'an's compilation are also covered in `A1b-preservation-of-the-glorious-quran.md`. These notes focus on how its preservation and unique qualities prove it is the authentic word of Allah.)*

## Proof of the Divine authenticity of the Glorious Qur’ān (Q.4:82) (Q.41:42)

The term **divine authenticity** means that the Glorious Qur'an is the genuine, unchanged word of Allah (SWT), revealed to Prophet Muhammad (SAW) through Angel Jibril (Gabriel), and is not the creation of any human mind. Muslims believe that the Qur'an provides its own internal evidence to prove its divine origin, particularly through its freedom from contradiction and its protection from falsehood.

### Freedom from Contradictions (Q.4:82)

One of the greatest proofs of the Qur'an's authenticity is its complete lack of contradiction, despite being revealed piecemeal over a period of 23 years. During these 23 years, the Prophet (SAW) faced extreme hardship, war, peace, victory, defeat, joy, and sorrow. Normally, a human author writing over such a long, turbulent period would show changes in style, conflicting ideas, or signs of emotional fluctuation. The Qur'an, however, remains completely consistent in its message, laws, and linguistic perfection from the first verse to the last.

Allah challenges humanity to reflect on this consistency in **Surah an-Nisā’ (Q.4:82)**:
> *"Will they not then ponder on the Qur'an? If it had been from other than Allah they would have found therein much incongruity."* (Pickthall's translation)

### Protection from Falsehood (Q.41:42)

The Qur'an is absolute truth. It does not contain myths, historical inaccuracies, or false doctrines. Its laws and teachings remain valid for all times and places. Allah declares that falsehood cannot penetrate the Qur'an in any way, shape, or form.

This is stated in **Surah Fussilat (Q.41:42)**:
> *"Falsehood cannot come at it from before it or from behind it. (It is) a revelation from the Wise, the Owner of Praise."* (Pickthall's translation)

### Evaluating the proof of the divine authenticity of the Glorious Qur’ān

To **evaluate** the proof of the Qur'an's divine authenticity, Islamic scholars point not only to scriptural claims but also to rational, historical, and scientific realities that support those claims:

1. **The Unlettered Prophet (*An-Nabiyy al-Ummi*):** Prophet Muhammad (SAW) could neither read nor write. He did not attend any formal school, nor did he study under any scholars of previous scriptures (such as the Tawrat or Injil). Yet, he brought a book containing profound historical knowledge of past nations, advanced legal systems, moral codes, and complex theological arguments. Rationally, it is impossible for an unlettered man in the Arabian desert to produce such a masterpiece unless it was divinely inspired by Allah.
2. **Scientific and Historical Accuracy:** The Qur'an contains detailed descriptions of natural phenomena—such as the development of the human embryo, the orbits of celestial bodies, and the water cycle—centuries before modern science discovered them. It also accurately relates the histories of past prophets and predicts future events (such as the victory of the Romans over the Persians) which came to pass exactly as prophesied.
3. **Consistency of Laws:** The legal and economic principles laid down in the Qur'an (such as laws on inheritance, marriage, and trade) provide a balanced framework that has successfully governed complex societies for over fourteen centuries.

## Uniqueness of the Glorious Qur’ān (Q.39:27) (Q.17:88) (Q.75:16-19)

The Qur'an is unique; there is no book like it in existence. This concept is known in Islamic theology as ***I'jaz al-Qur'an*** (the inimitability or miraculous nature of the Qur'an). Its uniqueness is demonstrated through its profound examples, its open challenge to creation, and the divine method by which it was delivered.

### Profound Similitudes and Parables (Q.39:27)

To make complex spiritual and moral lessons easy to understand, the Qur'an uses powerful parables (similitudes) drawn from nature and everyday life. These examples appeal to human intellect and emotion, ensuring that the message is universal and accessible to both the scholar and the common person. 

Allah states in **Surah az-Zumar (Q.39:27)**:
> *"And verily We have coined for mankind in this Qur'an all kinds of similitudes, that haply they may reflect."* (Pickthall's translation)

### The Ultimate Challenge (*Tahaddi*) (Q.17:88)

At the time of the revelation, the Arabs were masters of poetry and language. They prided themselves on their eloquence. Yet, the Qur'an's literary style—which is neither poetry nor ordinary prose—completely overwhelmed them. 

The Qur'an openly challenges anyone who doubts its divine origin to produce a single chapter (Surah) or book like it. This ultimate challenge to all creation is found in **Surah al-Isrā’ (Q.17:88)**:
> *"Say: Verily, though mankind and the jinn should assemble to produce the like of this Qur'an, they could not produce the like thereof though they were helpers one of another."* (Pickthall's translation)

### Divine Method of Retention (Q.75:16-19)

When Angel Jibril began reciting the Qur'an to Prophet Muhammad (SAW), the Prophet would hastily try to repeat the words out of fear of forgetting them. Allah commanded him not to rush, promising that Allah Himself would ensure the Prophet memorized, recited, and understood it perfectly. 

This divine guarantee of retention is mentioned in **Surah al-Qiyāmah (Q.75:16-19)**:
> *"Stir not thy tongue herewith to hasten it. Lo! upon Us (resteth) the putting together thereof and the reading thereof. And when We read it, follow thou the reading; Then lo! upon Us (resteth) the explanation thereof."* (Pickthall's translation)

### Evaluating the uniqueness of the Glorious Qur’ān

When evaluating the uniqueness of the Qur'an, one must consider that the challenge (*Tahaddi*) laid down over 1,400 years ago remains unmet. Despite the efforts of many critics and masters of the Arabic language, no one has been able to match the Qur'an in its rhythm, profound meaning, and rhetorical power. 

Furthermore, unlike human texts that become outdated or are revised over time, the Qur'an's style remains perpetually relevant. Its unique structure, which weaves laws, historical narratives, and spiritual admonitions seamlessly together, distinguishes it completely from human literature.

## Divine preservation of the Glorious Qur’ān (Q.15:9)

Unlike earlier scriptures which were lost, corrupted, or altered over time by humans (leading to different and conflicting versions), the Glorious Qur'an remains exactly as it was revealed. Muslims believe this is because Allah Himself took the direct responsibility of guarding it.

### Allah's Promise of Preservation (Q.15:9)

The foundational proof of the Qur'an's preservation is the promise made by Allah in **Surah al-Hijr (Q.15:9)**:
> *"Lo! We, even We, reveal the Reminder, and lo! We verily are its Guardian."* (Pickthall's translation)

Allah fulfilled this promise by guiding the Muslim community (the Ummah) to preserve the Qur'an through two complementary methods: **memorization** (in the hearts) and **writing** (on physical materials). 

### Examining the ways by which the Glorious Qur’ān was preserved

The physical preservation of the Qur'an happened systematically across three major historical phases:

#### 1. Preservation during the time of Prophet Muhammad (SAW)
* **Memorization (*Hifz*):** The primary method of preservation was memorization. The Prophet (SAW) memorized every revelation and recited it to his Companions (*Sahabah*), who immediately committed it to memory. Angel Jibril would also review the entire Qur'an with the Prophet once a year during Ramadan, and twice in the final year of his life.
* **Written records:** Whenever a verse was revealed, the Prophet (SAW) instructed his scribes (such as Zayd bin Thabit) to write it down. Because paper was not widely available, they wrote on flat stones, pieces of leather, palm-leaves, and the shoulder bones of animals. 
* **Placement of verses:** The Prophet (SAW) specifically instructed the scribes on exactly which Surah and position each newly revealed verse belonged to.

#### 2. Compilation under Caliph Abu Bakr (RA)
* During the Battle of Yamamah (after the Prophet's death), many Companions who had memorized the Qur'an (*Huffaz*) were martyred.
* Fearing that parts of the Qur'an might be lost if more *Huffaz* died, Umar bin al-Khattab advised Caliph Abu Bakr to compile the Qur'an into a single written volume.
* Abu Bakr appointed Zayd bin Thabit to lead this task. Zayd strictly gathered the scattered written fragments and cross-checked every single verse against the memories of multiple companions.
* The result was the first complete compiled manuscript (the *Suhuf*), which was kept with Abu Bakr, then Umar, and later with Umar's daughter, Hafsah (who was also a widow of the Prophet).

#### 3. Standardization under Caliph Uthman (RA)
* As Islam spread to non-Arab regions (like Persia and the Levant), people began pronouncing the Qur'an in different dialects, leading to disputes over the correct recitation.
* To prevent division, Caliph Uthman bin Affan (RA) requested the original *Suhuf* from Hafsah.
* He commissioned Zayd bin Thabit and a committee of scribes to make standard copies (known as the *Mushaf Uthmani*) based entirely on the dialect of the Quraysh (the original dialect of the Prophet).
* Uthman sent these master copies to the major centers of the Islamic empire (such as Makkah, Syria, Basra, and Kufa) and ordered all other divergent, personal written notes to be burned.
* Every single Arabic Qur'an printed in the world today—whether found in a mosque in Kano, Nigeria, or in Makkah—is an exact replica of this *Mushaf Uthmani*.

### Comparison of the Compilations

| Feature | Compilation of Abu Bakr (RA) | Standardization of Uthman (RA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reason** | Fear of losing the Qur'an due to the death of memorizers (*Huffaz*). | Fear of corruption in pronunciation and division among Muslims due to differing dialects. |
| **Action Taken** | Gathered scattered writings (leaves, bones) into one complete master volume. | Copied the master volume into several standard books (*Musahif*) and sent them to major cities. |
| **Result** | One single, verified master copy (*Suhuf*). | Uniformity of recitation across the entire Muslim world; eradication of variant texts. |

### Continuous Preservation to the Present Day
Beyond the initial compilation, the Qur'an's preservation continues today through:
* **Millions of *Huffaz*:** Today, millions of Muslims worldwide, including thousands in Nigerian *Tahfiz* schools (Madrasahs), memorize the entire 114 Suwar letter-for-letter.
* **The Science of *Tajwid*:** Strict rules govern exactly how every letter must be pronounced, preventing the gradual change of language over time (see `A3-introduction-to-tajwid.md`).
* **Strict Verification:** Modern printed copies undergo rigorous checking by committees of scholars before publication.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Authenticity** | The quality of being genuine and of unquestionable origin; in this case, being the true word of Allah. |
| **I'jaz al-Qur'an** | The inimitability or miraculous nature of the Qur'an; the impossibility of humans or jinn reproducing it. |
| **Tahaddi** | The open challenge presented in the Qur'an to humanity and jinn to produce anything like it. |
| **Ummi** | Unlettered or illiterate; a title of Prophet Muhammad (SAW) proving he could not have authored the Qur'an. |
| **Hafiz (pl. Huffaz)** | A person who has memorized the entire Glorious Qur'an. |
| **Suhuf** | Loose sheets or pages; refers to the first compiled collection of the Qur'an under Abu Bakr. |
| **Mushaf** | A bound volume or book containing the complete written text of the Qur'an. |
| **Zayd bin Thabit** | The chief scribe of the Prophet (SAW) who led both the compilation under Abu Bakr and the standardization under Uthman. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Know your Qur'an references:** JAMB frequently asks which chapter/verse supports a specific point.
  - **Q.4:82** = Freedom from contradictions (incongruity).
  - **Q.41:42** = Protection from falsehood.
  - **Q.17:88** = The challenge to Jinn and mankind.
  - **Q.15:9** = Allah's promise to preserve/guard the Qur'an.
- **Abu Bakr vs. Uthman:** Do not confuse their roles. Abu Bakr *compiled* the Qur'an into one volume because memorizers were dying. Uthman *standardized* the copies to prevent dialect disputes. 
- **The Unlettered Prophet:** Remember that the Prophet's inability to read or write is a major rational proof of the Qur'an's divine origin, as he could not have copied from earlier scriptures.
- **Zayd bin Thabit:** He is the key figure in the physical writing and compilation of the Qur'an. Always look for his name in questions about scribes.

## Quick summary

1. The divine authenticity of the Qur'an is proven by its complete lack of contradictions, despite being revealed over 23 years (Q.4:82).
2. Falsehood cannot enter the Qur'an from any direction, confirming it as a pure revelation from Allah (Q.41:42).
3. The Qur'an is unique (*I'jaz*); it challenged all mankind and Jinn to produce even a single chapter like it, a challenge which remains unmet (Q.17:88).
4. Allah assured Prophet Muhammad (SAW) that He would take charge of the Qur'an's collection and recitation, meaning the Prophet did not need to rush his tongue during revelation (Q.75:16-19).
5. Unlike previous scriptures, Allah explicitly promised to guard and preserve the Qur'an forever (Q.15:9).
6. The Qur'an was preserved initially through mass memorization and writing on crude materials (bones, leaves, stones) during the Prophet's (SAW) life.
7. Caliph Abu Bakr compiled the first complete written volume to save it after many memorizers died in battle.
8. Caliph Uthman standardized the Qur'an to a single dialect (the *Mushaf Uthmani*) to maintain unity and preserve the exact original pronunciation.

## Practice questions

**1. The verse "Will they not then ponder on the Qur'an? If it had been from other than Allah they would have found therein much incongruity" (Q.4:82) serves as proof that the Qur'an:**

- A. is superior to poetry
- B. contains no contradictions
- C. was preserved by Abu Bakr
- D. was revealed in Arabic

**Answer: B.** The verse specifically mentions that if it were not from Allah, people would find much "incongruity" (contradictions or inconsistencies) in it.

**2. Which of the following Caliphs was responsible for standardizing the Qur'an and destroying variant copies?**

- A. Abu Bakr
- B. Umar bin al-Khattab
- C. Uthman bin Affan
- D. Ali bin Abi Talib

**Answer: C.** Uthman bin Affan standardized the Qur'an into the *Mushaf Uthmani* to prevent disputes over dialects and pronunciation. 

**3. In Q.17:88, Allah challenges mankind and the Jinn to:**

- A. find a contradiction in the Qur'an
- B. produce a book like the Qur'an
- C. memorize the entire Qur'an
- D. translate the Qur'an into all languages

**Answer: B.** The verse states that even if mankind and jinn worked together, they could not produce the like of the Qur'an. This is known as the ultimate challenge (*Tahaddi*).

**4. The primary reason Caliph Abu Bakr ordered the compilation of the Qur'an into a single volume was:**

- A. the spread of different dialects in the conquered lands
- B. a direct command left by the Prophet (SAW) in his will
- C. the death of many Qur'an memorizers at the Battle of Yamamah
- D. the desire to send copies to non-Arab kings

**Answer: C.** After many *Huffaz* (memorizers) were martyred at Yamamah, Umar advised Abu Bakr to compile the Qur'an so it would not be lost.

**5. Which companion of the Prophet (SAW) was primarily tasked with leading the collection and compilation of the Qur'an?**

- A. Khalid bin Walid
- B. Abdullah bin Mas'ud
- C. Zayd bin Thabit
- D. Bilal ibn Rabah

**Answer: C.** Zayd bin Thabit was a chief scribe of the Prophet (SAW) and was chosen by both Abu Bakr and Uthman to lead the compilation and standardization efforts.

**6. Allah's promise to personally guard and preserve the Qur'an from any form of alteration is found in:**

- A. Q.15:9
- B. Q.4:82
- C. Q.17:88
- D. Q.39:27

**Answer: A.** Surah al-Hijr (Q.15:9) states: "Lo! We, even We, reveal the Reminder, and lo! We verily are its Guardian."

**7. The fact that Prophet Muhammad (SAW) was *Ummi* serves as a rational proof of the Qur'an's authenticity because:**

- A. it means he was highly educated by the Romans
- B. it proves he could not have read or copied previous scriptures to author the Qur'an
- C. it shows he was a wealthy merchant
- D. it demonstrates his mastery of Arabic poetry

**Answer: B.** *Ummi* means unlettered. Because he could not read or write, it is intellectually impossible for him to have authored a book of such profound historical, legal, and linguistic depth.

**8. According to Q.75:16-19 ("Stir not thy tongue herewith to hasten it..."), what divine guarantee was given to the Prophet (SAW) during revelation?**

- A. That he would eventually learn to read and write
- B. That the enemies of Islam would be defeated
- C. That Allah would ensure the Qur'an is collected and perfectly retained in his memory
- D. That Jibril would stay with him permanently

**Answer: C.** Allah told the Prophet (SAW) not to rush his recitation out of fear of forgetting, because Allah took upon Himself the responsibility of gathering it in his heart and making him recite it perfectly.

**9. Which of the following was NOT a material commonly used by scribes to write down the Qur'an during the Prophet's (SAW) lifetime?**

- A. Paper from China
- B. Flat stones
- C. Animal shoulder bones
- D. Palm leaves

**Answer: A.** Paper was not available in Arabia at the time. They used crude, available materials like stones, bones, leather, and palm leaves.

**10. "Falsehood cannot come at it from before it or from behind it." This statement from Q.41:42 confirms the Qur'an's:**

- A. grammatical structure
- B. protection from any form of corruption or untrue statements
- C. method of revelation through Jibril
- D. use of parables and similitudes

**Answer: B.** The verse declares that the Qur'an is absolutely protected from falsehood, proving its divine origin and continuous authenticity.
