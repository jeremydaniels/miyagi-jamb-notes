# JAMB Irk Notes: Tafsīr

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 2 of the JAMB Islamic Religious Knowledge syllabus: Tafsīr. It covers the history, importance, and types of Qur'anic exegesis (interpretation). 

This file covers the following content items:
* (i) Historical development of Tafsīr 
* (ii) Importance of Tafsīr
* (iii) Types of Tafsīr

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. trace the origin and sources of Tafsīr;
2. evaluate the importance of Tafsīr;
3. compare the types of Tafsīr.

## What is Tafsīr?

The word **Tafsīr** comes from the Arabic root word *fassara*, which means to explain, expound, or discover. In Islamic terminology, Tafsīr is the science of explaining and interpreting the meanings of the Glorious Qur'ān. 

A person who writes or teaches Tafsīr is called a **Mufassir** (plural: *Mufassirūn*). Their job is to explain the context, grammar, legal rulings, and underlying messages of the verses (Ayāt) so that Muslims can understand and apply them correctly.

## Historical development of Tafsīr

**Objective i: trace the origin and sources of Tafsīr.**
**Content (i): Historical development of Tafsīr.**

The history of Tafsīr can be divided into four major stages: the era of the Prophet (SAW), the era of the Companions (Sahābah), the era of the Successors (Tābi'ūn), and the era of compilation.

### 1. The Era of Prophet Muhammad (SAW)
The origin of Tafsīr dates back to the time of **Prophet Muhammad (SAW)**. He was the very first Mufassir. Whenever verses of the Qur'ān were revealed, the Companions would memorize them. If a verse was difficult to understand, they would ask the Prophet (SAW) directly, and he would explain it. For example, when the verse about avoiding "injustice" (*Zulm*) was revealed, the Companions were worried, but the Prophet explained that in that specific context, it meant *Shirk* (associating partners with Allah), not everyday sins.

### 2. The Era of the Companions (Sahābah)
After the death of the Prophet (SAW), the responsibility of explaining the Qur'ān fell to his Companions. They had lived with him, witnessed the events that led to specific revelations (**Asbāb al-Nuzūl**), and understood the classical Arabic language perfectly. 
Prominent Mufassirūn among the Sahābah included:
* **Abdullah ibn Abbas:** He was the Prophet's cousin. The Prophet (SAW) prayed for him, saying: "O Allah, give him a deep understanding of the religion and teach him the interpretation." He is widely known as the *Tarjumān al-Qur'ān* (The Translator/Interpreter of the Qur'ān) and the father of Tafsīr.
* **Abdullah ibn Mas'ūd:** He spent a lot of time with the Prophet (SAW) and claimed he knew where and why every verse of the Qur'ān was revealed.
* **Ali bin Abi Talib:** The fourth Caliph, known for his deep linguistic knowledge and wisdom.
* **Ubayy bin Ka'b:** One of the chief scribes of revelation.

### 3. The Era of the Successors (Tābi'ūn)
The Tābi'ūn were the generation of Muslims who learned directly from the Sahābah. As Islam spread beyond the Arabian Peninsula, many non-Arabs embraced the religion. These new Muslims needed the Qur'ān explained to them because they did not know classical Arabic or the history of the revelations. 
Famous Mufassirūn in this era included **Mujāhid ibn Jabr** (the foremost student of Ibn Abbas), **Qatādah**, and **Sa'īd ibn Jubayr**. During this time, Tafsīr was still mostly transmitted orally from teacher to student.

### 4. The Era of Compilation (Tadwīn)
Initially, Tafsīr was passed down by word of mouth as part of Hadīth transmission. By the late Umayyad and early Abbasid periods, scholars began to write it down. 
* At first, Tafsīr was just a chapter within major Hadīth books (like Sahīh al-Bukhārī).
* Later, scholars began writing independent books dedicated solely to Tafsīr, explaining the Qur'ān verse by verse from the beginning (Sūrah al-Fātihah) to the end (Sūrah an-Nās). 
* The most famous early comprehensive written Tafsīr is *Jāmi' al-Bayān* by **Ibn Jarīr al-Tabarī** (died 310 AH), widely considered the greatest foundational book of Tafsīr.

## Origin and sources of Tafsīr

**Objective i: trace the origin and sources of Tafsīr.**

For a Mufassir to interpret the Qur'ān correctly, they cannot simply guess the meaning. They must rely on specific, authoritative sources in a strict order.

1. **Tafsīr of the Qur'ān by the Qur'ān:** This is the most authentic source. Sometimes a concept is mentioned briefly in one Sūrah but explained in detail in another. For example, Sūrah al-Fātihah asks for the path of "those upon whom You have bestowed favor", and Sūrah an-Nisā' (Q.4:69) explains that these are the prophets, the steadfast affirmers of truth, the martyrs, and the righteous.
2. **Tafsīr of the Qur'ān by the Sunnah/Hadīth:** If the Qur'ān does not explain itself on a matter, scholars look to the sayings and actions of Prophet Muhammad (SAW). For example, the Qur'ān commands Muslims to establish Salāh (prayer), but it is the Hadīth that explains how to pray and how many units (Rak'ah) to perform.
3. **Statements of the Sahābah:** If the explanation is not found in the Sunnah, scholars use the statements of the Companions, because they witnessed the context of the revelations (*Asbāb al-Nuzūl*) directly.
4. **Statements of the Tābi'ūn:** If the Companions' views are unavailable, the explanations of their direct students (the Successors) are consulted.
5. **The Arabic Language:** The Qur'ān was revealed in classical Arabic. Mufassirūn use classical Arabic grammar, poetry, and lexicons to understand the exact meaning of words as they were used by Arabs at the time of revelation.
6. **Ijtihād (Independent Reasoning):** If none of the above provides a complete answer, qualified scholars use their deep knowledge to derive meaning through careful, independent reasoning, provided it does not contradict the Qur'ān or Sunnah.

## Importance of Tafsīr

**Objective ii: evaluate the importance of Tafsīr.**
**Content (ii): Importance of Tafsīr.**

Tafsīr is one of the most critical sciences in Islam. Without it, the Qur'ān could be easily misunderstood. Its importance includes:

* **Correct Understanding of the Message:** The primary purpose of Tafsīr is to clarify the will of Allah so that Muslims understand exactly what they are required to believe and do.
* **Knowing the Context of Revelation (*Asbāb al-Nuzūl*):** Many verses were revealed in response to specific historical events or questions. Knowing the background story prevents people from taking verses out of context. For example, verses commanding fighting were revealed during specific wartime conditions, and Tafsīr clarifies this context so they are not misapplied to times of peace.
* **Deriving Islamic Laws (Fiqh):** Islamic scholars (Fuqahā') rely on Tafsīr to extract legal rulings on marriage, inheritance, fasting, and commerce. Without understanding the exact meaning of the verses, Islamic law cannot be correctly formulated.
* **Preventing Misguidance and Extremism:** Without the guidelines of Tafsīr, unqualified individuals might interpret the Qur'ān based on their own desires or ignorance, leading to dangerous extremist ideologies or deviant sects.
* **Appreciating the Qur'ān's Eloquence:** Tafsīr explores the deep linguistic beauty, grammar, and rhetoric of the Qur'ān, which strengthens a believer's faith in its divine origin.
* **Moral and Spiritual Guidance:** Tafsīr brings the stories of the prophets and past nations to life, highlighting the moral lessons that Muslims should apply to their daily lives in modern times (like patience, honesty, and justice).

## Types of Tafsīr

**Objective iii: compare the types of Tafsīr.**
**Content (iii): Types of Tafsīr.**

Over the centuries, scholars have developed different approaches to interpreting the Qur'ān. These approaches are broadly divided into two main types: **Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr** (Tafsīr by Transmission) and **Tafsīr bir-Ra'y** (Tafsīr by Reason/Opinion).

### 1. Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr (Tafsīr by Transmission/Tradition)
This type of Tafsīr restricts itself strictly to explaining the Qur'ān using transmitted sources: the Qur'ān itself, the Hadīth of the Prophet (SAW), and the statements of the Sahābah and Tābi'ūn. It relies on history and authentic chains of narration. 
* **Strengths:** It is the safest and most authentic method because it relies on the Prophet and those who witnessed the revelation.
* **Examples of Books:** 
  * *Tafsīr al-Tabarī* (Jāmi' al-Bayān) by Ibn Jarīr al-Tabarī.
  * *Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr* by Ismā'īl ibn Kathīr (very popular worldwide and in Nigeria).

### 2. Tafsīr bir-Ra'y (Tafsīr by Reason/Opinion)
In this type of Tafsīr, the Mufassir uses personal reasoning (Ijtihād), intellectual deductions, linguistic analysis, and logic to interpret the verses, going beyond just the transmitted reports. 
This type is divided into two sub-categories:
* **Praiseworthy (Mahmūd):** Acceptable reasoning that stays within the boundaries of the Arabic language and does not contradict the Qur'ān or authentic Hadīth.
* **Blameworthy (Madhmūm):** Unacceptable interpretation where a person bends the meaning of the Qur'ān to support their own personal desires, political views, or heretical sect, ignoring classical Arabic rules and prophetic traditions.
* **Examples of Books:**
  * *Mafātīh al-Ghayb* (Tafsīr al-Rāzī) by Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī (a famous acceptable Tafsīr bir-Ra'y focusing on theology and philosophy).
  * *Al-Kashshāf* by al-Zamakhsharī (focuses heavily on language and grammar, though scholars note it contains some Mu'tazilite theological bias).

### Comparing the two main types of Tafsīr

| Feature | Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr (By Transmission) | Tafsīr bir-Ra'y (By Reason/Opinion) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main Basis** | Relies heavily on transmitted texts (Qur'ān, Hadīth, statements of Companions). | Relies heavily on human intellect, linguistic deduction, and logic (Ijtihād). |
| **Primary Skill Needed** | Knowledge of Hadīth chains, authenticity of narrators, and historical context. | High proficiency in classical Arabic grammar, philosophy, theology, and legal reasoning. |
| **Risk Level** | Very low risk of error, provided the Hadīth chains used are authentic. | Higher risk of error; relies on the Mufassir's personal understanding which might be flawed. |
| **Scope of Explanation** | Often focuses on historical context, rulings, and direct meaning. | Often explores deeper philosophical, theological, and scientific possibilities in the text. |
| **Key Example Book** | *Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr* | *Tafsīr al-Rāzī* |

*(Note: In reality, most great Mufassirūn blend both methods. For example, even books of Ma'thūr will discuss grammar, and books of Ra'y will quote Hadīth. However, they are classified based on which method dominates the book.)*

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Tafsīr | The science of explaining and interpreting the meanings of the Glorious Qur'ān. |
| Mufassir | A qualified scholar who explains and interprets the Qur'ān. |
| Asbāb al-Nuzūl | The circumstances, reasons, or context behind the revelation of specific Qur'anic verses. |
| Sahābah | The Companions of Prophet Muhammad (SAW). |
| Tābi'ūn | The Successors; the generation of Muslims who learned from the Sahābah. |
| Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr | Tafsīr by tradition/transmission (using Qur'ān, Hadīth, and early scholars). |
| Tafsīr bir-Ra'y | Tafsīr by reason, intellect, and linguistic opinion (Ijtihād). |
| Ijtihād | Independent reasoning exerted by a qualified scholar to derive an Islamic ruling or interpretation. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

* **Identify the "Father of Tafsīr":** JAMB frequently asks which companion is the most famous for Tafsīr. The answer is **Abdullah ibn Abbas** (often called *Tarjumān al-Qur'ān*).
* **The First Mufassir:** Do not mistake Ibn Abbas for the *first* Mufassir. The very first explainer of the Qur'ān was **Prophet Muhammad (SAW)**.
* **Order of Sources:** Always remember the hierarchy. The best explanation of the Qur'ān is **the Qur'ān itself**, followed by the **Sunnah/Hadīth**, then the **Sahābah**.
* **Distinguish the types:** *Ma'thūr* means transmitted/tradition (think of Hadīth). *Ra'y* means opinion/reason. If a question asks which type relies on intellect, choose Tafsīr bir-Ra'y.
* **Know the famous books:** Associate *Ibn Kathīr* and *Tabarī* with Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr.

## Quick summary

1. Tafsīr is the science of interpreting the Glorious Qur'ān, and a scholar who does this is called a Mufassir.
2. The history of Tafsīr developed through four stages: the Prophet (SAW), the Sahābah, the Tābi'ūn, and finally the era of written compilation.
3. The first and most authentic source for interpreting the Qur'ān is other verses of the Qur'ān itself, followed by the Prophet's Sunnah.
4. Abdullah ibn Abbas is highly celebrated as the *Tarjumān al-Qur'ān* (Interpreter of the Qur'ān) due to his vast knowledge.
5. Tafsīr is essential for understanding the true message of Islam, knowing the historical context of verses (*Asbāb al-Nuzūl*), deriving laws, and preventing extremism.
6. Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr relies strictly on transmitted texts (like the Qur'ān, Hadīth, and sayings of the Companions).
7. Tafsīr bir-Ra'y relies on a scholar's qualified independent reasoning (Ijtihād) and deep linguistic analysis, provided it does not contradict Islamic foundations.

## Practice questions

**1. The primary and most authentic source for interpreting the Glorious Qur'ān is**
- A. the statements of the Sahābah
- B. the Arabic language
- C. the Qur'ān itself
- D. the Hadīth of the Prophet

**Answer: C.** The highest and most accurate form of Tafsīr is when one part of the Qur'ān explains another part (Tafsīr of the Qur'ān by the Qur'ān).

**2. Which companion of the Prophet (SAW) is widely known as *Tarjumān al-Qur'ān* (the Interpreter of the Qur'ān)?**
- A. Ali bin Abi Talib
- B. Abdullah ibn Abbas
- C. Umar bin al-Khattab
- D. Abdullah ibn Mas'ūd

**Answer: B.** Abdullah ibn Abbas is known as *Tarjumān al-Qur'ān* because the Prophet (SAW) prayed for him to have a special understanding of the religion and its interpretation.

**3. Tafsīr that is based on the traditions of the Prophet (SAW) and the statements of his Companions is known as**
- A. Tafsīr bir-Ra'y
- B. Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr
- C. Tafsīr al-Lughawī
- D. Tafsīr al-Ishārī

**Answer: B.** Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr literally means "Tafsīr by transmission" or "Tafsīr by tradition", relying on texts passed down from the early generations.

**4. Who among the following is considered the very first Mufassir of the Glorious Qur'ān?**
- A. Angel Jibrīl
- B. Abdullah ibn Abbas
- C. Prophet Muhammad (SAW)
- D. Ibn Jarīr al-Tabarī

**Answer: C.** Prophet Muhammad (SAW) was the first Mufassir because it was his direct duty to explain the revealed verses to his Companions.

**5. Knowing the *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* is highly important in Tafsīr because it helps to**
- A. memorize the Qur'ān faster
- B. determine the exact classical Arabic pronunciation
- C. know how many verses are in a Sūrah
- D. understand the historical context and exact meaning of the verses

**Answer: D.** *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* refers to the reasons or circumstances of revelation. Knowing why a verse was revealed prevents it from being taken out of context.

**6. Which of the following best describes *Tafsīr bir-Ra'y*?**
- A. Interpretation based solely on weak Hadīth.
- B. Interpretation using a scholar's qualified independent reasoning and linguistic deductions.
- C. Interpretation using only the sayings of the Successors (Tābi'ūn).
- D. Interpretation of the Qur'ān by other verses of the Qur'ān.

**Answer: B.** *Ra'y* means opinion or reason. It refers to a qualified Mufassir using their intellect, reasoning (Ijtihād), and mastery of Arabic to deduce meanings.

**7. During which era did scholars begin to separate Tafsīr from general Hadīth collections and write it as independent books?**
- A. The era of the Prophet (SAW)
- B. The era of the Sahābah
- C. The era of the Tābi'ūn
- D. The era of Compilation (Tadwīn)

**Answer: D.** In the era of Compilation (Tadwīn), scholars like al-Tabarī began writing comprehensive books solely dedicated to interpreting the Qur'ān verse by verse.

**8. Which of the following is a famous example of a book of *Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr*?**
- A. Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr
- B. Tafsīr al-Rāzī
- C. Al-Kashshāf
- D. Sahīh Muslim

**Answer: A.** *Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr* is one of the most famous and widely accepted books of Tafsīr bil-Ma'thūr, as it relies heavily on Hadīth to explain the Qur'ān.

**9. In the hierarchy of sources for Tafsīr, if a meaning cannot be found in the Qur'ān or the Sunnah, the next source a Mufassir must consult is**
- A. their own personal opinion
- B. modern scientific discoveries
- C. the statements of the Sahābah
- D. the statements of the Tābi'ūn

**Answer: C.** After the Qur'ān and the Sunnah, the next authoritative source is the statements of the Companions (Sahābah), because they witnessed the revelations. 

**10. A blameworthy (Madhmūm) type of *Tafsīr bir-Ra'y* is one where the interpreter**
- A. uses classical Arabic poetry to understand a word.
- B. twists the meaning of verses to support their personal desires or sect.
- C. derives a new legal ruling based on strict Ijtihād.
- D. compares two similar verses in the Qur'ān.

**Answer: B.** Blameworthy Tafsīr bir-Ra'y occurs when someone ignores established rules of Arabic and authentic traditions to force the Qur'ān to fit their own preconceived ideas or desires.
