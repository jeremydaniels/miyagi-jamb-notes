# JAMB Irk Notes: Study of Selected Suwar (al-Fātihah to al-Mā'ūn)

## What JAMB expects

This topic covers Section A, Topic 4 of the JAMB syllabus: Study of the Arabic text of selected suwar with tajwīd. Because the syllabus lists fourteen suwar, we have split them across files to keep your study manageable. This file covers the first seven:

- **a.** al-Fātihah (Q.1)
- **b.** al-Ādiyāt (Q.100)
- **c.** al-Qāri'ah (Q.101)
- **d.** at-Takāthur (Q.102)
- **e.** al-Asr (Q.103)
- **f.** al-Humazah (Q.104)
- **g.** al-Mā'ūn (Q.107)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Recite with correct tajwīd the Arabic texts of the suwar.
2. Translate the verses.
3. Deduce lessons from them.
4. Evaluate the teachings of the verses.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** You might notice some odd typing in the official JAMB syllabus for this topic. The syllabus uses a small raised "c" for the Arabic letter *ʿayn* (for example, `al-cĀdiyāt` and `Mācūn`), which we write as *ʿ* in standard transliteration. It spells *al-Qāri'ah* as "al –Qari'cah", which is a typo. It also prints extra opening brackets for some references, such as `((Q.103)` and `((Q.107`. Our notes use the standard, correct spellings and references.

---

## Objective i: Reciting with correct tajwīd

Tajwīd means to beautify the recitation of the Glorious Qur'an by giving every Arabic letter its proper right (pronunciation from the correct part of the mouth) and applying the correct rules. 

To fulfill this objective, you must learn to recite the Arabic texts of the suwar correctly. In the sections for each surah below, you will find the Arabic text, its transliteration, and specific tajwīd rules to apply—such as **Ghunnah** (a nasal hum), **Qalqalah** (an echoing sound on certain consonants), and **Madd** (prolongation of vowels). 

*Crucial advice:* A wrongly pronounced Arabic letter or vowel can completely change the meaning of a verse, which is worse than not reading it at all. Therefore, reading transliteration is only a temporary aid; students must learn and practice recitation practically with a qualified Qur'an teacher.

## Objective ii: Translating the verses

Translating the verses involves conveying their original Arabic meaning into English so that we can understand Allah's message. In the breakdown of each surah below, we provide a clear translation summary. Where specific phrasing is famous, we quote short passages from the well-known public-domain English translation by Marmaduke Pickthall (1930). 

## Objective iii: Deducing lessons from the suwar

Deducing lessons means identifying the practical and moral takeaways from the verses. It answers the question: "What does this surah tell us to do, believe, or avoid?" JAMB frequently tests your ability to link a specific moral lesson (like avoiding greed or helping orphans) to its correct surah. These are listed under each surah below.

## Objective iv: Evaluating the teachings of the verses

Evaluating the teachings goes a step beyond listing lessons. It means understanding the broader impact of the surah's message on a Muslim's worldview, Islamic law, and society. For example, understanding how a surah builds the concept of accountability in the Hereafter or how it balances formal worship with social justice. These evaluations are provided in the specific surah sections.

## a. Surah al-Fātihah (Q.1)

Surah al-Fātihah ("The Opening") is the first surah of the Qur'an. It was revealed in Makkah and contains seven verses (ayāt). 

### Arabic text and transliteration
بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ (١)
*Bismillāhi r-raḥmāni r-raḥīm.*
الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ (٢)
*Al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn.*
الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ (٣)
*Ar-raḥmāni r-raḥīm.*
مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ (٤)
*Māliki yawmi d-dīn.*
إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ (٥)
*Iyyāka naʿbudu wa iyyāka nastaʿīn.*
اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ (٦)
*Ihdinā ṣ-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm.*
صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ (٧)
*Ṣirāṭa l-ladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim, ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa la ḍ-ḍāllīn.*

**Tajwīd application:** Pay attention to the **Madd Lāzim** (compulsory elongation) in the word *ḍāllīn* in verse 7. The vowel must be stretched for six counts before heavily pronouncing the doubled *lām*. 

### Translation summary
The surah begins by praising Allah, the Beneficent and Merciful, who is the Lord of the Worlds and the Owner of the Day of Judgment. Pickthall translates verse 5 as: "Thee (alone) we worship; Thee (alone) we ask for help." The believer then asks Allah to guide them to the straight path—the path of those He has favoured, not the path of those who have earned His anger, nor those who have gone astray.

### Lessons and teachings
- **Tawhīd (Monotheism):** It establishes that worship and the seeking of divine help must be directed to Allah alone.
- **Reliance on Allah:** Muslims are taught to constantly seek Allah's guidance to stay on the correct path.
- **Evaluation:** Al-Fātihah is the cornerstone of Islamic worship. It is a mandatory pillar of the daily prayers (Salāh); a prayer performed without reciting it is considered invalid. It perfectly summarizes the relationship between the Creator (who is merciful but also the ultimate Judge) and the creation (who are dependent servants).

## b. Surah al-Ādiyāt (Q.100)

Surah al-Ādiyāt ("The Coursers" or "The Charging Horses") is an early Makkan surah containing 11 verses.

### Arabic text and transliteration
وَالْعَادِيَاتِ ضَبْحًا (١) فَالْمُورِيَاتِ قَدْحًا (٢) فَالْمُغِيرَاتِ صُبْحًا (٣)
*Wa l-ʿādiyāti ḍabḥā. Fa l-mūriyāti qadḥā. Fa l-mughīrāti ṣubḥā.*
فَأَثَرْنَ بِهِ نَقْعًا (٤) فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا (٥)
*Fa atharna bihi naqʿā. Fa wasaṭna bihi jamʿā.*
إِنَّ الْإِنْسَانَ لِرَبِّهِ لَكَنُودٌ (٦) وَإِنَّهُ عَلَى ذَلِكَ لَشَهِيدٌ (٧) وَإِنَّهُ لِحُبِّ الْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ (٨)
*Inna l-insāna lirabbihi lakanūd. Wa innahu ʿalā dhālika lashahīd. Wa innahu liḥubbi l-khayri lashadīd.*
أَفَلَا يَعْلَمُ إِذَا بُعْثِرَ مَا فِي الْقُبُورِ (٩) وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ (١٠) إِنَّ رَبَّهُمْ بِهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَخَبِيرٌ (١١)
*Afalā yaʿlamu idhā buʿthira mā fī l-qubūr. Wa ḥuṣṣila mā fī ṣ-ṣudūr. Inna rabbahum bihim yawma'idhin lakhabīr.*

**Tajwīd application:** Observe the **Qalqalah** (echoing sound) on the letter *bāʾ* (ب) in *ḍabḥā* and *ṣubḥā*, and on the letter *dāl* (د) in *qadḥā*. When these specific letters have no vowel (sukūn), they are pronounced with a slight bounce.

### Translation summary
Allah swears an oath by the snorting war-horses that strike sparks of fire with their hooves, raiding at dawn and leaving a trail of dust as they plunge into the enemy. Pickthall translates verse 6 as: "Lo! man is an ingrate unto his Lord". The surah warns that man is violently passionate in his love for wealth. It ends by asking if man does not realize that when the graves are turned upside down and the secrets of the hearts are brought out, Allah will be perfectly informed of everything.

### Lessons and teachings
- **Human Ingratitude:** Mankind is naturally prone to forgetting Allah's favours.
- **Love of Wealth:** Extreme greed and love for worldly things make a person ungrateful and reckless.
- **Evaluation:** The surah uses powerful, fast-paced imagery of war-horses to grab attention before delivering a heavy psychological truth about human greed. It teaches that true accountability involves not just our actions, but the hidden motives inside our hearts (*mā fī ṣ-ṣudūr*), which will all be exposed on the Day of Judgement.

## c. Surah al-Qāri'ah (Q.101)

Surah al-Qāri'ah ("The Calamity" or "The Striking Hour") is a Makkan surah containing 11 verses focused on the end of the world.

### Arabic text and transliteration
الْقَارِعَةُ (١) مَا الْقَارِعَةُ (٢) وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْقَارِعَةُ (٣)
*Al-qāriʿah. Ma l-qāriʿah. Wa mā adrāka ma l-qāriʿah.*
يَوْمَ يَكُونُ النَّاسُ كَالْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ (٤) وَتَكُونُ الْجِبَالُ كَالْعِهْنِ الْمَنْفُوشِ (٥)
*Yawma yakūnu n-nāsu ka l-farāshi l-mabthūth. Wa takūnu l-jibālu ka l-ʿihni l-manfūsh.*
فَأَمَّا مَنْ ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ (٦) فَهُوَ فِي عِيشَةٍ رَاضِيَةٍ (٧)
*Fa ammā man thaqulat mawāzīnuh. Fahuwa fī ʿīshatin rāḍiyah.*
وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ (٨) فَأُمُّهُ هَاوِيَةٌ (٩)
*Wa ammā man khaffat mawāzīnuh. Fa ummuhu hāwiyah.*
وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا هِيَهْ (١٠) نَارٌ حَامِيَةٌ (١١)
*Wa mā adrāka mā hiyah. Nārun ḥāmiyah.*

**Tajwīd application:** Note the **Tāʾ Marbūṭah** (ة) at the end of many verses here (e.g., *al-qāriʿah*, *rāḍiyah*). When stopping on this letter, it is pronounced as an exhaled *hāʾ* (*h*) rather than a *t*. 

### Translation summary
"The Calamity! What is the Calamity?" (Pickthall). The surah describes the Day of Judgement as a time when mankind will be scattered like moths in confusion, and massive mountains will be reduced to floating carded wool. Those whose scales of good deeds are heavy will enjoy a pleasant life in Paradise. But for those whose scales are light, "The bereft and hungry one will be his mother" (Pickthall's figurative translation for *Hāwiyah*, meaning they will fall headfirst into a bottomless pit). The surah concludes by defining it as a Raging Fire.

![A decorative traditional balance scale with the left pan heavy and low holding a glowing light, while the empty right pan is raised.](images/A4a-01-mizan-balance-scales-deeds.png)

### Lessons and teachings
- **The Terror of the Last Day:** The universe as we know it will be violently destroyed.
- **The Divine Scale (Mīzān):** Good and bad deeds will literally be weighed.
- **Evaluation:** This surah teaches Muslims to prioritize actions that carry spiritual "weight" (like prayer, honesty, and charity) over empty worldly pursuits. The concept of the Mīzān reinforces strict divine justice.

## d. Surah at-Takāthur (Q.102)

Surah at-Takāthur ("Rivalry in Worldly Increase") is an 8-verse Makkan surah addressing the human obsession with accumulating more wealth and status than others.

### Arabic text and transliteration
أَلْهَاكُمُ التَّكَاثُرُ (١) حَتَّى زُرْتُمُ الْمَقَابِرَ (٢)
*Alhākumu t-takāthur. Ḥattā zurtumu l-maqābir.*
كَلَّا سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ (٣) ثُمَّ كَلَّا سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ (٤)
*Kallā sawfa taʿlamūn. Thumma kallā sawfa taʿlamūn.*
كَلَّا لَوْ تَعْلَمُونَ عِلْمَ الْيَقِينِ (٥)
*Kallā law taʿlamūna ʿilma l-yaqīn.*
لَتَرَوُنَّ الْجَحِيمَ (٦) ثُمَّ لَتَرَوُنَّهَا عَيْنَ الْيَقِينِ (٧)
*Latarawunna l-jaḥīm. Thumma latarawunnahā ʿayna l-yaqīn.*
ثُمَّ لَتُسْأَلُنَّ يَوْمَئِذٍ عَنِ النَّعِيمِ (٨)
*Thumma latus'alunna yawma'idhin ʿani n-naʿīm.*

**Tajwīd application:** Observe the **Ghunnah** on the doubled *mīm* in *thumma* and the doubled *nūn* in *latus'alunna*. These letters must be hummed through the nose for two beats.

### Translation summary
Pickthall translates the powerful opening: "Rivalry in worldly increase distracteth you / Until ye come unto the graves." The surah warns humanity that if they possessed the "certainty of knowledge" (*ʿilm al-yaqīn*), they would behave differently. Eventually, they will see Hellfire with the "certainty of sight" (*ʿayn al-yaqīn*). On that day, everyone will be questioned about the blessings and pleasures (*an-naʿīm*) they enjoyed in life.

### Lessons and teachings
- **The Danger of Distraction:** Competing for money, followers, or status blinds people to their true purpose until death suddenly arrives.
- **Accountability for Blessings:** We will be interrogated about how we used our health, wealth, time, and safety.
- **Evaluation:** At-Takāthur shifts the focus of a Muslim away from materialistic competition. It teaches that wealth is not merely a privilege, but a test for which severe questioning awaits.

## e. Surah al-Asr (Q.103)

Surah al-Asr ("The Declining Day" or "Time") is one of the shortest suwar in the Qur'an, containing just three verses. Despite its brevity, Imam ash-Shafi'i famously stated that if Allah had revealed only this surah, it would have been sufficient for the guidance of mankind.

### Arabic text and transliteration
وَالْعَصْرِ (١) إِنَّ الْإِنْسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ (٢)
*Wa l-ʿaṣr. Inna l-insāna lafī khusr.*
إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ (٣)
*Illa l-ladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilū ṣ-ṣāliḥāti wa tawāṣaw bi l-ḥaqqi wa tawāṣaw bi ṣ-ṣabr.*

**Tajwīd application:** Notice the heavy *rāʾ* (ر) sound when stopping at the end of each verse (*ʿaṣr*, *khusr*, *ṣabr*). Because the letter before the *rāʾ* has a sukūn, and the one before that has a fatha or damma, the *rāʾ* must be pronounced with a full, thick sound.

### Translation summary
Allah swears "By the declining day" (Pickthall) or "By Time". The surah declares a universal rule: all of mankind is in a state of absolute loss. However, it provides four exceptions to this ruin: those who have faith, do righteous deeds, advise each other to truth, and advise each other to patience.

### Lessons and teachings
- **The Value of Time:** Time is a vanishing asset. Simply doing nothing means you are losing it.
- **The Four Pillars of Salvation:** Faith alone is not enough; it must be backed by good deeds and a communal effort to promote truth and patience.
- **Evaluation:** This surah summarizes the entire Islamic philosophy of success. It teaches that personal piety is incomplete without social responsibility (advising others).

## f. Surah al-Humazah (Q.104)

Surah al-Humazah ("The Traducer" or "The Slanderer") is a 9-verse Makkan surah that severely condemns verbal abuse and the arrogant hoarding of wealth.

### Arabic text and transliteration
وَيْلٌ لِكُلِّ هُمَزَةٍ لُمَزَةٍ (١) الَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ (٢)
*Waylun likulli humazatin lumazah. Al-ladhī jamaʿa mālan wa ʿaddadah.*
يَحْسَبُ أَنَّ مَالَهُ أَخْلَدَهُ (٣) كَلَّا لَيُنْبَذَنَّ فِي الْحُطَمَةِ (٤)
*Yaḥsabu anna mālahu akhladah. Kallā layumbadhanna fī l-ḥuṭamah.*
وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْحُطَمَةُ (٥) نَارُ اللَّهِ الْمُوقَدَةُ (٦)
*Wa mā adrāka mā l-ḥuṭamah. Nāru llāhi l-mūqadah.*
الَّتِي تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى الْأَفْئِدَةِ (٧) إِنَّهَا عَلَيْهِمْ مُؤْصَدَةٌ (٨) فِي عَمَدٍ مُمَدَّدَةٍ (٩)
*Al-latī taṭṭaliʿu ʿala l-af'idah. Innahā ʿalayhim mu'ṣadah. Fī ʿamadin mumaddadah.*

**Tajwīd application:** Observe the **Iqlāb** rule in the word *layumbadhanna* (لَيُنْبَذَنَّ). Because a *nūn* with a sukūn is followed by a *bāʾ*, the *nūn* sound is flipped into a *mīm* sound and held with a nasal hum.

### Translation summary
"Woe unto every slandering traducer" (Pickthall). This warns those who backbite, mock others, and obsessively hoard wealth, thinking their money will make them immortal. The surah firmly rejects this, stating they will be thrown into the Consuming/Crushing Fire (*Al-Ḥuṭamah*). It is Allah's kindled fire which leaps right up over their hearts, closing in on them in huge outstretched columns.

### Lessons and teachings
- **Prohibition of Slander:** Backbiting (*ghībah*), mocking, and spreading rumours are grave sins in Islam.
- **The Delusion of Wealth:** Money cannot prevent death or save a person from divine punishment.
- **Evaluation:** Al-Humazah protects the dignity of individuals in society. It teaches that attacking people's reputations and relying on material wealth are traits of those destined for severe punishment (*Al-Ḥuṭamah*).

## g. Surah al-Mā'ūn (Q.107)

Surah al-Mā'ūn ("Small Kindnesses" or "Daily Necessities") is a 7-verse surah that boldly links formal worship with social justice and kindness. 

### Arabic text and transliteration
أَرَأَيْتَ الَّذِي يُكَذِّبُ بِالدِّينِ (١) فَذَلِكَ الَّذِي يَدُعُّ الْيَتِيمَ (٢)
*Ara'ayta l-ladhī yukadhdhibu bi d-dīn. Fadhālika l-ladhī yaduʿʿu l-yatīm.*
وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَى طَعَامِ الْمِسْكِينِ (٣) فَوَيْلٌ لِلْمُصَلِّينَ (٤)
*Wa lā yaḥuḍḍu ʿalā ṭaʿāmi l-miskīn. Fawaylun lil-muṣallīn.*
الَّذِينَ هُمْ عَنْ صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ (٥) الَّذِينَ هُمْ يُرَاءُونَ (٦) وَيَمْنَعُونَ الْمَاعُونَ (٧)
*Al-ladhīna hum ʿan ṣalātihim sāhūn. Al-ladhīna hum yurā'ūn. Wa yamnaʿūna l-mā'ūn.*

**Tajwīd application:** Notice the **Idghām** (merging) in *fawaylun lil-muṣallīn* (فَوَيْلٌ لِلْمُصَلِّينَ). The "un" sound (tanwīn) on *waylun* merges completely into the *lām* without a nasal hum, sounding like *fawaylul-lilmuṣallīn*.

### Translation summary
"Hast thou observed him who belieth religion?" (Pickthall). The surah answers that such a person is the one who violently repels the orphan and does not encourage feeding the poor. It then declares woe to those who pray, but are heedless of their prayers—those who pray only to be seen by people (showing off), yet refuse to share even small, basic kindnesses or household items (*al-Mā'ūn*).

### Lessons and teachings
- **Hypocrisy in Worship (Riyā'):** Performing Salah merely to show off to others nullifies the good deed and is considered a minor form of associating partners with Allah (*shirk*).
- **Social Responsibility:** Denying religion is not just a matter of belief; it is demonstrated by mistreating orphans and ignoring the poor.
- **Evaluation:** Al-Mā'ūn provides a profound evaluation of Islamic ethics: formal worship (like Salah) is completely empty and rejected by Allah if it does not make the worshipper compassionate towards the vulnerable in society.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Tajwīd** | The set of rules governing the correct pronunciation and recitation of the Qur'an. |
| **Qalqalah** | A tajwīd rule producing an echoing or bouncing sound on certain unvoweled letters (qāf, ṭāʾ, bāʾ, jīm, dāl). |
| **Ghunnah** | A nasal humming sound held for two beats, usually applied to doubled *mīm* and *nūn*. |
| **Mīzān** | The Divine Scale used on the Day of Judgement to weigh a person's good and bad deeds (mentioned in al-Qāri'ah). |
| **Al-Ḥuṭamah** | The Crushing Fire; a name for Hellfire mentioned in Surah al-Humazah, prepared for slanderers and hoarders. |
| **Riyā'** | Showing off or performing religious acts to gain human approval rather than for Allah; severely condemned in al-Mā'ūn. |
| **Al-Mā'ūn** | Small kindnesses, daily necessities, or simple acts of charity. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Match the Surah to its theme:** JAMB loves to test if you can differentiate the themes. Remember:
  - *al-Ādiyāt:* Human ingratitude and violent love of wealth (uses the imagery of war-horses).
  - *at-Takāthur:* Distraction by competition and hoarding until the grave.
  - *al-Humazah:* Condemnation of slandering, mocking, and hoarding (warns of the Crushing Fire).
- **The meaning of al-Mā'ūn:** It specifically refers to small kindnesses. The surah heavily condemns *Riyā'* (showing off in prayers) while mistreating orphans. 
- **The fate in al-Qāri'ah:** Be familiar with Pickthall's translation: "The bereft and hungry one will be his mother." This describes a person whose good deeds are light falling headfirst into the pit of Hell (*Hāwiyah*).
- **The four exceptions in al-Asr:** Mankind is in loss except those with (1) faith, (2) righteous deeds, (3) who advise to truth, and (4) who advise to patience.
- **Transliteration tricks:** Remember that the syllabus uses a raised "c" for *ʿayn* (e.g., `cAsr` for `ʿAṣr` and `Mācūn` for `Mā'ūn`).

## Quick summary

1. **Surah al-Fātihah** is the essential opening of the Qur'an; a required pillar of Salah that teaches pure monotheism and reliance on Allah's guidance.
2. **Surah al-Ādiyāt** uses the dramatic imagery of charging war-horses to highlight human ingratitude and extreme greed for wealth.
3. **Surah al-Qāri'ah** vividly describes the terrifying events of the Last Day and emphasizes the Divine Scale (Mīzān) that will weigh our deeds.
4. **Surah at-Takāthur** warns that worldly competition blinds people to their true purpose until they die, after which they will answer for every blessing they enjoyed.
5. **Surah al-Asr** establishes that all human beings are losing their most valuable asset (time), unless they combine faith, good deeds, truth, and patience.
6. **Surah al-Humazah** strictly forbids backbiting and arrogant hoarding, warning offenders that their wealth will not save them from the Crushing Fire (*Al-Ḥuṭamah*).
7. **Surah al-Mā'ūn** warns that ignoring the rights of orphans and the poor, and performing prayers just to show off, are signs of a person who practically denies the religion.

## Practice questions

**1. According to Surah al-Mā'ūn, performing Salah with the intention of showing off to others is a sign of someone who:**
- A. has heavy scales on the Day of Judgement
- B. belieth (denies) the religion
- C. possesses certainty of knowledge
- D. is protected from the Crushing Fire

**Answer: B.** The surah equates those who are heedless in their prayers (showing off) and those who repel the orphan with people who practically deny the religion.

**2. Which surah translates to "The Calamity" and describes mankind being scattered like moths?**
- A. al-Ādiyāt
- B. at-Takāthur
- C. al-Humazah
- D. al-Qāri'ah

**Answer: D.** Surah al-Qāri'ah describes the terrifying events of the Last Day, including mankind being like scattered moths and mountains like carded wool.

**3. In Tajwīd, the rule that produces an echoing or bouncing sound on unvoweled letters like 'bāʾ' and 'dāl' is called:**
- A. Ghunnah
- B. Qalqalah
- C. Madd Lāzim
- D. Idghām

**Answer: B.** Qalqalah is the echoing sound applied to the letters qāf, ṭāʾ, bāʾ, jīm, and dāl when they carry a sukūn.

**4. The phrase "Rivalry in worldly increase distracteth you" is Pickthall's translation of the opening of which surah?**
- A. at-Takāthur
- B. al-Humazah
- C. al-Asr
- D. al-Ādiyāt

**Answer: A.** "Alhākumu t-takāthur" (Rivalry in worldly increase distracts you) is the opening verse of Surah at-Takāthur.

**5. According to Surah al-Asr, humanity is in a state of absolute loss EXCEPT those who possess which of the following traits?**
- A. Wealth, health, faith, and patience
- B. Faith, righteous deeds, advising to truth, and advising to patience
- C. Daily prayers, fasting, charity, and pilgrimage
- D. Fear of the Fire, love of wealth, faith, and honesty

**Answer: B.** The surah explicitly lists these four conditions to escape the loss of time: faith, good works, mutual advice to truth, and mutual advice to patience.

**6. The "Crushing Fire" (Al-Ḥuṭamah) mentioned in Surah al-Humazah is specifically prepared for those who:**
- A. fight in battles and strike sparks of fire
- B. neglect their daily prayers
- C. slander others and arrogantly hoard wealth
- D. have light scales of good deeds

**Answer: C.** Surah al-Humazah severely condemns the slandering traducer who gathers and hoards wealth, stating they will be thrown into Al-Ḥuṭamah.

**7. In Surah al-Qāri'ah, Pickthall translates "Fa ummuhu hāwiyah" as "The bereft and hungry one will be his mother". This describes the fate of a person who:**
- A. repels the orphan
- B. has heavy scales of good works
- C. has light scales of good works
- D. prays only to show off

**Answer: C.** The verse refers to those whose scales of good deeds are light (*khaffat mawāzīnuh*); their destination is the bottomless pit of Hell (*Hāwiyah*).

**8. Which surah uses the imagery of charging war-horses to emphasize human ingratitude to Allah?**
- A. al-Fātihah
- B. al-Qāri'ah
- C. al-Ādiyāt
- D. at-Takāthur

**Answer: C.** Surah al-Ādiyāt swears by the snorting war-horses to introduce the lesson that mankind is violently ungrateful to their Lord.

**9. Surah al-Fātihah teaches pure Tawhīd and reliance on Allah. This is most clearly demonstrated in which of its verses?**
- A. "Thee (alone) we worship; Thee (alone) we ask for help."
- B. "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds."
- C. "Owner of the Day of Judgment."
- D. "The Beneficent, the Merciful."

**Answer: A.** Verse 5 (*Iyyāka naʿbudu wa iyyāka nastaʿīn*) perfectly summarizes monotheistic worship and absolute reliance on Allah for help.

**10. What does the term "al-Mā'ūn" refer to in the context of the surah that bears its name?**
- A. A severe calamity
- B. The Divine Scale
- C. Certainty of knowledge
- D. Small kindnesses or daily necessities

**Answer: D.** Al-Mā'ūn refers to the small kindnesses, acts of charity, or household items that hypocrites refuse to share with others.
