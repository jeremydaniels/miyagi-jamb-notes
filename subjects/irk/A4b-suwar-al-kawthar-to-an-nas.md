# JAMB Irk Notes: Study of Selected Suwar (al-Kawthar to an-Nās)

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section A, Topic 4 of the JAMB IRK syllabus: Study of the Arabic text of the following suwar/ayats with tajwīd. Because Topic 4 covers fourteen complete suwar, it has been split into two files to keep the notes manageable. This second part covers the final seven suwar of the Glorious Qur'ān:

- (h) al-Kawthar (Q.108)
- (i) al-Kāfirūn (Q.109)
- (j) an-Nasr (Q.110)
- (k) al-Masad (Q.111)
- (l) al-Ikhlās (Q.112)
- (m) al-Falaq (Q.113)
- (n) an-Nās (Q.114)

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:
1. Recite with correct tajwīd the Arabic texts of the suwar.
2. Translate the verses.
3. Deduce lessons from them.
4. Evaluate the teachings of the verses.

**Note on the syllabus wording:** The syllabus contains minor typing errors, placing double opening parentheses before the references for al-Masad and al-Falaq, printing them as `al -Masad ((Q.111)` and `al -Falaq ((Q.113)`. The correct standard references are Q.111 and Q.113. Additionally, English translations provided in these notes are from Marmaduke Pickthall's "The Meaning of the Glorious Koran" (1930), a standard public-domain text. Because Arabic pronunciation is complex, you must learn recitation and tajwīd from a qualified teacher; transliteration is provided here only as a basic guide.

## Content h: Surah al-Kawthar (Q.108)

Surah al-Kawthar (The Abundance) is the shortest chapter in the Qur'ān, containing only three ayāt (verses). It was revealed in Makkah during a very difficult time for Prophet Muhammad (SAW). When his infant sons died, his enemies among the Quraysh (such as al-'As bin Wa'il) mocked him, calling him *abtar* (a man cut off from having a male heir, meaning his name and legacy would die with him). Allah revealed this Surah to comfort him.

### Arabic text, transliteration, and tajwīd
- **Verse 1:** إِنَّا أَعْطَيْنَاكَ الْكَوْثَرَ
  - *Transliteration:* Innā a'taynākal-kawthar
  - *Tajwīd note:* The word *Innā* has a *shaddah* (doubling mark) on the Nūn, requiring a sustained nasal sound (*ghunnah*). There is a slight elongation (*madd*) on the alif.
- **Verse 2:** فَصَلِّ لِرَبِّكَ وَانْحَرْ
  - *Transliteration:* Fasalli lirabbika wanhar
- **Verse 3:** إِنَّ شَانِئَكَ هُوَ الْأَبْتَرُ
  - *Transliteration:* Inna shāni'aka huwal-abtar
  - *Tajwīd note:* The letter Bā' in *abtar* requires a bouncing sound (*qalqalah*) because it carries a sukūn (is vowelless).

### Translation
1. Lo! We have given thee Abundance;
2. So pray unto thy Lord, and sacrifice.
3. Lo! it is thy insulter (and not thou) who is without posterity.

### Lessons deduced
- **Allah's immense favours:** Allah granted the Prophet (SAW) *al-Kawthar*, which refers to abundant goodness, including a river in Paradise and a vast number of followers throughout history.
- **Gratitude through worship:** The correct response to Allah's blessings is to worship Him alone (Salāh) and offer sacrifices (such as during Eid al-Adha) solely for His sake.
- **Ultimate victory:** The enemies of Islam who mocked the Prophet (SAW) are the ones who were truly "cut off." Their legacy perished, while the Prophet's name is honoured globally every day.

### Evaluation of teachings
The Surah teaches Muslims to remain steadfast and trust in Allah during times of personal loss or mockery. It reinforces the Islamic principle that true legacy is not based on male offspring, but on righteousness and faith. It also strictly directs all acts of worship and animal sacrifice to Allah alone, rejecting pre-Islamic pagan practices.

## Content i: Surah al-Kāfirūn (Q.109)

Surah al-Kāfirūn (The Disbelievers) is a Makkan Surah consisting of six ayāt. It was revealed when the leaders of the Quraysh proposed a compromise to the Prophet (SAW): they offered to worship Allah for one year if the Prophet (SAW) would agree to worship their idols (al-Lāt, al-'Uzzā, etc.) for the next year. This Surah firmly rejected the offer.

### Arabic text, transliteration, and tajwīd
- **Verse 1:** قُلْ يَا أَيُّهَا الْكَافِرُونَ
  - *Transliteration:* Qul yā ayyuhal-kāfirūn
  - *Tajwīd note:* There is a major elongation (*madd*) on the word *yā*.
- **Verse 2:** لَا أَعْبُدُ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ
  - *Transliteration:* Lā a'budu mā ta'budūn
- **Verse 3:** وَلَا أَنْتُمْ عَابِدُونَ مَا أَعْبُدُ
  - *Transliteration:* Wa lā antum 'ābidūna mā a'bud
  - *Tajwīd note:* When stopping at *a'bud*, the Dāl receives a *qalqalah* (echoing bounce).
- **Verse 4:** وَلَا أَنَا عَابِدٌ مَا عَبَدْتُمْ
  - *Transliteration:* Wa lā ana 'ābidum mā 'abadtum
  - *Tajwīd note:* The letter Dāl in *'abadtum* merges into the Tā' (*idghām*), so the Dāl is not pronounced separately.
- **Verse 5:** وَلَا أَنْتُمْ عَابِدُونَ مَا أَعْبُدُ
  - *Transliteration:* Wa lā antum 'ābidūna mā a'bud
- **Verse 6:** لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ وَلِيَ دِينِ
  - *Transliteration:* Lakum dīnukum waliya dīn

### Translation
1. Say: O disbelievers!
2. I worship not that which ye worship;
3. Nor worship ye that which I worship.
4. And I shall not worship that which ye worship.
5. Nor will ye worship that which I worship.
6. Unto you your religion, and unto me my religion.

### Lessons deduced
- **No compromise in Tawhīd:** Islamic monotheism cannot be mixed with polytheism (shirk). There is no middle ground when it comes to the core belief of worshipping Allah alone.
- **Clarity of faith:** A Muslim must be clear and unapologetic about their faith and whom they worship.
- **Religious boundaries:** The final verse, "Unto you your religion, and unto me my religion," establishes a clear separation between the path of Islam and the path of disbelief.

### Evaluation of teachings
This Surah is highly relevant for establishing peaceful coexistence without religious compromise. It teaches Muslims that while they should not force their religion on others or adopt the religious practices of non-Muslims, they can acknowledge the differences and live without compromising their own faith.

## Content j: Surah an-Nasr (Q.110)

Surah an-Nasr (The Succour / The Help) is a Madinan Surah, consisting of three ayāt. It was one of the very last complete suwar to be revealed, shortly before the death of Prophet Muhammad (SAW). It references the Conquest of Makkah and the subsequent mass conversions to Islam.

### Arabic text, transliteration, and tajwīd
- **Verse 1:** إِذَا جَاءَ نَصْرُ اللَّهِ وَالْفَتْحُ
  - *Transliteration:* Idhā jā'a nasrullāhi wal-fath
  - *Tajwīd note:* The word *jā'a* contains a compulsory elongation (*madd wājib muttasil*).
- **Verse 2:** وَرَأَيْتَ النَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا
  - *Transliteration:* Wa ra'aytan-nāsa yadkhulūna fī dīnillāhi afwājā
  - *Tajwīd note:* The Nūn in *an-nāsa* has a *shaddah*, requiring *ghunnah* (nasal hum). The Dāl in *yadkhulūna* requires *qalqalah*.
- **Verse 3:** فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَاسْتَغْفِرْهُ إِنَّهُ كَانَ تَوَّابًا
  - *Transliteration:* Fasabbih bihamdi rabbika wastaghfirh, innahū kāna tawwābā

### Translation
1. When Allah's succour and the triumph cometh
2. And thou seest mankind entering the religion of Allah in troops,
3. Then hymn the praises of thy Lord, and seek forgiveness of Him. Lo! He is ever ready to show mercy.

### Lessons deduced
- **Victory belongs to Allah:** The success of the Islamic mission and the Conquest of Makkah were entirely due to Allah's help (*nasr*), not just human effort.
- **Humility in success:** Unlike worldly conquerors who celebrate with arrogance, a Muslim responds to victory with praise of Allah (Tasbīh) and by seeking His forgiveness (Istighfār).
- **The completion of the mission:** The Surah acted as an indicator (a sign) to the early Muslims that the Prophet's mission was complete, gently pointing toward his approaching passing.

### Evaluation of teachings
The Surah teaches the Islamic code of conduct during moments of great success and triumph. It reminds believers that moments of victory are times for profound humility, gratitude, and seeking forgiveness for any shortcomings.

## Content k: Surah al-Masad (Q.111)

Surah al-Masad (The Palm-Fibre / The Flame), also known as Surah Lahab, is a Makkan Surah with five ayāt. It is a severe condemnation of Abu Lahab (an uncle of the Prophet) and his wife, Umm Jamil. They were among the most bitter and cruel enemies of Islam in its early days, constantly physically and verbally abusing the Prophet (SAW).

### Arabic text, transliteration, and tajwīd
- **Verse 1:** تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ
  - *Transliteration:* Tabbat yadā abī lahabin wa tabb
  - *Tajwīd note:* The letter Bā' at the end of *tabb* has a *shaddah*, requiring a strong, held *qalqalah* when stopping.
- **Verse 2:** مَا أَغْنَى عَنْهُ مَالُهُ وَمَا كَسَبَ
  - *Transliteration:* Mā aghnā 'anhu māluhū wa mā kasab
- **Verse 3:** سَيَصْلَى نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ
  - *Transliteration:* Sayaslā nāran dhāta lahab
- **Verse 4:** وَامْرَأَتُهُ حَمَّالَةَ الْحَطَبِ
  - *Transliteration:* Wamra'atuhū hammālatal-hatab
  - *Tajwīd note:* The Mīm in *hammālata* requires *ghunnah*.
- **Verse 5:** فِي جِيدِهَا حَبْلٌ مِنْ مَسَدٍ
  - *Transliteration:* Fī jīdihā hablum-mim-masad

### Translation
1. The power of Abu Lahab will perish, and he will perish.
2. His wealth and gains will not exempt him.
3. He will be plunged in flaming Fire,
4. And his wife, the wood-carrier,
5. Will have upon her neck a halter of palm-fibre.

### Lessons deduced
- **Wealth cannot prevent divine punishment:** Abu Lahab was a wealthy and influential noble in Makkah, but his wealth (*māluhū*) and status could not save him from Allah's wrath.
- **Lineage does not guarantee salvation:** Despite being the Prophet's blood relative (his uncle), Abu Lahab's disbelief and enmity earned him damnation. Faith and actions matter, not family ties.
- **Consequences of enabling evil:** Abu Lahab's wife supported him in his hatred, throwing thorny bushes in the Prophet's path (hence "wood-carrier"). She shares his punishment.

### Evaluation of teachings
The Surah evaluates human worth strictly on the basis of faith and character rather than wealth, tribe, or royal blood. It stands as a timeless warning that those who actively oppose the truth and oppress believers will face ultimate ruin, no matter how powerful they seem on earth.

## Content l: Surah al-Ikhlās (Q.112)

Surah al-Ikhlās (Sincerity / The Purity of Faith) is a Makkan Surah of four ayāt. It was revealed when the polytheists asked Prophet Muhammad (SAW) to "describe the ancestry of your Lord." This Surah is considered the core summary of Islamic monotheism (Tawhīd) and is equivalent in reward to reciting one-third of the Qur'ān.

### Arabic text, transliteration, and tajwīd
- **Verse 1:** قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
  - *Transliteration:* Qul huwallāhu ahad
- **Verse 2:** اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ
  - *Transliteration:* Allāhus-samad
- **Verse 3:** لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
  - *Transliteration:* Lam yalid wa lam yūlad
- **Verse 4:** وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ
  - *Transliteration:* Wa lam yakul-lahū kufuwan ahad
  - *Tajwīd note:* Every verse ends with the letter Dāl. When stopping at the end of each verse, the Dāl must be pronounced with *qalqalah* (an echoing sound): *ahad*, *samad*, *yūlad*.

### Translation
1. Say: He is Allah, the One!
2. Allah, the eternally Besought of all!
3. He begetteth not nor was begotten.
4. And there is none comparable unto Him.

### Lessons deduced
- **Absolute Oneness (Tawhīd):** Allah is unique and indivisible (*Ahad*). He is not part of a trinity, nor does He share His divinity with any idols.
- **Self-Sufficiency:** Allah is *as-Samad* (the eternally Besought of all, the Self-Sufficient Master). All creations depend on Him, but He depends on nothing.
- **No family or offspring:** Allah has no parents, no children, and no spouse. This explicitly rejects the Christian concept of Jesus as the Son of God, and the pagan Arab belief that angels were daughters of Allah.
- **Incomparability:** Nothing in the universe is equal or comparable to Allah.

### Evaluation of teachings
This Surah purifies the concept of God from all human weaknesses, biological functions, and partnerships. Muslims believe that understanding and reciting this Surah protects a person's faith from the sin of *shirk* (associating partners with Allah), which is the most severely condemned sin in Islam.

## Content m: Surah al-Falaq (Q.113)

Surah al-Falaq (The Daybreak) is a Surah of five ayāt. Along with Surah an-Nās, it is known as *al-Mu'awwidhatayn* (the two verses of seeking refuge). The Prophet (SAW) frequently recited these two suwar for protection.

### Arabic text, transliteration, and tajwīd
- **Verse 1:** قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ
  - *Transliteration:* Qul a'ūdhu birabbil-falaq
- **Verse 2:** مِنْ شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ
  - *Transliteration:* Min sharri mā khalaq
- **Verse 3:** وَمِنْ شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ
  - *Transliteration:* Wa min sharri ghāsiqin idhā waqab
- **Verse 4:** وَمِنْ شَرِّ النَّفَّاثَاتِ فِي الْعُقَدِ
  - *Transliteration:* Wa min sharrin-naffāthāti fīl-'uqad
- **Verse 5:** وَمِنْ شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ
  - *Transliteration:* Wa min sharri hāsidin idhā hasad
  - *Tajwīd note:* Every verse ends with a letter that requires *qalqalah* when stopping: Qāf (*falaq*, *khalaq*), Bā' (*waqab*), and Dāl (*'uqad*, *hasad*).

### Translation
1. Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of the Daybreak
2. From the evil of that which He created;
3. From the evil of the darkness when it is intense,
4. And from the evil of malignant witchcraft,
5. And from the evil of the envier when he envieth.

### Lessons deduced
- **Seeking Allah's protection:** Humans are vulnerable to harms they cannot control. Protection must be sought from Allah, the Lord of the Daybreak.
- **Protection from external evils:** The Surah asks for safety from physical and unseen harms: dangerous creatures, the hidden dangers of the night, practitioners of black magic ("malignant witchcraft," literally those who blow on knots), and the destructive jealousy of envious people.

### Evaluation of teachings
The Surah teaches believers to rely entirely on Allah rather than amulets or superstitions for protection against external harm. It acknowledges the reality of envy (*hasad*) and magic in the world, guiding Muslims to use divine words as their shield.

## Content n: Surah an-Nās (Q.114)

Surah an-Nās (Mankind) is the final chapter of the Qur'ān, consisting of six ayāt. While al-Falaq focuses on external physical and supernatural dangers, an-Nās focuses on internal, spiritual dangers—specifically the whisperings that lead to sin.

### Arabic text, transliteration, and tajwīd
- **Verse 1:** قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ
  - *Transliteration:* Qul a'ūdhu birabbin-nās
- **Verse 2:** مَلِكِ النَّاسِ
  - *Transliteration:* Malikin-nās
- **Verse 3:** إِلَهِ النَّاسِ
  - *Transliteration:* Ilāhin-nās
- **Verse 4:** مِنْ شَرِّ الْوَسْوَاسِ الْخَنَّاسِ
  - *Transliteration:* Min sharril-waswāsil-khannās
- **Verse 5:** الَّذِي يُوَسْوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ النَّاسِ
  - *Transliteration:* Alladhī yuwaswisu fī sudūrin-nās
- **Verse 6:** مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَالنَّاسِ
  - *Transliteration:* Minal-jinnati wan-nās
  - *Tajwīd note:* The Nūn with a *shaddah* in the word *an-nās* (which ends nearly every verse) and *al-jinnati* requires a clear, prolonged nasal sound (*ghunnah*).

### Translation
1. Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind,
2. The King of mankind,
3. The God of mankind,
4. From the evil of the sneaking whisperer,
5. Who whispereth in the hearts of mankind,
6. Of the jinn and of mankind.

### Lessons deduced
- **Allah's supreme authority:** Allah is identified by three majestic titles regarding humanity: their Lord (Creator/Sustainer), their King (Sovereign Ruler), and their God (the only one worthy of worship).
- **The danger of whispers (waswasah):** The greatest spiritual danger is the hidden whispering of evil into the heart, leading a person toward doubt and sin.
- **Sources of evil whispers:** These deceptive suggestions can come from unseen beings (Jinn/Shaytan) or from evil human beings (bad company).

### Evaluation of teachings
The Surah emphasizes that mental and spiritual vigilance is necessary for a believer. It teaches that Shaytan is a "sneaking whisperer" who retreats when Allah's name is mentioned, showing that remembrance of Allah (Dhikr) is the ultimate cure for anxiety, doubt, and temptation.

### Comparing al-Mu'awwidhatayn (al-Falaq and an-Nās)

| Feature | Surah al-Falaq (Q.113) | Surah an-Nās (Q.114) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Type of evil targeted** | **External evils** that harm the body, wealth, or safety | **Internal evils** that harm the soul, faith, and mind |
| **Source of the evil** | Creatures, darkness, magic, envious people | The whispering of Shaytan (jinn) and evil humans |
| **Attributes of Allah used** | Lord of the Daybreak (One who brings light from darkness) | Lord, King, and God of mankind |
| **Primary focus** | Physical protection | Spiritual protection |

## Objective i: Reciting the Arabic texts of the suwar with correct tajwīd
Across the seven suwar studied in this section, several crucial tajwīd rules repeat frequently. To satisfy JAMB requirements, you must recognize these rules when reciting:
- **Qalqalah (Echoing):** Found prominently at the end of verses in al-Ikhlās (ahad, samad), al-Falaq (falaq, khalaq), al-Masad (tabb, kasab), and al-Kawthar (abtar).
- **Ghunnah (Nasalization):** Found in words with a doubled Nūn or Mīm, heavily featured in an-Nās (an-nās, al-jinnati) and al-Masad (hammālata).
- **Madd (Elongation):** Required in words like *jā'a* (an-Nasr) and *yā* (al-Kāfirūn).

## Objective ii: Translating the verses
Translating the Qur'ān requires understanding the historical context (Asbāb al-Nuzūl) to capture the true meaning. Pickthall's translation uses classical English (e.g., "thee", "hymn"), which accurately reflects the majestic tone of the Arabic text. The translations move from comforting the Prophet (al-Kawthar), establishing firm boundaries (al-Kāfirūn), announcing victory (an-Nasr), condemning enemies (al-Masad), defining God (al-Ikhlās), to seeking divine protection (al-Falaq and an-Nās).

## Objective iii: Deducing lessons from the suwar
The overarching lessons deduced from these final seven chapters center on **Tawhīd** (the oneness of Allah). Whether it is directing sacrifice to Him alone (al-Kawthar), refusing to worship idols (al-Kāfirūn), praising Him for victory (an-Nasr), or recognizing His absolute independence (al-Ikhlās), Tawhīd is the core. Additionally, these suwar teach complete reliance on Allah against physical enemies (al-Masad), external harms (al-Falaq), and internal spiritual whispers (an-Nās).

## Objective iv: Evaluating the teachings of the verses
Evaluating these teachings shows they provide a comprehensive guide for Muslim life. They teach resilience in the face of mockery, tolerance and firmness in inter-religious relations, humility during success, the irrelevance of wealth without faith, the purity of monotheism, and the daily necessity of seeking psychological and physical protection through prayer. These teachings remain highly applicable to modern daily life.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Know the translation differences:** JAMB frequently tests translations. Remember that *al-Kawthar* means Abundance, and *al-Falaq* means Daybreak.
- **Confusing al-Falaq and an-Nās:** A very common mistake is mixing up the types of protection. Remember: al-Falaq is for *external* harm (magic, darkness, envy), while an-Nās is for *internal* harm (whispers, bad thoughts).
- **Abu Lahab's identity:** Remember that Surah al-Masad condemns Abu Lahab, who was the Prophet's *uncle*, and his wife, the "wood-carrier."
- **The meaning of al-Ikhlās:** Know that Surah al-Ikhlās rejects both the Christian Trinity (He begetteth not nor was begotten) and polytheism. It is equal to one-third of the Qur'ān.
- **Surah indicating death:** Surah an-Nasr (The Help) is the Surah that indicated the Prophet's mission was complete and his passing was near.
- **No compromise:** "To you your religion, and to me my religion" is the closing verse of Surah al-Kāfirūn, teaching religious boundaries without compromise.

## Quick summary

1. **Surah al-Kawthar:** Comforts the Prophet (SAW) with the promise of Abundance, commanding prayer and sacrifice to Allah alone, and declaring his enemies as the ones truly "cut off."
2. **Surah al-Kāfirūn:** Firmly rejects any compromise in faith with polytheists, establishing clear religious boundaries.
3. **Surah an-Nasr:** Announces divine help and the Conquest of Makkah, teaching humility, praise, and seeking forgiveness during moments of victory.
4. **Surah al-Masad:** Condemns Abu Lahab and his wife to Hellfire, proving that wealth, status, and noble lineage cannot save enemies of Islam.
5. **Surah al-Ikhlās:** The definitive statement of Tawhīd, declaring Allah's oneness, self-sufficiency, lack of offspring, and absolute incomparability.
6. **Surah al-Falaq:** A prayer seeking refuge in Allah from external evils like darkness, magic, and envy.
7. **Surah an-Nās:** A prayer seeking refuge in the Lord, King, and God of mankind from the internal evil of whispers from Jinn and humans.

## Practice questions

**1. Surah al-Kawthar was revealed to comfort the Prophet (SAW) when his enemies called him "abtar". According to Pickthall's translation, "abtar" means:**

- A. A wood-carrier
- B. The eternally besought
- C. Without posterity
- D. The sneaking whisperer

**Answer: C.** "Abtar" refers to someone who is cut off from having male heirs, meaning without posterity or legacy.

**2. Which Surah ends with the verse, "Unto you your religion, and unto me my religion"?**

- A. Surah al-Masad
- B. Surah al-Ikhlās
- C. Surah al-Kāfirūn
- D. Surah an-Nasr

**Answer: C.** Surah al-Kāfirūn ends with this verse, establishing a firm boundary against compromising Islamic monotheism with polytheism.

**3. Surah an-Nasr commands Muslims to do two specific things when victory and success come. What are they?**

- A. Fast and give charity
- B. Praise Allah and seek His forgiveness
- C. Perform Hajj and offer a sacrifice
- D. Fight the disbelievers and take their wealth

**Answer: B.** The Surah says, "Then hymn the praises of thy Lord, and seek forgiveness of Him."

**4. Who is described as the "wood-carrier" in Surah al-Masad?**

- A. The Prophet's aunt
- B. Abu Sufyan's wife
- C. Abu Lahab's wife
- D. Abu Jahl

**Answer: C.** Abu Lahab's wife, Umm Jamil, is condemned as the "wood-carrier" for her actions against the Prophet (SAW).

**5. The phrase "He begetteth not nor was begotten" in Surah al-Ikhlās primarily rejects which concept?**

- A. The idea that Allah can be seen in this world
- B. The belief that Allah has children or parents
- C. The practice of malignant witchcraft
- D. The whispering of the Shaytan

**Answer: B.** It affirms Allah's uniqueness by rejecting the concept that God has biological functions, parents, or offspring (such as the Christian concept of the Trinity).

**6. Which tajwīd rule requires an echoing or bouncing sound, commonly heard at the end of the verses in Surah al-Falaq and Surah al-Ikhlās?**

- A. Ghunnah
- B. Qalqalah
- C. Idghām
- D. Madd

**Answer: B.** Qalqalah is the echoing sound applied to specific letters (like Dāl and Qāf) when they have a sukūn, as seen in *ahad*, *samad*, *falaq*, and *khalaq*.

**7. While Surah al-Falaq seeks protection from external dangers, Surah an-Nās primarily seeks protection from:**

- A. The evil of the envier when he envies
- B. Malignant witchcraft
- C. The internal evil of the sneaking whisperer
- D. The intensity of darkness

**Answer: C.** Surah an-Nās focuses on internal, spiritual dangers, specifically the evil whispers (*waswasah*) placed in the hearts of mankind.

**8. In Surah al-Falaq, protection is sought from the evil of "the envier when he envieth." The Arabic word for envy used in this Surah is:**

- A. Shirk
- B. Hasad
- C. Fath
- D. Jinn

**Answer: B.** The Arabic term for destructive envy or jealousy is *hasad*.

**9. Surah al-Masad demonstrates that in the sight of Allah, noble lineage and wealth:**

- A. Can save a person if they give charity
- B. Are guaranteed signs of Allah's love
- C. Cannot save a person from punishment if they reject faith
- D. Protect a person from the evils of magic

**Answer: C.** Despite Abu Lahab being wealthy and the uncle of the Prophet, his wealth and gains did not exempt him from the Fire due to his disbelief and cruelty.

**10. Which Surah served as a sign to the early Muslims that the Prophet Muhammad's (SAW) earthly mission was complete and his passing was approaching?**

- A. Surah an-Nasr
- B. Surah al-Kawthar
- C. Surah an-Nās
- D. Surah al-Ikhlās

**Answer: A.** Surah an-Nasr, detailing the mass entry of people into Islam after the Conquest of Makkah, was understood by the Companions as an indication that the Prophet's work was done.
