# JAMB Irk Notes: Study of Selected Suwar (al-A'lā to al-'Alaq)

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Part I, Section A, Topic 5 of the JAMB Islamic Religious Knowledge syllabus: Study of the Arabic text of the following suwar/ayats with tajwīd. This file is part one of a split topic and covers the first five short chapters (suwar) in the sequence:

- **a. al-A'lā (Q.87)**
- **b. ad-Duhā (Q.93)**
- **c. al-Inshirāh (Q.94)**
- **d. at-Tīn (Q.95)**
- **e. al-'Alaq (Q.96)**

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. **Recite with correct tajwīd the Arabic texts of the suwar** (Objective i).
2. **Deduce lessons from them** (Objective ii).
3. **Evaluate their teachings** (Objective iii).

**Note on the syllabus wording:** The syllabus uses a special notation for transliteration, writing the Arabic letter *'ayn* (ع) as a small raised `c`. For example, it prints `al-A$^c$alā` and `al-$^c$Alaq`. In these notes, we will use the standard English apostrophe ('), writing them as **al-A'lā** and **al-'Alaq**, which is how you will see them in most textbooks. We will also use the standard spelling *al-Inshirāh* (also known as *ash-Sharh*). The translations provided are from Marmaduke Pickthall's "The Meaning of the Glorious Koran" (1930). 

Other suwar and ayāt required by this section of the syllabus (from al-Qadr to Laqad Jā'akum) are covered in `A5b-suwar-al-qadr-to-selected-ayats.md`.

## Content a: Surah al-A'lā (Q.87)

**Surah al-A'lā** ("The Most High") is the 87th chapter of the Glorious Qur'ān. It contains 19 verses (ayāt) and was revealed in Makkah. 

**Background:** 
This Surah is highly revered, and Prophet Muhammad (SAW) frequently recited it during the Friday congregational prayers (Jumu'ah), as well as in the 'Id prayers and Witr prayers. Its central theme is the glorification of Allah, the Creator who perfected everything, and a reminder to mankind that the Hereafter is far better and more lasting than the temporary life of this world. It concludes by linking its message to the ancient scriptures given to Prophets Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and Mūsā (Moses).

## Content b: Surah ad-Duhā (Q.93)

**Surah ad-Duhā** ("The Morning Hours" or "The Early Hours") is the 93rd chapter, containing 11 verses. It was revealed in Makkah.

**Background:**
There was a period during the early days of Prophet Muhammad's (SAW) mission when the revelation (Wahy) temporarily stopped. This pause is known in Islamic history as *Fatra al-Wahy*. The polytheists of Makkah mocked the Prophet, claiming that his Lord had abandoned him and hated him. This caused the Prophet immense distress. Surah ad-Duhā was revealed to console and reassure him that Allah had neither forsaken him nor hated him, reminding him of Allah's past favors (sheltering him as an orphan, guiding him, and enriching him) and promising a brighter future.

## Content c: Surah al-Inshirāh (Q.94)

**Surah al-Inshirāh** ("The Relief" or "The Solace"), also known as *Surah ash-Sharh*, is the 94th chapter and has 8 verses. It was revealed in Makkah immediately after Surah ad-Duhā.

**Background:**
This Surah continues the theme of comfort for the Prophet (SAW). It speaks of the spiritual expansion of the Prophet's chest (making him ready to receive and bear the heavy burden of revelation), the removal of his distress, and the elevation of his status. It establishes a fundamental Islamic principle: with every hardship comes ease.

## Content d: Surah at-Tīn (Q.95)

**Surah at-Tīn** ("The Fig") is the 95th chapter, containing 8 verses. It is a Makkan Surah.

**Background:**
The Surah begins with four powerful oaths sworn by sacred places and symbols (the fig and olive representing the lands of Prophet 'Īsā/Jesus; Mount Sinai representing Prophet Mūsā/Moses; and the safe city of Makkah representing Prophet Muhammad). The core message focuses on human nature: Allah created mankind in the best physical and spiritual form (*ahsani taqwīm*), but without faith and righteous deeds, humans fall to the lowest of the low.

## Content e: Surah al-'Alaq (Q.96)

**Surah al-'Alaq** ("The Clot") is the 96th chapter, containing 19 verses. It is a Makkan Surah.

**Background:**
The first five verses of this Surah are universally recognized as the **very first revelation** given to Prophet Muhammad (SAW) by Angel Jibrīl (Gabriel) in the Cave of Hirā'. It emphasizes reading, learning, and the origins of human life. The later verses of the Surah were revealed later; they address the arrogance of humans when they feel self-sufficient, specifically referencing Abu Jahl, a leader of the Quraysh who violently tried to stop the Prophet (SAW) from praying at the Ka'bah.

## Objective i: Recite with correct tajwīd the Arabic texts of the suwar

**Important Tajwīd Note:** While these notes provide the Arabic text, transliteration, and translation, proper recitation (*tajwīd*) must be learned directly from a qualified teacher who can correct your pronunciation. Pay attention to rules like *Qalqalah* (the echoing sound on letters ق، ط، ب، ج، د when they have a sukūn), *Madd* (elongation of vowels), and the rules of *Nūn Sākinah* and *Mīm Sākinah*.

Below are the Arabic texts with transliteration and Marmaduke Pickthall's translation.

## Objective ii: Deduce lessons from the suwar

From studying the translations and contexts of these chapters, we can deduce specific moral and spiritual lessons.

### Lessons from Surah al-A'lā
- **Allah's Perfection:** Everything in the universe was created, measured, and guided perfectly by Allah.
- **The Quran's Preservation:** Allah promised Prophet Muhammad (SAW) that He would ensure the Prophet never forgot the Quran, securing its preservation in his heart.
- **The Ultimate Priority:** The worldly life is temporary and deceptive; the Hereafter (*al-ākhirah*) is eternal and far superior.
- **Continuity of the Message:** The core message of Islam (Tawhīd, purification, and prayer) is not new; it was preached in the ancient scrolls given to Prophets Ibrāhīm and Mūsā.

### Lessons from Surah ad-Duhā
- **Allah Never Abandons Believers:** Even when times feel dark or prayers seem unanswered, Allah is present and does not hate or abandon His sincere servants.
- **Hope for the Future:** For the believer, the "latter portion" (the future, and ultimately the Hereafter) will always be better than the difficult past.
- **Gratitude and Social Justice:** Remembering one's past struggles should lead to empathy. Because the Prophet was once an orphan and destitute, Muslims are commanded never to oppress orphans or drive away beggars.
- **Proclaiming Blessings:** Recognizing and speaking about Allah's bounties is a form of gratitude.

### Lessons from Surah al-Inshirāh
- **Ease Follows Hardship:** A fundamental law in Islam is stated twice for emphasis: "Verily, with hardship comes ease." Hardship is never permanent.
- **Spiritual Relief:** Turning to Allah relieves psychological and spiritual burdens.
- **Continuous Effort:** A Muslim must never be idle. When one worldly or religious task is finished, one must immediately exert effort in another, striving to please Allah.

### Lessons from Surah at-Tīn
- **Humanity's Noble Creation:** Human beings are created in the most excellent physical and spiritual mould.
- **The Fall of Mankind:** Despite this noble origin, humans can degrade themselves to the lowest spiritual level ("lowest of the low") through sin, arrogance, and disbelief.
- **The Path to Salvation:** The only way to preserve human dignity and avoid this fall is by combining true faith (Imān) with righteous actions (Good deeds).
- **Absolute Justice:** The Day of Judgment is a certainty because Allah is the Most Just of all judges, ensuring that good and evil receive their exact due.

### Lessons from Surah al-'Alaq
- **The Importance of Knowledge:** Islam began with the command to "Read" (*Iqra'*). Seeking knowledge (through reading and writing) is highly valued, and the ability to use the pen is a profound gift from Allah.
- **Human Origins:** Man's physical origin from a humble "clot" (leech-like substance) should prevent arrogance.
- **The Danger of Wealth and Pride:** The illusion of self-sufficiency (feeling wealthy and independent) causes people to rebel against Allah.
- **Truth vs. Falsehood:** No human authority (like Abu Jahl) has the right to stop a servant from worshipping Allah. Oppressors face severe punishment in the Hereafter.
- **Closeness to Allah:** The highest state of nearness to Allah is achieved in a state of prostration (*Sajdah*).

## Objective iii: Evaluate their teachings

To evaluate the teachings of these suwar is to understand their broader impact on Islamic practice and the psychological wellbeing of Muslims.

### Evaluation of Surah al-A'lā
This Surah fundamentally shifts a believer's worldview. By evaluating its teachings, we see that it acts as a constant spiritual compass, pulling the Muslim away from materialism (*"But ye prefer the life of the world"*) toward long-term, eternal success. Its frequent recitation in large gatherings ('Id, Jumu'ah) ensures the community collectively remembers the greatness of Allah and the insignificance of worldly wealth compared to the Hereafter. 

### Evaluation of Surah ad-Duhā
The teachings of ad-Duhā offer profound psychological healing. It evaluates the human tendency toward despair during difficult times and counters it with divine reassurance. Furthermore, it transforms spiritual gratitude into concrete social action: it is not enough to simply feel thankful for Allah's help; one must actively protect the vulnerable—the orphans and the beggars. This establishes a socially responsible society.

### Evaluation of Surah al-Inshirāh
This Surah's teaching that "with hardship comes ease" provides immense resilience to the Muslim community. It evaluates the nature of trials in life, reframing them not as punishments, but as temporary states that are always accompanied by divine relief. The concluding teaching—to continue toiling and striving towards Allah—promotes a dynamic, hardworking attitude where rest is found in worshipping Allah rather than in laziness.

### Evaluation of Surah at-Tīn
Surah at-Tīn provides a comprehensive evaluation of human worth. It teaches that dignity is not guaranteed merely by being born human; rather, humans only retain their high status if they choose to believe and act righteously. This eliminates any sense of racial or tribal superiority, as true value is based on *Imān* and *good works*. It also offers comfort by assuring that Allah, the Most Conclusive of judges, will ultimately ensure justice.

### Evaluation of Surah al-'Alaq
By emphasizing reading and the pen in its opening verses, the teachings of al-'Alaq evaluate ignorance as a flaw and knowledge as a divine blessing. This laid the foundation for the Islamic Golden Age and the strong tradition of education in Islam. Furthermore, its evaluation of human rebellion shows that arrogance usually stems from a false sense of independence (wealth or power). The Surah concludes by placing humility (prostration) as the ultimate remedy for arrogance and the direct path to closeness with Allah.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Surah** | A chapter of the Glorious Qur'ān. |
| **Ayah (pl. Ayāt)** | A verse or sign of the Qur'ān. |
| **Tajwīd** | The set of rules governing the correct pronunciation and recitation of the Qur'ān. |
| **Fatra al-Wahy** | The period when revelation temporarily paused, causing the Prophet (SAW) distress (context for Surah ad-Duhā). |
| **Ahsani Taqwīm** | The "best stature" or most excellent mould in which Allah created mankind (Surah at-Tīn). |
| **Iqra'** | "Read" or "Recite"; the very first word of the Qur'ān revealed to Prophet Muhammad (SAW). |
| **Al-Ākhirah** | The Hereafter, which Surah al-A'lā declares is better and more lasting. |
| **Sajdah** | Prostration; the act commanded at the end of Surah al-'Alaq to draw near to Allah. |
| **Suhuf** | Scrolls or scriptures, referring specifically to those given to Ibrāhīm and Mūsā in Surah al-A'lā. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Context matching:** Know exactly which Surah matches which context. E.g., The *pause in revelation* matches **ad-Duhā**. The *first revelation at Cave Hirā'* matches **al-'Alaq**.
- **Identify the Prophets:** Surah **al-A'lā** is the only one in this set that mentions specific ancient prophets by name (Ibrāhīm and Mūsā).
- **Match the Oaths:** If a question asks about the fig, the olive, Mount Sinai, or the safe city of Makkah, the answer is always **at-Tīn**.
- **Translations:** Be able to recognize the translations of key opening phrases, such as *"Alam nashrah laka sadrak"* ("Have We not caused thy bosom to dilate" - al-Inshirāh). 
- **Common mistake:** Students often confuse the teachings of ad-Duhā and al-Inshirāh because both were revealed to comfort the Prophet (SAW). Remember: ad-Duhā commands care for the orphan and beggar; al-Inshirāh contains the famous rule "with hardship comes ease".
- **Spelling variations:** Remember that JAMB might print *al-A'lā* as `al-A$^c$alā` or *al-'Alaq* as `al-$^c$Alaq`. Do not let this confuse you; it is the same chapter.

## Quick summary

1. **Surah al-A'lā (Q.87)** glorifies Allah's perfect creation, reminds mankind that the Hereafter is superior to the worldly life, and notes that this message was also in the scrolls of Ibrāhīm and Mūsā.
2. **Surah ad-Duhā (Q.93)** was revealed to comfort the Prophet (SAW) after a pause in revelation, reminding him of Allah's favors and commanding good treatment of orphans and beggars.
3. **Surah al-Inshirāh (Q.94)** highlights the spiritual relief given to the Prophet (SAW) and establishes the universal principle that ease always accompanies hardship.
4. **Surah at-Tīn (Q.95)** swears by sacred places to assert that humans are created in the best stature but will fall to the lowest state unless they possess faith and do righteous deeds.
5. **Surah al-'Alaq (Q.96)** contains the very first revelation (commanding the pursuit of knowledge) and warns against the arrogance and rebellion of those who feel self-sufficient (wealthy).
6. Reciting these suwar properly requires learning **tajwīd** rules such as Qalqalah and Madd from a qualified teacher.

## Practice questions

**1. Which Surah was revealed to reassure the Prophet (SAW) after a temporary pause in revelation?**
- A. Surah al-A'lā
- B. Surah at-Tīn
- C. Surah ad-Duhā
- D. Surah al-'Alaq

**Answer: C.** Surah ad-Duhā was revealed after *Fatra al-Wahy* (the pause in revelation) to console the Prophet (SAW) and assure him that Allah had not forsaken him.

**2. The scrolls (suhuf) of which two Prophets are explicitly mentioned at the end of Surah al-A'lā?**
- A. Nūh and Ibrāhīm
- B. Ibrāhīm and Mūsā
- C. Mūsā and 'Īsā
- D. Dāwūd and Sulaymān

**Answer: B.** The final verse of Surah al-A'lā is translated as "The Books of Abraham and Moses" (*Suhufi Ibrāhīma wa-Mūsā*).

**3. "Verily, with hardship goeth ease." This famous principle is emphasized in which Surah?**
- A. Surah al-Inshirāh
- B. Surah al-'Alaq
- C. Surah at-Tīn
- D. Surah ad-Duhā

**Answer: A.** Surah al-Inshirāh contains the verse "Fa'inna ma'al-'usri yusrā" (But lo! with hardship goeth ease).

**4. According to Surah at-Tīn, human beings are reduced to "the lowest of the low" EXCEPT those who:**
- A. possess great wealth and status
- B. believe and do good works
- C. perform the pilgrimage
- D. memorize the Qur'ān

**Answer: B.** Verse 6 states, "Save those who believe and do good works, and theirs is a reward unfailing" (*Illal-ladhīna āmanū wa'amilūs-sālihāti*).

**5. The very first word of the Qur'ān revealed to Prophet Muhammad (SAW) was:**
- A. Qul
- B. Al-Hamdu
- C. Iqra'
- D. Sabbihi

**Answer: C.** "Iqra'" (Read) is the first word of Surah al-'Alaq, which was the first revelation given to the Prophet (SAW) at Cave Hirā'.

**6. What moral injunction regarding vulnerable people is explicitly given in Surah ad-Duhā?**
- A. Do not steal from the wayfarer
- B. Do not oppress the orphan or drive away the beggar
- C. Do not cheat orphans in their inheritance
- D. Give zakah to the poor annually

**Answer: B.** Verses 9 and 10 state: "Therefor the orphan oppress not, / Therefor the beggar drive not away."

**7. In Surah at-Tīn, the oath "And by this land made safe" (*Wahādhal-baladil-amīn*) refers to which city?**
- A. Madinah
- B. Jerusalem
- C. Ta'if
- D. Makkah

**Answer: D.** The safe city referenced in the oath is Makkah, the sacred sanctuary.

**8. According to Surah al-'Alaq, what is the main reason a human being becomes rebellious and arrogant?**
- A. Having a large family
- B. Thinking himself independent and self-sufficient
- C. Lacking education
- D. Living in a safe city

**Answer: B.** Verses 6-7 state: "Nay, but verily man is rebellious / That he thinketh himself independent" (usually referring to wealth and power).

**9. The Arabic phrase *"Sabbihi isma rabbikal-a'lā"* means:**
- A. Read in the name of thy Lord
- B. By the morning hours
- C. Praise the name of thy Lord the Most High
- D. Have We not caused thy bosom to dilate

**Answer: C.** This is the first verse of Surah al-A'lā (Q.87) and translates to "Praise the name of thy Lord the Most High".

**10. How does Surah al-'Alaq command the believer to react to the oppressors who try to stop them from praying?**
- A. Fight them with the sword
- B. Ignore them, prostrate, and draw near to Allah
- C. Pay them a tax
- D. Migrate to another land

**Answer: B.** The final verse (19) instructs the Prophet (and believers): "Nay, Obey not thou him. But prostrate thyself, and draw near (unto Allah)."
