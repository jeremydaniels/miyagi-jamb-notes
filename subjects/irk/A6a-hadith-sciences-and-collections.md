## History of Hadīth literature

**Objective 1: evaluate the history of Hadīth from the time of the Prophet (SAW) to the period of six authentic collectors.**

A **Hadīth** (plural: Ahādīth) is a record of the sayings, actions, and silent approvals of Prophet Muhammad (SAW). The history of how these were collected spans several generations.

### 1. The Time of the Prophet (SAW)
During the Prophet's lifetime, the primary focus was on preserving the Qur'an. Initially, the Prophet (SAW) forbade the writing down of his own words. He said: "Do not write down anything from me except the Qur'an. Whoever has written anything from me other than the Qur'an, let him erase it." (Sahih Muslim, numbering varies by edition). This was to prevent any confusion or mixing of the Qur'an with his own sayings. 

However, as Muslims learned to distinguish the Qur'an from Hadīth, the Prophet later permitted certain companions to write down his sayings. For example, Abdullah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As was given permission and compiled a personal collection called *As-Sahīfah As-Sādiqah*. Despite this, Hadīth was mostly preserved through **oral memorisation** and practical application in the daily lives of the Companions.

### 2. The Period of the Companions (Sahābah)
After the Prophet's death, the Companions were extremely careful about narrating Hadīth. Caliphs like Abu Bakr and 'Umar ibn al-Khattāb were very strict; they sometimes demanded witnesses before accepting a Hadīth to ensure nobody accidentally attributed a lie to the Prophet. 

During the political unrest (Fitnah) that occurred in the time of 'Uthman and 'Ali, some political and sectarian groups began inventing false ahādīth. In response, the scholars among the Companions began demanding the chain of narrators (*Isnād*), saying: "Name your men to us."

### 3. The Period of the Successors (Tābi'ūn)
As the Companions began passing away and the Islamic empire expanded widely, the need to permanently record Hadīth became urgent to prevent its loss and protect it from forgery. 

At the beginning of the 2nd century AH, the Umayyad Caliph **'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Azīz** issued the first official state command to compile Hadīth. He ordered scholars like Abu Bakr ibn Hazm and Muhammad ibn Shihāb al-Zuhrī to gather and write down all authentic ahādīth. This marked the shift from personal, unofficial collections to formal, structured compilation.

### 4. The Period of the Six Authentic Collectors (3rd Century AH)
The 3rd century AH (roughly the 9th century CE) is known as the **Golden Age of Hadīth**. During this era, scholars separated Hadīth from legal opinions (Fiqh) and Qur'anic commentary (Tafsīr). They travelled extensively across the Muslim world (a practice known as *Rihlah*) to track down narrators. 

They established strict rules for authentication and produced the **Sihāh al-Sittah** (The Six Authentic Books). These six collectors (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, an-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah) rigorously sifted through hundreds of thousands of reports to preserve only what was sound.

## Authentication of Hadīth

To ensure a Hadīth truly came from the Prophet (SAW), scholars developed the science of Hadīth (*'Ulūm al-Hadīth*). A Hadīth has two main parts: the **Isnād** and the **Matn**.

### Isnād (Asma’ur-rijāl)

**Objective 2: analyse the Isnād.**

The **Isnād** (also called *Sanad*) is the **chain of narrators** that leads back to the Prophet (SAW). It is the historical path the text took. For example: "Ahmad narrated to us, who heard from Zayd, who heard from Abu Hurairah, who said that the Messenger of Allah (SAW) said..."

To analyse the Isnād, scholars created a unique science called **'Ilm Asmā'ur-Rijāl** (The Science of the Men/Narrators). This is the detailed study of the biographies of every narrator in the chain. When examining the Isnād, scholars check for two critical qualities in every single person in the chain:
1. **'Adālah (Uprightness/Piety):** The narrator must be a practicing Muslim of excellent moral character. They must be known for truthfulness and never known to have lied, even in a joke.
2. **Dabt (Precision/Memory):** The narrator must have an excellent memory if narrating from memory, or keep flawlessly accurate books if narrating from writing.

If the chain is continuous (no missing links) and every person in the chain passes the tests of *'Adālah* and *Dabt*, the Isnād is considered sound.

### Matn

**Objective 3: analyse the Matn.**

The **Matn** is the actual **text or the message** of the Hadīth. It is the words spoken, the action described, or the approval given by the Prophet (SAW).

A sound Isnād alone is not enough; the text (Matn) itself must also be analysed to ensure it is authentic. Scholars examine the Matn against the following criteria:
1. It must not contradict the **Glorious Qur'an**. If a Hadīth directly contradicts a clear Qur'anic verse, it is rejected as fabricated.
2. It must not contradict a known, well-established **Sunnah** or another completely verified Hadīth.
3. It must not contradict basic **historical facts** or established human reason (e.g., claiming a major event happened at a time when we know for a fact it did not).
4. The language used must be fitting of the eloquence of the Prophet (SAW). A text with poor Arabic grammar or crude language is rejected as it could not have come from him.

### Classification of Hadīth into Sahīh, Hassan and Da'īf

**Objective 4: distinguish between Hadīth Sahīh, Hassan and da$^c$īf.**

Based on the rigorous analysis of both the Isnād and the Matn, Hadīth scholars classify ahādīth into three main categories of authenticity. 

| Classification | Meaning | Criteria | Usage in Islamic Law |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sahīh** (Authentic) | Sound, perfect | 1. A continuous, unbroken chain of narrators.<br>2. All narrators are deeply pious and truthful.<br>3. All narrators have flawless memory/precision.<br>4. No hidden defects (*'illah*).<br>5. Does not contradict a stronger narration (*shādh*). | Highly authoritative; fully accepted as a source of Islamic law and belief. |
| **Hasan** (Good)<br>*(Syllabus spelling: Hassan)* | Good, acceptable | Has the same conditions as *Sahīh*, EXCEPT one or more narrators in the chain have slightly lesser precision or memory. Their memory is good, but not flawless. | Completely acceptable for Islamic law, though slightly lower in rank than Sahīh. |
| **Da'īf** (Weak)<br>*(Syllabus spelling: da$^c$īf)* | Weak, deficient | Fails to meet the conditions of Sahīh or Hasan. This could be due to a broken chain (a missing narrator), or a narrator who is a known liar, has a bad memory, or makes many mistakes. | Generally rejected for establishing Islamic laws or beliefs. Some scholars allow their use for moral encouragement (fada'il al-a'mal) if the weakness is not severe. |

*(Note: There is a fourth category called **Mawdū'** (Fabricated/Forged), which is a lie falsely attributed to the Prophet. It is completely rejected).*

## The relationship between Hadīth and the Glorious Qur’ān

### The importance of Hadīth

**Objective 5: examine the importance of Hadīth.**

1. **Second Source of Islamic Law:** After the Qur'an, the Hadīth is the second primary source of the Sharī'ah (Islamic Law). The Qur'an commands Muslims to obey the Prophet (SAW): "And whatsoever the messenger giveth you, take it. And whatsoever he forbiddeth, abstain (from it)." (Q.59:7, Pickthall's translation).
2. **Explains the Qur'an (Tafsīr):** The Qur'an often gives general commands, and the Hadīth provides the details. For example, the Qur'an commands Muslims to establish Salāh (prayer), but does not state the number of Rak'ahs or exactly how to bow and prostrate. The Hadīth provides these details.
3. **Demonstrates Practical Application:** The Hadīth shows how the Prophet (SAW) lived out the Qur'anic teachings in his daily life, serving as the perfect role model for Muslims.
4. **Establishes Independent Laws:** The Hadīth sometimes introduces rules on matters that the Qur'an is silent about. For instance, the prohibition of men wearing gold and silk, or eating birds of prey, is established by Hadīth, not the Qur'an.

### Differences between Hadīth and the Glorious Qur’ān

**Objective 6: distinguish between Hadīth and the Glorious Qur’ān.**

Both the Qur'an and authentic Hadīth are forms of divine inspiration (Wahy), but they have distinct differences.

| Feature | The Glorious Qur'an | Hadīth |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin of Wording** | The exact, literal words are from Allah. | The meaning is inspired by Allah, but the wording is from Prophet Muhammad (SAW).* |
| **Recitation in Salāh** | Compulsory to recite during the ritual prayers (Salāh). | Cannot be recited as part of the ritual prayer. |
| **Preservation** | Allah explicitly promised to protect it from any alteration (Q.15:9). It is entirely authentic (Mutawātir). | Preserved through human effort; some ahādīth are Sahīh, some are Hasan, and some are Da'īf. |
| **Format and Style** | Divided into Sūrahs (chapters) and Āyāt (verses) with a unique miraculous style. | Divided into books/chapters based on subject matter (e.g., Book of Zakah, Book of Fasting). |
| **Ritual Purity** | One must be in a state of ritual purity (Wudu/Ghusl) to touch the Arabic text. | Touching a book of Hadīth does not strictly require ritual purity. |

*\*Note on **Hadīth Qudsī** (Sacred Hadīth): This is a special category of Hadīth where the Prophet (SAW) narrates the words of Allah (e.g., "Allah the Exalted says..."). However, unlike the Qur'an, its wording is not miraculous, it is not recited in Salāh, and it is subject to the rules of Isnād classification.*

## The six sound collectors of Hadīth (Sihāh al-Sittah)

**Objective 7: evaluate their biographies and works.**

The most authoritative collections of Hadīth in Sunni Islam were compiled in the 3rd century AH by six great scholars. Their books are collectively known as *al-Kutub as-Sittah* (The Six Books) or *Sihāh al-Sittah* (The Six Authentic Books).

### 1. Imām al-Bukhārī
* **Full Name:** Muhammad ibn Ismā'īl al-Bukhārī
* **Biography:** Born in Bukhara (modern-day Uzbekistan) in 194 AH and died in 256 AH. He lost his sight as a child but miraculously regained it. He began learning Hadīth at age 10 and had an extraordinary photographic memory. He travelled for 16 years gathering over 600,000 ahādīth. 
* **His Work:** *Al-Jāmi' as-Sahīh* (commonly known as **Sahīh al-Bukhārī**).
* **Significance:** It contains about 7,275 ahādīth (including repetitions). He famously performed a ritual bath (Ghusl) and prayed two Rak'ahs before writing down any Hadīth. It is universally considered by Muslims as the most authentic book after the Qur'an.

### 2. Imām Muslim
* **Full Name:** Muslim ibn al-Hajjāj an-Naysābūrī
* **Biography:** Born in Nishapur (modern-day Iran) around 202 AH and died in 261 AH. He was a close student and admirer of Imām Bukhārī. 
* **His Work:** *Al-Jāmi' as-Sahīh* (commonly known as **Sahīh Muslim**).
* **Significance:** It contains about 7,500 ahādīth (including repetitions). His collection is highly praised for its strict authentication and its excellent arrangement, often grouping all versions of a Hadīth in one place without breaking them up into different chapters like Bukhari did.

### 3. Imām Abu Dawūd
* **Full Name:** Sulaymān ibn al-Ash'ath as-Sijistānī
* **Biography:** Born in Sistan (modern-day Iran/Afghanistan) in 202 AH and died in 275 AH. He travelled widely and eventually settled in Basra, Iraq.
* **His Work:** **Sunan Abu Dawūd**.
* **Significance:** He collected about 4,800 ahādīth out of the 500,000 he knew. His work specifically focuses on **legal traditions (Fiqh)**—the ahādīth dealing with laws and rituals, largely omitting those about history or virtues.

### 4. Imām at-Tirmidhī
* **Full Name:** Muhammad ibn 'Īsā at-Tirmidhī
* **Biography:** Born in Termez (modern-day Uzbekistan) in 209 AH and died in 279 AH. He was a prominent student of Imām Bukhārī and became blind in the later years of his life due to excessive weeping out of the fear of Allah.
* **His Work:** *Al-Jāmi'* (commonly known as **Jāmi' at-Tirmidhī** or Sunan at-Tirmidhī).
* **Significance:** It is unique because after quoting a Hadīth, he evaluates its grade (Sahīh, Hasan, or Da'īf) and mentions the legal opinions of different scholars regarding that Hadīth.

### 5. Imām an-Nasa'ī
* **Full Name:** Ahmad ibn Shu'ayb an-Nasa'ī
* **Biography:** Born in Nasa (modern-day Turkmenistan) in 214 AH and died in 303 AH. He settled in Egypt and later moved to Damascus, where he died as a result of being beaten by a mob for refusing to praise Mu'awiyah over 'Ali.
* **His Work:** *As-Sunan as-Sughrā* (commonly known as **Sunan an-Nasa'ī**).
* **Significance:** He had very stringent conditions for accepting narrators, sometimes even stricter than Imām Muslim. His collection contains very few weak ahādīth.

### 6. Imām Ibn Mājah
* **Full Name:** Muhammad ibn Yazīd Ibn Mājah al-Qazwīnī
* **Biography:** Born in Qazvin (modern-day Iran) in 209 AH and died in 273 AH. He travelled across the Islamic world gathering knowledge.
* **His Work:** **Sunan Ibn Mājah**.
* **Significance:** His collection is beautifully organized into chapters. While it is the sixth of the major collections, it contains a slightly higher number of weak ahādīth compared to the other five.

## Muwatta and its author

**Content (e): Muwatta and its author – The biography of Imam Malik and the study of his book.**

### Biography of Imam Malik

**Objective 8: evaluate his biography.**

*   **Full Name:** Mālik ibn Anas ibn Mālik al-Asbahī.
*   **Birth and Life:** He was born in **Madinah** around **93 AH** (approx. 711 CE) and died there in **179 AH** (approx. 795 CE). 
*   **Significance:** Imam Malik was a towering figure in Islamic jurisprudence and Hadīth. He is the founder of the **Maliki School of Law (Madhhab)**, which is the dominant Islamic legal school in North and **West Africa, including Nigeria**.
*   **Attachment to Madinah:** He deeply loved the city of the Prophet (SAW). He rarely left Madinah, never rode an animal inside the city out of respect for the soil where the Prophet (SAW) was buried, and earned the title *Imām Dār al-Hijrah* (The Imam of the City of Migration).
*   **His Teachers and Students:** He learned from great scholars of Madinah and taught many famous students, including Imām ash-Shāfi'ī (founder of the Shafi'i school).
*   **Character:** He was known for his piety, immense dignity, and caution in giving religious verdicts (Fatwa). He famously said "I do not know" to many questions he was asked, showing his fear of answering wrongly.

### Analysis of the Muwatta

**Objective 9: analyse his work.**

The **Muwatta** (Al-Muwatta') is Imām Malik's masterpiece. 

1. **Meaning of the Title:** The word *Muwatta* means **"The Well-Trodden Path"** or "The Approved/Agreed Upon". It indicates that the contents are established practices agreed upon by the scholars of Madinah.
2. **Nature of the Book:** It is one of the earliest surviving books of Hadīth and Fiqh (Islamic Jurisprudence). It is not merely a collection of the Prophet’s sayings; it is heavily focused on establishing the law.
3. **Structure and Contents:** 
   * It is arranged logically according to **Fiqh topics** (e.g., Purification, Prayer, Fasting, Business transactions).
   * It contains **Hadīth of the Prophet (SAW)**.
   * It contains **Athār (sayings and actions of the Companions and Successors)**.
   * Crucially, it documents the **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah** (the living, practical tradition and consensus of the people of Madinah). Imam Malik considered the collective practice of the people of Madinah in his time to be a strong proof of the Prophet's Sunnah, since their forefathers lived directly with the Prophet (SAW).
4. **Historical Importance:** The Caliph al-Mansur asked Imam Malik to make the Muwatta the official state law book for the entire Muslim empire, but Imam Malik humbly refused, acknowledging that other Companions had settled in other regions (like Iraq) and brought valid knowledge with them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hadīth** | A report of the sayings, actions, and silent approvals of Prophet Muhammad (SAW). |
| **Isnād** (Sanad) | The chain of narrators who transmitted the Hadīth. |
| **Matn** | The actual text, words, or core message of the Hadīth. |
| **Asmā'ur-Rijāl** | The science of studying the biographies, memory, and piety of the narrators. |
| **Sahīh** | An authentic, fully sound Hadīth with an unbroken chain of highly reliable narrators. |
| **Hasan** | A good, acceptable Hadīth, slightly lower than Sahīh due to a narrator having good but not flawless memory. (JAMB syllabus spelling: Hassan). |
| **Da'īf** | A weak Hadīth that fails to meet the criteria of Sahīh or Hasan. (JAMB syllabus spelling: da$^c$īf). |
| **Sihāh al-Sittah** | The Six Authentic Books of Hadīth compiled in the 3rd century AH. |
| **Muwatta** | "The Well-Trodden Path", the famous Hadīth and Fiqh book compiled by Imam Malik. |
| **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah** | The established practice of the people of Madinah, used by Imam Malik as a source of law. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Isnād vs Matn:** Never confuse the two. The Isnād is the *chain of people* (the "who"). The Matn is the *actual message* (the "what").
*   **The 3rd Century AH:** Remember that the famous six collectors (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, etc.) lived in the 3rd century AH. This is considered the Golden Age of Hadīth collection.
*   **'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Azīz:** Do not confuse him with the second Caliph, 'Umar ibn al-Khattāb. It was the Umayyad Caliph 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Azīz who gave the first *official state command* to collect Hadīth.
*   **Imam Malik and Nigeria:** Questions often link Imam Malik to West Africa/Nigeria because his Madhhab (Maliki school) is the one practiced by the vast majority of Muslims in the region.
*   **Qur'an vs Hadith differences:** A common JAMB trick is to test whether Hadīth can be recited in Salāh. The answer is always no; only the Glorious Qur'an is recited in the ritual prayer.
*   **Syllabus Spellings:** Be aware if you see "Hassan" or "da$^c$īf" in your exam; treat them as the standard "Hasan" (good) and "Da'īf" (weak).

## Quick summary

1. Hadīth compilation began with oral transmission and personal writings during the Prophet's time, became strictly verified during the Companions' time, was officially compiled during the Successors' time, and perfected by the six major collectors in the 3rd Century AH.
2. A Hadīth consists of an Isnād (the chain of narrators) and a Matn (the text/message itself). 
3. The science of Asmā'ur-Rijāl studies narrators to ensure they possess piety ('Adālah) and excellent memory (Dabt).
4. Based on the Isnād and Matn, a Hadīth is classified as Sahīh (Authentic), Hasan (Good), or Da'īf (Weak).
5. Hadīth is the second source of Islamic law, essential for explaining the Qur'an and establishing practical life rules.
6. The Six Sound Collectors (Sihāh al-Sittah) are Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, an-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah.
7. Imam Malik ibn Anas, the founder of the Maliki school (widely followed in West Africa), lived entirely in Madinah and wrote the Muwatta, a pioneering book combining Hadīth and the established practices of Madinah.

## Practice questions

**1. The historical chain of narrators that traces a Hadīth back to the Prophet (SAW) is known as the**
- A. Matn
- B. Isnād
- C. Sahīh
- D. Tafsīr

**Answer: B.** The Isnād (or Sanad) is the chain of narrators. The Matn is the text itself.

**2. The Umayyad Caliph who issued the first official command for the collection and compilation of Hadīth was**
- A. 'Umar ibn al-Khattāb
- B. Abu Bakr as-Siddīq
- C. 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Azīz
- D. Mu'āwiyah ibn Abi Sufyān

**Answer: C.** 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Azīz (often called Umar II) officially commanded scholars like Ibn Hazm and al-Zuhri to compile Hadīth to prevent its loss.

**3. In the classification of Hadīth, a narration that fails to meet the criteria of Sahīh or Hasan due to a broken chain or a weak narrator is classified as**
- A. Mawdū'
- B. Da'īf
- C. Matn
- D. Mutawātir

**Answer: B.** Da'īf (weak) is the classification for a Hadīth that lacks the conditions of a sound or good narration. 

**4. The science of Asmā'ur-Rijāl in the study of Hadīth primarily deals with**
- A. the grammatical correctness of the Arabic text
- B. checking if the text contradicts the Qur'an
- C. the biographies, memory, and character of the narrators
- D. the application of the Hadīth in Islamic law

**Answer: C.** 'Ilm Asmā'ur-Rijāl literally means the "science of the men" (narrators). It evaluates their piety and memory to determine if they can be trusted.

**5. Which of the following is NOT one of the Six Sound Collectors (Sihāh al-Sittah) of Hadīth?**
- A. Imām al-Bukhārī
- B. Imām Muslim
- C. Imām Mālik
- D. Imām an-Nasa'ī

**Answer: C.** While Imam Malik's Muwatta is highly respected, the famous "Six Books" compiled in the 3rd century AH are by Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, an-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah.

**6. A major difference between the Glorious Qur'an and the Hadīth is that**
- A. the Qur'an is recited during Salāh, while the Hadīth is not
- B. the Hadīth contains the literal words of Allah, while the Qur'an does not
- C. the Hadīth is divided into Surahs, while the Qur'an is divided into chapters
- D. the Qur'an is the second source of law, while the Hadīth is the first

**Answer: A.** A fundamental difference is that the precise Arabic words of the Qur'an are mandatory for ritual prayer (Salāh), whereas Hadīth cannot be recited as part of the prayer.

**7. The famous book of Hadīth and Fiqh compiled by Imam Malik is called**
- A. Sahīh al-Bukhārī
- B. Jāmi' at-Tirmidhī
- C. Sunan Abu Dawūd
- D. Al-Muwatta

**Answer: D.** Imam Malik compiled Al-Muwatta ("The Well-Trodden Path"), which records ahādīth and the established practice of the people of Madinah.

**8. The text or the actual message containing the words or actions of the Prophet (SAW) is referred to as the**
- A. Isnād
- B. Asmā'ur-Rijāl
- C. Matn
- D. Sunnah

**Answer: C.** The Matn is the core text of the Hadīth, following the Isnād (the chain).

**9. Imām Mālik ibn Anas is highly significant to Muslims in Nigeria and West Africa mainly because**
- A. he migrated to West Africa to teach Islam
- B. his school of law (the Maliki Madhhab) is the dominant one practiced in the region
- C. he wrote his books in a West African language
- D. he was born in Nigeria

**Answer: B.** Imam Malik lived his entire life in Madinah, but his school of jurisprudence (Maliki Madhhab) spread extensively across North and West Africa, becoming the standard in Nigeria.

**10. Which of the following criteria must a Hadīth text (Matn) pass to be considered authentic?**
- A. It must contradict the Glorious Qur'an to provide a new law
- B. It must not contradict basic historical facts or the Glorious Qur'an
- C. It must be recited in the Friday sermon to be valid
- D. It must be written by the Prophet (SAW) himself

**Answer: B.** For a Matn to be sound, it must be completely free of contradictions with the Qur'an, established Sunnah, historical facts, and reason.
