## Study of the Ahādīth from an-Nawawī's collection (Content f)

Imam Yahyā ibn Sharaf an-Nawawī compiled a famous book known as *Al-Arba'ūn an-Nawawiyyah* (an-Nawawī's Forty Hadīth). Although called "Forty", most standard editions contain 42 ahādīth. He selected these specific sayings of Prophet Muhammad (SAW) because each one represents a fundamental principle, rule, or cornerstone of the Islamic faith. The JAMB syllabus requires you to study twenty specific ahādīth from this collection, understand their Arabic texts, interpret their meanings, and apply their lessons to daily life.

[IMAGE: A classical Islamic manuscript open on a wooden stand (Rihal), showing elegant Arabic calligraphy of "Al-Arba'un an-Nawawiyyah" on the right page, with a subtle glowing background. Clean flat textbook illustration, white background.]

Because the syllabus focuses heavily on both interpreting the text and applying it, these notes are divided into two main sections: first, the interpretation of the Arabic texts, and second, their practical application.

## Objective i: Interpreting the Ahādīth in Arabic

As required by the syllabus, below is a study of the meaning of each of the twenty selected ahādīth. For each, a key foundational phrase is given in Arabic with its transliteration and translation, followed by a summary of the hadīth's overall interpretation. Students are reminded that reciting the Arabic text accurately is best learned from a qualified teacher.

### 1. Hadīth 1: Actions and Intentions
- **Arabic:** إِنَّمَا الْأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ
- **Transliteration:** Innamā al-a‘mālu bin-niyyāt.
- **Translation:** "Actions are but by intentions."
- **Interpretation:** This hadīth explains that every deed a person performs is judged by Allah based on the underlying intention (niyyah). The Prophet (SAW) used the example of migration (Hijrah): whoever migrated for the sake of Allah and His Messenger will be rewarded for it, but whoever migrated for worldly gain or to marry someone will only get what they aimed for. Sincerity is the condition for any deed to be accepted.

### 3. Hadīth 3: The Five Pillars of Islam
- **Arabic:** بُنِيَ الْإِسْلَامُ عَلَى خَمْسٍ
- **Transliteration:** Buniya al-islāmu ‘alā khamsin.
- **Translation:** "Islam is built upon five [pillars]."
- **Interpretation:** The Prophet (SAW) metaphorically describes Islam as a building supported by five foundational pillars. These are: testifying that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is His Messenger (Shahādah), establishing the prayer (Salāh), paying the poor-due (Zakāh), performing pilgrimage to the House (Hajj), and fasting the month of Ramadān (Sawm).

### 5. Hadīth 5: Rejection of Innovation (Bid'ah)
- **Arabic:** مَنْ أَحْدَثَ فِي أَمْرِنَا هَذَا مَا لَيْسَ مِنْهُ فَهُوَ رَدٌّ
- **Transliteration:** Man ahdatha fī amrinā hadhā mā laysa minhu fahuwa radd.
- **Translation:** "Whoever introduces into this matter of ours that which is not of it, it is rejected."
- **Interpretation:** This is a core principle guarding the purity of Islam. It warns against introducing any new, invented practices (Bid'ah) into the religion that have no basis in the Qur'ān or the Sunnah. Any act of worship that contradicts or adds to what the Prophet (SAW) taught will not be accepted by Allah.

### 6. Hadīth 6: The Lawful, the Unlawful, and Doubtful Matters
- **Arabic:** إِنَّ الْحَلَالَ بَيِّنٌ، وَإِنَّ الْحَرَامَ بَيِّنٌ، وَبَيْنَهُمَا مُشْتَبِهَاتٌ
- **Transliteration:** Inna al-halāla bayyinun, wa inna al-harāma bayyinun, wa baynahumā mushtabihātun.
- **Translation:** "Truly, what is lawful (halāl) is clear, and what is unlawful (harām) is clear, and between the two are doubtful matters."
- **Interpretation:** The Prophet (SAW) explains that things which are clearly permitted and clearly forbidden are well known. However, there are grey areas (mushtabihāt). A believer should avoid these doubtful matters to protect their faith and honour. The hadīth concludes by highlighting that the body contains a morsel of flesh—the heart (qalb). If the heart is pure and sound, the whole body acts rightly; if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupted.

### 7. Hadīth 7: Religion is Sincerity (Nasīhah)
- **Arabic:** الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ
- **Transliteration:** Ad-dīnu an-nasīhah.
- **Translation:** "Religion is sincerity (good advice)."
- **Interpretation:** When the Prophet (SAW) said this, the Companions asked, "To whom?" He replied that sincerity is due to Allah (believing in Him properly), to His Book (reciting and following it), to His Messenger (obeying him), to the leaders of the Muslims (advising and supporting them in good), and to the common Muslims (guiding them and wishing them well).

### 9. Hadīth 9: Avoiding Prohibitions
- **Arabic:** مَا نَهَيْتُكُمْ عَنْهُ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ، وَمَا أَمَرْتُكُمْ بِهِ فَأْتُوا مِنْهُ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ
- **Transliteration:** Mā nahaytukum ‘anhu fajtanibūhu, wa mā amartukum bihi fa'tū minhu ma-stata‘tum.
- **Translation:** "What I have forbidden you, avoid; and what I have commanded you, do as much of it as you can."
- **Interpretation:** This hadīth establishes that avoiding harām is absolute—there is no compromise. However, performing obligations is tied to a person's capability. The Prophet (SAW) also warned his followers not to ask excessive, unnecessary questions or disagree with their Prophets, as this destroyed previous nations.

### 10. Hadīth 10: Allah Accepts Only What is Pure
- **Arabic:** إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا
- **Transliteration:** Inna Allāha ta‘ālā tayyibun lā yaqbalu illā tayyiban.
- **Translation:** "Verily, Allah the Exalted is pure and accepts only that which is pure."
- **Interpretation:** Allah commands the believers to eat from good, lawful sources just as He commanded the Messengers. The hadīth illustrates a tragic example: a man traveling on a long, difficult journey raises his hands to the sky crying out, "O Lord, O Lord!" but because his food, drink, and clothing were bought with harām money, his prayers (du'ā) are rejected. 

### 11. Hadīth 11: Avoiding Doubt
- **Arabic:** دَعْ مَا يَرِيبُكَ إِلَى مَا لَا يَرِيبُكَ
- **Transliteration:** Da‘ mā yarībuka ilā mā lā yarībuka.
- **Translation:** "Leave that which makes you doubt for that which does not make you doubt."
- **Interpretation:** This provides a simple standard for peace of mind. If a Muslim is unsure whether an action, business deal, or food is lawful or unlawful, the safest and most righteous path is to leave it entirely and choose something about which they have absolute certainty. Truth brings tranquility, while falsehood brings doubt.

### 12. Hadīth 12: Leaving What Does Not Concern You
- **Arabic:** مِنْ حُسْنِ إِسْلَامِ الْمَرْءِ تَرْكُهُ مَا لَا يَعْنِيهِ
- **Transliteration:** Min husni islāmi al-mar'i tarkuhu mā lā ya‘nīhi.
- **Translation:** "Part of the perfection of a person's Islam is his leaving that which does not concern him."
- **Interpretation:** A sign of true, mature faith is minding one's own business. A Muslim should not waste time getting involved in idle talk, gossip, or affairs that do not bring benefit to their worldly life or their Hereafter.

### 13. Hadīth 13: Loving for Your Brother What You Love for Yourself
- **Arabic:** لَا يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى يُحِبَّ لِأَخِيهِ مَا يُحِبُّ لِنَفْسِهِ
- **Transliteration:** Lā yu'minu ahadukum hattā yuhibba li-akhīhi mā yuhibbu li-nafsihi.
- **Translation:** "None of you [truly] believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself."
- **Interpretation:** Faith is incomplete if a person is selfish. A true believer wishes the same blessings, success, and guidance for their fellow Muslims (and humanity in general) that they wish to have for themselves, and they hate for others what they hate for themselves.

### 15. Hadīth 15: Good Speech and Hospitality
- **Arabic:** مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ فَلْيَقُلْ خَيْرًا أَوْ لِيَصْمُتْ
- **Transliteration:** Man kāna yu'minu billāhi wal-yawmi al-ākhiri falyaqul khayran aw liyasmut.
- **Translation:** "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him speak good or remain silent."
- **Interpretation:** The Prophet (SAW) directly connects faith in the Day of Judgement with three specific social manners: controlling the tongue (speaking beneficially or staying quiet), honouring one's neighbour, and being hospitable and generous to one's guest.

### 16. Hadīth 16: Controlling Anger
- **Arabic:** لَا تَغْضَبْ
- **Transliteration:** Lā taghdab.
- **Translation:** "Do not become angry."
- **Interpretation:** A man came to the Prophet (SAW) asking for a piece of advice he could easily remember and follow. The Prophet (SAW) gave him this short reply. Even when the man repeated his request several times, the Prophet (SAW) kept giving the same answer. It highlights that anger is the root of many sins and destructive actions.

### 18. Hadīth 18: Fear of Allah and Good Character
- **Arabic:** اتَّقِ اللَّهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ
- **Transliteration:** Ittaqi Allāha haythumā kunta.
- **Translation:** "Fear Allah wherever you are."
- **Interpretation:** This hadīth contains three commands: maintain Taqwā (God-consciousness) in private just as in public; if you commit a sin, quickly follow it up with a good deed (like repentance or charity) so that the good deed wipes the sin away; and interact with people using excellent manners and character.

### 19. Hadīth 19: Mindfulness and Trust in Allah
- **Arabic:** احْفَظْ اللَّهَ يَحْفَظْكَ
- **Transliteration:** Ihfaz Allāha yahfazka.
- **Translation:** "Be mindful of Allah, and He will protect you."
- **Interpretation:** Addressed to a young Ibn 'Abbās, this powerful hadīth teaches that if a person guards Allah's commandments, Allah will guard them. It stresses absolute reliance (Tawakkul) on Allah: if you ask, ask only of Allah. It affirms divine decree (Qadar), noting that even if the whole world gathered to harm or benefit a person, they could only do what Allah has already written.

### 21. Hadīth 21: Steadfastness (Istiqāmah)
- **Arabic:** قُلْ آمَنْتُ بِاللَّهِ، ثُمَّ اسْتَقِمْ
- **Transliteration:** Qul āmantu billāhi, thumma istaqim.
- **Translation:** "Say, 'I believe in Allah,' and then remain steadfast."
- **Interpretation:** A Companion asked for a comprehensive statement about Islam so he would never need to ask anyone else. The Prophet (SAW) summarized Islam in two steps: declaring true faith (Imān), and then remaining upright, firm, and steadfast (Istiqāmah) upon the commandments without deviating.

### 22. Hadīth 22: Sufficiency of Obligatory Acts for Paradise
- **Arabic:** أَرَأَيْتَ إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ الْمَكْتُوبَاتِ
- **Transliteration:** Ara'ayta idhā sallaytu al-maktūbāti...
- **Translation:** "Tell me, if I perform the obligatory prayers..."
- **Interpretation:** A man asked the Prophet (SAW): if he only prays the five daily prayers, fasts Ramadān, strictly considers the lawful as lawful and avoids the unlawful, and adds absolutely no voluntary acts to this, will he enter Paradise? The Prophet (SAW) simply replied: "Yes." It shows the core of salvation lies in fulfilling obligations and avoiding sins.

### 25. Hadīth 25: Different Forms of Charity (Sadaqah)
- **Arabic:** إِنَّ بِكُلِّ تَسْبِيحَةٍ صَدَقَةً
- **Transliteration:** Inna bikulli tasbīhatin sadaqah.
- **Translation:** "Indeed, every declaration of the glory of Allah (Subhānallāh) is a charity..."
- **Interpretation:** Poor Companions complained that the wealthy were taking all the rewards because they had money to give in charity. The Prophet (SAW) expanded the definition of Sadaqah. He explained that saying *Subhānallāh*, *Alhamdu lillāh*, *Lā ilāha illallāh*, enjoining good, forbidding evil, and even fulfilling one's lawful desires in marriage are all forms of charity that bring great reward.

### 27. Hadīth 27: Righteousness is Good Character
- **Arabic:** الْبِرُّ حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ
- **Transliteration:** Al-birru husnu al-khuluqi.
- **Translation:** "Righteousness is good character."
- **Interpretation:** The Prophet (SAW) defined righteousness (al-birr) simply as having excellent character. He then defined sin as that which wavers or causes uneasiness in your heart, and which you would hate for people to find out about. It proves that the human conscience is an internal guide.

### 34. Hadīth 34: Forbidding Evil
- **Arabic:** مَنْ رَأَى مِنْكُمْ مُنْكَرًا فَلْيُغَيِّرْهُ بِيَدِهِ
- **Transliteration:** Man ra'ā minkum munkaran falyughayyirhu biyadihi.
- **Translation:** "Whoever among you sees an evil action, let him change it with his hand."
- **Interpretation:** Every Muslim has a duty to stop evil (munkar). If they have the authority and physical ability, they must change it with their hand. If they cannot, they must speak out against it with their tongue. If they cannot even do that out of fear or weakness, they must at least hate it in their heart, which the Prophet (SAW) described as the "weakest of faith".

The middle step is labelled "2. With the Tongue (Speaking out)". The bottom step is labelled "3. With the Heart (Hating it - Weakest of Faith)". Clean flat textbook style, white background.]

### 41. Hadīth 41: Subordinating Desires to Revelation
- **Arabic:** لَا يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى يَكُونَ هَوَاهُ تَبَعًا لِمَا جِئْتُ بِهِ
- **Transliteration:** Lā yu'minu ahadukum hattā yakūna hawāhu taba‘an limā ji'tu bihi.
- **Translation:** "None of you [truly] believes until his desires are subservient to that which I have brought."
- **Interpretation:** True faith requires a person to surrender their personal wishes, cultural opinions, and worldly desires to the laws and teachings brought by Prophet Muhammad (SAW). (Note: While included by an-Nawawī in his collection, scholars note the chain of this specific hadīth is technically weak, but its meaning is completely correct and supported by the Qur'ān).

## Objective ii: Applying the Ahādīth in their daily lives

Understanding the translation of the ahādīth is only the first step. The syllabus requires students to know how to apply these teachings practically in their environment.

1. **Applying Hadīth 1 (Intentions):** Before starting any good deed, whether it is praying in the mosque, studying for your JAMB exams, or helping your parents, correct your intention. Do it for the sake of Allah, not to show off to others (riyā') or to get mere praise.
2. **Applying Hadīth 3 (The Five Pillars):** Build a strong foundation. Prioritise your five daily prayers and fasting before worrying about voluntary acts. A Muslim who ignores obligatory prayers but gives heavy charity has misunderstood the pillars of their faith.
3. **Applying Hadīth 5 (Rejecting Innovation):** In religious worship, stick only to what the Prophet (SAW) practiced. Do not invent new rituals, prayers, or religious festivals assuming they are "good ideas." Islam is already complete.
4. **Applying Hadīth 6 (Lawful and Unlawful):** Stay away from grey areas. If you are offered a business deal but it smells slightly of fraud or interest, walk away. Protecting your honour and faith is more important than taking a risk on something doubtful.
5. **Applying Hadīth 7 (Sincerity):** Be genuine. If a fellow student or community member is making a mistake, pull them aside privately and give them sincere advice (Nasīhah) out of love, not to embarrass them.
6. **Applying Hadīth 9 (Avoiding Prohibitions):** Never compromise on harām—do not drink alcohol, steal, or cheat under any circumstance. However, for obligations, do your best. For example, if you are sick and cannot stand to pray, pray sitting down.
7. **Applying Hadīth 10 (Pure Earnings):** Ensure your source of income is clean. Wealth from cybercrime (such as "419"), bribery in government offices, or cheating in the market is impure. Giving charity from stolen money is rejected by Allah, and it blocks your prayers from being answered.
8. **Applying Hadīth 11 (Leaving Doubt):** If you hear a rumour and doubt whether it is true or backbiting, stop talking about it. Choose the path of certainty and peace of mind over anxiety.
9. **Applying Hadīth 12 (Minding Your Business):** Avoid scrolling endlessly through social media to gossip about the private lives of celebrities or neighbours. Leave alone what does not concern you and focus on your own self-improvement.
10. **Applying Hadīth 13 (Brotherhood):** Remove envy from your heart. If your classmate gets a higher score or a scholarship, be happy for them, just as you would be happy for yourself. Treat others the way you wish to be treated.
11. **Applying Hadīth 15 (Speech and Hospitality):** Think before you speak; if you have nothing positive or useful to say, stay quiet. Show good manners in the community by sharing food with your neighbours and warmly welcoming visitors to your home.
12. **Applying Hadīth 16 (Controlling Anger):** When provoked, do not react immediately. Walk away, say *A'ūdhu billāhi minash-shaytānir-rajīm* (I seek refuge in Allah from Satan), or perform ablution. Many relationships and lives are destroyed in a few seconds of uncontrollable anger.
13. **Applying Hadīth 18 (Taqwā and Good Character):** Remember that Allah watches you even when you are alone in your room with your phone. If you slip up and commit a sin, immediately follow it with seeking forgiveness and doing a good deed, and always treat people politely.
14. **Applying Hadīth 19 (Trusting Allah):** In difficult times, rely completely on Allah. Do not fear human beings or turn to fortune-tellers, because no one can harm you or benefit you unless Allah has already decreed it.
15. **Applying Hadīth 21 (Steadfastness):** Being a Muslim is a lifelong commitment. Keep your faith firm through hardships and temptations, and stay consistent in your religious duties without giving up.
16. **Applying Hadīth 22 (Focusing on Obligations):** Do not overwhelm yourself or judge others harshly. If a Muslim strictly fulfills the basic obligations and avoids major sins, they are on the path to Paradise. 
17. **Applying Hadīth 25 (Charity for All):** Never feel useless because you have no money to give. Smiling at someone, removing a stone or broken glass from the road, or simply praising Allah are all counted as Sadaqah.
18. **Applying Hadīth 27 (Conscience as a Guide):** When faced with a moral choice, listen to your heart. If an action makes you feel deeply uncomfortable and you would be ashamed if your parents or Imam found out about it, it is a sin—avoid it.
19. **Applying Hadīth 34 (Forbidding Evil):** If you witness exam malpractice or bullying, take action. If you are a prefect, use your authority (hand) to stop it. If you are a student, report it or advise the person (tongue). At the very least, hate the action in your heart and refuse to join in.
20. **Applying Hadīth 41 (Subordinating Desires):** When your personal desire (e.g., wanting to dress immodestly to follow a trend, or wanting to take revenge) clashes with the teaching of the Prophet (SAW), you must force your desire to submit to Islamic rules.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Niyyah | Intention; the sincere motive behind an action, which determines its acceptance |
| Bid'ah | Innovation in religious matters; creating new ways of worship not taught by the Prophet (SAW) |
| Halāl | Lawful, permissible, and pure in Islam |
| Harām | Unlawful, strictly forbidden in Islam |
| Mushtabihāt | Doubtful or unclear matters that lie between the clearly lawful and clearly unlawful |
| Nasīhah | Sincerity, genuine advice, and wishing well for others |
| Tayyib | Pure, wholesome, and lawful (often used regarding wealth and food) |
| Sadaqah | Charity, which broadly includes both giving wealth and performing good deeds |
| Taqwā | God-consciousness, piety, mindfulness, and fear of Allah |
| Munkar | Evil, unacceptable, or forbidden actions that must be changed |
| Istiqāmah | Steadfastness; remaining firm and consistent on the right path |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Match the Hadīth to its number:** JAMB frequently tests if you know the central theme of a numbered hadīth. For example, knowing that **Hadīth 1** is about intention (niyyah), **Hadīth 16** is about anger, and **Hadīth 34** is about changing evil.
- **The "Weakest of Faith":** In Hadīth 34, remember the exact sequence for stopping evil. The highest level is the hand, then the tongue, and the lowest (the weakest of faith) is hating it in the heart. 
- **Doubtful Matters:** Hadīth 6 and Hadīth 11 both discuss doubt. Remember that Hadīth 6 introduces the concept of *Mushtabihāt* (doubtful matters) lying between Halāl and Harām, while Hadīth 11 gives the direct rule to "leave that which makes you doubt".
- **Pure Income:** Hadīth 10 strongly links the acceptance of prayers (du'ā) to earning lawful (tayyib) income. Giving harām money in charity does not make it halāl.
- **Broadening Charity:** Do not assume Sadaqah only means money. Hadīth 25 explicitly states that words of praise (Subhānallāh) and physical acts of goodness are Sadaqah.

## Quick summary

1. An-Nawawī's collection highlights fundamental principles of Islam; the JAMB syllabus focuses on twenty specific ahādīth regarding belief, worship, and social conduct.
2. **Hadīth 1 and 3** establish the core of Islam: actions rely entirely on sincere intention (niyyah), and Islam stands on five obligatory pillars.
3. **Ahādīth 5, 6, 9, 10, and 11** provide rules for worship and consumption: avoid religious innovations (bid'ah), strictly observe the clear halāl and harām, stay away from doubtful matters, and ensure your income is pure so your prayers are accepted.
4. **Ahādīth 7, 13, 15, and 27** focus on social relations and good character: religion is sincerity (nasīhah), true believers love for others what they love for themselves, speak good or remain silent, and recognise that righteousness is essentially good character.
5. **Ahādīth 16, 18, and 19** teach self-control and God-consciousness: avoid anger completely, fear Allah in private and public, and rely only on Allah because He alone protects and decrees fate.
6. **Ahādīth 22 and 25** show that strictly observing obligatory acts guarantees Paradise, but believers can always do more because every good deed—including remembering Allah and enjoining good—counts as charity (sadaqah).
7. **Ahādīth 34 and 41** command action and submission: Muslims must actively forbid evil with their hands, tongue, or heart, and subordinate their personal desires to the teachings of the Prophet (SAW).

## Practice questions

**1. According to Hadīth 1 of an-Nawawī's collection, a person's actions are evaluated by Allah based on their:**
- A. wealth
- B. knowledge
- C. intentions
- D. physical strength

**Answer: C.** The hadīth begins with "Innamā al-a‘mālu bin-niyyāt" (Actions are but by intentions).

**2. "Whoever introduces into this matter of ours that which is not of it, it is rejected." This Hadīth (Hadīth 5) strictly warns Muslims against:**
- A. travelling to non-Muslim lands
- B. innovation in religion (bid'ah)
- C. trading on Fridays
- D. asking unnecessary questions

**Answer: B.** Hadīth 5 guards the purity of Islam by stating that any new, invented religious practice (bid'ah) is rejected by Allah.

**3. According to Hadīth 34, what is described by the Prophet (SAW) as the "weakest of faith" when confronting an evil act?**
- A. Changing it with the hand
- B. Changing it with the tongue
- C. Hating it in the heart
- D. Ignoring it completely

**Answer: C.** If a person cannot physically stop the evil or speak out against it, they must hate it in their heart, which is the weakest level of faith.

**4. The central message of Hadīth 16 (Lā taghdab) is a direct instruction to avoid:**
- A. laziness
- B. greed
- C. anger
- D. pride

**Answer: C.** A man asked for advice and the Prophet (SAW) repeatedly told him, "Do not become angry."

**5. In Hadīth 13, the Prophet (SAW) stated that a person does not truly believe until he loves for his brother:**
- A. more than he loves for himself
- B. what he loves for himself
- C. peace and wealth
- D. the forgiveness of Allah

**Answer: B.** True faith requires empathy; a believer must wish for others the exact same good they wish for themselves.

**6. Hadīth 6 teaches that between the clearly lawful (Halāl) and the clearly unlawful (Harām) are:**
- A. completely forbidden acts
- B. doubtful matters (Mushtabihāt)
- C. obligatory duties
- D. recommended actions

**Answer: B.** The hadīth warns Muslims to avoid doubtful matters (Mushtabihāt) to protect their religion and honour.

**7. According to Hadīth 10, Allah is Tayyib (Pure) and therefore He:**
- A. forgives all sins immediately
- B. accepts only that which is pure
- C. accepts the charity of the rich only
- D. commands fasting every day

**Answer: B.** Because Allah is pure, He only accepts pure and lawful things, meaning charity or prayers fueled by harām income are rejected.

**8. In Hadīth 19, Prophet Muhammad (SAW) advised Ibn 'Abbās: "Be mindful of Allah, and He will..."**
- A. give you immense wealth
- B. make you a leader
- C. protect you
- D. remove your enemies

**Answer: C.** The hadīth states "Ihfaz Allāha yahfazka", meaning if you guard Allah's limits, He will guard and protect you.

**9. Hadīth 25 teaches that saying *Subhānallāh* (Glory be to Allah) is considered as:**
- A. fasting
- B. a minor pilgrimage (Umrah)
- C. charity (Sadaqah)
- D. a pillar of Islam

**Answer: C.** The Prophet (SAW) expanded the meaning of charity (Sadaqah) to include words of praise, showing that even poor people can easily give charity.

**10. What does Hadīth 12 identify as part of the perfection of a person's Islam?**
- A. Waking up for night prayers
- B. Leaving that which does not concern him
- C. Memorising the entire Qur'ān
- D. Fighting in the cause of Allah

**Answer: B.** The hadīth says, "Min husni islāmi al-mar'i tarkuhu mā lā ya‘nīhi", meaning perfection of faith includes minding one's own business.
