# JAMB Irk Notes: The Rightly Guided Caliphs (al-Khulafā'u ar-Rāshidūn)

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C, Topic 17 of the JAMB IRK syllabus: The Rightly Guided Caliphs (al-Khulafa’u rashidun) – the lives and contributions of the four Rightly Guided Caliphs. 

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. **trace the biographies of the four Rightly Guided Caliphs;** (Objective i)
2. **evaluate their contributions to the development of Islam.** (Objective ii)

## Who were the Rightly Guided Caliphs?

After the death of Prophet Muhammad (SAW) in 632 CE (11 AH), the Muslim community needed a leader to guide the state, uphold the law, and protect the faith. The title given to this leader was **Khalīfah** (Caliph), which means "successor" or "deputy". 

The first four successors are known in Islamic history as **al-Khulafā'u ar-Rāshidūn** (The Rightly Guided Caliphs). They were among the closest companions (Sahabah) of the Prophet (SAW) and ruled strictly according to the Glorious Qur'an and the Sunnah. 

In chronological order, they are:
1. **Abu Bakr as-Siddīq** (11–13 AH / 632–634 CE)
2. **'Umar ibn al-Khattāb** (13–23 AH / 634–644 CE)
3. **'Uthmān ibn 'Affān** (23–35 AH / 644–656 CE)
4. **'Alī ibn Abī Tālib** (35–40 AH / 656–661 CE)

Below is a detailed look at the biography and contributions of each Caliph.

---

## 1. Abu Bakr as-Siddīq (RA)

**Objective i: trace the biography of Abu Bakr.**

### Early Life and Conversion
Abu Bakr (Radiyallahu 'anhu - may Allah be pleased with him) was born in Makkah into the Banu Taym clan of the Quraysh tribe. His real name was **'Abdullah ibn Abi Quhafah**. Even during the pre-Islamic period (Jahiliyyah), he was a wealthy, highly respected merchant known for his good character and for never worshipping idols or drinking alcohol. 

He was the closest friend of Prophet Muhammad (SAW) and the **first free adult male to accept Islam**. When the Prophet experienced the Night Journey and Ascension (*al-Isra wal-Mi'raj*), Abu Bakr believed him immediately without hesitation, earning him the title **as-Siddīq** (The Truthful / The Testifier to the Truth). He was the father of 'A'ishah (RA), the Prophet's wife.

### Role During the Prophet's Life
Abu Bakr spent his immense wealth to support Islam, famously buying the freedom of persecuted slaves like Bilal ibn Rabah. He was the Prophet's sole companion during the difficult Hijrah (migration) to Madinah, hiding with him in the Cave of Thawr. When the Prophet (SAW) was fatally ill, he instructed Abu Bakr to lead the Muslims in congregational prayer (Salah), heavily indicating him as a successor.

### Succession and Death
Following the Prophet's death, a brief dispute arose at *Saqifah Bani Sa'idah* between the Ansar (Helpers of Madinah) and the Muhajirun (Emigrants from Makkah) over leadership. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab pledged allegiance to Abu Bakr, and the rest of the community followed. Abu Bakr became the first Caliph. He ruled for only two years and three months. He died of a fever in 634 CE (13 AH) at the age of 63 and was buried next to the Prophet (SAW).

**Objective ii: evaluate the contributions of Abu Bakr.**

### Contributions to Islam
1. **The Riddah Wars (Wars of Apostasy):** Upon the Prophet's death, several Arab tribes rebelled. Some refused to pay Zakah (the obligatory charity), while others followed false prophets like Musaylimah the Liar. Abu Bakr took a firm stand, declaring that he would fight anyone who separated Salah and Zakah. Through a series of brilliant military campaigns led by Khalid ibn al-Walid, he defeated the rebels, preserved the unity of the Islamic state, and secured the Arabian Peninsula.
2. **First Compilation of the Qur'an:** During the Battle of Yamamah against Musaylimah, many Companions who had memorized the Qur'an (*Huffaz*) were killed. Fearing the loss of the Qur'an, 'Umar suggested that it be written down into a single book. Abu Bakr initially hesitated to do what the Prophet had not done, but eventually agreed. He commissioned Zayd ibn Thabit to gather all written fragments and compile them into a single manuscript (*Suhuf*).
3. **Expansion of the Islamic State:** Abu Bakr initiated military campaigns beyond Arabia, sending armies to confront the powerful Byzantine (Roman) Empire in Syria and the Sassanid (Persian) Empire in Iraq, laying the foundation for the massive expansions that followed.

## 2. 'Umar ibn al-Khattāb (RA)

**Objective i: trace the biography of 'Umar.**

### Early Life and Conversion
'Umar ibn al-Khattab belonged to the Banu 'Adi clan of the Quraysh. In his early life, he was a fierce warrior, a wrestler, and a staunch enemy of Islam. The story of his conversion is famous: he set out with his sword to kill the Prophet (SAW) but was redirected to his sister's house, who had secretly become Muslim. Upon hearing her recite the opening verses of Surah Ta-Ha (Q.20), his heart softened, and he embraced Islam.

His conversion brought great strength to the Muslims, allowing them to pray openly at the Ka'bah for the first time. The Prophet (SAW) gave him the title **al-Fārūq** (The Distinguisher between right and wrong). He was the father of Hafsah (RA), another wife of the Prophet.

### Succession and Death
Before Abu Bakr passed away, he consulted senior Companions and nominated 'Umar to succeed him. The community accepted 'Umar unanimously. He ruled for exactly ten and a half years (634–644 CE / 13–23 AH). In 644 CE, while leading the Fajr prayer in the Prophet's Mosque, he was stabbed by a Persian slave named Abu Lu'lu'ah. 'Umar died a few days later and was buried next to Abu Bakr and the Prophet.

**Objective ii: evaluate the contributions of 'Umar.**

### Contributions to Islam
1. **Massive Territorial Expansion:** Under 'Umar, the Islamic empire expanded immensely. The Muslim armies conquered Syria, Egypt, Iraq, Persia, and the holy city of Jerusalem. When Jerusalem surrendered, the Christian patriarch insisted 'Umar come in person; 'Umar traveled with a single servant, sharing his camel, showcasing extreme humility.
2. **Administrative Reforms:** 
   * **The Diwan:** He established a register (*Diwan*) to record the names of soldiers and citizens to distribute pensions and state revenue based on their priority in Islam.
   * **Provinces:** He divided the vast empire into provinces (like Kufa, Basra, Egypt, Syria) and appointed capable governors (*Wali*) to manage them.
   * **Justice System:** He separated the judiciary from the executive, appointing independent judges (*Qadis*) to ensure fair trials.
3. **Establishment of the Hijrah Calendar:** 'Umar created the formal Islamic calendar. He chose the year of the Hijrah (the migration of the Prophet from Makkah to Madinah) as the starting point (Year 1 AH).
4. **Social Welfare and the Baitul-Mal:** He formalized the *Baitul-Mal* (Public Treasury). He was deeply concerned for the poor, famously patrolling the streets of Madinah at night in disguise to find and help anyone in need.

## 3. 'Uthmān ibn 'Affān (RA)

**Objective i: trace the biography of 'Uthman.**

### Early Life and Conversion
'Uthman was born into the wealthy Banu Umayyah clan of the Quraysh. He was a highly successful cloth merchant. Known for his extreme modesty, shyness, and generosity, he was one of the earliest converts to Islam, brought in by Abu Bakr. 

He had the unique honor of marrying two of the Prophet's daughters. He first married Ruqayyah (RA); after she died, the Prophet (SAW) gave him Umm Kulthum (RA) in marriage. Because of this, 'Uthman was given the title **Dhun-Nūrayn** (The Possessor of Two Lights). He migrated twice for the sake of Islam: first to Abyssinia (Ethiopia), and later to Madinah. 

### Succession and Death
Before 'Umar died, he appointed a committee (*Shura*) of six prominent Companions to choose the next Caliph from among themselves. They chose 'Uthman. He ruled for twelve years (644–656 CE / 23–35 AH). His reign started peacefully but ended in turmoil. Discontent in the provinces (especially Egypt and Iraq) led to a rebellion. Rebels besieged his house in Madinah for weeks. Finally, they broke in and assassinated him while he was sitting and reading the Qur'an. He was 82 years old.

**Objective ii: evaluate the contributions of 'Uthman.**

### Contributions to Islam
1. **Standardization of the Qur'an:** This is his greatest contribution. As Islam spread to non-Arab lands, people began reciting the Qur'an in different Arabic dialects, leading to disputes. 'Uthman borrowed the original manuscript compiled under Abu Bakr (kept by Hafsah), and ordered a committee led by Zayd ibn Thabit to make standard copies in the Quraysh dialect. These standardized copies (the **'Uthmani Mushaf**) were sent to major cities, and all other variant copies were ordered to be burned. This ensured the Qur'an remained unchanged and unified the Muslim world. 
   *(Note: This is covered further in `A1b-preservation-of-the-glorious-quran.md`)*.
2. **Creation of the First Islamic Navy:** Realizing the threat from Byzantine warships in the Mediterranean, 'Uthman permitted Mu'awiyah, the governor of Syria, to build the first Muslim naval fleet. This fleet successfully conquered the island of Cyprus and secured the coastlines.
3. **Economic and Infrastructural Growth:** His reign brought immense economic prosperity. He used his personal wealth for the public good, such as buying the well of Ruma for the Muslims in Madinah. He significantly expanded Al-Masjid an-Nabawi in Madinah and Al-Masjid al-Haram in Makkah.
4. **Further Expansion:** The borders of the state were pushed further west into North Africa (Libya/Tunisia) and further east into parts of modern-day Afghanistan and Pakistan.

## 4. 'Alī ibn Abī Tālib (RA)

**Objective i: trace the biography of 'Ali.**

### Early Life and Conversion
'Ali was the son of Abu Talib, the Prophet's uncle who raised Muhammad (SAW) after his grandfather died. 'Ali grew up in the Prophet's household. When the Prophet received the revelation, 'Ali became the **first child to accept Islam** at about ten years of age. He belonged to the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh. 

'Ali was fiercely loyal. On the night of the Hijrah, he risked his life by sleeping in the Prophet's bed to trick the assassins, allowing the Prophet to escape to Madinah. 'Ali married Fatimah (RA), the Prophet’s youngest and most beloved daughter, and they had two sons, Hasan and Husayn. 'Ali was renowned for his unmatched bravery in battle and was given the title **Asadullah** (The Lion of Allah).

### Succession and Death
'Ali became Caliph in 656 CE (35 AH) in an atmosphere of chaos following the murder of 'Uthman. Many demanded immediate justice for 'Uthman’s killers, but 'Ali argued that the state needed stability first before the murderers could be brought to trial. This disagreement led to the tragic First Islamic Civil War (*First Fitna*). 'Ali ruled for almost five turbulent years. In 661 CE (40 AH), he was struck on the head with a poisoned sword by a Kharijite named Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam while heading to the mosque for Fajr prayer in Kufa. He died a few days later.

**Objective ii: evaluate the contributions of 'Ali.**

### Contributions to Islam
1. **Preservation of the State's Integrity:** 'Ali's primary contribution was his determination to keep the Islamic state together during its most severe internal crisis. He faced several rebellions, most notably at the *Battle of the Camel* (against forces led by 'A'ishah, Talhah, and Zubayr) and the *Battle of Siffin* (against Mu'awiyah, the governor of Syria, who refused to pledge allegiance until 'Uthman’s killers were punished). Though it was a time of civil war, 'Ali fought on principle to maintain the rule of law and the legitimacy of the Caliphate.
2. **Transfer of the Capital:** Because of the rebellions in Arabia and Syria, and because most of his supporters were in Iraq, 'Ali moved the capital of the Islamic state from Madinah to **Kufa** (in modern-day Iraq). This was a major strategic shift in Islamic history.
3. **Intellectual and Legal Contributions:** 'Ali was highly regarded for his profound wisdom, eloquence, and knowledge of the Qur'an and Sunnah. He was considered the chief judge of Madinah during the reigns of the previous Caliphs. He laid down the foundational rules for Arabic grammar to help non-Arabs understand the Qur'an properly, and left behind a legacy of wise sayings, sermons, and theological insights.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Khalīfah (Caliph)** | Successor or deputy; the political and religious leader of the Muslim state after Prophet Muhammad (SAW). |
| **al-Khulafā'u ar-Rāshidūn** | The Rightly Guided Caliphs (Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali). |
| **Riddah** | Apostasy. The Riddah Wars were fought by Abu Bakr against tribes that rebelled and refused to pay Zakah. |
| **Diwān** | The official register established by 'Umar to record names for state pensions and military pay. |
| **Baitul-Māl** | The public treasury of the Islamic state, formalized by 'Umar. |
| **Shūra** | Mutual consultation; the council 'Umar set up to choose the next Caliph ('Uthman). |
| **Mushaf** | A physical copy of the Qur'an. The *Uthmani Mushaf* is the standardized version compiled under 'Uthman. |
| **Fitna** | Trial, civil war, or tribulation. The First Fitna occurred during 'Ali's reign. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Who compiled vs. who standardized:** This is the most common JAMB trap. Abu Bakr did the **first compilation** (gathering the loose fragments into one book). 'Uthman did the **standardization** (making official copies in one dialect and destroying the variants). 
*   **Know the Titles:** JAMB often asks questions using only the title. Match them perfectly: Siddiq = Abu Bakr. Faruq = 'Umar. Dhun-Nurayn = 'Uthman. Asadullah = 'Ali.
*   **The Firsts:** 
    *   First free adult male to accept Islam: Abu Bakr.
    *   First child to accept Islam: 'Ali.
    *   First to establish a navy: 'Uthman.
    *   First to establish the Hijrah Calendar: 'Umar.
*   **Capitals:** Madinah was the capital for the first three Caliphs. 'Ali moved the capital to Kufa.
*   **The Apostasy Wars (Riddah):** Always associate the Riddah Wars (and defeating false prophets like Musaylimah) with Abu Bakr.

## Quick summary

1. The Rightly Guided Caliphs (*al-Khulafā'u ar-Rāshidūn*) were Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Uthman, and 'Ali. They led the Muslim community after the Prophet's death.
2. Abu Bakr as-Siddiq ruled for about two years, successfully defeated the apostates during the Riddah Wars, and ordered the first compilation of the Qur'an.
3. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (al-Faruq) ruled for ten years. He vastly expanded the empire and established key administrative structures like the *Diwan*, the *Baitul-Mal*, and the Hijrah calendar.
4. 'Uthman ibn 'Affan (Dhun-Nurayn) ruled for twelve years. He standardized the Qur'an into the *Uthmani Mushaf* to prevent variations in recitation and built the first Islamic naval fleet.
5. 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (Asadullah) was the Prophet’s cousin and son-in-law. His five-year rule was marked by civil war (Fitna), during which he moved the capital to Kufa to preserve the state's integrity.
6. Abu Bakr died of illness, whereas 'Umar, 'Uthman, and 'Ali were all tragically assassinated.

## Practice questions

**1. Who among the Rightly Guided Caliphs is known by the title *al-Fārūq*?**
- A. Abu Bakr
- B. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab
- C. 'Uthman ibn 'Affan
- D. 'Ali ibn Abi Talib

**Answer: B.** 'Umar was given the title *al-Fārūq* (The Distinguisher between right and wrong) by Prophet Muhammad (SAW) after his conversion, which allowed Muslims to pray openly.

**2. The standardization of the Glorious Qur'an into a single uniform dialect to prevent disputes was carried out during the Caliphate of**
- A. Abu Bakr
- B. 'Umar
- C. 'Uthman
- D. 'Ali

**Answer: C.** 'Uthman ordered the creation of standard copies (the Uthmani Mushaf) and sent them to major cities to unite the Muslims on one recitation. Abu Bakr ordered the first compilation, but 'Uthman standardized it.

**3. The Caliph who successfully suppressed the rebellion of the false prophets and those who refused to pay Zakah was**
- A. 'Ali ibn Abi Talib
- B. Abu Bakr as-Siddiq
- C. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab
- D. Mu'awiyah

**Answer: B.** Abu Bakr fought the Riddah (Apostasy) wars to maintain the pillars of Islam, stating he would fight anyone who separated Salah and Zakah.

**4. Which Caliph established the Hijrah calendar and the *Diwan* (state register)?**
- A. Abu Bakr
- B. 'Uthman
- C. 'Ali
- D. 'Umar

**Answer: D.** 'Umar instituted major administrative reforms, including the official Islamic Hijrah calendar and the Diwan for pensions and military logistics.

**5. 'Uthman ibn 'Affan was given the title *Dhun-Nūrayn* (Possessor of Two Lights) because he**
- A. compiled the Qur'an and built the first navy
- B. migrated to both Abyssinia and Madinah
- C. married two daughters of Prophet Muhammad (SAW)
- D. expanded the mosque in Makkah and Madinah

**Answer: C.** He earned the title because he married the Prophet's daughter Ruqayyah, and after she passed away, the Prophet gave him his other daughter, Umm Kulthum, in marriage.

**6. The capital of the Islamic State was moved from Madinah to Kufa during the reign of**
- A. Abu Bakr
- B. 'Umar
- C. 'Uthman
- D. 'Ali

**Answer: D.** 'Ali moved the capital to Kufa in Iraq due to political unrest in Arabia and Syria, and because the majority of his loyal supporters were based there.

**7. Who was the first child to accept Islam?**
- A. Usamah ibn Zayd
- B. Hasan ibn 'Ali
- C. 'Ali ibn Abi Talib
- D. Zayd ibn Harithah

**Answer: C.** 'Ali, who grew up in the Prophet's household, was about ten years old when he embraced Islam, making him the first child to do so.

**8. The first Islamic naval fleet was constructed under the approval of Caliph**
- A. 'Uthman ibn 'Affan
- B. 'Ali ibn Abi Talib
- C. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab
- D. Abu Bakr as-Siddiq

**Answer: A.** 'Uthman permitted Mu'awiyah, the governor of Syria, to build the first Muslim navy to protect the state's coastlines from the Byzantines.

**9. During the Caliphate of 'Ali, the major challenge he faced was**
- A. the threat of the Byzantine Empire
- B. internal civil wars and rebellions
- C. mass starvation in Madinah
- D. the refusal of tribes to pay Zakah

**Answer: B.** 'Ali's reign was marked by the First Fitna (Islamic civil war), including the Battles of the Camel and Siffin, as factions demanded immediate justice for 'Uthman's murder. (Refusal of Zakah was Abu Bakr's challenge).

**10. Which of the following Caliphs died a natural death from illness rather than being assassinated?**
- A. 'Ali
- B. 'Umar
- C. 'Uthman
- D. Abu Bakr

**Answer: D.** Abu Bakr died of a severe fever. 'Umar, 'Uthman, and 'Ali were all assassinated.
