## Theme in Drama

**Objective c.i: determine the theme of any prescribed text.**
**Content c.i: Theme.**

### What is a theme?

A **theme** is the central, underlying idea, message or controlling thought of a literary work. It is the broad truth about life, human nature or society that the playwright wants to communicate to the audience. 

A play can have one **major theme** (the most important idea that runs through the entire play) and several **minor themes** (smaller ideas that appear in specific scenes or sub-plots).

### Theme vs. Subject Matter

Students often confuse the "theme" with the "subject matter". 
*   **Subject matter** is the topic the play is about (e.g., love, war, corruption, marriage). It can usually be stated in one or two words.
*   **Theme** is what the play *says* about that subject matter. It is a complete thought or statement. 

**Example (made up for teaching):**
If a play tells the story of two young people whose families hate each other, but they fall in love and eventually bring their families together:
*   **Subject matter:** Love and family rivalry.
*   **Theme:** "True love has the power to overcome deep-rooted hatred and heal divided communities."

### How to determine the theme of a play

When reading your prescribed texts, you will not usually find the theme written directly on the page. You have to figure it out by looking at the whole play. Use these steps to determine the theme:

1.  **Analyse the main conflict:** What is the biggest problem the protagonist (main character) faces? How is that problem solved? The way the conflict is resolved usually points directly to the theme. If a corrupt king is eventually overthrown and punished, the theme might be "the inevitable downfall of corrupt leaders".
2.  **Look at the title:** Sometimes the playwright hides a clue in the title. 
3.  **Study the protagonist's growth:** How does the main character change from Act 1 to the final Act? What important lesson do they learn? That lesson is often the theme of the play.
4.  **Pay attention to repeated words and symbols:** If characters constantly talk about "honour", "money", or "betrayal", the theme is likely connected to those concepts. 
5.  **Listen to the "chorus" or a wise character:** In many plays, there is an older, wiser character (or a group of people called a chorus) who comments on the action. Their dialogue often directly states the moral message of the play.

## Plot in Drama

**Objective c.ii: identify the plot of the play.**
**Content c.ii: Plot.**

### What is a plot?

The **plot** is the logical sequence of events in a play, linked together by **cause and effect**. It is the carefully organised structure of actions that make up the story. 

### Plot vs. Story

In literature, "story" and "plot" are not exactly the same thing. The famous English writer E. M. Forster explained the difference clearly:
*   **Story** is a simple sequence of events arranged in the order of time: *"The king died, and then the queen died."*
*   **Plot** is a sequence of events linked by cause and effect: *"The king died, and then the queen died of grief."*

When JAMB asks you to identify the plot, they want you to understand *why* one event led to the next.

### The structure of a plot (Freytag's Pyramid)

Most traditional plays follow a standard five-part plot structure. This was mapped out by a 19th-century critic named Gustav Freytag, and is often called **Freytag's Pyramid**. To identify the plot of your prescribed play, try to break the events into these five stages:

1.  **Exposition (Introduction):** This occurs at the very beginning of the play. The playwright introduces the main characters, the setting, and the basic situation. The audience gets the background information they need to understand the story.
2.  **Rising Action:** A specific event (the "inciting incident") triggers a conflict. The protagonist begins to face obstacles and complications. Tension builds up through a series of events, making the audience wonder what will happen next.
3.  **Climax (Turning Point):** This is the peak of the tension and the most intense moment of the play. The protagonist makes a crucial decision or takes an action from which there is no turning back. It is the turning point that determines whether the play will end happily (comedy) or sadly (tragedy).
4.  **Falling Action:** These are the events that happen as a result of the climax. The tension begins to drop, the conflict unravels, and the play moves towards its conclusion. 
5.  **Resolution (Denouement):** This is the end of the play. All the loose ends are tied up, mysteries are explained, and a new normal is established. ("Denouement" is a French word meaning "untying the knot").

### Main plot and Sub-plot

*   **Main plot:** The primary storyline involving the major characters.
*   **Sub-plot:** A secondary, smaller storyline involving minor characters. Sub-plots are often used to contrast with the main plot, add comic relief, or highlight the play's themes.

### How to identify the plot in an exam

If you are given an unseen passage or asked a question about a prescribed text's plot:
*   Look for the **cause-and-effect relationship**. If a character loses their job in Scene 1, and robs a bank in Scene 2, the job loss is the cause, and the robbery is the effect.
*   Ask yourself: Where does this event fit in the pyramid? Is it building tension (Rising Action) or wrapping things up (Resolution)?

## Setting: Spatial and Temporal

**Objective c.iv: identify the spatial setting and temporal period of the play.**
**Content c.iv: Setting.**

The **setting** of a play is the environment in which the action takes place. Because drama is meant to be performed on a stage, the setting must be established clearly for the audience. The syllabus requires you to identify two types of setting: spatial and temporal.

### 1. Spatial Setting (Physical place)

The **spatial setting** refers to the physical, geographical locations where the play occurs. It answers the question: **Where?**
*   It can be a broad geographical area (e.g., Nigeria, Ancient Rome, a fictional kingdom).
*   It can also refer to specific, immediate locations on the stage (e.g., a village square, the king's throne room, a modern office, an uncompleted building).

**Example:** In Wole Soyinka's famous play *The Lion and the Jewel* (used here as a general teaching example), the spatial setting is the rural Yoruba village of Ilujinle, specifically focusing on the village centre and the Bale's palace.

### 2. Temporal Period (Time)

The **temporal setting** refers to the time period during which the play's events happen. It answers the question: **When?**
*   **Historical period:** Is it set in the 19th century, during the pre-colonial era, during the Nigerian Civil War, or in modern times?
*   **Time of day or season:** Is it happening at night, during the dry season, or over a period of ten years?

### How to identify the setting in a play

When reading a play, you cannot rely on a narrator to describe the setting the way a novelist would. Instead, you must look for clues:
1.  **Stage Directions:** These are the instructions written by the playwright (usually in italics or brackets) at the start of an Act or Scene. They often tell the director exactly what the stage should look like. *Example stage direction: [The scene opens in a dimly lit mud hut. It is midnight. A small oil lamp burns in the corner.]* Here, the spatial setting is the mud hut; the temporal setting is midnight.
2.  **Costume and Props:** If the stage directions state that characters are wearing colonial uniforms or carrying spears, this points to a specific historical temporal setting.
3.  **Dialogue and Language:** Characters often mention where they are or what time it is. Furthermore, if they speak in old-fashioned, archaic English (like "thee" and "thou"), the play is likely set in an older temporal period. If they mention smartphones and the internet, it is a contemporary (modern) setting.
4.  **Historical references:** If a character talks about the Aba Women's Riot or the World War, the temporal setting is tied to those real-life historical timelines.

## Socio-political Context and Social Reality

**Objective c.iii: apply the lessons of the play to social reality.**
**Content c.iii: Socio-political context.**

Plays do not exist in a vacuum; they are written by people who are influenced by the world around them. 

### What is Socio-political Context?

**Context** means the background conditions and environment. **Socio-political context** refers to the social and political conditions of the time and place the play was written about (or written in). 

*   **Social context:** This includes the culture, traditions, religion, class divisions, family structures, gender roles, and daily life of the society in the play. 
*   **Political context:** This involves the government, the laws, the distribution of power, leadership, rebellion, wars, and political corruption.

**Example (made up for teaching):** 
Imagine a play written about a poor farmer whose land is forcefully taken by a wealthy, untouchable politician. The *socio-political context* of this play is a society suffering from inequality, a corrupt justice system, and a huge gap between the rich (elite class) and the poor (working class). 

### Applying the lessons to Social Reality

JAMB requires you to not only understand the play as a story, but to connect its themes to **social reality** (real life today). Literature is a mirror that reflects society. Playwrights write to teach a lesson, expose a problem, or suggest a solution for the real world.

To apply a play's lessons to social reality, ask yourself:
1.  **What societal flaw is the playwright exposing?** (e.g., greed, bad leadership, forced marriage, tribalism).
2.  **How do the events in the play resemble events in modern Nigeria, Africa, or the world?** 
3.  **What moral or practical lesson can the audience take home?** 

**Example of applying lessons (using a general African drama context):**
If you read a play where an arrogant ruler ignores the advice of his elders, leading to the destruction of his community, you can apply this to social reality by stating: *"The play serves as a warning to modern political leaders that arrogance, dictatorship, and failure to listen to the masses will ultimately lead to the downfall of both the leader and the nation."*

When studying your prescribed texts (*Marriage of Anansewa* and *Antony and Cleopatra*), pay close attention to how the playwrights treat issues like marriage customs, deceit, political power, and loyalty, and think about how those issues appear in real life today.

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Do not confuse Story with Plot:** If an exam asks for the plot, look for the option that shows *how one event caused another*, not just a random list of events.
*   **Do not confuse Theme with Subject Matter:** If the question asks for the theme, do not pick an option that is just one word (e.g., "Jealousy"). Look for a complete thought (e.g., "The destructive nature of uncontrolled jealousy").
*   **Spatial vs Temporal:** "Spatial" relates to space (where). "Temporal" relates to time (when). This is a very common vocabulary trick in JAMB questions.
*   **Read Stage Directions Carefully:** If you are given a short extract in the exam, the text in the brackets `[like this]` often contains direct answers about the spatial setting and temporal period. Do not skip reading them!
*   **Applying Lessons:** When asked about a play's relevance or social reality, look for the option that connects the play's events to broad human behaviour or modern societal issues (like corruption, justice, or family values).

## Quick summary

1. The **theme** is the central message or universal truth the playwright wants to share, whereas the subject matter is simply what the play is about.
2. The **plot** is the sequence of events driven by cause and effect. 
3. A traditional plot is structured into five stages (Freytag's Pyramid): Exposition, Rising Action, Climax, Falling Action, and Resolution (Denouement).
4. The **spatial setting** is the physical place where the play happens (e.g., a village, a palace).
5. The **temporal setting** is the time period in which the events occur (e.g., the 18th century, midnight).
6. **Socio-political context** refers to the social norms, political systems, and historical background that influence the play.
7. Playwrights use drama to reflect **social reality**, exposing flaws in society and offering moral or political lessons applicable to the real world.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following best defines the theme of a play?**
- A. The physical location where the characters live.
- B. The sequential arrangement of events in the story.
- C. The central underlying idea or message of the work.
- D. The specific historical period in which it was written.

**Answer: C.** The theme is the central message or universal truth that the playwright communicates to the audience.

**2. Which statement correctly distinguishes between "plot" and "story"?**
- A. A story is written in prose, while a plot is written in verse.
- B. A story is a sequence of events in time order, while a plot links events through cause and effect.
- C. A story contains a climax, but a plot only contains rising action.
- D. A story focuses on minor characters, while a plot focuses on the protagonist.

**Answer: B.** As defined by E. M. Forster, a story is chronological (and then, and then), while a plot relies on cause and effect (because of this, that happened).

**3. In the structure of a dramatic plot, the point of highest tension and the major turning point is called the:**
- A. Exposition
- B. Denouement
- C. Climax
- D. Rising action

**Answer: C.** The climax is the peak of Freytag's Pyramid, where the tension is highest and a crucial decision or action takes place.

**4. Read the following stage direction from a made-up play:**
*[The curtain rises to reveal a busy marketplace in Kano. Traders are shouting. It is the height of the dry season, 1982.]*
**What is the spatial setting of this scene?**
- A. 1982
- B. The dry season
- C. A busy marketplace in Kano
- D. The curtain rising

**Answer: C.** The spatial setting refers to the physical location (space), which in this case is the marketplace in Kano. 1982 and the dry season refer to the temporal setting.

**5. "Temporal setting" in literature refers exclusively to:**
- A. The emotional state of the characters.
- B. The geographical location of the play.
- C. The political climate of the society.
- D. The time or historical period of the play.

**Answer: D.** "Temporal" relates to time. It identifies when the events of the play are happening.

**6. If a play explores how an innocent man is thrown into prison because he refused to bribe a corrupt judge, applying this to "social reality" means:**
- A. Recognising that the play is meant to make the audience laugh.
- B. Connecting the play's events to the actual problem of corruption in the modern justice system.
- C. Determining the physical dimensions of the stage.
- D. Identifying the playwright's date of birth.

**Answer: B.** Applying lessons to social reality involves taking the themes and events of the play and connecting them to real-life situations and problems in society.

**7. Which part of the plot structure is responsible for introducing the characters, the background, and the basic situation?**
- A. Exposition
- B. Falling action
- C. Climax
- D. Resolution

**Answer: A.** The exposition is the introductory part of the play where the audience is given the background information needed to understand the story.

**8. The final unravelling of the plot, where conflicts are resolved and mysteries are explained, is known as the:**
- A. Inciting incident
- B. Denouement
- C. Rising action
- D. Exposition

**Answer: B.** The denouement (or resolution) is the final stage of the plot where loose ends are tied up.

**9. Why is it important to understand the socio-political context of a play?**
- A. It helps the director choose the correct lighting equipment.
- B. It reveals the exact amount of money the playwright earned.
- C. It helps the reader understand the cultural and governmental forces influencing the characters' actions.
- D. It determines how many acts and scenes the play will have.

**Answer: C.** Socio-political context gives background on the society's culture, rules, and politics, which explains *why* characters behave the way they do and face certain conflicts.

**10. Read the following summary of a made-up play:**
*Two kings argue over a piece of border land. Their argument escalates into a brutal war that lasts ten years. In the end, both kingdoms are completely destroyed, and the survivors realise the land is now poisoned and useless.*
**Which of the following is the most likely THEME of this play?**
- A. Border disputes and land ownership.
- B. The destruction of two kingdoms.
- C. The futile and self-destructive nature of war and pride.
- D. Ten years of warfare.

**Answer: C.** Options A, B, and D describe the subject matter and the plot events. Option C is a complete thought that extracts a universal, moral lesson (the theme) from the events.
