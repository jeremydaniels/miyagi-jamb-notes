## Textual Analysis

Textual analysis is the method of closely examining a piece of literature to understand its meaning, structure, and the author's intentions. When you are asked to analyse a prescribed prose text, you must look beyond simply reading the story for entertainment. You must break the literary work down into its fundamental elements: its overarching theme, its structured plot, its detailed setting, and the socio-political environment that shapes it. 

### Determining the thematic pre-occupation (Objective c.i)

A **theme** is the central idea, underlying message, or main lesson that a writer explores in a literary work. It is the core meaning behind the story. A novel usually has one major theme (the main idea) and several minor themes (sub-themes). 

When JAMB asks for the **thematic pre-occupation** of a text, they are asking for the dominant, overriding theme that the author focuses on the most. 

#### Differentiating Subject Matter from Theme

Many students confuse "subject matter" with "theme". 
*   **Subject matter** is what the story is generally about (the topic). It can usually be stated in one or two words.
*   **Theme** is what the author is *saying* about that subject matter. It is a complete thought, an opinion, or a universal truth.

| Feature | Subject Matter | Theme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition** | The broad topic or raw material of the text. | The specific message or viewpoint about that topic. |
| **Format** | Usually a single word or short phrase. | Usually a statement or a complex idea. |
| **Examples (made up)** | Love, war, corruption, poverty, education. | "True love requires sacrifice", "War destroys both the victor and the vanquished", "Corruption limits a nation's progress". |

#### How to identify themes in a prescribed text

To find the thematic pre-occupation of a novel in your JAMB exam, look for these clues:
1.  **Repetition:** What ideas, words, or struggles keep coming up chapter after chapter?
2.  **Character Growth:** What is the main lesson the protagonist learns by the end of the story?
3.  **Conflict:** What is the main problem the characters are fighting against? If the hero is fighting a corrupt government, the theme likely revolves around "the destructive nature of political corruption".
4.  **The Title:** Sometimes the title of the book gives a huge clue about the theme. 

### Indicating the plot of the novel (Objective c.ii)

The **plot** is the logical sequence of events in a literary work, linked by cause and effect. It is the roadmap of the story from the beginning to the end.

The famous English writer E.M. Forster explained the difference between a simple story and a plot:
*   **Story:** "The king died and then the queen died." (This is just a timeline of events).
*   **Plot:** "The king died, and then the queen died *of grief*." (This shows cause and effect. Event A caused Event B).

#### The Elements of Plot (Freytag's Pyramid)

Most novels follow a standard plot structure, often visualised as a triangle or pyramid (named after the German writer Gustav Freytag). To fully indicate the plot of any prescribed text, you must break it down into these five stages:

1.  **Exposition (Introduction):** The beginning of the novel. The author introduces the main characters, the setting, and the basic situation. 
2.  **Rising Action:** A conflict or problem arises (the "inciting incident"). The characters take actions to solve the problem, but things get more complicated. Tension builds up.
3.  **Climax:** The turning point or the peak of the tension. It is the most intense moment in the novel, where the main character makes a crucial decision or faces their biggest challenge. The outcome of the conflict is decided here.
4.  **Falling Action:** The events that happen immediately after the climax. The tension begins to drop, and the consequences of the climax are shown.
5.  **Resolution (Denouement):** The conclusion of the story. The conflicts are finally resolved (happily or tragically), loose ends are tied up, and a new normal is established for the characters.

When answering questions about the plot of your prescribed texts, you will need to know the specific sequence of events: what triggered the conflict, what the climax was, and how it all ended.

### Identifying the setting and period/timing (Objective c.iii)

**Setting** is the time and place in which the events of a story occur. It forms the backdrop against which the characters live and act. Setting is extremely important because it influences how characters behave, what they believe, and the rules of the society they live in.

Setting is divided into two main components:

#### 1. Spatial Setting (Place/Physical Setting)
This is the geographical or physical location where the story happens.
*   **Macro-level (large scale):** A continent, a country, or a region (e.g., West Africa, pre-colonial Nigeria, a fictional country in Europe).
*   **Micro-level (small scale):** A specific village, city, street, house, or even a single room (e.g., a university campus in Lagos, a dark prison cell, a crowded market).
*   *How to identify it:* Look for names of cities, descriptions of the landscape, weather, vegetation, or the types of buildings.

#### 2. Temporal Setting (Time/Period)
This is the historical period, time of day, or season when the story takes place.
*   **Historical period:** Is the novel set in the 19th century, during the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970), in the present day, or in the future?
*   **Specific timing:** Does the story take place over a few days, a month, or spanning twenty years? Is it set during the rainy season or the harmattan? 
*   *How to identify it:* Look for dates, historical events mentioned in the background, the type of technology characters use (e.g., mobile phones vs. telegrams), or descriptions of the time of day.

#### The Function of Setting
*   **Creates Mood/Atmosphere:** A story set in a dark, raining, abandoned house will have a scary atmosphere, while one set at a sunny village festival will feel joyful.
*   **Shapes Characters:** A character growing up in a war-torn country in the 1990s will behave very differently from one growing up in a peaceful, modern city today. 
*   **Drives the Plot:** Sometimes the setting itself is the conflict (e.g., characters trying to survive a harsh desert).

### Relating the text to social reality (Objective c.iv)

Literature does not exist in a vacuum. Writers are members of society, and they often write about the world around them. **Socio-political context** refers to the social, cultural, economic, and political environment that surrounds the author, the novel, or the characters.

#### What makes up the Socio-political Context?
*   **Social and Cultural issues:** Traditions, religion, gender roles, marriage customs, class divisions (rich vs. poor), and education.
*   **Political issues:** The type of government (democracy, military rule, monarchy), laws, corruption, colonialism, wars, and human rights abuses.

To understand a novel fully, you must look at the real-world conditions that influenced it. For example, many African novels written in the 1960s focus on the clash between African traditions and Western colonialism. Novels written in the 1980s and 1990s often focus on military dictatorship, corruption, and the disillusionment of the post-independence era.

#### Relating a text to social reality
When JAMB asks you to relate a prescribed text to social reality, you are expected to compare the fictional world of the book with the real world. 
*   **Fictional Mirror:** How does the novel act as a mirror reflecting real-life issues? If a prescribed text is about a young girl denied education because of her gender, this reflects the real-world social reality of gender inequality and female marginalisation in certain societies.
*   **Author's Purpose:** The writer usually highlights these socio-political issues to criticise society (satire), create awareness, or call for change. 
*   **Example (made up for teaching):** Imagine a novel titled *The Empty Treasury*, where a fictional governor steals public funds while the citizens starve. The socio-political context is bad governance and embezzlement. To relate it to social reality, you would identify that the novel mirrors the real-life political corruption and economic hardship experienced in many developing nations.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Theme** | The central idea, underlying message, or main lesson of a literary work. |
| **Subject Matter** | The general topic or raw material of a text, distinct from the theme. |
| **Thematic Pre-occupation** | The dominant or primary theme that an author focuses on in a text. |
| **Plot** | The logical sequence of events in a story, linked by cause and effect. |
| **Exposition** | The introductory stage of the plot where characters and setting are revealed. |
| **Climax** | The turning point and the moment of highest tension in the plot. |
| **Resolution (Denouement)**| The final stage of the plot where conflicts are resolved and the story concludes. |
| **Setting** | The time and place in which the events of a story occur. |
| **Spatial Setting** | The physical or geographical location of a story. |
| **Temporal Setting** | The historical period, time, or season in which a story takes place. |
| **Socio-political context** | The social, cultural, and political environment that influences the text or is reflected within it. |
| **Social Reality** | The real-world societal conditions and issues that a fictional text mirrors or critiques. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

*   **Plot vs. Story:** Remember Forster's rule. A plot must have *cause and effect*. Event A causes Event B. A story is just a chronological list of events.
*   **Theme vs. Subject Matter:** Never pick a one-word answer (like "Love" or "Corruption") if the question asks for the *theme*. "Love" is subject matter. "The destructive nature of blind love" is a theme.
*   **Identify the specific setting:** Pay attention to whether a question asks for the *spatial* setting (place) or the *temporal* setting (time). Do not confuse the two.
*   **Freytag's Pyramid:** Memorise the five stages of a plot: Exposition → Rising Action → Climax → Falling Action → Resolution. JAMB often asks what happens immediately after the climax (falling action) or what introduces the main conflict (rising action).
*   **Relating to Reality:** When asked about social reality, look for the option that connects the events in the book to real-life societal problems (e.g., bad leadership, poverty, cultural clashes).

## Quick summary

1.  **Theme** is the central, underlying message the author wants to convey, while **subject matter** is simply the topic the text is about.
2.  The **thematic pre-occupation** is the main, overriding theme of the novel.
3.  **Plot** is the logical sequence of events linked by cause and effect, not just a timeline.
4.  The standard plot structure contains five stages: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution (denouement).
5.  **Setting** gives a story its backdrop and is divided into **spatial** (physical location) and **temporal** (historical period or time).
6.  **Socio-political context** involves the political climate, cultural norms, and social issues surrounding the novel.
7.  Relating a text to **social reality** means identifying how the fictional events and themes mirror actual conditions, problems, and realities in the real world.

## Practice questions

**1. The sequence of events linked by cause and effect in a novel is referred to as the**

- A. theme
- B. setting
- C. plot
- D. subject matter

**Answer: C.** The plot is the logical arrangement of events where one event causes the next to happen.

**2. Which of the following best defines the theme of a prose text?**

- A. The physical location where the characters live
- B. The central message or underlying idea explored by the author
- C. The sequence of actions from the beginning to the end
- D. The broad topic of the story, such as 'war' or 'education'

**Answer: B.** The theme is the core message or opinion the writer is expressing, whereas the broad topic is the subject matter.

**3. In a novel's plot structure, the point of highest tension and the turning point of the conflict is known as the**

- A. exposition
- B. denouement
- C. rising action
- D. climax

**Answer: D.** The climax is the peak of the action where the main conflict reaches its most intense point and is decided.

**4. A novel is set in Lagos during the Nigerian Civil War in 1968. The year 1968 represents the text's**

- A. temporal setting
- B. spatial setting
- C. thematic pre-occupation
- D. resolution

**Answer: A.** The temporal setting refers to the time, historical period, or season when the story takes place.

**5. Which of the following is an example of a spatial setting?**

- A. The 19th Century
- B. A rainy Wednesday morning
- C. A crowded market in Kano
- D. The era of military dictatorship

**Answer: C.** Spatial setting refers to the physical or geographical place where the events happen. 

**6. When an author uses a fictional story about a corrupt village chief to criticise modern political leaders, the author is relating the text to**

- A. temporal setting
- B. social reality
- C. falling action
- D. narrative point of view

**Answer: B.** Relating a text to social reality means showing how the fictional events mirror or critique real-world societal issues.

**7. The final stage of a plot, where conflicts are resolved and loose ends are tied up, is called the**

- A. climax
- B. denouement
- C. rising action
- D. exposition

**Answer: B.** The denouement (or resolution) is the final part of the plot where matters are concluded.

**8. Which of the following is true about the difference between a plot and a story?**

- A. A story has a climax, but a plot does not.
- B. A plot highlights cause and effect, while a story is merely a chronological sequence of events.
- C. A story must contain social reality, but a plot only contains a setting.
- D. A plot is found in poetry, while a story is found in prose.

**Answer: B.** As E.M. Forster explained, a story says "and then", while a plot says "and therefore" (cause and effect).

**9. The socio-political context of a novel primarily helps the reader to understand**

- A. the number of chapters in the text
- B. the author's use of difficult vocabulary
- C. the real-world cultural, economic, and political issues influencing the text
- D. the specific time of day a character wakes up

**Answer: C.** Socio-political context involves the external societal factors, politics, and culture that shape the novel.

**10. "The harmful effects of unchecked political ambition" is best described as a**

- A. setting
- B. theme
- C. subject matter
- D. plot

**Answer: B.** It is a complete idea conveying a specific message, making it a theme. "Political ambition" alone would be the subject matter.
