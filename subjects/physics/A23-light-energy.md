## Sources of light

Light is a form of electromagnetic energy that stimulates the sensation of sight in our eyes. It allows us to see the world around us. A body that produces or gives out light is called a source of light.

### Natural and artificial sources of light

Sources of light can be grouped into natural and artificial sources depending on whether they occur in nature or are man-made.

| Feature | Natural sources of light | Artificial sources of light |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | Sources of light created by nature, not made by humans. | Sources of light created by human technology and invention. |
| **Examples** | The Sun, stars, lightning, volcanic magma, and some living organisms like fireflies (glow-worms) and certain deep-sea fishes (bioluminescence). | Electric bulbs, fluorescent tubes, candles, kerosene lamps, torches, gas flares, and matches. |

### Luminous and non-luminous objects

Another way to classify objects in terms of light is whether they generate their own light.

| Feature | Luminous objects | Non-luminous objects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | Objects that emit their own light energy. | Objects that do not emit their own light energy. |
| **How we see them** | We see them because light travels directly from them into our eyes. | We see them because they **reflect** light from a luminous source into our eyes. |
| **Examples** | The Sun, stars, a lighted candle, a glowing electric bulb, a firefly. | The Moon, planets, mirrors, books, wood, a wall, and most everyday objects. |

**Textbook variation:** Some textbooks refer to non-luminous objects that reflect light well enough to be seen as **illuminated objects** (for example, the Moon is an illuminated object because it reflects the Sun's light).

## Propagation of light

Light travels in straight lines. This fundamental property is called the **rectilinear propagation of light**. We can observe this when sunlight passes through a small hole in a roof, creating a straight beam of light in a dusty room. The rectilinear propagation of light is responsible for the formation of shadows, eclipses, and how a pin-hole camera works.

### Speed, frequency and wavelength of light

Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave. Like all waves, it has a speed ($v$ or $c$), a frequency ($f$), and a wavelength ($\lambda$).

- **Speed ($v$ or $c$):** The distance light travels in one second. In a vacuum (or air, approximately), the speed of light is a constant $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. It is the fastest speed possible in the universe. When light enters a denser medium like water or glass, its speed decreases.
- **Frequency ($f$):** The number of complete light waves (cycles) produced per second. Its unit is the Hertz ($\text{Hz}$). **Crucial JAMB rule:** The frequency of a light wave depends *only* on the source producing it. When light moves from one medium to another (e.g., from air to glass), its frequency remains constant, even though its speed and wavelength change.
- **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** The distance between two consecutive crests or troughs of the wave. Its unit is the metre ($\text{m}$).

The relationship between these three quantities is given by the wave equation:
$$v = f \lambda$$
For light in a vacuum or air, we usually write:
$$c = f \lambda$$

where:
- $c$ = speed of light in a vacuum ($3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$)
- $f$ = frequency in Hertz ($\text{Hz}$)
- $\lambda$ = wavelength in metres ($\text{m}$)

#### Worked Examples: Wave Equation

**1. Calculate the frequency of red light if its wavelength in a vacuum is $7.5 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$. (Speed of light $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$)**
1. Write the wave equation: $c = f \lambda$.
2. Rearrange to make frequency the subject: $f = \frac{c}{\lambda}$.
3. Substitute the given values: $f = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}}{7.5 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}}$.
4. Calculate the result: $f = \frac{3.0}{7.5} \times 10^{15} = 0.4 \times 10^{15} = 4.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$.
5. The frequency of the red light is $4.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$.

**2. A radio station in Abuja broadcasts electromagnetic waves at a frequency of $90.0\ \text{MHz}$. Calculate the wavelength of the broadcast. (Speed of light $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$)**
1. Convert the frequency to standard SI units. The prefix "M" (Mega) means $10^6$.
   $f = 90.0 \times 10^6\ \text{Hz} = 9.0 \times 10^7\ \text{Hz}$.
2. Write the formula and rearrange for wavelength: $\lambda = \frac{c}{f}$.
3. Substitute the values: $\lambda = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}}{9.0 \times 10^7\ \text{Hz}}$.
4. Calculate: $\lambda = \frac{3.0}{9.0} \times 10^1 = \frac{1}{3} \times 10 = 3.33\ \text{m}$.
5. The wavelength of the radio wave is $3.33\ \text{m}$.

**3. Light travels from air into a rectangular glass block. If the speed of light in the glass is $2.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ and the frequency of the light is $5.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$, what is the wavelength of the light inside the glass?**
1. Recall the rule: Frequency does not change when light enters a new medium. Only speed and wavelength change.
2. Write the wave equation for the new medium: $v = f \lambda$.
3. Substitute the values: $2.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1} = (5.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}) \times \lambda$.
4. Calculate wavelength: $\lambda = \frac{2.0 \times 10^8}{5.0 \times 10^{14}} = 0.4 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m} = 4.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$.
5. The wavelength of the light in the glass is $4.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$.

### Formation of shadows and eclipses

Because light travels in straight lines, it cannot bend around opaque (solid) objects. When an opaque object is placed in the path of light, a dark patch is formed behind it on a screen or the ground. This dark region is a **shadow**.

#### Types of Shadows

The type of shadow formed depends on the size of the light source:
- **Point source of light:** A very tiny source of light. It produces a sharp, completely dark shadow called the **umbra**.
- **Extended source of light:** A larger, wider source of light (like an electric bulb or the Sun). It produces a central completely dark region (the **umbra**) surrounded by a partially dark, fuzzy outer region called the **penumbra**.

#### Eclipses

An eclipse is a natural demonstration of shadow formation on a planetary scale. It occurs when the Sun, the Earth, and the Moon lie on a straight line.

**1. Eclipse of the Sun (Solar Eclipse)**
This happens when the **Moon** comes directly between the **Sun** and the **Earth** ($S - M - E$). The opaque Moon casts its shadow on the Earth.
- People on Earth who are in the Moon's **umbra** experience a **total solar eclipse** (complete darkness during the day).
- People in the **penumbra** experience a **partial solar eclipse** (only part of the Sun is blocked).
- Sometimes, the Moon is farther away from Earth in its orbit, so its umbra cone does not quite reach the Earth's surface. Observers directly underneath see an **annular eclipse**, where a bright ring (annulus) of the Sun is visible around the dark Moon.

[IMAGE: Diagram of a solar eclipse. The bright Sun on the left, the small Moon in the middle, and the Earth on the right in a straight line. Light rays from the top and bottom of the Sun touch the edges of the Moon and form a dark, narrowing cone (umbra) that reaches a tiny spot on the Earth, and a lighter diverging region (penumbra) covering a larger area of the Earth. Labels: "Sun", "Moon", "Earth", "Umbra (Total eclipse)", "Penumbra (Partial eclipse)". Clean flat textbook style, white background.]

**2. Eclipse of the Moon (Lunar Eclipse)**
This happens when the **Earth** comes directly between the **Sun** and the **Moon** ($S - E - M$). The Earth prevents sunlight from reaching the Moon by casting its massive shadow onto it. A total lunar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes entirely into the Earth's umbra.

### The pin-hole camera

A pin-hole camera is a simple device that demonstrates the rectilinear propagation of light. It consists of a light-tight box with a tiny hole (pin-hole) pierced in the centre of one face, and a translucent screen (or photographic film) on the opposite face.

**How it works:**
Light rays from an object travel in straight lines through the pin-hole to form an image on the screen. The rays from the top of the object travel diagonally downwards through the hole to the bottom of the screen, and rays from the bottom of the object travel upwards to the top of the screen.

**Characteristics of the pin-hole camera image:**
- It is **real** (it can be caught on a screen).
- It is **inverted** (upside down).
- The size of the image depends on the distance of the object from the pin-hole ($u$) and the distance of the screen from the pin-hole ($v$).

**What happens if you make the pin-hole larger?**
A large hole acts like many small pin-holes clustered closely together. Each small hole forms its own slightly displaced image. These many images overlap on the screen. As a result, the final combined image becomes **brighter** (because more light enters) but **blurred or fuzzy** (due to the overlapping images).

#### Pin-hole Camera Magnification Formula

Linear magnification ($m$) is the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object. For a pin-hole camera, it is also equal to the ratio of the image distance (the length of the camera box) to the object distance.

$$m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$$

where:
- $h_i$ = height of the image
- $h_o$ = height of the object
- $v$ = distance of the image from the pin-hole (this is exactly the **length of the camera box**)
- $u$ = distance of the object from the pin-hole

#### Worked Examples: Pin-hole Camera

**1. A boy stands $4.0\ \text{m}$ away from a pin-hole camera. The camera box is $20.0\ \text{cm}$ long. Calculate the magnification of the image.**
1. Identify the formula for magnification: $m = \frac{v}{u}$.
2. Ensure both distances are in the same units.
   Object distance, $u = 4.0\ \text{m}$
   Image distance, $v = 20.0\ \text{cm} = 0.20\ \text{m}$
3. Substitute the values: $m = \frac{0.20\ \text{m}}{4.0\ \text{m}}$
4. Calculate: $m = 0.05$.
5. The magnification is $0.05$. (Note: Magnification is a ratio and has no unit).

**2. A pin-hole camera of length $15.0\ \text{cm}$ is used to view a generator that is $3.0\ \text{m}$ away from the pin-hole. If the height of the generator is $1.2\ \text{m}$, what is the height of its image formed on the screen?**
1. Write the formula: $\frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$
2. Rearrange to find the image height: $h_i = h_o \times \frac{v}{u}$
3. Keep units consistent. Let's convert all lengths to metres to be safe:
   $h_o = 1.2\ \text{m}$
   $u = 3.0\ \text{m}$
   $v = 15.0\ \text{cm} = 0.15\ \text{m}$
4. Substitute the values: $h_i = 1.2\ \text{m} \times \frac{0.15\ \text{m}}{3.0\ \text{m}}$
5. Calculate: $h_i = 1.2 \times 0.05 = 0.06\ \text{m}$.
6. The height of the image is $0.06\ \text{m}$ (which is $6.0\ \text{cm}$).

**3. An object is placed $6.0\ \text{m}$ in front of a pin-hole camera and produces an image of height $4.0\ \text{cm}$. If the object is moved closer to a distance of $4.0\ \text{m}$ from the pin-hole, what will be the new height of the image?**
1. This is a common JAMB comparative question. From the formula $\frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$, we can cross-multiply to get: $h_i \times u = h_o \times v$.
2. The height of the object ($h_o$) and the length of the camera ($v$) do not change. Therefore, the product $h_i \times u$ must remain constant.
3. Equate the two situations: $h_{i1} \times u_1 = h_{i2} \times u_2$
4. Substitute the given values (you can leave $h_i$ in $\text{cm}$ and $u$ in $\text{m}$ as long as you use identical units on both sides):
   $4.0\ \text{cm} \times 6.0\ \text{m} = h_{i2} \times 4.0\ \text{m}$
5. Calculate: $24.0 = 4.0 \times h_{i2}$, so $h_{i2} = \frac{24.0}{4.0} = 6.0\ \text{cm}$.
6. The new image height is $6.0\ \text{cm}$. (Notice that moving the object closer made the image larger).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Luminous object | An object that emits its own light energy (e.g., the Sun, a glowing light bulb). |
| Non-luminous object | An object that does not emit its own light but is seen by reflecting light from luminous objects. |
| Rectilinear propagation | The principle that light travels in straight lines. |
| Speed of light ($c$) | The distance light travels in one second; taken as $3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ in a vacuum. |
| Umbra | The region of completely dark shadow formed by an opaque object. |
| Penumbra | The region of partially dark (fuzzy) shadow surrounding the umbra, formed only by extended light sources. |
| Solar eclipse | Occurs when the Moon is directly between the Sun and the Earth, casting a shadow on the Earth. |
| Lunar eclipse | Occurs when the Earth is directly between the Sun and the Moon, casting a shadow on the Moon. |
| Linear magnification ($m$) | The ratio of the image height to the object height, or image distance to object distance. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Mixing up units in magnification calculations:** The most common mistake in pin-hole camera problems is dividing $v$ in centimetres by $u$ in metres without converting them. Always ensure $v$ and $u$ are in the same unit (usually metres) before you divide.
- **Wrong formula for magnification:** Do not invert the magnification formula. It is always image properties divided by object properties: $m = \frac{v}{u}$ and $m = \frac{h_i}{h_o}$. Using $\frac{u}{v}$ is a fatal error.
- **Forgetting that frequency is constant:** When light passes from air into water or glass, its speed and wavelength decrease, but its **frequency remains exactly the same**. JAMB frequently tests this concept!
- **Confusion between solar and lunar eclipses:** Remember the planetary order. Solar eclipse = **S-M-E** (Moon in the middle). Lunar eclipse = **S-E-M** (Earth in the middle).
- **Effect of hole size:** Remember that widening the pin-hole does not make the image sharper; it makes the image brighter but sadly blurred. A tiny pin-hole yields a sharper but dimmer image.

## Quick summary

1. Natural sources of light include the Sun and stars, while artificial sources include light bulbs and candles.
2. Luminous objects emit their own light, while non-luminous objects are seen solely by reflecting light.
3. Light travels in straight lines (rectilinear propagation), which accounts for the formation of shadows, eclipses, and the working of the pin-hole camera.
4. The wave equation for light is $c = f \lambda$. The frequency of light depends only on its source and remains constant when the light enters a different medium.
5. A point source produces only a sharp, dark shadow (umbra), whereas an extended source produces an umbra and a lighter, partial shadow (penumbra).
6. A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon is between the Sun and Earth; a lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth is between the Sun and Moon.
7. The image formed by a pin-hole camera is real and inverted.
8. The magnification of a pin-hole camera is given by $m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$.

## Practice questions

**1. Which of the following is a natural source of light?**
- A. A glowing filament bulb
- B. A kerosene lamp
- C. The moon
- D. A firefly

**Answer: D.** A firefly is a living organism that naturally produces its own light (bioluminescence). The moon is non-luminous, and the bulb and lamp are artificial sources.

**2. An object that does not produce its own light but is seen because it reflects light from other sources is described as**
- A. luminous
- B. non-luminous
- C. transparent
- D. artificial

**Answer: B.** Non-luminous objects (like chairs, walls, or the Moon) do not emit their own light; they only reflect light from luminous sources into our eyes.

**3. An extended source of light is used to illuminate an opaque sphere. The shadow formed on a screen behind the sphere will consist of**
- A. a completely dark region only
- B. a partially dark region only
- C. a completely dark central region surrounded by a partially dark ring
- D. a bright central region surrounded by a dark ring

**Answer: C.** An extended (large) light source casts a shadow with a completely dark centre (the umbra) surrounded by a partially dark outer region (the penumbra).

**4. In a pin-hole camera, what is the effect of making the pin-hole larger?**
- A. The image becomes smaller and sharper
- B. The image becomes larger and sharper
- C. The image becomes brighter but blurred
- D. The image remains unchanged but the magnification increases

**Answer: C.** A larger pin-hole allows more light in (making the image brighter), but it acts like many overlapping pin-holes, causing the final image to become blurred or fuzzy.

**5. A tree $12.0\ \text{m}$ tall is viewed using a pin-hole camera of length $20.0\ \text{cm}$. If the tree is $30.0\ \text{m}$ away from the pin-hole, what is the height of its image?**
- A. $8.0\ \text{mm}$
- B. $8.0\ \text{cm}$
- C. $8.0\ \text{m}$
- D. $18.0\ \text{m}$

**Answer: B.** 
1. Formula: $h_i = h_o \times \frac{v}{u}$
2. Convert all to metres: $h_o = 12.0\ \text{m}$, $u = 30.0\ \text{m}$, $v = 0.20\ \text{m}$
3. Substitute: $h_i = 12.0 \times \frac{0.20}{30.0}$
4. Calculate: $h_i = 12.0 \times \frac{1}{150} = 0.08\ \text{m}$
5. Convert to $\text{cm}$: $0.08\ \text{m} = 8.0\ \text{cm}$.
*Mistake behind Option D:* Using the inverted formula $h_i = h_o \times \frac{u}{v}$ and forgetting to convert $v$ to metres, resulting in $12.0 \times \frac{30.0}{20.0} = 18.0\ \text{m}$.

**6. The speed of light in a vacuum is $3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Calculate the wavelength of yellow light if its frequency is $5.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$.**
- A. $6.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$
- B. $1.7 \times 10^6\ \text{m}$
- C. $1.5 \times 10^{23}\ \text{m}$
- D. $6.0 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}$

**Answer: A.** 
1. Formula: $c = f \lambda \implies \lambda = \frac{c}{f}$
2. Substitute: $\lambda = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{5.0 \times 10^{14}}$
3. Calculate: $\lambda = 0.6 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m} = 6.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$.
*Mistake behind Option C:* Multiplying speed by frequency instead of dividing.

**7. Which of the following statements about the propagation of light is correct?**
- A. The frequency of light changes when it passes from air into water.
- B. Light requires a material medium to travel from one point to another.
- C. A point source of light produces both an umbra and a penumbra.
- D. The frequency of light remains constant when it enters a denser medium like glass.

**Answer: D.** The frequency of a light wave depends only on the source and never changes when moving between media, even though its speed and wavelength change.

**8. A student places an object $5.0\ \text{m}$ in front of a pin-hole camera and obtains an image of height $6.0\ \text{cm}$ on the screen. If the object is moved to a distance of $10.0\ \text{m}$ from the camera, the new height of the image will be**
- A. $3.0\ \text{cm}$
- B. $6.0\ \text{cm}$
- C. $12.0\ \text{cm}$
- D. $24.0\ \text{cm}$

**Answer: A.** 
1. Formula logic: $h_i \times u = \text{constant}$
2. Equate the situations: $h_{i1} \times u_1 = h_{i2} \times u_2$
3. Substitute: $6.0\ \text{cm} \times 5.0\ \text{m} = h_{i2} \times 10.0\ \text{m}$
4. Calculate: $30.0 = 10.0 \times h_{i2} \implies h_{i2} = 3.0\ \text{cm}$.
*Mistake behind Option C:* Assuming direct proportion instead of inverse proportion (thinking that doubling the distance from the camera doubles the image height).

**9. A total solar eclipse is seen by observers situated in the**
- A. penumbra of the Moon's shadow on the Earth
- B. umbra of the Moon's shadow on the Earth
- C. penumbra of the Earth's shadow on the Moon
- D. umbra of the Earth's shadow on the Moon

**Answer: B.** During a solar eclipse, the Moon casts a shadow on the Earth. Observers in the completely dark inner part of this shadow (the umbra) experience a total eclipse.

**10. Calculate the magnification of a pin-hole camera if an object $4.0\ \text{m}$ away produces an image that is $5.0\ \text{cm}$ away from the pin-hole.**
- A. $0.0125$
- B. $0.8$
- C. $1.25$
- D. $80$

**Answer: A.** 
1. Formula: $m = \frac{v}{u}$
2. Convert units: $v = 5.0\ \text{cm} = 0.05\ \text{m}$, $u = 4.0\ \text{m}$
3. Substitute: $m = \frac{0.05}{4.0}$
4. Calculate: $m = 0.0125$.
*Mistake behind Option D:* Calculating $\frac{u}{v}$ correctly using SI units ($\frac{4.0}{0.05} = 80$), which wrongly uses the inverted formula for magnification.
