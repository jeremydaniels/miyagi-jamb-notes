## Define Monochromatic Light

**Objective vi: define monochromatic light.**

The word "monochromatic" comes from "mono" (one) and "chroma" (colour). **Monochromatic light** is light that consists of only a single wavelength (and therefore a single frequency). Because the colour of light depends on its wavelength, monochromatic light has only one pure colour.

A common example is the yellow light emitted by a sodium vapour street lamp, or the pure red light emitted by a red laser pointer. In contrast, white light from the Sun or a torch is **polychromatic** (many colours) because it is a mixture of different wavelengths.

## Dispersion of White Light by a Triangular Prism

**Content item i: dispersion of white light by a triangular Prism.**

When a narrow beam of white light (like sunlight) passes through a triangular glass prism, it splits into a band of seven visible colours. This splitting of white light into its component colours is called **dispersion**. 

The band of colours produced is called a **spectrum**. The colours, in order from the top (least bent) to the bottom (most bent), are Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, and Violet. You can remember this sequence using the acronym **ROYGBIV**.

### Why does dispersion happen?
In a vacuum or in air, all colours of light travel at exactly the same speed ($3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$). However, when light enters a denser material like glass or water, it slows down. 

Crucially, **different colours travel at different speeds in glass**:
- **Red light** travels the fastest in glass. Because it is slowed down the least, it is refracted (bent) the least. It has the lowest refractive index in glass.
- **Violet light** travels the slowest in glass. Because it is slowed down the most, it is refracted the most. It has the highest refractive index in glass.

Because each colour bends by a slightly different amount, they separate from each other as they pass through the prism.

## Production of a Pure Spectrum

**Content item ii: production of pure spectrum.**

When white light is dispersed by a single prism, the colours often overlap at their edges, creating blurred boundaries. This is called an **impure spectrum**. 

To separate the colours completely so that they do not overlap, we produce a **pure spectrum** using an optical instrument called a **spectrometer**. 

The production of a pure spectrum requires four main components:
1. **A narrow slit**: Acts as a very fine point source of white light.
2. **A collimator**: A tube containing a converging lens. The narrow slit is placed exactly at the principal focus of this lens. The collimator lens turns the diverging light from the slit into a **parallel beam** of white light.
3. **A triangular prism**: Because the white light enters the prism as a parallel beam, the rays of each specific colour (e.g., all the red rays) travel parallel to each other inside the prism and do not mix.
4. **A telescope (or focusing lens)**: A second converging lens collects the parallel beams of different colours and brings all rays of the same colour to focus at exactly the same point on a screen.

## Colour Mixing by Addition and Subtraction

**Content item iii: colour mixing by addition and subtraction.**

There are two completely different sets of rules for mixing colours. One applies to **coloured light** (addition), and the other applies to **paints and pigments** (subtraction). You must not confuse them in the exam.

### Identify Primary Colours and Obtain Secondary Colours by Mixing

**Objective i: identify primary colours and obtain secondary colours by mixing.**

**1. Mixing Coloured Light (Addition)**
When you shine different coloured lights onto a white screen, their lights mix together on the screen.
- **Primary colours of light**: **Red, Green, and Blue**. They are called primary because they cannot be produced by mixing any other colours of light, but they can be mixed to produce almost any other colour.
- **Secondary colours of light**: Produced by mixing two primary colours in equal amounts.
  - Red + Green = **Yellow**
  - Red + Blue = **Magenta** (purple-pink)
  - Blue + Green = **Cyan** (blue-green)
- Mixing all three primary colours of light together (Red + Green + Blue) gives **White light**.
- **Complementary colours**: Any two colours of light that add together to produce white light. For example, since Blue + (Red + Green) = White, and Red + Green = Yellow, it means **Blue + Yellow = White**. (Similarly, Red + Cyan = White, and Green + Magenta = White).

**2. Mixing Pigments (Subtraction)**
Paints and pigments create colour by *absorbing* (subtracting) light. A pigment looks blue because it absorbs red and green light, reflecting only blue.
- **Primary pigments**: **Cyan, Magenta, and Yellow**. (Note: In basic art classes, you may have been taught that Red, Yellow, and Blue are primary paints, but in physics, the true subtractive primaries are CMY).
- Mixing all three primary pigments together absorbs all light, giving **Black**.

## Colour of Objects and Colour Filters

**Content item iv: colour of objects and colour filters.**

### Deduce Why Objects Have Colours

**Objective iii: deduce why objects have colours.**

An object has a specific colour because of the way it reflects and absorbs light. When white light shines on an object, the object absorbs certain wavelengths and reflects others into your eyes. 
- A **red shirt** looks red because it reflects red light and absorbs all other colours in the spectrum.
- A **white object** reflects all colours of light equally.
- A **black object** absorbs all colours of light and reflects nothing.

**Objects under coloured light:**
What happens if you look at a green leaf in a dark room using only a pure red light?
The leaf normally looks green because it reflects green light and absorbs other colours (including red). When only red light shines on it, the leaf absorbs the red light. Because there is no green light for it to reflect, no light reaches your eye from the leaf. Therefore, the green leaf appears **black**. 

### Analyse Colours Using Colour Filters

**Objective iv: analyse colours using colour filters.**

A colour filter is a transparent piece of glass or plastic that only allows certain colours of light to pass through it (this is called **transmission**) while it absorbs all other colours.

- A **primary filter** (e.g., a pure blue filter) transmits only blue light and absorbs all others.
- A **secondary filter** (e.g., a yellow filter) transmits yellow light, but it also transmits the primary colours that make up yellow (red and green).

**Filter combinations (Classic JAMB scenario):**
Imagine white light shines through a yellow filter, and then through a cyan filter. What colour emerges?
1. The yellow filter transmits **Red and Green** light (it absorbs Blue).
2. The light hitting the cyan filter is now only Red and Green. 
3. The cyan filter is designed to transmit **Blue and Green** light (it absorbs Red).
4. The cyan filter absorbs the Red light, but it allows the **Green** light to pass through. 
5. The final colour you see is **Green**.

## Rainbow and Formation

**Content item v: rainbow and formation.**

### Understand the Formation of a Rainbow

**Objective ii: understand the formation of rainbow.**

A rainbow is a natural spectrum of sunlight in the sky. It is formed by the dispersion of sunlight by millions of tiny water droplets acting as tiny prisms after a rain shower.

For you to see a rainbow, the Sun must be shining behind you, and the rain droplets must be in front of you. 

**Steps in forming a primary rainbow:**
1. **Refraction and Dispersion**: Sunlight enters the spherical water droplet. As it moves from air (less dense) into water (denser), it slows down and refracts. Because water has a different refractive index for different colours, the white light disperses into the colours of the spectrum.
2. **Total Internal Reflection**: The dispersed light hits the back wall of the water droplet. Because the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle for water, the light undergoes total internal reflection and bounces back towards the front of the droplet.
3. **Refraction**: The light exits the front of the droplet into the air, refracting again and spreading the colours even further apart.

In a **primary rainbow**, Red is seen on the outer edge (top) and Violet on the inner edge (bottom). Sometimes, a fainter **secondary rainbow** is seen outside the primary one; this is caused by light undergoing total internal reflection *twice* inside the droplet, which reverses the colours (Violet on top, Red on bottom).

## The Electromagnetic Spectrum

**Content item b: Electromagnetic spectrum.** (Including description of sources and uses of various types of radiation.)

The **Electromagnetic (EM) Spectrum** is the complete range of all electromagnetic waves, arranged in order of their wavelengths or frequencies.

**Properties common to all electromagnetic waves:**
- They are all **transverse waves** (their electric and magnetic fields vibrate perpendicularly to the direction of wave travel).
- They do not require a material medium (they can travel through a vacuum).
- They all travel at the same speed in a vacuum: the speed of light, **$c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$**.
- They obey the wave equation $c = f \lambda$. (Because $c$ is constant, as frequency $f$ increases, wavelength $\lambda$ must decrease).
- They transfer energy. The energy of an EM wave is given by $E = hf$ (where $h$ is Planck's constant). Waves with higher frequencies carry more energy.

### Analyse the Electromagnetic Spectrum in Relation to Their Wavelengths, Sources, Detection and Uses

**Objective v: analyse the electromagnetic spectrum in relation to their wavelengths, sources, detection and uses.**

The table below analyses the entire spectrum from the longest wavelength (lowest frequency, lowest energy) to the shortest wavelength (highest frequency, highest energy).

| Radiation | Wavelength Range | Sources | Detectors | Common Uses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Radio waves** | $> 1\ \text{m}$ | Oscillating electrical circuits, radio transmitters | Aerials (antennas), radio and TV receivers | Radio broadcasting (AM/FM), television transmission. |
| **Microwaves** | $10^{-3}\ \text{m}$ to $1\ \text{m}$ | Magnetron tubes, special electronic circuits | Solid-state diodes, radar antennas | Radar systems, satellite communication, mobile networks, heating food in microwave ovens. |
| **Infrared (IR)** | $700\ \text{nm}$ to $1\ \text{mm}$ | Hot bodies (the Sun, fires, human bodies) | Thermopiles, blackened thermometers, IR cameras | Night vision goggles, TV remote controls, treating muscular pain. |
| **Visible light** | $400\ \text{nm}$ to $700\ \text{nm}$ | The Sun, lamps, lasers, very hot objects | The human eye, photographic film, photocells | Seeing, photography, optical fibres, photosynthesis. |
| **Ultraviolet (UV)** | $10\ \text{nm}$ to $400\ \text{nm}$ | The Sun, mercury vapour lamps, electric arcs | Photographic film, fluorescent materials | Detecting forged bank notes (fluorescence), sterilising water and medical equipment, producing Vitamin D in the skin. |
| **X-rays** | $10^{-12}\ \text{m}$ to $10^{-9}\ \text{m}$ | Fast-moving electrons bombarding a heavy metal target (X-ray tubes) | Photographic film, Geiger-Müller tubes | Medical imaging (viewing broken bones), checking for cracks in metal pipes, crystallography. |
| **Gamma rays** | $< 10^{-12}\ \text{m}$ | Radioactive decay of unstable atomic nuclei | Geiger-Müller tubes, photographic film | Killing cancer cells (radiotherapy), sterilising medical equipment. *Hazard: very penetrating, causes mutations.* |

*(Note: $1\ \text{nm} = 10^{-9}\ \text{m}$, and $1\ \text{mm} = 10^{-3}\ \text{m}$.)*

#### Worked Examples on the EM Spectrum

Since all EM waves travel at $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ in a vacuum, you can calculate frequency, wavelength, or energy.

**Example 1: Wavelength of a radio broadcast**
A Nigerian radio station broadcasts its FM signal at a frequency of $150\ \text{MHz}$. Calculate the wavelength of the radio waves. ($c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$)

1. **Write the formula:** $v = f \lambda \implies \lambda = \frac{v}{f}$
2. **Convert units:** $f = 150\ \text{MHz} = 150 \times 10^6\ \text{Hz}$.
3. **Substitute values:** $\lambda = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}}{150 \times 10^6\ \text{Hz}}$
4. **Calculate and state the answer:** 
   $\lambda = \frac{300 \times 10^6}{150 \times 10^6} = 2\ \text{m}$.
   The wavelength is **$2\ \text{m}$**.

**Example 2: Frequency from wavelength (Visible light)**
Red light has a wavelength of $750\ \text{nm}$. Calculate its frequency. ($c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$)

1. **Write the formula:** $f = \frac{v}{\lambda}$
2. **Convert units:** $\lambda = 750\ \text{nm} = 750 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{m} = 7.5 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$.
3. **Substitute values:** $f = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}}{7.5 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}}$
4. **Calculate and state the answer:** 
   $f = \frac{3.0}{7.5} \times 10^{15} = 0.4 \times 10^{15} = 4.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$.
   The frequency is **$4.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$**.

**Example 3: Energy of a photon**
Calculate the energy of an X-ray photon that has a frequency of $3.0 \times 10^{18}\ \text{Hz}$. (Planck's constant, $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$)

1. **Write the formula:** $E = hf$
2. **Substitute values:** $E = (6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s}) \times (3.0 \times 10^{18}\ \text{Hz})$
3. **Calculate and state the answer:** 
   $E = 19.89 \times 10^{-16}\ \text{J} = 1.989 \times 10^{-15}\ \text{J}$.
   The energy is **$1.989 \times 10^{-15}\ \text{J}$**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Monochromatic light** | Light consisting of only a single wavelength (one pure colour). |
| **Dispersion** | The splitting of white light into its component colours by a medium (like a prism). |
| **Spectrum** | The band of colours produced when white light is dispersed. |
| **Collimator** | A device containing a lens used to produce a parallel beam of light. |
| **Primary colours (light)** | Red, Green, and Blue. They cannot be made by mixing other colours. |
| **Complementary colours** | Two colours of light that combine to form white light (e.g., Blue and Yellow). |
| **Total internal reflection** | The complete reflection of a light ray back into its original medium when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle. |
| **Electromagnetic spectrum** | The entire range of electromagnetic waves arranged by frequency or wavelength. |
| **Photon energy** | Energy carried by a wave particle, calculated by $E = hf$. |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Mixing light vs. mixing paint:** JAMB usually tests the mixing of *light*. Remember that Red + Green light makes **Yellow**. If you think about paint, you will get this wrong!
- **Object colours in coloured light:** An object can only reflect the colours it is made of. If a blue shirt is placed in pure red light, it has no blue light to reflect, so it absorbs the red light and appears **black**.
- **Rainbow sequence:** A primary rainbow involves **Refraction $\rightarrow$ Total Internal Reflection $\rightarrow$ Refraction**. It does *not* involve diffraction.
- **Spectrum order:** Red light bends the *least* in a prism because it travels the *fastest* in glass. Violet bends the *most* because it travels the *slowest* in glass.
- **EM wave speeds:** All electromagnetic waves (from radio to gamma) travel at exactly the same speed ($3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$) in a vacuum. None is faster than the other.
- **Unit conversions:** Always convert nanometres ($\text{nm}$) to $10^{-9}\ \text{m}$ and Megahertz ($\text{MHz}$) to $10^6\ \text{Hz}$ before using $v = f\lambda$.

## Quick summary

1. Monochromatic light has a single wavelength (one colour), while white light is polychromatic.
2. Dispersion occurs because different colours travel at different speeds in a denser medium like glass. Red travels fastest and bends least; Violet travels slowest and bends most.
3. A pure spectrum is produced using a spectrometer, which uses a slit and a collimator lens to produce a parallel beam of light before it enters the prism.
4. The primary colours of light are Red, Green, and Blue. Mixing them all gives White.
5. An object's colour is the colour of light it reflects; it absorbs all other colours. A colour filter transmits its own colour and absorbs the rest.
6. A rainbow is formed when water droplets disperse sunlight via refraction, total internal reflection, and a second refraction.
7. The electromagnetic spectrum, from longest wavelength to shortest, is: Radio, Microwave, Infrared, Visible, Ultraviolet, X-ray, Gamma ray. All travel at $3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ in a vacuum.

## Practice questions

**1. A radio wave has a wavelength of $300\ \text{m}$. If the speed of light in a vacuum is $3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, calculate its frequency.**
- A. $1.0 \times 10^5\ \text{Hz}$
- B. $1.0 \times 10^6\ \text{Hz}$
- C. $9.0 \times 10^{10}\ \text{Hz}$
- D. $1.0 \times 10^8\ \text{Hz}$

**Answer: B.** 
1. Formula: $v = f\lambda$
2. Rearrange: $f = \frac{v}{\lambda}$
3. Substitute: $f = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}}{300\ \text{m}}$
4. Calculate: $f = 1.0 \times 10^6\ \text{Hz}$.
*Mistake behind option C:* Multiplying velocity and wavelength ($f = v \times \lambda$), which is the wrong formula.

**2. When red and green lights are mixed on a white screen, the resulting colour is**
- A. yellow
- B. cyan
- C. magenta
- D. white

**Answer: A.** Red and Green are primary colours of light, and their addition produces the secondary colour Yellow.

**3. A girl wears a yellow dress with a blue belt. Under a pure red light, her dress and belt will appear respectively as**
- A. yellow and black
- B. red and black
- C. red and blue
- D. black and black

**Answer: B.** A yellow dress reflects red and green light. Under red light, it reflects the red light, appearing red. The blue belt only reflects blue light; under red light, it absorbs the red light and reflects nothing, appearing black.

**4. Which of the following colours of light deviates the most when white light passes through a triangular glass prism?**
- A. Red
- B. Yellow
- C. Green
- D. Violet

**Answer: D.** Violet light travels the slowest in glass, giving it the highest refractive index. Therefore, it is bent (deviated) the most.

**5. In the production of a pure spectrum, what is the primary function of the collimator?**
- A. To disperse the white light into its component colours
- B. To produce a parallel beam of light
- C. To focus the coloured light onto a screen
- D. To recombine the colours into white light

**Answer: B.** The collimator has a converging lens with a slit at its principal focus. It turns the diverging rays from the slit into a parallel beam before they strike the prism, preventing the dispersed colours from overlapping.

**6. Which of the following electromagnetic waves has the highest frequency?**
- A. Infrared rays
- B. Radio waves
- C. X-rays
- D. Ultraviolet rays

**Answer: C.** In the electromagnetic spectrum, frequency increases as wavelength decreases. From the options provided, X-rays have the shortest wavelength and therefore the highest frequency. (Gamma rays would be higher, but are not an option).

**7. The formation of a primary rainbow involves the following processes in the water droplet EXCEPT**
- A. refraction
- B. dispersion
- C. total internal reflection
- D. diffraction

**Answer: D.** A rainbow is formed by refraction (which causes dispersion) as light enters the drop, total internal reflection at the back of the drop, and refraction as light exits. Diffraction is not involved in this process.

**8. Calculate the energy of an electromagnetic wave with a frequency of $5.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$. ($h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$)**
- A. $3.315 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}$
- B. $1.326 \times 10^{-48}\ \text{J}$
- C. $3.315 \times 10^{-20}\ \text{J}$
- D. $1.326 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}$

**Answer: A.** 
1. Formula: $E = hf$
2. Substitute: $E = (6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s}) \times (5.0 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz})$
3. Calculate: $E = 33.15 \times 10^{-20}\ \text{J} = 3.315 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}$.
*Mistake behind option B:* Dividing $h$ by $f$ instead of multiplying them.

**9. Which of the following pairs of light colours are complementary?**
- A. Red and green
- B. Blue and yellow
- C. Green and yellow
- D. Cyan and magenta

**Answer: B.** Complementary colours of light are any two colours that combine to produce white light. Since yellow is (red + green), mixing blue and yellow is equivalent to mixing blue + red + green, which yields white.

**10. A beam of white light passes through a yellow filter and then through a cyan filter. The colour of light that emerges from the second filter is**
- A. green
- B. blue
- C. black
- D. cyan

**Answer: A.** The yellow filter only transmits red and green light. When this red and green mixture hits the cyan filter (which only transmits blue and green), the cyan filter absorbs the red light but allows the green light to pass through.
