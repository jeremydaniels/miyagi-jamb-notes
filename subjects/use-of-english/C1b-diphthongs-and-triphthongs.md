# JAMB Use of English Notes: Diphthongs and Triphthongs

## What JAMB expects

This topic is Section C (Oral Forms), content item (a) of the JAMB Use of English syllabus: **Vowels (monophthongs, diphthongs and triphthongs)**. The monophthongs (pure vowels) are in `C1a-vowels-monophthongs.md`. These notes cover the **diphthongs** and **triphthongs**.

The syllabus objectives for Oral Forms are:

1. make distinctions among vowel types;
2. differentiate among consonant types; and
3. identify correct pronunciation of individual words and articulation of connected speech.

Objectives 1 and 3 apply here. In the exam, **vowels carry 2 questions**, and a diphthong word is often one of the options or the key word.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Explain what a diphthong and a triphthong are, and how they differ from monophthongs.
2. Name and describe the 8 RP diphthongs and the 5 triphthongs, using IPA symbols.
3. Group the diphthongs into closing and centring diphthongs.
4. Recognise the common spellings of each sound, and the spelling traps.
5. Tell apart diphthongs and pure vowels that many Nigerian speakers confuse, using minimal pairs.
6. Answer JAMB-style "same vowel sound" and "different vowel sound" questions involving these sounds.

> **How italics are used in these notes:** JAMB prints the tested letters underlined. In these notes, the tested letters are shown in *italics*, for example pl*ai*n.

> **Stress marking:** in transcriptions, **ˈ** comes before the stressed syllable, e.g. /əˈɡeɪn/ = aGAIN.

## Vowel types: monophthongs, diphthongs and triphthongs (objective i)

| Type | What happens in the mouth | Number in RP | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monophthong (pure vowel) | Tongue and lips stay in **one** position | 12 | /ɑː/ in *car* |
| Diphthong | The tongue **glides from one vowel position to another** within one syllable | 8 | /aɪ/ in *my* |
| Triphthong | The tongue glides through **three** vowel positions in one syllable (a diphthong + /ə/) | 5 | /aɪə/ in *fire* |

**How to feel the difference:** say "ah" (/ɑː/) and hold it; your mouth stays still. Now say "eye" (/aɪ/) slowly; your jaw closes and your tongue rises as you move from [a] to [ɪ]. That movement is the glide.

### Parts of a diphthong

Every diphthong has a **starting point** and an **ending point**. The first part is **longer and louder**; the second part is short and weak. For example, in /aɪ/ you hear mostly the [a], with a short glide towards [ɪ]. All diphthongs are treated as **long** vowels in English (they last about as long as /iː/ or /ɑː/).

Even though a diphthong is written with two symbols, it is **one vowel sound** and forms **one syllable**. *Day* /deɪ/ has one syllable; *my* /maɪ/ has one syllable.

## The 8 diphthongs

RP has 8 diphthongs, grouped by where they **end**:

| Group | Ends in | Diphthongs | Key words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Closing diphthongs (glide to /ɪ/) | /ɪ/ | /eɪ/ /aɪ/ /ɔɪ/ | day, my, boy |
| Closing diphthongs (glide to /ʊ/) | /ʊ/ | /əʊ/ /aʊ/ | go, now |
| Centring diphthongs (glide to /ə/) | /ə/ | /ɪə/ /eə/ /ʊə/ | here, hair, tour |

- **Closing diphthongs** are so called because the tongue moves **up** towards a close (high) vowel, /ɪ/ or /ʊ/, so the mouth becomes more closed. There are **5**.
- **Centring diphthongs** are so called because the tongue moves towards the **centre** of the mouth, to /ə/. There are **3**.

**Memory aid:** "**Day, my boy, go now; here's the hair tour**" gives the 8 diphthongs in order: /eɪ aɪ ɔɪ əʊ aʊ ɪə eə ʊə/.

### 1. /eɪ/ (as in "day")

**How it is made:** starts at /e/ (front, about half height) and glides up towards /ɪ/. Lips spread.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| a-e | make /meɪk/, late /leɪt/, name /neɪm/, gate /ɡeɪt/, cake /keɪk/ |
| ai | rain /reɪn/, pain /peɪn/, wait /weɪt/, main /meɪn/, train /treɪn/ |
| ay | day /deɪ/, say /seɪ/, pay /peɪ/, play /pleɪ/, Monday /ˈmʌndeɪ/ |
| ey | they /ðeɪ/, grey /ɡreɪ/, obey /əˈbeɪ/, prey /preɪ/ |
| ei, eigh | vein /veɪn/, rein /reɪn/, eight /eɪt/, weigh /weɪ/, neighbour /ˈneɪbə/ |
| ea | great /ɡreɪt/, break /breɪk/, steak /steɪk/ |
| a (open syllable) | table /ˈteɪbl/, paper /ˈpeɪpə/, baby /ˈbeɪbi/, nation /ˈneɪʃn/ |
| au | gauge /ɡeɪdʒ/ |
| et (French loanwords) | ballet /ˈbæleɪ/ |

**Traps:**
- *said* /sed/ and *says* /sez/ do **not** have /eɪ/, even though *say* /seɪ/ does.
- *Great, break, steak* (ea = /eɪ/) are exceptions; most "ea" words have /iː/ (*meat, speak*).
- *Height* has /aɪ/, not /eɪ/, even though *weight* /weɪt/ has /eɪ/.

### 2. /aɪ/ (as in "my")

**How it is made:** starts at an open, fairly front vowel (like [a]) and glides up towards /ɪ/. Jaw closes during the glide.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| i-e | time /taɪm/, bite /baɪt/, wife /waɪf/, nine /naɪn/ |
| i (open syllable, or before ld, nd) | final /ˈfaɪnl/, idea /aɪˈdɪə/, child /tʃaɪld/, kind /kaɪnd/, mind /maɪnd/ |
| y, y-e | my /maɪ/, cry /kraɪ/, why /waɪ/, type /taɪp/, style /staɪl/ |
| igh | high /haɪ/, night /naɪt/, light /laɪt/, sigh /saɪ/ |
| ie | die /daɪ/, lie /laɪ/, tie /taɪ/, pie /paɪ/ |
| uy, ui | buy /baɪ/, guy /ɡaɪ/, guide /ɡaɪd/, guise /ɡaɪz/ |
| eigh, ei | height /haɪt/, either /ˈaɪðə/ (also /ˈiːðə/), neither /ˈnaɪðə/ (also /ˈniːðə/) |
| eye, aye | eye /aɪ/, aye /aɪ/ |
| is | island /ˈaɪlənd/, aisle /aɪl/ |

**Traps:** *build* /bɪld/ and *guilt* /ɡɪlt/ have /ɪ/, not /aɪ/, even though *guide* has /aɪ/. *Live* (verb) is /lɪv/, but *live* (adjective, as in "live broadcast") is /laɪv/.

**Variation:** *either* and *neither* have two accepted RP pronunciations, with /aɪ/ or /iː/. JAMB should not test these, but if it does, both forms exist in dictionaries.

### 3. /ɔɪ/ (as in "boy")

**How it is made:** starts at a rounded back vowel near /ɔː/ and glides forward and up towards /ɪ/. Lips start rounded and become spread.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| oy | boy /bɔɪ/, toy /tɔɪ/, joy /dʒɔɪ/, enjoy /ɪnˈdʒɔɪ/, employ /ɪmˈplɔɪ/, voyage /ˈvɔɪɪdʒ/ |
| oi | coin /kɔɪn/, oil /ɔɪl/, voice /vɔɪs/, noise /nɔɪz/, point /pɔɪnt/, avoid /əˈvɔɪd/, choice /tʃɔɪs/ |

This is the easiest diphthong to recognise: it is almost always spelt **oi** or **oy**.

**Trap:** *choir* /ˈkwaɪə/ is spelt "oi" but has the triphthong /aɪə/, not /ɔɪ/. *Tortoise* is /ˈtɔːtəs/; its "oi" is a schwa.

### 4. /əʊ/ (as in "go")

**How it is made:** starts at the central vowel /ə/ and glides up and back towards /ʊ/. Lips round during the glide.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| o (open syllable), o-e | go /ɡəʊ/, no /nəʊ/, open /ˈəʊpən/, home /həʊm/, hope /həʊp/, nose /nəʊz/ |
| oa | boat /bəʊt/, coat /kəʊt/, road /rəʊd/, soap /səʊp/, goat /ɡəʊt/ |
| ow | snow /snəʊ/, know /nəʊ/, show /ʃəʊ/, grow /ɡrəʊ/, window /ˈwɪndəʊ/, yellow /ˈjeləʊ/ |
| oe | toe /təʊ/, foe /fəʊ/, hoe /həʊ/ |
| ou, ough | soul /səʊl/, shoulder /ˈʃəʊldə/, though /ðəʊ/, dough /dəʊ/, although /ɔːlˈðəʊ/ |
| o (before ld, lt, st) | old /əʊld/, cold /kəʊld/, bolt /bəʊlt/, most /məʊst/, post /pəʊst/, both /bəʊθ/ |
| ew | sew /səʊ/ |
| oo | brooch /brəʊtʃ/ |
| eau | plateau /ˈplætəʊ/, bureau /ˈbjʊərəʊ/ |

**Symbol variation:** the *Cambridge English Pronouncing Dictionary* and most current British books write **/əʊ/**. Older books (and American dictionaries) write **/oʊ/**, **/ou/** or **/o/**. Some old Nigerian books use /əu/. They all refer to the vowel in *go*.

**Traps:**
- *Sew* /səʊ/ rhymes with *so*, not with *new*.
- *Cost* /kɒst/, *lost* /lɒst/ have /ɒ/, but *most* /məʊst/, *post* /pəʊst/, *ghost* /ɡəʊst/ have /əʊ/.
- *Do, who, move, lose* have /uː/; *love, come, done* have /ʌ/; *go, no, so* have /əʊ/. Same letter, three sounds.
- "ow" is either /əʊ/ (*know, show, bowl*) or /aʊ/ (*now, cow, owl*). *Bow* and *row* can be both, with different meanings (see /aʊ/).

### 5. /aʊ/ (as in "now")

**How it is made:** starts at an open vowel (like [a]) and glides up and back towards /ʊ/. Lips round at the end.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| ow | now /naʊ/, cow /kaʊ/, how /haʊ/, town /taʊn/, down /daʊn/, owl /aʊl/, crowd /kraʊd/ |
| ou | house /haʊs/, out /aʊt/, loud /laʊd/, mouth /maʊθ/, sound /saʊnd/, about /əˈbaʊt/, south /saʊθ/ |
| ough | plough /plaʊ/, bough /baʊ/, drought /draʊt/ |

**Same spelling, different words:**

| Word | With /əʊ/ | With /aʊ/ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bow | /bəʊ/ a weapon for arrows, or a ribbon knot | /baʊ/ to bend forward in respect; also the front of a ship |
| row | /rəʊ/ a line of seats; to paddle a boat | /raʊ/ a noisy quarrel |
| sow | /səʊ/ to plant seeds | /saʊ/ a female pig |

**Trap, the "ough" family:** *plough, bough, drought* (/aʊ/); *though, dough* (/əʊ/); *through* (/uː/); *thought, bought* (/ɔː/); *cough* (/ɒf/); *tough, rough, enough* (/ʌf/). Learn each one separately.

### 6. /ɪə/ (as in "here")

**How it is made:** starts at /ɪ/ and glides to the centre, to /ə/. Lips spread then neutral.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| ere | here /hɪə/, mere /mɪə/, sincere /sɪnˈsɪə/, severe /sɪˈvɪə/, interfere /ˌɪntəˈfɪə/ |
| ear | near /nɪə/, ear /ɪə/, fear /fɪə/, clear /klɪə/, year /jɪə/, beard /bɪəd/, dear /dɪə/ |
| eer | beer /bɪə/, deer /dɪə/, cheer /tʃɪə/, career /kəˈrɪə/, engineer /ˌendʒɪˈnɪə/ |
| ier | pier /pɪə/, fierce /fɪəs/, pierce /pɪəs/ |
| eir | weird /wɪəd/ |
| ea, eo, e (before r + vowel) | idea /aɪˈdɪə/, theory /ˈθɪəri/, period /ˈpɪəriəd/, serious /ˈsɪəriəs/ |

**The "ear" trap:** "ear" is not always /ɪə/:

| /ɪə/ | /eə/ | /ɜː/ | /ɑː/ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| near, fear, hear, beard | bear, pear, wear, swear, tear (rip) | heard, learn, earth, early, pearl | heart, hearth |

And *tear* has two pronunciations: /tɪə/ (a drop of water from the eye) and /teə/ (to rip).

### 7. /eə/ (as in "hair")

**How it is made:** starts at /e/ (front, about half height) and glides to the centre, /ə/.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| air | hair /heə/, chair /tʃeə/, fair /feə/, pair /peə/, air /eə/, repair /rɪˈpeə/ |
| are | care /keə/, share /ʃeə/, square /skweə/, rare /reə/, parent /ˈpeərənt/ |
| ear | bear /beə/, pear /peə/, wear /weə/, swear /sweə/ |
| ere, eir | there /ðeə/, where /weə/, their /ðeə/, heir /eə/ |
| ar | Mary /ˈmeəri/, various /ˈveəriəs/, scarce /skeəs/ |
| aye, ayer | prayer (words said to God) /preə/, mayor /meə/ |

**Symbol variation:** the *Cambridge English Pronouncing Dictionary* (18th edition) and most Nigerian textbooks write **/eə/**. Some newer British books write **/ɛː/**, because many modern RP speakers now say this sound as a long pure vowel, with little or no glide. Older books may write /ɛə/. All mean the vowel in *hair*.

**The "prayer" trap:** *prayer* meaning the words said to God is /preə/ (one syllable, /eə/). *Prayer* meaning a person who prays is /ˈpreɪə/ (two syllables, /eɪ/ + /ə/). Most textbooks give /preə/ for the common meaning.

**Homophones:** *hair/hare, fair/fare, pair/pear/pare, there/their/they're, where/wear/ware, air/heir, stair/stare, bear/bare*. See C3.

### 8. /ʊə/ (as in "tour")

**How it is made:** starts at /ʊ/ (lips rounded) and glides to the centre, /ə/.

| Spelling | Example words |
| --- | --- |
| our | tour /tʊə/, tourist /ˈtʊərɪst/ |
| ure | cure /kjʊə/, pure /pjʊə/, endure /ɪnˈdjʊə/ |
| ur (+ vowel) | during /ˈdjʊərɪŋ/, jury /ˈdʒʊəri/, curious /ˈkjʊəriəs/, furious /ˈfjʊəriəs/, plural /ˈplʊərəl/ |
| oor | moor /mʊə/, poor (see note) |

**Important variation:** in modern RP, many speakers no longer use /ʊə/ in common words. They say *poor, sure, tour, moor, your* with /ɔː/: *poor* /pɔː/, *sure* /ʃɔː/. The *Cambridge English Pronouncing Dictionary* lists both pronunciations for words such as *poor* and *sure*. Nigerian textbooks and JAMB keys usually treat *poor, sure, tour, cure, pure* as /ʊə/. Safest approach for JAMB:

- Words with /j/ before the vowel (*cure, pure, endure, during, curious*) keep /ʊə/ for most RP speakers. These are the safest /ʊə/ examples.
- *Tour* and *tourist* also usually keep /ʊə/.
- For *poor, sure, your, moor*, expect /ʊə/ in older-style keys, but know that /ɔː/ is now common in RP too.

**Trap:** /ʊə/ is the **rarest** diphthong. Don't confuse *tour* /tʊə/ with *tower* /ˈtaʊə/ (triphthong) or *tore* /tɔː/.

| Diphthong | Start | End | Type | Key words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| /eɪ/ | /e/ front, half height | /ɪ/ | closing | day, rain, great |
| /aɪ/ | open, front-central | /ɪ/ | closing | my, night, buy |
| /ɔɪ/ | /ɔ/ back, rounded | /ɪ/ | closing | boy, coin, voice |
| /əʊ/ | /ə/ central | /ʊ/ | closing | go, boat, though |
| /aʊ/ | open, front-central | /ʊ/ | closing | now, house, plough |
| /ɪə/ | /ɪ/ | /ə/ | centring | here, near, beer |
| /eə/ | /e/ | /ə/ | centring | hair, care, there |
| /ʊə/ | /ʊ/ | /ə/ | centring | tour, cure, pure |

### Monophthongs vs diphthongs compared

| Feature | Monophthong | Diphthong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tongue movement | None; stays in one position | Glides from one position to another |
| Symbols | One symbol (with or without ː) | Two symbols |
| Length | Long or short | Always long |
| Syllables | One | One |
| Number in RP | 12 | 8 |
| Example | /ɑː/ in *car* | /aɪ/ in *my* |

## The 5 triphthongs

A **triphthong** is a glide through **three** vowel qualities in one syllable. In RP, each triphthong is one of the five **closing diphthongs** followed by **/ə/**:

| Triphthong | Made of | Example words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| /eɪə/ | /eɪ/ + /ə/ | player /ˈpleɪə/, layer /ˈleɪə/, greyer /ˈɡreɪə/, payer /ˈpeɪə/ |
| /aɪə/ | /aɪ/ + /ə/ | fire /ˈfaɪə/, tyre /ˈtaɪə/, hire /ˈhaɪə/, buyer /ˈbaɪə/, liar /ˈlaɪə/, choir /ˈkwaɪə/, iron /ˈaɪən/, diary /ˈdaɪəri/, science /ˈsaɪəns/, society /səˈsaɪəti/ |
| /ɔɪə/ | /ɔɪ/ + /ə/ | royal /ˈrɔɪəl/, loyal /ˈlɔɪəl/, employer /ɪmˈplɔɪə/, destroyer /dɪˈstrɔɪə/ |
| /əʊə/ | /əʊ/ + /ə/ | lower /ˈləʊə/, slower /ˈsləʊə/, mower /ˈməʊə/, grower /ˈɡrəʊə/ |
| /aʊə/ | /aʊ/ + /ə/ | hour /ˈaʊə/, our /ˈaʊə/, flower /ˈflaʊə/, flour /ˈflaʊə/, power /ˈpaʊə/, shower /ˈʃaʊə/, tower /ˈtaʊə/ |

**Memory aid:** the five triphthongs are "**player, fire, royal, lower, hour**".

### Is a triphthong one syllable or two?

Textbooks differ here, and you should know both views:

- **Many Nigerian Oral English textbooks** treat a triphthong as **one** vowel sound in **one** syllable, so *fire* /faɪə/ and *hour* /aʊə/ are single-syllable words.
- **Roach and the Cambridge dictionary** note that the triphthong is often said as **two** syllables (a diphthong + /ə/), especially in careful speech or when it is split by a word ending: *play-er*, *low-er*. That is why the dictionary often writes the syllable break inside it.
- In **fast RP speech**, triphthongs are often **smoothed**: the middle part is weakened or dropped, so *fire* can sound like [faə] and *tower* like [taə] or even [tɑː]. This is sometimes called **smoothing**.

For JAMB, what matters is that you can recognise the sound sequence in words like *fire, hour, player, royal, lower*.

**Spelling traps:**
- *Our* and *hour* are pronounced the same in RP (/ˈaʊə/), so they are homophones. (*Our* is also sometimes said /ɑː/.)
- *Flower* and *flour* are homophones: /ˈflaʊə/.
- *Choir* /ˈkwaɪə/ sounds like *quire*; it does not contain /ɔɪ/.
- *Iron* is /ˈaɪən/: the "r" is not pronounced in RP.

### How Nigerian speakers often say triphthongs

Many Nigerian speakers split triphthongs into two clear syllables with a glide consonant in the middle and a full final vowel, for example *fire* as [faja], *power* as [pawa], *flower* as [flawa], *player* as [plejar]. RP keeps the middle element weak and ends in a weak /ə/, with no /r/.

## Diphthong distinctions Nigerian speakers often miss (objective i)

### 1. /eɪ/ and /əʊ/ said as pure vowels

Many Nigerian speakers say /eɪ/ as a pure [e] and /əʊ/ as a pure [o], with no glide. In RP the glide matters, and it separates words that would otherwise sound alike.

**/eɪ/ vs /e/:**

| --- | --- |
| late /leɪt/ | let /let/ |
| pain /peɪn/ | pen /pen/ |
| gate /ɡeɪt/ | get /ɡet/ |
| wait /weɪt/ | wet /wet/ |
| sale /seɪl/ | sell /sel/ |
| main /meɪn/ | men /men/ |
| taste /teɪst/ | test /test/ |

**/əʊ/ vs /ɒ/ vs /ɔː/:**

| boat /bəʊt/ | | bought /bɔːt/ |

| hope /həʊp/ | hop /hɒp/ | |
| woke /wəʊk/ | | walk /wɔːk/ |
| low /ləʊ/ | | law /lɔː/ |

## How to answer JAMB diphthong questions

### Question type 1: "same vowel sound as the letters in italics"

> Choose the option that has the same vowel sound as the one represented by the letters in italics: h*ai*r
> A. here  B. heir  C. hire  D. hear

**Step by step:**

1. Say the key word: *hair* /heə/. The sound is /eə/.
2. Say each option: here /hɪə/, heir /eə/, hire /ˈhaɪə/, hear /hɪə/.
3. Match: **B (heir)**. *Heir* has a silent "h" and sounds exactly like *air* /eə/.

### Question type 2: "different vowel sound"

> Which of these words has a different vowel sound from the others?
> A. tour  B. cure  C. pure  D. pour

tour, cure, pure: /ʊə/. pour: /pɔː/. **Answer: D.**

### Question type 3: identify a diphthong or triphthong

> Which of the following words contains a triphthong?
> A. fire  B. fair  C. far  D. fear

fire /ˈfaɪə/ (/aɪə/), fair /feə/ (diphthong), far /fɑː/ (monophthong), fear /fɪə/ (diphthong). **Answer: A.**

### Question type 4: name the type

> Which of the following is a centring diphthong?
> A. /eɪ/  B. /aʊ/  C. /ɪə/  D. /ɔɪ/

Centring diphthongs end in /ə/: /ɪə/, /eə/, /ʊə/. **Answer: C.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Diphthong | A vowel sound in which the tongue glides from one vowel position to another within one syllable, e.g. /aɪ/ in "my" |
| Triphthong | A glide through three vowel positions in one syllable: a closing diphthong + /ə/, e.g. /aɪə/ in "fire" |
| Glide | The movement of the tongue from the first part of a diphthong to the second |
| Closing diphthong | A diphthong that glides towards a close vowel (/ɪ/ or /ʊ/): /eɪ aɪ ɔɪ əʊ aʊ/ |
| Centring diphthong | A diphthong that glides towards the central vowel /ə/: /ɪə eə ʊə/ |
| Monophthong | A pure vowel with no glide (see C1a) |
| Smoothing | Weakening or dropping the middle part of a triphthong in fast speech, e.g. "fire" as [faə] |
| Minimal pair | Two words differing in only one sound, e.g. here /hɪə/ and hair /heə/ |
| Homophones | Words with the same sound but different spelling and meaning, e.g. hour and our (see C3) |
| RP | Received Pronunciation: the British model accent used for JAMB answers |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Learn the 8 diphthongs with key words:** day, my, boy, go, now, here, hair, tour.
- **Closing = ends in /ɪ/ or /ʊ/ (5); centring = ends in /ə/ (3).** JAMB may ask which group a diphthong belongs to.
- **Don't say /eɪ/ and /əʊ/ as pure vowels.** *Late* is not *let*; *coat* is not *cot* or *caught*.
- **Separate /ɪə/ and /eə/:** here/hair, beer/bear, fear/fair, cheer/chair.
- **"ear" has four sounds:** near (/ɪə/), bear (/eə/), heard (/ɜː/), heart (/ɑː/).
- **"ow" has two sounds:** know (/əʊ/), now (/aʊ/). *Bow, row, sow* have both, with different meanings.
- **"ough" is unpredictable:** plough (/aʊ/), though (/əʊ/), through (/uː/), thought (/ɔː/), cough (/ɒ/), tough (/ʌ/). Learn each word.
- **Triphthongs:** player, fire, royal, lower, hour. *Hour/our* and *flower/flour* are homophones.
- **Textbooks differ:** /əʊ/ may be written /oʊ/ or /ou/; /eə/ may be written /ɛː/ or /ɛə/; /ʊə/ is often replaced by /ɔː/ in modern RP (*poor, sure*). Use *cure, pure, tour, during* as your safest /ʊə/ words.

## Quick summary

1. A diphthong is a single vowel sound that glides from one position to another in one syllable; a triphthong glides through three.
2. RP has 8 diphthongs: /eɪ aɪ ɔɪ əʊ aʊ/ (closing) and /ɪə eə ʊə/ (centring).
3. RP has 5 triphthongs, each a closing diphthong + /ə/: /eɪə aɪə ɔɪə əʊə aʊə/ (player, fire, royal, lower, hour).
4. Diphthongs are always long and always one syllable; triphthongs are treated as one syllable in many textbooks but are often split or smoothed in real speech.
5. Many spellings are misleading: "ea", "ow", "ear" and "ough" each represent several different sounds.
6. Nigerian speakers often flatten /eɪ/ and /əʊ/ into pure vowels and merge /ɪə/ with /eə/; JAMB tests the RP distinctions.
7. Symbols vary between books (/əʊ/ or /oʊ/; /eə/ or /ɛː/), and /ʊə/ is giving way to /ɔː/ in modern RP; recognise all forms.

## Practice questions

In questions 1, 3, 7 and 9, the tested letters are in *italics* (JAMB underlines them).

**1. Choose the option that has the same vowel sound as the one represented by the letter in italics: pl*a*ne**

- A. said
- B. great
- C. heart
- D. plant

**Answer: B.** *Plane* /pleɪn/ and *great* /ɡreɪt/ both have /eɪ/. *Said* has /e/, *heart* has /ɑː/ and *plant* has /ɑː/ in RP.

**2. Which of the following words contains the diphthong /əʊ/?**

- A. cow
- B. bought
- C. sew
- D. new

**Answer: C.** *Sew* /səʊ/ rhymes with *so*. *Cow* has /aʊ/, *bought* has /ɔː/ and *new* has /juː/.

**3. Choose the option that has the same vowel sound as the one represented by the letters in italics: h*ai*r**

- A. here
- B. heir
- C. hire
- D. hear

**Answer: B.** *Hair* /heə/ and *heir* /eə/ share /eə/. *Here* and *hear* have /ɪə/; *hire* has /aɪə/.

**4. Which of the following words has a different vowel sound from the others?**

- A. here
- B. beer
- C. hair
- D. near

**Answer: C.** *Hair* /heə/ has /eə/. *Here* /hɪə/, *beer* /bɪə/ and *near* /nɪə/ all have /ɪə/. Many Nigerian speakers say *here* and *hair* alike, so this pair is a favourite JAMB trap.

**5. Which of the following is a centring diphthong?**

- A. /eɪ/
- B. /aʊ/
- C. /ɪə/
- D. /ɔɪ/

**Answer: C.** Centring diphthongs glide towards /ə/: /ɪə/, /eə/ and /ʊə/. The other three are closing diphthongs.

**6. Which of the following words contains a triphthong?**

- A. fire
- B. fair
- C. far
- D. fear

**Answer: A.** *Fire* /ˈfaɪə/ has the triphthong /aɪə/. *Fair* (/eə/) and *fear* (/ɪə/) have diphthongs; *far* (/ɑː/) has a monophthong.

**7. Choose the option that has the same vowel sound as the one represented by the letters in italics: b*uy***

- A. build
- B. guide
- C. guess
- D. busy

**Answer: B.** *Buy* /baɪ/ and *guide* /ɡaɪd/ have /aɪ/. *Build* and *busy* have /ɪ/; *guess* has /e/.

**8. Which of the following words contains the diphthong /aʊ/?**

- A. though
- B. through
- C. plough
- D. cough

**Answer: C.** *Plough* /plaʊ/. *Though* has /əʊ/, *through* has /uː/ and *cough* has /ɒ/.

**9. Choose the option that has the same vowel sound as the one represented by the letters in italics: f*ear***

- A. pear
- B. beard
- C. heard
- D. bear

**Answer: B.** *Fear* /fɪə/ and *beard* /bɪəd/ share /ɪə/. *Pear* and *bear* have /eə/; *heard* has /ɜː/.

**10. Which of the following words contains the triphthong /aʊə/?**

- A. power
- B. player
- C. lower
- D. buyer

**Answer: A.** *Power* /ˈpaʊə/. *Player* has /eɪə/, *lower* has /əʊə/ and *buyer* has /aɪə/.
