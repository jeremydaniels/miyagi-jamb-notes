## Agricultural Science-specific rules (these override the style example wherever they differ)

CORRECTNESS
- Wherever a file contains a calculation (plant density per hectare, seed rate, spacing, area of a plot, soil component proportions, water-holding capacity, soil pH, fertilizer ratios and rates such as NPK, Mendelian ratios and probabilities, ration formulation and the Pearson square, feed conversion, stocking rate, egg and milk yield, gross and net margin, profit and loss, depreciation, appreciation and salvage value, elasticity of demand and supply, measures of central tendency, experimental error, percentages), you MUST use the code execution tool to verify it before writing it, and check every wrong option of each practice question. A file with no calculations does not need the tool. Never state an unverified number. Run the checks silently; do not mention the tool or paste code in the notes.
- SHOW FULL WORKING AS NUMBERED STEPS in every worked example and every practice-question explanation: Step 1, Step 2, and so on, each saying what is done and why, ending with the answer in a sentence. State the formula first and explain each term.
- UNITS: put the unit on every quantity, in the working and in every option: m, cm, ha, m², kg, kg per ha, tonnes, litres, plants per ha, eggs per hen, %, °C, mm, and ₦ for money (for example ₦85,000 per ha). Convert units step by step (for example 1 ha = 10,000 m²). Never leave a bare number.
- All working must be doable by hand with no calculator: use neat numbers.
- NEVER INVENT FACTS, FIGURES, DOSES, DATES OR NAMES. Use only information you are confident is correct. Yields, prices, fertilizer rates, seed rates, spacing, stocking rates, gestation and incubation periods, nutrient requirements and statistics vary with the crop variety, the place, the season and the textbook: give typical or example values, say they are examples or approximate, and tell students not to memorise them as fixed. Never give a pesticide, drug or vaccine dose as advice; explain the principle and the safety rules instead.
- Scientific names must be correct and written in italics (for example *Zea mays*); if you are not certain of a scientific name, give only the common name. Give common Nigerian names (Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba) only when you are sure of them.
- Where textbooks classify or define differently (types of farming systems and cropping systems, classification of farm inputs, the groups of crops, fish culture systems, types of rations, types of labour and land tenure, the stages of production), say so and give the commonly used Nigerian secondary school view.
- Follow the Transcription notes for this subject. In particular: names of Nigerian research institutes, programmes and agencies (NCRI, IAR, IAR&T, CRIN, NIFOR, FRIN, RRI, NRCRI, NIHORT, LCRI, RTEP, FADAMA, OFN, NAFPP, NALDA, NARP and so on) must be spelled out, explained by function and hedged as "names and status may have changed"; teach both "gross and net profits" and "gross and net margins"; use "salvage value"; explain the fish culture classification as printed and say that textbooks differ; and cover climate elements as well as climate change in animal physiology.
- Safety and animal welfare: describe farm tools, machinery, agrochemicals and animal handling with their safety precautions.

FORMAT
- Write formulas in Markdown/LaTeX (`$...$` inline, `$$...$$` display) and explain every symbol. Write naira as ₦ and never use a bare dollar sign for money. Keep each `$...$` on one line. Use plain text, not LaTeX, in headings.
- Use tables for comparisons, schedules, crop and animal data, record sheets, Punnett squares, ration tables and budgets, and give the numbers in them.

HOW TO TEACH
- Explain the idea in plain words first, then with an example, then (where the syllabus asks) with a diagram described in words. Always give Nigerian and West African examples: cassava, yam, maize, sorghum (guinea corn), rice, cowpea, groundnut, cocoa, oil palm, rubber, cotton, tomatoes and pepper; cattle (White Fulani, Sokoto Gudali), sheep, goats (West African Dwarf, Red Sokoto), poultry, pigs, rabbits, tilapia and catfish; the ecological zones from the mangrove and rainforest to the Sudan and Sahel savanna; Fadama farming, ADPs, cooperatives, the Land Use Act, and the Niger, Benue and Kainji areas.
- Explain every content item, named example and objective fully with its own heading. Use comparison tables wherever the syllabus says compare, differentiate, distinguish or contrast (for example branches and types of agriculture, monocot and dicot, organic and inorganic fertilizer, sexual and asexual propagation, capillary, gravitational and hygroscopic water, biting, boring and sucking insects, ruminant and non-ruminant digestion, traditional and modern bee-keeping).
- Explain processes step by step (for example soil formation, pollination and fertilization, life cycles of insects, digestion in a ruminant, egg formation and incubation, processing of gari, rice and groundnut).
- Add an `[IMAGE: ...]` placeholder wherever a diagram helps (soil profile, soil texture triangle, monocot and dicot structure, flower parts, life cycles, farm tools and machinery, digestive systems, fish ponds, a beehive, a farm layout), with a detailed description including every label, so it can be drawn accurately later.
- Include a "Common mistakes" note in each major section (for example confusing weeds with pests, seed rate with plant density, gross margin with net profit, pollination with fertilization).

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (exactly 10)
- Real JAMB style: short multiple-choice questions like past UTME papers, mixing easy, medium and harder items and covering all objectives of the file (definitions, processes, identification, diagrams described in words, and calculations where the topic has them).
- The wrong options must be plausible and each based on a typical misunderstanding or calculation slip (for example forgetting to convert hectares to square metres). Only one option may be correct. Every option with a quantity carries its unit, and money carries ₦.
- After "**Answer: X.**" (on the same line) give a clear explanation with numbered working for calculations, then name the misunderstanding or mistake behind the most tempting wrong option, and make sure that explanation is TRUE: the option must really come from the mistake you describe.
