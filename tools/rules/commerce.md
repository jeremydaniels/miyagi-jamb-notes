## Commerce-specific rules (these override the style example wherever they differ)

CORRECTNESS
- LENGTH: the word target is a guide, not a quota. Write as long as the topic needs to cover every content item and objective fully, with no padding and no repetition, and never cut required content to save space.
- SYLLABUS ERRORS THAT AFFECT TEACHING: first teach what JAMB expects (the syllabus wording), then add a short "Note" (a blockquote starting "**Note:**") giving the correct or current position. Hedge anything time-sensitive ("at the time of writing").
- Wherever a file contains a calculation (capital types, gross and net profit, turnover, mark-up and margin, discounts, C.I.F. and F.O.B. prices, tariffs and duties, balance of trade and balance of payments, insurance premiums and claims, interest and loans, share capital, dividends, commission, exchange-rate conversions for the Bureau de change), you MUST use the code execution tool to verify it before writing it, and check every wrong option of each practice question. A file with no calculations does not need the tool. Never state an unverified number. Run the checks silently; do not mention the tool or paste code in the notes.
- SHOW FULL WORKING AS NUMBERED STEPS in every worked example and every practice-question explanation: Step 1, Step 2, and so on, each saying what is done and why, ending with the answer in a sentence. State the formula first and explain each term.
- UNITS: put the unit on every quantity, in the working and in every option. Money is always in naira, written ₦ (for example ₦120,000); foreign money names its currency (for example US$ or £), and exchange rates are written as "₦ per US$" with the rate stated as an assumed example, never as a real current rate. Use %, kg, tonnes and so on where they apply. Never leave a bare number where an amount of money is meant.
- All working must be doable by hand with no calculator: use neat numbers.
- NEVER INVENT FACTS, FIGURES, LAWS OR DATES. Laws, agencies, rates, tariffs, institutions and statistics about Nigeria and the world change over time. Describe how things work, name the institution and its function, and where a date, rate or figure may have changed say "at the time of writing" and tell students not to memorise it. If textbooks define or classify a term differently (for example types of retailers and wholesalers, forms of business units, types of capital, types of insurance, the meaning of "aids to trade"), say so.
- Follow the Transcription notes for this subject. In particular: teach the printed content and objectives even where they do not match (for example Topic 1 asks for the functions of e-commerce); teach the bodies under the names JAMB uses, then add a Note giving the current names (the Nigerian Stock Exchange is now Nigerian Exchange Limited, NGX; the Consumer Protection Council has been replaced by the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission, FCCPC); and describe the Second-Tier Securities Market as it is taught in the textbooks, with a Note that details may be out of date.
- Never give legal, tax or investment advice. Explain what laws and agencies do, in general terms.

FORMAT
- Write formulas in Markdown/LaTeX (`$...$` inline, `$$...$$` display) and explain every symbol. Write naira as ₦ and never use a bare dollar sign for money. Keep each `$...$` on one line. Use plain text, not LaTeX, in headings.
- Use tables for comparisons, trading and profit statements, documents (invoice, quotation, bill of lading and so on, with sample fields filled in using made-up Nigerian businesses), and give the numbers in them.

HOW TO TEACH
- Explain the idea in words first, then with an example, then (where the syllabus asks) with a diagram or a sample document. Always give Nigerian examples (markets such as Onitsha, Ariaria, Alaba and Balogun, cocoa and garri trade, NAFDAC, NDLEA, Customs, the Nigerian Ports Authority, banks, mobile money, courier services, GSM operators, Nigerian cooperative societies).
- Explain every content item, named example and objective fully with its own heading. Use comparison tables wherever the syllabus says compare, differentiate, distinguish or contrast (for example retail and wholesale, cash and credit, home and foreign trade, trade and cash discount, dissolution and liquidation, patents, trademarks and copyrights).
- Add an `[IMAGE: ...]` placeholder wherever a diagram helps (channels of distribution, the communication process, organisation charts, the structure of the Stock Exchange, a sample invoice), with a detailed description including every label and figure, so it can be drawn accurately later.
- Include a "Common mistakes" note in each major section (for example confusing mark-up with margin, trade discount with cash discount, balance of trade with balance of payments).

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (exactly 10)
- Real JAMB style: short multiple-choice questions like past UTME papers, mixing easy, medium and harder items and covering all objectives of the file (definitions, concepts, Nigerian institutions and, where the topic has them, calculations).
- The wrong options must be plausible and each based on a typical misunderstanding or calculation slip. Only one option may be correct. Every money option carries ₦.
- After "**Answer: X.**" (on the same line) give a clear explanation with numbered working for calculations, then name the misunderstanding or mistake behind the most tempting wrong option, and make sure that explanation is TRUE: the option must really come from the mistake you describe.
