## CRK-specific rules (these override the style example wherever they differ)

BIBLE REFERENCES AND QUOTATIONS
- Every Bible reference must be exact: book, chapter and verse, written as in standard usage (for example "Genesis 22:1-19", "Matthew 6:14-15"). Never invent or guess a reference. If you are not certain which verse says something, cite the chapter only, or say "see the passage".
- The syllabus prints some references with errors (see the transcription notes you are given). Give the reference exactly as printed, then say plainly that it appears to be a misprint and give the probable correct one (for example the Forgiveness topic prints "Matt. 7;14-15", which is probably Matthew 6:14-15). Teach the intended passage and say that the printed one is what the syllabus lists.
- Quote only short passages (one or two verses at most) from the King James Version, in quotation marks with the reference. The KJV is public domain. Summarise everything else in your own words. Never paste long passages. Verse numbering and wording differ slightly between versions (the syllabus recommends the Revised Standard Version); say so where it matters.
- Tell each Bible story as a clear numbered sequence of events (who, where, what happened, outcome), using only what the passage says. Do not add details that are not in the text; label traditions and later interpretations as such.

PRESENTATION
- Neutral, as the syllabus is: describe what the biblical text says and what Christians generally teach, and mention differences between denominations (for example on baptism, communion, spiritual gifts) where they exist. Do not argue for or against any belief. Use "the Bible says" and "Christians believe", not "we believe".
- Apply the teaching to Nigerian life in a respectful, practical way (family, school, leadership, corruption, giving, labour, community) when the objective asks to apply or relate it.
- Use comparison tables wherever the syllabus says compare, distinguish or differentiate (for example the covenants with Noah, Abraham and Israel; Nehemiah and Ezra; the mission of the twelve and the seventy; the conversions of Saul and Cornelius).
- Each file covers every content item (each named passage) and every objective with its own heading.

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (exactly 10)
- JAMB style multiple choice (who, where, what happened, what lesson, which reference). Wrong options plausible; one correct answer only. Test only facts stated in the passages. Never ask for a verse number unless you are certain it is right.
- After "**Answer: X.**" (same line) give a short explanation that cites the passage.
