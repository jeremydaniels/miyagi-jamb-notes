## Economics-specific rules (these override the style example wherever they differ)

CORRECTNESS
- Wherever a file contains a calculation (price elasticity, income elasticity, cross elasticity, national income, GDP/GNP/NNP, costs and revenue, averages and marginals, index numbers and the price level, inflation and growth rates, the multiplier, the money multiplier, terms of trade, dependency ratio, population growth, comparative cost and opportunity cost, taxes and percentages), you MUST use the code execution tool to verify it before writing it, and check each wrong option of practice questions. A file with no calculations does not need the tool. Never state an unverified number. Run the checks silently; do not mention the tool or paste code in the notes.
- Show units and signs in every calculation: ₦, %, units of output, price per unit. State the formula and explain every symbol. Give elasticity as a number with its sign and say what it means (elastic, inelastic, unitary). All working must be doable by hand with no calculator: use neat numbers.
- NEVER INVENT FACTS, FIGURES OR DATES. Use only information you are confident is correct. Institutions, laws, policies, rates, budgets and statistics about Nigeria and the world change over time: describe how things work, name the institutions and their functions, and where you give a date or a figure say it is approximate or "at the time of writing" and that students should not memorise figures. If textbooks define a term differently or classify differently (for example the classification of factors of production, types of unemployment, measures of money supply M1/M2, or the names of Nigerian institutions), say so.
- Use the economic terms the syllabus uses. Explain each in simple English.

FORMAT
- Write formulas in Markdown/LaTeX (`$...$` inline, `$$...$$` display) and explain every symbol. Write naira as ₦ and never use a bare dollar sign for money. Keep each `$...$` on one line. Use plain text, not LaTeX, in headings.
- Use tables for comparisons, schedules (demand and supply schedules, cost tables, national income tables) and give the numbers in them.

HOW TO TEACH
- Explain the theory in words first, then with a schedule or table, then (where the syllabus asks) with a diagram. Always give Nigerian examples (markets in Lagos, Kano and Onitsha, cocoa, cassava, crude oil and NNPC, the Central Bank of Nigeria, the naira, Nigerian banks, ECOWAS, OPEC, the Niger Delta, the informal sector).
- Explain every content item, named example and objective fully with its own heading. Use comparison tables wherever the syllabus says compare, differentiate, distinguish or contrast (for example perfect competition versus monopoly, private versus public limited companies, direct and indirect taxes).
- Add an `[IMAGE: ...]` placeholder wherever a diagram helps (demand and supply curves, cost curves, the circular flow, production possibility curves, a Lorenz curve, organisation charts) with a detailed description including axis labels, the exact curves, points and values, so it can be drawn accurately later.
- Include a "Common mistakes" note in each major section (for example shifts of a curve versus movement along it, confusing GDP with GNP, confusing nominal and real values, mixing up average and marginal).

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (exactly 10)
- Real JAMB style: short multiple-choice questions like past UTME papers, mixing easy, medium and harder items and covering all objectives of the file (definitions, concepts, Nigerian institutions, diagrams in words and calculations).
- The wrong options must be plausible and each based on a typical misunderstanding or calculation slip. Only one option may be correct.
- After "**Answer: X.**" (on the same line) give a clear explanation (numbered working for calculations), then name the misunderstanding or mistake behind the most tempting wrong option, and make sure that explanation is TRUE: the option must really come from the mistake you describe.
