## Geography-specific rules (these override the style example wherever they differ)

CORRECTNESS
- LENGTH: the word target is a guide, not a quota. Write as long as the topic needs to cover every content item and objective fully, with no padding and no repetition, and never cut required content to save space.
- SYLLABUS ERRORS THAT AFFECT TEACHING: first teach what JAMB expects (the syllabus wording), then add a short "Note" (a blockquote starting "**Note:**") giving the correct or current position. Hedge anything time-sensitive ("at the time of writing").
- Wherever a file contains a calculation (map scale and conversion, distance, area, gradient, bearings, vertical exaggeration, longitude and time, latitude and distance, population density, birth, death and natural increase rates, growth rate, dependency ratio, percentages, averages, ranges, rainfall and temperature statistics, trade figures), you MUST use the code execution tool to verify it before writing it, and check every wrong option of each practice question. A file with no calculations does not need the tool. Never state an unverified number. Run the checks silently; do not mention the tool or paste code in the notes.
- SHOW FULL WORKING AS NUMBERED STEPS in every worked example and every practice-question explanation: Step 1, Step 2, and so on, each saying what is done and why, ending with the answer in a sentence. State the formula first and explain each term.
- UNITS: put the unit on every quantity, in the working and in every option: cm, m, km, cm², km², hectares, degrees (°), hours and minutes, persons per km², births per 1,000 people, %, °C, mm, tonnes, ₦ for money. Convert units step by step (for example 1 : 50,000 means 1 cm on the map is 50,000 cm = 500 m on the ground). Never leave a bare number.
- All working must be doable by hand with no calculator: use neat numbers. Say which standard values you use (for example 15° of longitude = 1 hour, the earth turns 360° in 24 hours; about 111 km per degree of latitude, as an approximate value).
- NEVER INVENT FACTS, FIGURES, PLACE FACTS OR DATES. Use only information you are confident is correct. Statistics (populations, areas, production, trade, climate figures, state and local government numbers) and political facts change, so give approximate values, say "at the time of writing", and tell students not to memorise them; if you are not sure of a place fact, describe it generally. Never invent a map reference, a contour value or a sheet of a real topographical map; use made-up exercise maps and say they are made up.
- Where textbooks classify or define differently (Köppen groups and letters, the Greek zone system, soil classification and soil names, vegetation zones of Nigeria and West Africa, the divisions of the earth's layers, the number of spheres, types of rocks and landforms, the names of climate types, definitions of urban, rural and settlement patterns), say so and give the commonly used Nigerian secondary school view.
- Follow the Transcription notes for this subject. In particular: teach the three spheres JAMB lists (atmosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere) in the energy-balance and water-cycle explanation, then add a Note that the lithosphere is the fourth sphere (the content item names "four spheres") and explain it; explain flooding as both natural and human-influenced; treat the missing objective vi in Weather and Climate by teaching weather instruments in full; treat "Greek" climate classification as the ancient Greek zones, saying that textbooks vary; and explain that the Part I practical topics have no printed contents.

FORMAT
- Write formulas in Markdown/LaTeX (`$...$` inline, `$$...$$` display) and explain every symbol. Write naira as ₦ and never use a bare dollar sign for money. Keep each `$...$` on one line. Use plain text, not LaTeX, in headings.
- Use tables for comparisons, data sets, climate tables, population tables and worked-data exercises, and give the numbers in them. Use the SI-style units above.

HOW TO TEACH
- Explain the idea in plain words first, then with an example, then (where the topic needs it) with a diagram or a map exercise described in words. Always give Nigerian and African examples (the Niger and Benue rivers, Lake Chad, the Jos Plateau, the Niger Delta, Kainji Dam, Lagos, Kano, Onitsha, Abuja, the Sahel, the Rift Valley, Mount Cameroon, the Sahara, Cape Verde and West African states for ECOWAS), and the other regions the syllabus names (the Amazon Basin, north-eastern USA, India, Japan, the "West Coast of Southern Africa" exactly as the syllabus prints it (explain it as the south-western coast of Africa and say the wording is unclear), Western Europe, the Middle East).
- Explain every content item, named example and objective fully with its own heading. Use comparison tables wherever the syllabus says compare, differentiate, distinguish or contrast (for example latitudes and longitudes, weather and climate, Köppen and Greek classifications, rural and urban settlements, the types of rock, tensional and compressional forces, renewable and non-renewable resources, chain and prismatic surveying).
- Explain processes step by step (for example how rocks form, how a volcano erupts, how a river erodes and deposits, how soils form, how an ocean current is caused, how climate change arises).
- Add an `[IMAGE: ...]` placeholder wherever a diagram, map or cross-section helps (the solar system, latitude and longitude, the structure of the earth, plate boundaries, rock cycle, river stages, climate graphs, soil profiles, settlement patterns, contour and cross-profile drawings, GIS layers), with a detailed description including every label, scale and figure, so it can be drawn accurately later.
- Include a "Common mistakes" note in each major section (for example mixing up latitude and longitude, reading a scale the wrong way round, confusing weather with climate).

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (exactly 10)
- Real JAMB style: short multiple-choice questions like past UTME papers, mixing easy, medium and harder items and covering all objectives of the file (definitions, processes, places, diagrams described in words, and calculations where the topic has them).
- The wrong options must be plausible and each based on a typical misunderstanding or calculation slip (for example forgetting to square a scale factor for area). Only one option may be correct. Every option with a measurement carries its unit.
- After "**Answer: X.**" (on the same line) give a clear explanation with numbered working for calculations, then name the misunderstanding or mistake behind the most tempting wrong option, and make sure that explanation is TRUE: the option must really come from the mistake you describe.
