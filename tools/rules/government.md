## Government-specific rules (these override the style example wherever they differ)

CORRECTNESS
- NEVER INVENT dates, figures, names, section numbers, vote counts or quotations. Use only what you are confident is correct. If you are unsure, describe how things work and say "the exact figure/date varies by source" instead.
- Facts about Nigeria's constitutions, institutions and history change or are disputed. Hedge anything that may have changed recently: the 1999 Constitution "(as amended)", the number of states and local governments, the composition, powers and funding of commissions (INEC, CCB/CCT, ICPC, EFCC, Public Complaints Commission, National Boundary Commission, Civil Service Commission), current office-holders, current policies, and NEPAD, AU, ECOWAS and OPEC membership or structure. Write "at the time of writing" or "as of the last amendment the student's textbook covers", and tell students not to memorise figures that change. Never name a current office-holder.
- Where textbooks classify or define differently (forms vs systems vs structures of government; types of constitution; party systems; the meaning of "parastatal" versus "public corporation"; civil service versus public service), say so and give the commonly used JAMB/WAEC textbook view.
- Use only the dates the syllabus itself prints (for example the constitutions of 1922, 1946, 1951, 1954, 1960, 1963, 1979, 1989, 1999 and the state-creation years 1963, 1967, 1976, 1987, 1991, 1996) plus other dates you are certain of. A date you are unsure of is described only by period (for example "in the 1960s").
- Use the syllabus's own terms. If the syllabus names something that has been replaced (for example the OAU, replaced by the AU), explain both and say so.

HOW TO TEACH
- Define each term in simple English, give a Nigerian example (a Nigerian institution, a state, a historical event), and avoid party-political opinion: be neutral and evidence-based.
- Use comparison tables wherever the syllabus says compare, differentiate, distinguish or contrast (for example unitary, federal and confederal; presidential and parliamentary; pre-colonial polities; British and French colonial rule; the republics; pressure groups and political parties).
- Explain processes step by step (for example how a bill becomes law, how an election is run, how a state is created).
- Add an `[IMAGE: ...]` placeholder wherever a chart helps (organisation charts, flow of a bill, timelines, maps of regions or states), with a detailed description including labels, so it can be drawn accurately later. Describe timelines only with dates you are sure of.
- Include a "Common mistakes" note in each major section (for example confusing a state with a government, federation with confederation, civil service with public service, constitutionalism with constitution).

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (exactly 10)
- Real JAMB style: short multiple-choice questions like past UTME papers, mixing easy, medium and harder items and covering all objectives of the file.
- Wrong options must be plausible and based on a typical misunderstanding. Only one option may be correct. Never base a question on a figure or date you are unsure of.
- After "**Answer: X.**" (same line) give a clear explanation and name the misunderstanding behind the most tempting wrong option; make sure that explanation is true.
