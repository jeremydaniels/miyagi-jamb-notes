## IRK-specific rules (these override the style example wherever they differ)

QUR'AN AND HADITH REFERENCES
- Every Qur'an reference must be exact: surah number and name, and ayah number, written as in the syllabus (for example Q.2:255). Never invent or guess a reference. If you are not certain which ayah says something, cite the surah only.
- The syllabus prints some references oddly or wrongly (see the transcription notes you are given). Give the reference as printed, say that it looks like a misprint, and give the usual reference (for example the verse on theft is usually cited as Q.5:38 while the syllabus prints Q.5:41). Do not silently change a reference.
- Quote only short passages (a few words to one ayah) from a well-known public-domain English translation: Marmaduke Pickthall, "The Meaning of the Glorious Koran" (1930). Say "Pickthall's translation". Summarise everything else in your own words. Never paste long passages. Verse numbering follows the standard Qur'an numbering; some translations count the Basmalah as ayah 1 in Surah 1.
- Hadith: give the collection (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, al-Nasa'i, Malik's Muwatta, an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith) and, only when certain, the number; numbering differs between editions, so say "numbering varies by edition". For an-Nawawi's hadiths use his numbering. Quote at most a short sentence; otherwise paraphrase. Never invent a hadith or an isnad; if the syllabus gives a partial wording, quote only what it prints.
- Arabic text: for the topics that require studying the Arabic text of suwar with tajwid, give the Arabic only for very short suwar and short ayat where you are certain of the standard (Hafs/Uthmani) text; for longer suwar give transliteration of key phrases and a summary. Write each Arabic word, transliteration and translation separately. A wrong Arabic letter is worse than none. Say that students must learn recitation from a qualified teacher.
- Use consistent transliteration (the syllabus writes the letter ayn as a raised c); mention common spelling variants (Qur'an/Koran, Hadith/Hadeeth, Salah/Salat, Zakah/Zakat, Hajj/Haj).

PRESENTATION
- Neutral, as the syllabus is: describe what the Qur'an and Sunnah teach and how Muslims generally practise, and where the four Sunni schools (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) or scholars differ (for example in details of salah, zakah, divorce and inheritance), say so. Do not argue for or against any belief. Use "Muslims believe", "Islamic teaching holds".
- Write "Prophet Muhammad (SAW)" as the syllabus does at first mention, and use respectful phrasing throughout.
- Use Nigerian examples where they help (Muslim communities in the north and south-west, the Sokoto caliphate, Kano, Ilorin, Nigerian pilgrims on Hajj, zakah and sadaqah in the community), only when you are certain of the facts.
- Use comparison tables wherever the syllabus says compare, differentiate or distinguish (for example zakah and sadaqah, Hajj and Umrah, Makkan and Madinan suwar, types of divorce, the Islamic and Western economic or political systems).
- Dates: use Hijrah and Gregorian dates only when certain; otherwise describe the order of events.

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (exactly 10)
- JAMB style multiple choice. Wrong options plausible; one correct answer only. Test only facts you are certain of. Never ask for an ayah or hadith number unless you are certain.
- After "**Answer: X.**" (same line) give a short explanation that cites the source.
