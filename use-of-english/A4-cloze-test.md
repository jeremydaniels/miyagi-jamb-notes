# JAMB Use of English Notes: The Cloze Test

## What JAMB expects

This topic is part of Section A of the JAMB Use of English syllabus: Comprehension and Summary. The syllabus says that of the passages set, "one will be a cloze test", that each passage "should reflect various disciplines" and that each is "about 200 words long". In the examination (Section D), there is **1 cloze passage with 10 questions**, which makes it the single biggest block of questions in Section A.

The cloze test draws on all four Section A objectives, especially:

- objective i: **identify main points/topic sentences** (you must know what the passage is about to fill the gaps);
- objective ii: **determine implied meanings**;
- objective iii: **identify the grammatical functions of words, phrases and clauses**;
- content item (ii)(c): **coherence and logical reasoning**.

It also tests many Section B skills (vocabulary, collocation, tense, concord, prepositions, linking words). Those are taught in detail in the B files; this file shows you how to use them in a cloze passage.

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Explain what a cloze test is and how JAMB sets it.
2. Recognise the different kinds of gap (vocabulary, grammar, linking, register).
3. Use context clues before and after a gap to choose the right word.
4. Follow a step-by-step method to answer all 10 questions accurately.
5. Avoid the common traps in cloze options.

## What is a cloze test?

A **cloze test** is a passage from which some words have been removed and replaced with numbered gaps. For each gap you are given four options (A to D), and you must choose the one that **best fits both the grammar and the meaning** of the passage.

The name comes from the idea of **closure**: the human mind's habit of completing something that is incomplete. When you see "Bread and ____", your mind supplies "butter". A cloze test checks whether your mind supplies the right word in English.

A typical JAMB instruction reads like this (in spirit):

> In the passage below, the numbered gaps indicate missing words. Against each number in the list below the passage, options are given in columns A to D. Choose the word that is the most suitable to fill the numbered gap in the passage.

### Why "various disciplines" matters

The syllabus says passages "should reflect various disciplines". This means the cloze passage may be about agriculture, banking, medicine, law, sport, religion, computing, the military, journalism, building and so on. Many gaps test the **special vocabulary (register)** of that field. For example:

| Discipline | Typical register words that may be tested |
| --- | --- |
| Agriculture | harvest, yield, fertiliser, irrigation, pest, crop rotation, tillage, livestock |
| Medicine/health | diagnosis, symptoms, prescription, surgeon, ward, outpatient, vaccine, epidemic |
| Banking/finance | deposit, withdrawal, interest, loan, collateral, overdraft, account, teller |
| Law | plaintiff, defendant, verdict, adjourned, counsel, bail, sentence, witness |
| Education | curriculum, syllabus, semester, lecturer, matriculation, convocation, examination |
| Sport | referee, penalty, fixture, tournament, spectators, coach, trophy, foul |
| Journalism/media | editor, headline, broadcast, correspondent, column, press, publication |
| Religion | congregation, sermon, pilgrimage, worship, clergy, mosque, cathedral |
| Building/engineering | foundation, contractor, blueprint, bricklayer, scaffolding, site, architect |
| Computing | software, data, download, network, password, browser, upload |

If you know the passage is about a court, a word like "verdict" is far more likely than "result", even though both mean "outcome".

## The different kinds of gap

Most gaps in a cloze passage fall into a few types. Learn to recognise them quickly.

### 1. Vocabulary (lexical) gaps

The options are all the same part of speech (for example, all nouns), and only one has the right **meaning** for the context.

> The farmers were happy because the rains came early and the ____ was the best in ten years.
> A. crop B. harvest C. yield D. plant

Both "harvest" and "yield" could fit; "crop" also could. When several look possible, look for **extra clues**: "the best in ten years" suggests the amount gathered, so **harvest** or **yield**. If the next sentence says "they gathered twice as many bags of maize", either is good, and JAMB would normally not give two correct options; so read the whole passage to see which one the writer uses elsewhere or which collocates best.

### 2. Collocation gaps

A **collocation** is a pair or group of words that naturally go together in English. You "**make** a mistake", not "do a mistake"; you "**pay** attention", not "give attention" (in the fixed expression); "**heavy** rain", not "strong rain".

| Correct collocation | Common wrong form |
| --- | --- |
| make a decision | take a decision (also used in British English; see note) |
| commit a crime | do a crime |
| heavy traffic | big traffic |
| strong tea | powerful tea |
| pass an examination | succeed an examination |
| lay the foundation | put the foundation |
| raise a family | grow a family |
| deliver a lecture | give out a lecture |

**Note:** "take a decision" and "make a decision" are both used in British English; "make a decision" is more common worldwide. JAMB would not normally give both as options.

### 3. Grammatical gaps

The options test grammar rather than meaning:

| Grammar point | Example gap | Answer and reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tense | Yesterday, the principal ____ the new hall. (opens / opened / has opened / will open) | **opened**: "yesterday" needs the simple past |
| Concord | Each of the students ____ a laptop. (have / has / are having / were having) | **has**: "each" is singular |
| Prepositions | She is good ____ Mathematics. (in / on / at / with) | **at**: "good at" |
| Articles | He is ____ honest man. (a / an / the / no article) | **an**: "honest" begins with a vowel sound |
| Pronouns | The committee submitted ____ report. (its / their / it's / theirs) | **its**: committee acting as one unit (British usage also allows "their"; see B5b) |
| Relative pronouns | The man ____ car was stolen reported to the police. (who / whom / whose / which) | **whose**: possession |
| Verb forms | She insisted on ____ the bill. (pay / paid / paying / to pay) | **paying**: after a preposition, use the -ing form |

### 4. Linking (connective) gaps

The options are linking words, and you must see the **logical relationship** between ideas (content item (ii)(c): coherence and logical reasoning).

| Relationship | Linking words |
| --- | --- |
| Addition | and, also, moreover, furthermore, in addition, besides |
| Contrast | but, however, although, though, yet, nevertheless, whereas, on the other hand |
| Cause | because, since, as, owing to, due to |
| Result | so, therefore, thus, consequently, as a result, hence |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, as long as |
| Time/sequence | first, then, after, before, finally, meanwhile, eventually |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as |
| Concession | although, even though, despite, in spite of |

> The road was badly damaged. ____, the bus arrived on time.
> A. Therefore B. Because C. Nevertheless D. Moreover

The second sentence is **surprising** given the first, so we need contrast: **Nevertheless** (C). "Therefore" would mean the bad road caused the bus to arrive on time, which is illogical.

### 5. Gaps that depend on the whole passage

Some gaps can only be filled by understanding the **main idea** or by looking at a word used earlier or later. For example, if paragraph 1 is about a **drought**, a later gap is unlikely to be "flood".

## Using context clues

The words around a gap are your clues. Use them in this order.

### Clue 1: The words immediately before and after

These usually tell you the **part of speech** needed.

- "the ____ of the river" → a noun
- "she ____ quickly" → a verb
- "a very ____ man" → an adjective
- "he ran ____" → probably an adverb

### Clue 2: The rest of the sentence

The sentence tells you the **meaning** and the **grammar** (tense, number).

> The doctor examined the patient and ____ some drugs.
> "examined" is past tense, so we need a past form: **prescribed**.

### Clue 3: Neighbouring sentences

The sentences before and after the gap may give a synonym, an antonym or an explanation.

> The new manager was very ____. Unlike her predecessor, she never lost her temper, even when workers were rude to her.
> The next sentence explains the gap: **patient** or **calm** (not "strict", not "angry").

### Clue 4: The whole passage

The topic and the writer's attitude limit the choices. A passage praising a policy will not suddenly call it "disastrous".

### Clue 5: Repeated words and patterns

Writers often repeat key words. If the passage talks about "the borrower" and "the loan" in paragraph 1, a later gap may be "**lender**" or "**interest**".

## Step-by-step method for the cloze passage

### Step 1: Read the whole passage first, ignoring the gaps

Do not look at the options yet. Get the **topic**, the **discipline** (register) and the **general direction** of the passage. Many candidates rush straight to gap 1 and lose the meaning.

### Step 2: Identify the type of each gap

Ask: Is this a vocabulary gap, a collocation, a grammar point, or a linking word?

### Step 3: Predict before you look

Cover the options and think of a word that would fit. Then look for it, or something close to it, in the options. This prevents the options from confusing you.

### Step 4: Test each option in the sentence

Read the full sentence with each option in the gap. Ask:

- Is it **grammatically** correct (tense, number, part of speech, preposition)?
- Does it make **sense** in the sentence?
- Does it fit the **paragraph and the passage**?
- Does it **collocate** naturally with the words around it?

### Step 5: Eliminate

Cross out options that fail any test. Often two can be eliminated on grammar alone, leaving two that must be decided by meaning.

### Step 6: Re-read the completed passage

At the end, read the passage again with all your answers in place. Does it flow? Are the tenses consistent? If something sounds wrong, check it again.

## Worked example: a full cloze passage

Read this original passage, then follow the reasoning for each gap.

> Every year, thousands of young Nigerians apply for admission into universities, but only a fraction are **(1)** ____. One reason is that the number of places is **(2)** ____ compared with the number of applicants. **(3)** ____, many candidates do not meet the required scores in the examinations.
>
> To improve their chances, wise candidates begin their preparation **(4)** ____. They study the syllabus carefully, practise past questions and **(5)** ____ their weak areas. Some join study groups, **(6)** ____ others prefer to read alone.
>
> Parents also have a part to play. They can provide a quiet place for study and **(7)** ____ their children when they are discouraged. A candidate who **(8)** ____ support at home is more likely to remain focused.
>
> Finally, candidates must avoid examination **(9)** ____. Those who are caught risk having their results **(10)** ____ and may be barred from future examinations.

| No. | A | B | C | D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | admitted | accepted into | rejected | dismissed |
| 2 | large | limited | enough | plenty |
| 3 | Moreover | However | Therefore | Instead |
| 4 | lately | late | early | earlier |
| 5 | strengthen | weakening | strong | strength |
| 6 | because | while | so | unless |
| 7 | discourage | encourage | courage | encouraging |
| 8 | receive | receives | receiving | have received |
| 9 | malpractice | practice | mistake | malpractise |
| 10 | cancelled | cancel | cancelling | cancellation |

**Gap 1:** "only a fraction are ____" needs a past participle that matches "apply for admission". "Accepted into" leaves a dangling preposition (accepted into what?). "Rejected" and "dismissed" contradict the meaning (most applicants are turned away, so "only a fraction" must be the ones let in). "Admitted" also collocates with "admission". **Answer: A (admitted).**

**Gap 2:** The sentence explains why few are admitted. The places must be **limited**. "Large", "enough" and "plenty" give the opposite sense; "plenty" is also ungrammatical after "is" here. **Answer: B.**

**Gap 3:** This sentence adds a **second reason**. We need an addition word: **Moreover**. "However" signals contrast; "Therefore" signals result; "Instead" signals replacement. **Answer: A.**

**Gap 4:** "begin their preparation ____" needs an adverb of time. "Wise candidates" start **early**. "Earlier" needs a comparison (earlier than what?). "Late" and "lately" give the wrong meaning ("lately" means "recently"). **Answer: C.**

**Gap 5:** Parallel structure: "They study…, practise… and ____". We need a base-form verb like "study" and "practise": **strengthen**. **Answer: A.**

**Gap 6:** Two contrasting groups: some do X, others do Y. **While** (meaning "whereas") shows the contrast. **Answer: B.**

**Gap 7:** Parallel with "provide": base-form verb. Parents should **encourage** discouraged children. **Answer: B.**

**Gap 8:** "A candidate who ____" is singular, third person, present (general truth). **receives**. **Answer: B.**

**Gap 9:** The fixed expression is **examination malpractice**. "Malpractise" is not a standard English word. "Practice" and "mistake" do not give the meaning of cheating. **Answer: A.**

**Gap 10:** "having their results ____" uses the structure **have + object + past participle** (causative/passive meaning): "having their results cancelled". **Answer: A.**

## Common traps in cloze options

### Trap 1: The right meaning, wrong form

The options may all come from the same root word: **encourage, encouraging, courage, encouragement**. Only one has the right **part of speech** for the gap. Decide the part of speech first.

### Trap 2: The right form, wrong meaning

All four options may be past-tense verbs, for example. Then the meaning decides.

### Trap 3: Near-synonyms that do not collocate

> He ____ a crime. (did / made / committed / performed) → **committed**.

All four can mean "carry out", but only one collocates with "crime".

### Trap 4: Words that look alike (confusables)

| Pair | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| affect / effect | affect = verb (to influence); effect = usually a noun (a result) |
| principal / principle | principal = head of a school, or main; principle = a rule or belief |
| stationary / stationery | stationary = not moving; stationery = writing materials |
| advice / advise | advice = noun; advise = verb |
| practice / practise | British: practice = noun, practise = verb (American uses "practice" for both) |
| loose / lose | loose = not tight; lose = to misplace or fail to win |
| council / counsel | council = a governing group; counsel = advice, or a lawyer |
| complement / compliment | complement = complete; compliment = praise |
| eminent / imminent | eminent = famous; imminent = about to happen |
| economic / economical | economic = to do with the economy; economical = saving money |

### Trap 5: Linking words with the wrong logic

Choosing "therefore" when the ideas contrast, or "however" when they agree. Always ask: **what is the relationship between the two ideas?**

### Trap 6: Ignoring the tense of the passage

If the passage is narrated in the past tense, a present-tense option is usually wrong, unless the sentence states a general truth.

### Trap 7: Reading only the sentence with the gap

Sometimes the clue is two sentences away. Read before and after.

## Worked example 2: register (banking)

> Mrs Okon went to the bank to **(1)** ____ some money from her savings account. The **(2)** ____ asked for her identification and checked her **(3)** ____ before giving her the cash. She later applied for a **(4)** ____ to expand her shop, and the bank asked her to provide **(5)** ____ in case she could not repay.

| No. | A | B | C | D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | withdraw | remove | collect out | draw out of |
| 2 | cashier | customer | depositor | borrower |
| 3 | balance | money | weight | budget |
| 4 | loan | gift | grant | salary |
| 5 | collateral | interest | deposit | profit |

- **Gap 1: withdraw (A).** The banking term for taking money out of an account. "Remove" is not the register word; C and D are ungrammatical in this sentence.
- **Gap 2: cashier (A).** The bank worker who pays out cash. ("Teller" means the same; JAMB would not offer both.) A customer, depositor or borrower would not check her identification.
- **Gap 3: balance (A).** The amount in an account is its balance.
- **Gap 4: loan (A).** Money borrowed and repaid. A grant is not repaid.
- **Gap 5: collateral (A).** Property pledged to secure a loan. "Interest" is the charge on a loan, not security.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Cloze test | A passage with numbered gaps, each to be filled from four options |
| Closure | The mind's tendency to complete something incomplete; the origin of the name "cloze" |
| Context clue | Information in the surrounding words or sentences that helps you choose the right word |
| Collocation | Words that naturally go together, e.g. "commit a crime", "heavy rain" |
| Register | The special vocabulary of a field or situation, e.g. banking, law, medicine |
| Linking word (connective) | A word that shows the relationship between ideas, e.g. however, therefore |
| Parallel structure | Using the same grammatical form for items in a list or series |
| Part of speech | The word class of a word: noun, verb, adjective, adverb, etc. |
| Confusables | Words that look or sound alike but differ in meaning, e.g. affect/effect |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Read the entire passage before filling any gap.** The meaning of the whole helps with each part.
- **Decide the part of speech first**, then the meaning.
- **Predict, then check.** Think of your own word before looking at the options.
- **Watch the tense of the passage** and keep it consistent.
- **Check concord**: singular subjects take singular verbs (see B5b).
- **Identify the register.** If the passage is about a hospital, prefer hospital words.
- **Use British spelling and usage**: practise (verb), licence (noun), programme (TV, event).
- **Do not leave gaps blank.** There is no negative marking in JAMB; eliminate and make your best choice.
- **Re-read the completed passage** to make sure it flows.

## Quick summary

1. A cloze test is a passage of about 200 words with numbered gaps; JAMB sets 1 cloze passage with 10 questions.
2. Gaps test vocabulary, collocation, grammar (tense, concord, prepositions, articles, pronouns) and linking words.
3. Passages come from various disciplines, so knowing the register (special vocabulary) of each field is important.
4. Use context clues: the words around the gap, the sentence, neighbouring sentences and the whole passage.
5. Method: read the whole passage, identify the gap type, predict, test each option, eliminate, then re-read.
6. Common traps include right meaning but wrong form, near-synonyms that do not collocate, confusable words and wrong linking logic.

## Practice questions

Read the passage below and choose the option that best fills each numbered gap.

> Malaria remains one of the most serious health problems in Nigeria. The disease is **(1)** ____ by a parasite that enters the body through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito. Its common **(2)** ____ include fever, headache, chills and body pains.
>
> Children under five and pregnant women are especially at risk. **(3)** ____, health workers advise families to sleep under insecticide-treated nets and to clear stagnant water where mosquitoes **(4)** ____.
>
> When a person shows signs of malaria, he or she should be **(5)** ____ at a clinic before treatment begins, because many fevers have other causes. Self-medication can be dangerous, **(6)** ____ the wrong drug may be taken. Patients who **(7)** ____ the full course of prescribed drugs are more likely to recover completely.
>
> With better prevention and **(8)** ____ treatment, the number of deaths could be greatly **(9)** ____. Government, health workers and families must work together to **(10)** ____ this goal.

**1. Gap (1)**

- A. carried
- B. caused
- C. made
- D. brought

**Answer: B.** A disease is "caused by" a parasite. The parasite is carried by the mosquito, not the disease "carried by a parasite".

**2. Gap (2)**

- A. symptoms
- B. signals
- C. signatures
- D. signposts

**Answer: A.** "Symptoms" is the medical register word for signs of a disease.

**3. Gap (3)**

- A. However
- B. Nevertheless
- C. Therefore
- D. Although

**Answer: C.** The advice is a result of the risk just stated, so a result connector is needed. "Although" cannot begin this sentence structure.

**4. Gap (4)**

- A. breed
- B. breeds
- C. bred
- D. breeding

**Answer: A.** "Mosquitoes" is plural and the sentence states a general truth, so the plural present form "breed" is needed.

**5. Gap (5)**

- A. tested
- B. testing
- C. test
- D. tests

**Answer: A.** "Should be" + past participle forms the passive: "should be tested".

**6. Gap (6)**

- A. unless
- B. since
- C. although
- D. whereas

**Answer: B.** The clause gives a reason, and "since" means "because" here.

**7. Gap (7)**

- A. complete
- B. completes
- C. completing
- D. completion

**Answer: A.** "Patients who" is plural, so the plural present verb "complete" is needed.

**8. Gap (8)**

- A. prompt
- B. late
- C. slow
- D. careless

**Answer: A.** The writer wants deaths reduced, so the treatment must be prompt (quick). The others give the opposite sense.

**9. Gap (9)**

- A. reduce
- B. reducing
- C. reduced
- D. reduction

**Answer: C.** "Could be greatly ____" needs a past participle for the passive: "could be greatly reduced".

**10. Gap (10)**

- A. achieve
- B. achieving
- C. achieved
- D. achievement

**Answer: A.** "To" + base form of the verb: "to achieve this goal".
