# JAMB Use of English Notes: Spelling

## What JAMB expects

This topic is part of Section B of the JAMB Use of English syllabus: Lexis and Structure. It covers the spelling part of content item (f), **mechanics**. (Punctuation and capitalisation, the other parts of mechanics, are in B6b-punctuation-and-capitalisation.md.)

It serves this objective directly:

- **Objective iii:** differentiate between correct and incorrect spellings.

The exam structure (Section D of the syllabus) does not give spelling its own number of questions. Spelling questions are part of "Basic Grammar" (10 questions in Section B). Spelling also matters in every other part of the paper: in a cloze or synonym question, an option that is misspelt is often a wrong option. Typical spelling questions look like this:

> Choose the option that is correctly spelt.
> A. accomodation B. acommodation C. accommodation D. accommodatian

> Choose the option that is wrongly spelt.
> A. receive B. separate C. goverment D. necessary

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

1. Explain why English spelling is difficult, and use rules to make it easier.
2. Follow British spelling, which is the standard for JAMB, and recognise American spellings.
3. Explain the *-ise* and *-ize* question in British English.
4. Apply the main spelling rules: doubling the final consonant, dropping *-e*, changing *-y* to *-i*, *ie* and *ei*, prefixes and suffixes.
5. Spell the words that Nigerian candidates most often get wrong.
6. Tell apart words that sound alike but are spelt differently and mean different things.
7. Tackle JAMB spelling questions step by step.

## Why English spelling is hard

English spelling does not always match pronunciation. There are several reasons:

- **Borrowed words.** English has taken words from Latin, Greek, French and many other languages, and often keeps their original spelling: *psychology* (Greek), *rendezvous* (French), *ballet* (French).
- **Sound changes.** Pronunciation has changed over hundreds of years, but spelling has stayed the same. The *k* in *knife* and *knee* was once pronounced.
- **Silent letters.** *debt, doubt, island, Wednesday, receipt, honest*.
- **One sound, many spellings.** The sound /iː/ is spelt in many ways: *see, sea, field, receive, machine, people, key*.

Because of this, you cannot spell English by sound alone. You need **rules** (which work most of the time) and **memory** (for the exceptions).

## Standard British spelling: the JAMB standard

Nigeria follows **British** spelling in schools, government and examinations. JAMB tests **Standard British English** (the syllabus says this directly for idioms, and the same standard applies to spelling).

American spelling is correct in the United States, but in a Nigerian exam you should always use the British form. If a question asks for the "correctly spelt" word, it will normally not include an American spelling as one of the options, because that would make the question unclear.

### British vs American spelling

| Pattern | British (use in JAMB) | American |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -our / -or | colour, honour, labour, neighbour, favour, behaviour, rumour, harbour, vapour | color, honor, labor, neighbor, favor, behavior, rumor, harbor, vapor |
| -re / -er | centre, theatre, metre (unit), litre, fibre, calibre, meagre, sombre | center, theater, meter, liter, fiber, caliber, meager, somber |
| -ce / -se (nouns) | defence, offence, pretence, licence (noun) | defense, offense, pretense, license |
| -ll- / -l- (before suffixes) | travelled, cancelled, labelled, modelled, jeweller, traveller, counselling | traveled, canceled, labeled, modeled, jeweler, traveler, counseling |
| -l / -ll (at the end) | fulfil, enrol, instil, distil, skilful, wilful | fulfill, enroll, instill, distill, skillful, willful |
| -ogue / -og | catalogue, dialogue, analogue, monologue | catalog (also catalogue), dialog (also dialogue in most uses) |
| -yse / -yze | analyse, paralyse, catalyse | analyze, paralyze, catalyze |
| ae / oe / e | anaemia, haemoglobin, paediatrics, oesophagus, manoeuvre, foetus | anemia, hemoglobin, pediatrics, esophagus, maneuver, fetus |
| -mme / -m | programme (a plan or TV show) | program |
| -que / -ck | cheque (for money) | check |
| Other | grey, tyre, kerb, plough, mould, moustache, pyjamas, aluminium, jewellery, storey (of a building), sceptical, draught (of air) | gray, tire, curb, plow, mold, mustache, pajamas, aluminum, jewelry, story, skeptical, draft |

**Notes on the table:**

- **Programme / program:** in British English, a *computer program* is spelt *program*. All other meanings use *programme* (*a TV programme*, *a degree programme*).
- **Metre / meter:** in British English, *metre* is the unit of length; *meter* is a measuring device (*a gas meter*, *a prepaid meter*). Nigerians say "prepaid meter" correctly.
- **Judgement / judgment:** British English usually uses *judgement* in general use, and *judgment* in legal writing. Both are correct in British English. *Acknowledgement* and *acknowledgment* are also both accepted.
- **Ageing / aging:** British English prefers *ageing*; *aging* is American and is also seen in Britain.

### Practice and practise; licence and license

In British English, the **noun** ends in **-ce** and the **verb** ends in **-se**. (American English uses *practice* for both and *license* for both.)

| Noun (-ce) | Verb (-se) |
| --- | --- |
| I go to football **practice** on Saturdays. | I **practise** football every Saturday. |
| A doctor's **practice** | She **practises** medicine in Enugu. |
| A driving **licence** | The government **licensed** the new radio station. |
| | a **licensed** pharmacist |

**Memory trick:** *advice* (noun) and *advise* (verb) follow the same pattern, and you can **hear** the difference in those two: *advice* ends in /s/, *advise* ends in /z/. So: *c* for the noun, *s* for the verb.

The same noun/verb pattern: *device* (noun) / *devise* (verb); *prophecy* (noun) / *prophesy* (verb).

- *He gave me good **advice**. He **advised** me to read.*
- *The **prophecy** came true. The pastor **prophesied** rain.*

## The -ise and -ize question (textbook variation)

**This is an area where British books and dictionaries genuinely differ. Read it carefully.**

Many verbs can end in **-ise** or **-ize**: *organise/organize, realise/realize, recognise/recognize, criticise/criticize, apologise/apologize, civilisation/civilization, specialise/specialize, emphasise/emphasize, memorise/memorize*.

| Spelling system | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Many British newspapers, schools and publishers | **-ise** | organise, realise, recognise |
| **Oxford spelling** (Oxford University Press, the *Oxford English Dictionary*, many international organisations such as the United Nations) | **-ize** for words that come from the Greek ending *-izein* | organize, realize, recognize |
| American English | **-ize** | organize, realize, recognize |

**Both -ise and -ize are correct in British English.** The *-ize* spelling is not "American only": it is the traditional Oxford University Press style, and it is fully British. What matters is to be **consistent** within one piece of writing.

**Exam advice:** a well-set JAMB question will not offer both *organise* and *organize* as options, because both are correct. If you meet only one of them among the options, it is correct.

### Words that are ALWAYS -ise (even in Oxford spelling and American English)

These are not formed from the Greek *-izein*, so they never take *-ize*:

*advertise, advise, apprise, arise, chastise, comprise, compromise, demise, despise, devise, disguise, enterprise, excise, exercise, franchise, improvise, incise, merchandise, premise, revise, supervise, surmise, surprise, televise*

- **Wrong:** "advertize", "supervize", "surprize", "exercize", "televize"
- **Right:** *advertise, supervise, surprise, exercise, televise*

### Words that are ALWAYS -yse in British English

*analyse, paralyse, catalyse, electrolyse, breathalyse* (American: *analyze, paralyze*). The Oxford spelling also uses *analyse*.

### Words that are ALWAYS -ize

*capsize* and *size* (and *prize* in the sense of a reward) are always spelt with *z*.

## Rule 1: Doubling the final consonant

When you add a suffix that begins with a **vowel** (*-ing, -ed, -er, -est, -able, -ance*), you sometimes double the last consonant of the word.

### One-syllable words

Double the final consonant if the word ends in **one vowel + one consonant**:

| Word | + -ing | + -ed / -er |
| --- | --- | --- |
| run | running | runner |
| stop | stopping | stopped |
| sit | sitting | sitter |
| plan | planning | planned |
| hop | hopping | hopped |
| big | — | bigger, biggest |
| swim | swimming | swimmer |
| shop | shopping | shopped |

**Do not double** when:

- the word ends in **two consonants**: *help → helping*, *jump → jumped*
- the word has **two vowels** before the consonant: *read → reading*, *rain → rained*, *cool → cooler*
- the word ends in **w, x or y**: *show → showing*, *fix → fixed*, *play → played*
- the suffix starts with a **consonant**: *ship → shipment*, *glad → gladness*, *sad → sadly*

**Compare:**

| Short vowel (double) | Long vowel with silent *e* (do not double; drop *e*) |
| --- | --- |
| hop → hop**p**ing | hope → hoping |
| plan → plan**n**ed | plane → planed |
| din → din**n**ing (making a din) | dine → dining (eating) |
| scrap → scrap**p**ing | scrape → scraping |
| star → star**r**ing | stare → staring |

### Words of two or more syllables

Double the final consonant only if the **stress is on the last syllable** (marked here in capitals).

| Word (stress) | Double? | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| beGIN | Yes | beginning, beginner |
| ocCUR | Yes | occurred, occurring, occurrence |
| preFER | Yes | preferred, preferring |
| reFER | Yes | referred, referring, referral |
| adMIT | Yes | admitted, admitting, admittance |
| comMIT | Yes | committed, committee, commitment (no: suffix starts with a consonant) |
| conTROL | Yes | controlled, controlling, controller |
| forGET | Yes | forgetting, forgettable |
| reGRET | Yes | regretted, regrettable |
| equIP | Yes | equipped, equipping (but *equipment*: consonant suffix) |
| VISit | No | visited, visiting, visitor |
| OFFer | No | offered, offering |
| LISten | No | listened, listener |
| HAPpen | No | happened, happening |
| DEVelop | No | developed, development |
| TARget | No | targeted, targeting |
| BUDget | No | budgeted, budgeting |
| LIMit | No | limited, limitation |
| GALlop | No | galloped, galloping |

**When the stress moves**, the doubling depends on the new word:

- *preFER → preFERRED* (double), but *PREFerence, PREFerable* (single *r*, because the stress has moved to the first syllable)
- *reFER → reFERRED, reFERRal*, but *REFerence* (single *r*)
- *conFER → conFERRED*, but *CONference*

**Textbook variation:** some words are spelt both ways in British English. *Benefit* gives *benefited* (more common) or *benefitted*; *focus* gives *focused* (more common) or *focussed*; *bias* gives *biased* or *biassed* (rare). Exams avoid these.

### The British -l rule

In British English, a final **-l** after a single vowel is **doubled** before a vowel suffix, **even when the stress is not on the last syllable**. American English does not double it.

| Word | British | American |
| --- | --- | --- |
| travel | travelled, travelling, traveller | traveled, traveling, traveler |
| cancel | cancelled, cancelling, cancellation (both) | canceled, canceling, cancellation |
| label | labelled, labelling | labeled, labeling |
| model | modelled, modelling | modeled, modeling |
| quarrel | quarrelled, quarrelling | quarreled, quarreling |
| signal | signalled, signalling | signaled, signaling |
| marvel | marvelled, marvellous | marveled, marvelous |
| jewel | jeweller, jewellery | jeweler, jewelry |
| counsel | counselled, counsellor | counseled, counselor |
| dial | dialled, dialling | dialed, dialing |
| fuel | fuelled, fuelling | fueled, fueling |

**But:** *parallel → paralleled* (an exception in British English as well), and *appeal → appealed* (two vowels before *l*: no doubling).

A few other words also double in British English despite first-syllable stress: *worship → worshipped, worshipper*; *kidnap → kidnapped, kidnapper*; *handicap → handicapped*.

## Rule 2: Words ending in silent -e

### Drop the -e before a suffix that begins with a vowel

| Word | Suffix | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| make | -ing | making |
| write | -ing | writing (one *t*) |
| come | -ing | coming |
| hope | -ed | hoped |
| love | -able | lovable (also *loveable*) |
| believe | -able | believable |
| use | -able | usable |
| value | -able | valuable |
| move | -able | movable (also *moveable*) |
| fame | -ous | famous |
| desire | -ous | desirous |
| arrive | -al | arrival |
| guide | -ance | guidance |
| sense | -ible | sensible |

**Very common error:** "writting" (wrong). *Write* has a long vowel, so you drop *e* and do **not** double: *writing*. But *written* has a short vowel, so it doubles: *written*.

### Keep the -e before a suffix that begins with a consonant

| Word | Suffix | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hope | -ful | hopeful |
| care | -ful | careful |
| excite | -ment | excitement |
| manage | -ment | management |
| love | -ly | lovely |
| safe | -ty | safety |
| use | -less | useless |
| complete | -ly | completely |
| sincere | -ly | sincerely |
| definite | -ly | definitely |

**Exceptions** (the *-e* is dropped even before a consonant):

*argue → argument*; *true → truly*; *due → duly*; *whole → wholly*; *nine → ninth*; *awe → awful*.

*Judge → judgement* (British, keeps the *e*) / *judgment* (also correct). *Acknowledge → acknowledgement* / *acknowledgment*.

### Keep the -e after soft *c* or soft *g* before *-able* and *-ous*

The *e* keeps the *c* sounding /s/ and the *g* sounding /dʒ/.

| Word | Result |
| --- | --- |
| notice | noticeable |
| peace | peaceable |
| replace | replaceable |
| change | changeable |
| manage | manageable |
| knowledge | knowledgeable |
| courage | courageous |
| outrage | outrageous |
| advantage | advantageous |

**Common errors:** "noticable", "managable", "knowledgable", "couragous" are all **wrong**.

### Keep the -e to avoid confusion

- *dye → dyeing* (colouring cloth) vs *die → dying* (losing life)
- *singe → singeing* (slightly burning) vs *sing → singing*
- *see → seeing*, *agree → agreeing*, *flee → fleeing* (double *ee* stays)
- *canoe → canoeing*, *hoe → hoeing*, *shoe → shoeing*

### Words ending in -ie: change to -y before -ing

- *die → dying*, *lie → lying*, *tie → tying*, *vie → vying*

## Rule 3: Words ending in -y

### Consonant + y: change y to i (except before -ing)

| Word | Suffix | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| happy | -ness, -ly, -er | happiness, happily, happier |
| carry | -ed, -er, -es | carried, carrier, carries |
| study | -ed, -es, -ous | studied, studies, studious |
| beauty | -ful | beautiful |
| pity | -ful | pitiful |
| mercy | -ful, -less | merciful, merciless |
| busy | -ness, -ly | business, busily |
| marry | -age | marriage |
| apply | -cation | application |
| deny | -al | denial |
| rely | -able | reliable |
| vary | -ous | various |

**But keep the y before -ing** (otherwise you would get *ii*):

*carry → carrying*; *study → studying*; *try → trying*; *cry → crying*; *fly → flying*; *apply → applying*; *marry → marrying*

Also keep the *y* before *-ish* and in a few short words: *babyish*; *shy → shyly, shyness*; *dry → dryness, dryly* (also *drily*); *sly → slyly*.

### Vowel + y: keep the y

*play → played, player, playful*; *enjoy → enjoyed, enjoyment*; *obey → obeyed*; *employ → employer*; *pray → prayed*; *convey → conveyed*; *destroy → destroyed*; *key → keys*; *journey → journeys*

**Exceptions:** *say → said*; *pay → paid*; *lay → laid*; *day → daily*; *gay → gaily*.

## Rule 4: *ie* and *ei*

The traditional rule is:

> **"I before E, except after C, when the sound is EE."**

### *i* before *e*

*believe, achieve, chief, field, piece, niece, relief, relieve, grief, grieve, brief, siege, yield, shield, thief, priest, fierce, pierce, retrieve, besiege, mischief*

### *e* before *i* after *c* (sound /iː/)

*receive, receipt, deceive, deceit, conceive, conceit, perceive, ceiling*

### *ei* when the sound is "ay" (/eɪ/)

*eight, weigh, weight, neighbour, vein, reign, rein, freight, sleigh, beige, veil, feign, heinous* (pronunciations vary for *heinous*; the spelling does not)

### Important exceptions (learn by heart)

| Word | Why it is an exception |
| --- | --- |
| seize | *ei* not after *c*, with an /iː/ sound |
| weird | *ei* not after *c* |
| protein, caffeine, codeine | *ei* not after *c* |
| either, neither | *ei* not after *c* (pronounced /aɪ/ or /iː/) |
| leisure | *ei* not after *c* |
| height | *ei*, sound /aɪ/ |
| foreign, sovereign, forfeit, counterfeit | *ei*, unstressed vowel |
| their, heir | *ei* |
| species, science, ancient, efficient, sufficient, conscience, deficient | *ie* after *c*, because *ci* makes a /ʃ/ or /s/ + /aɪ/ sound, not /iː/ |

**Common errors:** "recieve", "reciept", "beleive", "acheive", "seige", "wierd", "concieve", "decieve", "freind" (should be *friend*), "neice" (should be *niece*).

## Rule 5: Prefixes

When you add a **prefix**, do not change the spelling of the base word. Just join them. If the prefix ends in the same letter that the base word begins with, you get a **double letter**.

| Prefix | Base word | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dis- | satisfied | **diss**atisfied |
| dis- | similar | **diss**imilar |
| dis- | appear | **dis**appear (one *s*, because *appear* starts with *a*) |
| dis- | appoint | **dis**appoint |
| mis- | spell | **miss**pell |
| mis- | step | **miss**tep |
| mis- | take | **mis**take |
| un- | necessary | **unn**ecessary |
| un- | natural | **unn**atural |
| im- | mature | **imm**ature |
| im- | mortal | **imm**ortal |
| il- | legal | **ill**egal |
| il- | literate | **ill**iterate |
| ir- | regular | **irr**egular |
| ir- | relevant | **irr**elevant |
| ir- | responsible | **irr**esponsible |
| inter- | national | international |
| over- | rule | **overr**ule |
| under- | rate | **underr**ate |
| with- | hold | **withh**old |

**Common errors:** "dissappear", "disapoint", "mispell", "unecessary", "iregular", "irelevant", "witheld" are all **wrong**. Correct: *disappear, disappoint, misspell, unnecessary, irregular, irrelevant, withheld*.

## Rule 6: Suffixes and word endings

### -ful (one *l*)

The suffix **-ful** has only **one *l***: *hopeful, careful, beautiful, grateful, successful, useful, faithful, plentiful, spoonful, handful*.

When you add *-ly*, you get *-fully*: *hopefully, carefully, beautifully, successfully*.

**Common errors:** "beautifull", "carefull", "successfull", "usefull". All wrong.

### Words formed from *all*, *well*, *full* and *till*

| Word | Meaning | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| already | by this time | *all + ready*, one *l* |
| all ready | everyone/everything is ready | two words |
| altogether | completely | *all + together*, one *l* |
| all together | everyone in one place | two words |
| always | at all times | one *l* |
| almost, also, although | — | one *l* |
| welcome | — | one *l* |
| welfare | — | one *l* |
| fulfil (British), fulfilment | — | one *l* in the middle and one at the end in British |
| until | — | one *l* (but *till* has two) |
| skilful | — | British; American *skillful* |

- *The guests had **already** left.* (by then)
- *The guests were **all ready** to leave.* (all of them were ready)
- *That is **altogether** wrong.* (completely)
- *Let's sing **all together**.* (at the same time)

### All right and alright (textbook variation)

*All right* (two words) is the traditional, standard spelling. *Alright* is common in informal writing, but most British style guides and exam guides still treat it as **non-standard**. Use ***all right*** in examinations.

### A lot (always two words)

*A lot* is always **two words**. "Alot" is **always wrong**. (*Allot*, with two *l*s, is a different word meaning "to give out a share": *Each student was allotted a bed space.*)

### Other one-word and two-word pairs

| One word | Two words |
| --- | --- |
| **everyday** (adjective: ordinary): *everyday clothes* | **every day** (each day): *I read every day.* |
| **maybe** (perhaps): *Maybe he will come.* | **may be** (verb: might be): *He may be late.* |
| **sometimes** (now and then) | **some time** (a period of time): *I need some time to think.* |
| **anyway** (in any case) | **any way** (any method): *Is there any way to help?* |
| **into** (movement inside): *She walked into the room.* | **in to** (in + to as separate words): *He handed his work in to the teacher.* |
| **cannot** (normally one word in British English) | **can not** (only for special emphasis or in *can not only…but also*) |
| **nevertheless, nonetheless, notwithstanding, whereas, wherever, furthermore** | — |
| **outside, inside, upstairs** | — |
| **anybody, nobody, somebody** | **any body, some body** (only for a physical body) |

## Rule 7: Tricky word endings

These endings sound alike, so the only way is to learn the words. Group them to help your memory.

### -able and -ible

- **-able** (more common; often added to a complete word): *acceptable, available, comfortable, dependable, manageable, noticeable, reliable, remarkable, suitable, valuable, understandable, considerable, inevitable, indispensable, irreplaceable*
- **-ible** (often added to a root that is not a full word): *audible, compatible, credible, edible, eligible, feasible, flexible, horrible, incredible, invisible, irresistible, legible, permissible, possible, responsible, sensible, terrible, visible, accessible, admissible, susceptible*

**Common errors:** "indispensible" (correct: *indispensable*), "irresistable" (correct: *irresistible*), "responsable" (correct: *responsible*), "permissable" (correct: *permissible*).

### -ance / -ence and -ant / -ent

| -ance / -ant | -ence / -ent |
| --- | --- |
| appearance | existence |
| attendance | independence, independent |
| assistance, assistant | persistence, persistent |
| guidance | interference |
| maintenance | occurrence |
| perseverance | reference |
| relevance, relevant | excellence, excellent |
| tolerance, tolerant | confidence, confident |
| significance, significant | convenience, convenient |
| abundance, abundant | consistence, consistent |
| resistance, resistant | permanence, permanent |
| acquaintance | correspondence, correspondent |
| ignorance, ignorant | preference |
| dominance, dominant | dependence, dependent |

**Note:** *maintain* → *maintenance* (not "maintainance"). *Persevere* → *perseverance* (not "perseverence"). *Exist* → *existence* (not "existance"). *Occur* → *occurrence* (not "occurence" or "occurrance").

**Dependant / dependent (British):** in British English, *dependant* (with *-ant*) is a **noun** (a person who depends on someone): *He has five dependants.* *Dependent* (with *-ent*) is the **adjective**: *She is financially dependent on her parents.* American English uses *dependent* for both.

### -cede, -ceed and -sede

| Ending | Words | How many? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -sede | supersede | only **one** word |
| -ceed | exceed, proceed, succeed | only **three** words |
| -cede | accede, concede, intercede, precede, recede, secede | all the others |

**Watch out:** *proceed* has *-ceed*, but *procedure* has only one *e* before the *d*. "Procedeure" and "proceedure" are wrong.

### -ary and -ery

- **-ary**: *library, February, secretary, dictionary, temporary, ordinary, necessary, January, contemporary, stationary* (not moving)
- **-ery**: *cemetery, stationery* (writing materials), *bakery, surgery, mystery, monastery, brewery*

**Memory tricks:**

- *stationEry* = **E**nvelopes (writing materials); *stationAry* = st**A**nding still.
- *cemetery* has three *e*s and no *a*.
- *February* has an *r* after *Feb*: *Feb-ru-ary*. *Library* has an *r* after *lib*: *lib-rar-y*.

### -ous

*famous, dangerous, nervous, generous, courageous, gorgeous, outrageous, mischievous (not "mischievious"), humorous (no *u* after *o* before *r*, unlike *humour*), vigorous (unlike *vigour*), glamorous (unlike *glamour*)*

**Note:** British *humour, vigour, glamour, honour, labour* keep *-our*, but when *-ous* is added, the *u* goes: *humorous, vigorous, glamorous*. However, *honour* gives *honourable*, *colour* gives *colourful*, and *favour* gives *favourable* (the *u* stays before *-able* and *-ful*).

### -tion, -sion, -ssion, -cian

- **-tion**: *education, information, nation, position, action, competition, attention*
- **-sion** (often after *l, n, r*, or from verbs ending in *-d/-de*): *decision (decide), division (divide), explosion (explode), tension, extension, version, confusion*
- **-ssion** (from verbs ending in *-ss* or *-mit*): *discussion (discuss), permission (permit), admission (admit), transmission, possession, expression, profession*
- **-cian** (a person with a skill): *musician, electrician, politician, magician, physician, technician, beautician, mathematician*

### Pronounce, pronunciation

*pronounce* → ***pronunciation*** (the *o* drops: "pro-NUN-ciation"). "Pronounciation" is **wrong**. In the same way, *denounce* → *denunciation*, *renounce* → *renunciation*.

## How to answer JAMB spelling questions: step by step

### Type 1: "Choose the option that is correctly spelt"

1. **Read all four options before choosing.** They usually differ by just one or two letters.
2. **Find where they differ.** Cover the parts that are the same, and look only at the parts that change (double letters, *ie/ei*, endings).
3. **Apply the rules:** doubling, dropping *-e*, *y → i*, *ie/ei*, prefixes and suffixes.
4. **Use word families.** If you are unsure of *definitely*, think of *definite* (with *finite* inside). If you are unsure of *government*, think of *govern*.
5. **Say the word slowly in syllables** (*Wed-nes-day*, *Feb-ru-ar-y*, *lib-rar-y*). This helps with missing letters, though not with silent letters.
6. **Eliminate** the options that break a rule. Choose the one left.

#### Worked example

> Choose the option that is correctly spelt.
> A. occurence B. ocurrence C. occurrence D. occurrance

1. The options differ in *cc/c*, *rr/r* and *-ence/-ance*.
2. *Occur* has double *c*: B is out.
3. *Occur* is stressed on the last syllable (*ocCUR*), so *r* doubles before a vowel suffix: A is out.
4. The noun ending is *-ence*: D is out.

**Answer: C. occurrence.**

### Type 2: "Choose the option that is wrongly spelt" (or "misspelt")

This is the opposite task. **Read the question twice**: many candidates choose the correctly spelt word by mistake.

1. Check each word against the rules.
2. Three of the words will be correct. The odd one out is the answer.

#### Worked example

> Choose the word that is wrongly spelt.
> A. privilege B. separate C. embarrass D. accomodate

A, B and C are correct. *Accommodate* needs double *c* **and** double *m*. **Answer: D.**

### Type 3: Fill in the gap with the correctly spelt (and correctly meaning) word

Here two of the options may be real words with different meanings (*principal / principle*, *stationary / stationery*). You must check both **spelling** and **meaning**.

#### Worked example

> The bus remained ______ for two hours because of the traffic jam.
> A. stationery B. stationary C. stationnary D. stationairy

C and D are not real words. A means writing materials. B means not moving. **Answer: B.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Mechanics | The technical rules of writing: spelling, punctuation and capitalisation |
| Standard British spelling | The spelling used in Britain and in Nigerian schools and examinations |
| Oxford spelling | The British style of Oxford University Press that uses -ize (e.g. *organize*) but keeps other British spellings |
| Prefix | Letters added to the front of a word, e.g. *un-*, *dis-*, *mis-* |
| Suffix | Letters added to the end of a word, e.g. *-ing*, *-ful*, *-ment* |
| Vowel suffix | A suffix that begins with a vowel, e.g. *-ing*, *-ed*, *-able* |
| Consonant suffix | A suffix that begins with a consonant, e.g. *-ful*, *-ment*, *-ly* |
| Silent letter | A letter that is written but not pronounced, e.g. the *k* in *knife* |
| Homophones | Words that sound the same but are spelt differently and mean different things, e.g. *principal / principle* |
| Stress | The extra force on one syllable of a word; it decides whether a final consonant doubles |
| Word family | Words built from the same root, e.g. *define, definite, definitely* |

## Exam tips and common mistakes

- **Use British spelling:** *colour, centre, travelled, programme, defence, cheque, jewellery*.
- **-ise and -ize are both British.** JAMB will not normally make you choose between them. But some words are **always -ise** (*advertise, advise, supervise, surprise, exercise, televise, compromise*), and *analyse* and *paralyse* are always **-yse** in British English.
- **Double letters** are the biggest trap: *accommodate, embarrass, committee, occurrence, millennium, necessary (one c, two s), harass (one r, two s), recommend (one c, two m)*.
- **Double the final consonant when the stress is on the last syllable:** *beginning, occurred, preferred, referred, controlled*. But *visited, offered, developed, targeted*.
- **British English doubles final -l:** *travelled, cancelled, labelled, counsellor, jewellery*.
- **Noun -ce, verb -se:** *practice/practise, licence/license, advice/advise*.
- **I before E except after C** (when the sound is /iː/), but remember *seize, weird, protein, foreign, height, leisure, their, species, science*.
- ***Pronunciation*** has no *o* after the *n*; ***privilege*** has no *d*; ***supersede*** ends in *-sede*; ***a lot*** is always two words.
- **Read "wrongly spelt" questions twice.** Many candidates pick the correct word by mistake.
- **Check meaning as well as spelling** in fill-in-the-gap questions (*principal/principle*, *stationary/stationery*, *affect/effect*).

## Quick summary

1. JAMB tests **Standard British spelling**: *colour, centre, travelled, defence, programme, jewellery*. American forms are correct in America but not the target in a Nigerian exam.
2. Both **-ise** and **-ize** are accepted in British English (Oxford spelling uses -ize), but some words are always -ise (*advertise, supervise, surprise*), and *analyse/paralyse* are always -yse.
3. **Double the final consonant** for one-syllable words ending in one vowel + one consonant (*running*), and for longer words stressed on the last syllable (*beginning, occurred*). British English also doubles final *-l* (*travelled*).
4. **Drop silent -e** before a vowel suffix (*making*) and keep it before a consonant suffix (*hopeful*), with exceptions (*argument, truly, noticeable*).
5. **Consonant + y** changes to **i** (*happiness, carried*) except before *-ing* (*carrying*). Vowel + y keeps the *y* (*played*).
6. **"I before E except after C"** (when the sound is /iː/), with important exceptions such as *seize, weird, foreign, height, science*.
7. **Prefixes** do not change the base word, which can create double letters: *dissatisfied, misspell, unnecessary, irrelevant*. The suffix **-ful** has one *l*.
8. Learn the **commonly misspelt words** (*accommodate, embarrass, occurrence, privilege, separate, definitely, necessary, pronunciation*) and the **sound-alike pairs** (*principal/principle, stationary/stationery*).

- B. ocurrence
- C. occurrance
- D. occurrence

**Answer: D.** *Occur* has a double *c*. Its final *r* is doubled before a vowel suffix because the stress falls on the last syllable (*ocCUR*), and the noun ends in *-ence*.

**4. Choose the option that is correctly spelt.**

- A. privilege
- B. priviledge
- C. privelege
- D. privilage

**5. Choose the word that is wrongly spelt.**

- A. separate
- B. definitely
- C. goverment
- D. necessary

**Answer: C.** The correct spelling is *government* (*govern* + *-ment*). The other three words are spelt correctly.

- B. principle
- C. principul
- D. principel

- C. centre
